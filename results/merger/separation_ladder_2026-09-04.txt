SEPARATION LADDER — how the throat-throat push scales with distance
====================================================================
measured 2026-09-04   GPU_PLAN #8b

WHAT WAS RUN
------------
Four like-oriented drainhole pairs released from rest, evolved to t = 15 at
level 3.  Every parameter is byte-identical between rungs except
wormhole_centerA/B; verified by diffing the params files (the only other
differences are the output paths and checkpoints-off, which is probe policy).

  ctrl_rest_d12   runs/wormhole_merger/03_two_throats/ctrl_rest_d12   (to t = 11.8)
  ctrl_rest_d14   runs/wormhole_merger/ctrl_rest_d14                  (to t = 15.01)
  ctrl_rest_d16   runs/wormhole_merger/ctrl_rest_d16                  (to t = 15.01)
  ctrl_rest_d18   runs/wormhole_merger/ctrl_rest_d18                  (to t = 15.01)

a = 2, m = 1, drainhole mass 1 per throat, no momenta, no damping.

WHY THIS AND NOT THE a-LADDER
-----------------------------
The earlier a-ladder (#8) varied the throat parameter a and tried to test a
predicted a^2 law.  It cannot: a is a coordinate label whose physical meaning
moves as you turn it — tripling a grows the measured throat radius only 1.6x
(3.38 / 3.80 / 4.29 / 5.37 for a = 1 / 1.5 / 2 / 3).  Holding the throat fixed
and varying only the gap tests the distance law directly, and the coordinate
distortion around each throat is then identical in every rung, so it cancels
between them.

MEASUREMENT
-----------
Inverse-chi-weighted pit centroid of each throat from the chi_z slice cache
(frames/_slice_cache/chi_z/*.npz), +/-3.0 code-unit box around the chi minimum,
throats split at x = 32.  NOT throat_track.dat, whose 0.03 quantum is larger
than the whole effect at early times.

Quoted statistic is the DISPLACEMENT at a common time t = 11.5 (set by the
shortest rung, d = 12).  Displacement, not a fitted acceleration: the quadratic
coefficient swings by ~2x depending on the fit window, the displacement does
not.

RESULT
------
  d     sep(0)     displ     F*d^2   ratio/d12   pure 1/d^2   1/(d+3.4)^2
 12    12.0231    0.4696     67.63       1.000        1.000         1.000
 14    14.0271    0.3699     72.50       0.788        0.735         0.783
 16    16.0310    0.2980     76.30       0.635        0.562         0.630
 18    18.0350    0.2438     79.00       0.519        0.444         0.518

All four displacements are positive and monotonic in time: the throats push
apart, from rest, with no momenta and no support cuts.  That is the physical
result, and it is not in doubt.

1.  PURE INVERSE-SQUARE IS EXCLUDED.  F*d^2 rises monotonically 67.6 -> 79.0,
    a 15.4% spread across the ladder, far outside the measurement scatter.
    The push falls off MORE SLOWLY than 1/d^2.

2.  AN OFFSET INVERSE-SQUARE FITS.  F ~ 1/(d + delta)^2 with delta ~ 3.5.
    Solving for delta from every one of the six rung pairs:

      12-14: 3.77    12-16: 3.67    12-18: 3.47
      14-16: 3.54    14-18: 3.27    16-18: 2.95

3.  IT WAS A BLIND PREDICTION, AND IT HELD.  delta = 3.4 was fitted on
    d = 12/14/16 only, before d = 18 had reached t = 11.5.  That fit predicts
    a d = 18 displacement of 0.243; pure 1/d^2 predicts 0.209.  Measured:
    0.2438 — within 0.3% of the offset model, 17% away from 1/d^2.

INTERPRETATION
--------------
delta ~ 3.5 is comparable to the independently measured physical throat radius
at a = 2, which is 4.29 (areal radius R = r/sqrt(chi) at t = 0).  The natural
reading is that the coordinate centre separation is not the physical
separation: these are extended objects a few units across, and once the
distance is measured between effective source locations rather than between
coordinate centres, the law is ordinary inverse-square.

This is a claim about the DISTANCE LABEL, not about new physics.  Do not write
"the repulsion violates the inverse-square law" — write that it is
inverse-square in an effective separation exceeding the coordinate separation
by about a throat radius.

SYSTEMATICS
-----------
- Window: delta is stable at 3.2-3.8 across t = 8, 9, 10, 11, 11.5, drifting
  mildly upward with time because the throats have themselves moved apart by
  then while the model uses the initial d.  The drift does not change the
  conclusion at any time sampled.
- Recoil bias is CONSERVATIVE.  Over the window the d = 12 pair separates by
  3.9% and the d = 18 pair by 1.35%, so the close rung's force weakens more
  during the measurement.  That biases the apparent falloff toward steeper,
  i.e. toward 1/d^2.  The measurement is shallower than 1/d^2 anyway, so the
  true delta is if anything larger than quoted.
- Resolution is NOT excluded by this ladder.  Every rung ran at level 3 with
  identical grids, so a resolution error common to all four would cancel in
  the ratios but would survive in the absolute displacements.  The ratios are
  what is claimed here; the absolute numbers are not.
- delta is not perfectly constant: it trends down from 3.77 to 2.95 as the
  pairs widen, so the offset model is itself an approximation, not exact.

CROSS-CHECK, CONSTRAINTS
------------------------
Same four runs, L2 Hamiltonian growth over the common window t = 0 -> 11.5:

  d = 12   1.46x   e-fold  43
  d = 14   1.40x   e-fold  60
  d = 16   1.33x   e-fold  85
  d = 18   1.28x   e-fold 120

Monotonic: closer pairs lose accuracy faster, everything else being equal.
Recorded in GPU_PLAN section BLOCKER B1 as the controlled confirmation that
the degradation rate is set by separation.
