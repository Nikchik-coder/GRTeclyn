The scalar-charge law from four rest-release a-points (GPU_PLAN #8)
===================================================================
Measured 2026-09-04.  Tool: grteclyn-wrapper/scripts/validation/apoint_repulsion.py

THE EXPERIMENT.  Two like-oriented drainhole throats, equal mass m = 1,
released FROM REST at separation d = 12, nothing pushing them.  Gravity pulls
them together; the phantom field that holds the throats open pushes them
apart.  The four arms are byte-identical apart from the throat radius a:

  a = 1.0   03_two_throats/ctrl_rest_a1     archived
  a = 1.5   ctrl_rest_a15                   new, 2026-09-04
  a = 2.0   03_two_throats/ctrl_rest_d12    archived (the Stage-2.5 control)
  a = 3.0   ctrl_rest_a3                    new, 2026-09-04

PREDICTION.  |F_phi / F_grav| = (a^2 + m^2) / m^2, so the NET outward force
goes as (a^2 + m^2)/m^2 - 1 = a^2 for m = 1: quadrupling from a = 1 to a = 2,
ninefold from a = 1 to a = 3.

HOW IT IS MEASURED.  Inverse-chi-weighted centroid of each throat pit, taken
from the cached chi_z slices (sub-cell, smooth at 1e-4) -- never from
throat_track.dat, whose 0.03 quantum is the entire early displacement.  The
tool reproduces the two archived numbers before touching the new arms:

  published  ctrl_rest_a1  +0.0042      this tool  +0.00441
  published  ctrl_rest_d12 +0.0127      this tool  +0.01274

WHAT THE QUOTED "ACCELERATION" IS WORTH -- READ THIS BEFORE USING IT.
The archived statistic (quadratic coefficient of the separation over a fixed
time window) is NOT stable, because by t = 10 the four arms sit at different
separations and the force is therefore being sampled at different distances:

  fit window       a=1.5   a=2.0   a=3.0   (ratio to a = 1)
  t = 3.5 .. 10.5   1.92    2.89    4.52
  t = 6.0 .. 10.5   1.64    2.52    3.93
  t = 8.0 .. 14.5   3.77    5.28    8.91

A statistic that moves a = 3 from 3.9 to 8.9 on the choice of window cannot
carry a claim, and the formal fit errors (+/-0.0003) badly understate it.
Reading the local acceleration at matched separation instead is worse still:
the a = 1 arm only ever traverses 0.17 in separation, so differentiating it
is dominated by noise.

THE ROBUST STATISTIC is the displacement itself -- no differentiation, no fit
window, evaluated at matched times:

  1.0     0.0268    0.0592    0.1156    0.1474
  1.5     0.0470    0.1138    0.2158    0.2832
  2.0     0.0678    0.1653    0.3176    0.4155

  ratio to a = 1                                     predicted
    a      t=6.0     t=8.0    t=10.0    t=11.0        (a^2)
  1.0       1.00      1.00      1.00      1.00         1.00
  1.5       1.75      1.92      1.87      1.92         2.25
  2.0       2.53      2.79      2.75      2.82         4.00

These ratios are stable to a few per cent across every time sampled, and are
insensitive to the centroid window (+/-1.5, +/-3, +/-5 code units all agree to
2 %).  This is the measurement.

RESULT.  The push is real, it is outward at every width, and it grows with
throat WIDTH -- but far more slowly than a^2.  Fitting a power law to the
four ratios gives an exponent of about 1.36, and the local slope FALLS with
a (1.58 between a = 1 and 1.5, down to 1.27 between a = 2 and 3), so it is
not a clean power law either: it is flattening.  At a = 3 the prediction
overshoots the measurement by a factor of 2.2.

WHAT COULD CAUSE THE SHORTFALL.  Three candidates; one is now eliminated.

  1. Coordinate under-read.  Already flagged in 03_two_throats/NOTES.md, and
     it does NOT cancel between arms of different width.  If the gauge
     distortion grows with throat size, it suppresses the wide arms more --
     the observed direction, and it grows with a as observed.  ALIVE.
  2. Finite size.  (a^2 + m^2)/m^2 is a point-charge formula.  At a = 3 the
     throat half-width is a quarter of the separation, so higher multipoles
     matter and the point formula should overshoot.  ALIVE.
  3. Under-resolution of the wide throats.  ELIMINATED 2026-09-04: both new
     throats sit on the FINEST level (dx = 0.0625), giving 48 cells across
     the a = 1.5 throat and 96 across the a = 3 one.  The widest arm is the
     best resolved, not the worst, so resolution cannot be making the wide
     arms under-perform.

WHAT THIS LADDER CANNOT DO, AND THE FIX.  Four like-charge arms cannot
separate cause 1 from cause 2: both suppress wide arms, both grow with a.
The campaign already owns the instrument that removes cause 1 -- the
orientation flip, where the two arms have the SAME width so the coordinate
under-read cancels between them.  It exists at a = 2 only, and there it
worked: predicted 1.500, measured 1.511 +/- 0.033.

  Flip arms at the other widths would settle it, and the predictions are far
  apart, so the test is sharp:

     |infall| / |escape| = (1 + R) / (R - 1),   R = (a^2 + m^2)/m^2
       a = 1.0   R = 2.00   ->  3.000
       a = 1.5   R = 3.25   ->  1.889
       a = 2.0   R = 5.00   ->  1.500   (measured 1.511 +/- 0.033)
       a = 3.0   R = 10.0   ->  1.222

  Two runs (flip at a = 1.5 and a = 3) at about 2 GPU-hours each would turn
  this from "the law overshoots, cause unknown" into a decided question: if
  the flip ratios land on 1.889 and 1.222, the law is right and the
  like-charge shortfall is entirely the coordinate under-read; if they do not,
  the (a^2 + m^2)/m^2 scaling itself needs revisiting at finite size.

STATUS.  The sign rule and the existence of the push are unaffected and
remain measured.  The MAGNITUDE law is, as of this file, supported at a = 2
by the flip pair and NOT confirmed by the width ladder; the paper should say
the push grows with throat width, quote the four displacements, and not quote
a^2 as verified until the flip arms exist.
