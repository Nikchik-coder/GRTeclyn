The rate is **flat over t = 49-61** (the readable window ends at t = 61):

0.1706 +/- 0.0031 per unit,
i.e. **tau = 5.86 coordinate units**, holding across 2.0 e-foldings while
the deviation grows from 2.42% to 18.7% of the throat radius.

Before that plateau the apparent rate is much larger and falling. That is not
a faster early mode: R swells before it contracts, so the growing mode and the
settling drift cancel near the crossing and the logarithm of a near-zero
difference has a spurious derivative. **The small-amplitude window is the one
that cannot be fitted**, which is the opposite of the usual situation and the
reason a fit was not used.

## 4. Physics or truncation error? The resolution test

A physical mode seeded by truncation error is *delayed* by refining, at fixed
rate: halving dx shrinks the seed by 2^p and buys tau*p*ln2 more units before
the mode is visible. A numerical departure changes its rate instead.

| arm | dx (finest) | varies | turnover t | peak height | ran to |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `single_hold_t100` | 0.0625 | production arm, t = 100 | 26.0 | +0.0181% | 99.0 |
| `s16ml3_lapse5_sg01_fg` | 0.0625 | same resolution, other tagger | 26.5 | +0.0181% | 39.5 |
| `s15_lapse5_sg01_fg` | 0.125 | half resolution | 20.5 | +0.1163% | 23.5 |
| `s15_lapse5_sg00_fg` | 0.125 | half resolution, no dissipation | 20.5 | +0.0637% | 23.0 |
| `s15_lapse6_sg00_fg` | 0.125 | half resolution, origin collar | none | monotone from t = 0 | 26.0 |

Turnover times are resolved only to each arm's output cadence (1.0 unit for the
production arm, 0.5 for the rest), so the two dx = 0.0625 rows differ by sampling
and not by evolution. The collar arm carries a finite perturbation at t = 0 and
never swells, so it has no knee to compare and is excluded from the shift below.

Halving dx delays the turnover by **+5.5 units** (20.5 -> 26.0)
and shrinks the peak by **3.5x**, i.e. the seed converges at order
1.8. Delay and seed are consistent with a fixed-rate
mode if tau = dt/ln(ratio) = 4.4, against the measured 5.9 above.

**This is suggestive, not decisive.** Both coarse arms die within ~3 units of
their own turnover, so neither ever shows a growth *rate* to compare against.
Two resolutions with one rate between them cannot separate `delayed at fixed
rate` from `slower at coarse dx`. The test that settles it is stated below.

## 5. The same-resolution arm is not a second opinion

`s16ml3_lapse5_sg01_fg` differs from the production arm only in the tagging scheme, and
agrees with it bit for bit -- max |dR| = 0.0e+00 over every shared output
time, and the constraint
norms are bit-identical. For a single centred throat the two taggers build the
same grids, so this is one evolution computed twice. It is a determinism check
and **must not be reported as independent corroboration**.

## 6. What ends the run, and what does not

| | |
|---|---|
| NaN | **none** -- the run reached its stop time t = 100 |
| apparent horizon | never formed (max r_AH = 0 throughout) |
| L2_Ham while the mode grows (t = 1-65) | 2.509e-03 -> 1.166e-03, flat |
| chi at the compactified origin | hits the 1e-8 floor at **t = 61.3** |

The constraint norms are flat -- slightly *decreasing* -- through the entire
growth phase. A growing mode of the constrained system satisfies the constraints,
so global norms give no warning whatever; they cannot be used to certify a
wormhole run as healthy.

After the floor is hit, L2_Ham grows exponentially: doubling every 4.2 units (t >= 70).
After the floor is hit, L2_Mom grows exponentially: doubling every 4.9 units (t >= 70).

That blow-up is numerical, not the mode: it starts when chi is clamped, not
when the throat departs, and the error profile at late times is a front
peaking at the origin and decaying outward -- the opposite of the
throat-peaked profile measured during the growth phase.

## 7. What this does not settle

- **The rate has been measured at one resolution.** Repeat at dx = 0.03125 to
  t = 100. If tau is unchanged and the turnover moves later by a further
  ~5 units, the mode is physical and its rate is converged. If tau moves with
  dx, it is not.
- **The coarse arms must be re-run to t = 100** with the tagger that lets them
  survive, so there is a rate at dx = 0.125 to compare rather than a turnover only.
- **Time discretisation is untested.** One arm at half the CFL factor separates
  it from spatial error.
- **No controlled perturbation was applied.** The seed here is truncation error,
  whose amplitude is not known independently. A deliberate +/- eps perturbation of
  known sign and size turns this into a measurement rather than an observation,
  and the sign test also checks that both signs grow at the same rate.
- **The profile was only scored at the ends.** The plotfiles that would show the
  throat-peaked profile turning into an origin-born front were pruned as the run
  went; keep t = 40-70 next time.
