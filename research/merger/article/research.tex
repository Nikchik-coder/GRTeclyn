\begin{abstract}
We present the first three-dimensional numerical-relativity evolutions of wormhole binaries: pairs of massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainholes held open by a ghost scalar field, at equal masses, one throat compactness and zero spin. Each throat is an unstable fixed point whose e-fold time matches the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach linear rate to within $\clmGgsAgreementFour\%$ at both resolutions, so its lifetime grows only as the logarithm of its initial perturbation. Two throats obey a charge-sign rule: like scalar charges repel and opposite charges attract, with a measured attraction-to-repulsion ratio of $\clmMatchedSignRatio\pm\clmMatchedSignRatioErr$ against the predicted $\clmScalarQRatio$, so only opposite-signed pairs merge. They do not fuse into one wormhole. A head-on collision forms a common marginally outer trapped surface around both still-open throats, born with about their combined area; its irreducible mass exceeds the ADM mass, so the Penrose inequality fails for this matter. The horizon then loses $\clmHeadonCsmShrinkArea\%$ of its area as it swallows the phantom field and approaches the Schwarzschild radius of the pair's mass: a black hole with two wormholes inside it. An orbital merger leaves a remnant of nearly the same size. Between $\clmSpiralBoundaryLow\%$ and $\clmSpiralBoundaryHigh\%$ of the circular momentum a scattering orbit turns into a plunge; the scatterers form no horizon and both mouths inflate, the one nearest that boundary radiating the most gravitational-wave energy measured. Gravitational waves are measured from both conversions. A lone collapsing throat sheds its seeded quadrupole as a burst ringing at the Schwarzschild frequency of the black hole it becomes, and every encounter radiates its own burst of gravitational waves and a scalar dipole of comparable energy and negative sign, which a horizon switches off. A throat survives at most a few orbital periods for any plausible perturbation, and the negative-energy dipole would pump rather than drain the orbit, so these binaries have no inspiral. Their signal is a single burst with no chirp before it; a search of $\clmDetLivetime$\,h of LIGO O3b data finds none. If such throats formed as late as $z\approx\clmDetZConv$, their collapse would supply halo-independent heavy black-hole seeds, and their encounters millihertz bursts resolvable by LISA. The throats that inflate would create expanding anti-trapped regions from regular data, which the null energy condition otherwise forbids.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Wheeler's spacetime foam is a sea of handles---wormholes that open and close on the Planck scale~\cite{wheeler1955,wheeler1957,misner_wheeler1957}. Several mechanisms promote them to macroscopic size: inflation stretches a Planck-scale Lorentzian wormhole with the background~\cite{roman1993}; bubble and domain-wall cosmologies produce post-inflationary regions joined through wormholes~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017,deng_vilenkin2017,gouttenoire2024}; and a gas of relic wormholes has been studied as a cosmology in its own right~\cite{kirillov2008,kirillov2011}. The bubble and wall wormholes are not traversable and pinch off into black holes~\cite{deng2017,deng_vilenkin2017}; none of this work evolves a traversable relic---what one does, and what two of them do to each other. That is the subject of this paper.

Two classical facts govern the dynamics. First, the simplest evolvable traversable wormhole---the Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole held open by a ghost scalar~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973,morris_thorne1988,visser1995}---is unstable~\cite{gonzalez2009lin,bronnikov2011,cremona2019}: depending on the sign of the perturbation, the throat collapses to a black hole or inflates, on a timescale set by the throat radius~\cite{hayward1999,shinkai2002,gonzalez2009,doroshkevich2009,hayward2009,novikov2019}, as 3+1 evolutions of the massless throat confirm nonlinearly~\cite{shirokov2026}. As a quantum field coupled to gravity a ghost destabilises the vacuum unless its effective theory is cut off at low energy with Lorentz violation~\cite{cline2004}; we treat it as a classical phenomenological model. Second, Geroch's theorem forbids classical topology change~\cite{geroch1967,tipler1977}: two wormholes cannot fuse into one, and none can be created classically. Creation is quantum~\cite{hawking1988}, so our evolutions start at $t=0$ from the newborn state---the stipulated initial data of every numerical binary, here with a physical origin attached.

Together these facts set selection rules. No astrophysical assembly channel exists: a throat decays faster than dynamical friction or radiation reaction could pair it. A primordial pair born from foam needs no assembly---birth and collision coincide, at a separation comparable to the objects---and its evolution is a race between orbital collision and throat decay, both rates fixed by the classical solution. The scalar adds a second rule: a phantom field repels like charges and attracts opposite ones, so like-signed pairs scatter while opposite-signed pairs fall together; and the sign of a lone throat's perturbation selects collapse---a wormhole-to-primordial-black-hole channel beside the standard ones~\cite{zeldovich1967,carr_hawking1974,carr2021}---or inflation. A foam population thus splits into four fates---merger, scattering, collapse, inflation---across birth separation, perturbation and relative sign, the space mapped here. The binary evolved has three asymptotic ends: two independent wormholes, each leading to its own far universe. A foam handle differs---its two mouths belong to one wormhole and carry opposite scalar charge by construction---so our opposite-signed pairs reproduce a handle's charges but not its topology. What merges is not two wormholes into one but two wormholes into one black hole containing both throats.

The observable is not a chirp but a population of bursts from the merger epoch, redshifted into whatever band the mass scale selects~\cite{caprini2018,nanograv2023,lisa2017,lvk2021}. The epoch-independent deliverable is the energy radiated per event and its spectrum in units of the source's ADM mass (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst}). Every waveform behind it is extracted in the near zone, on spheres at $R=\clmExtractRadiusTen$--$\clmExtractRadiusFortyFour$, and none is extrapolated to null infinity: each radiated energy carries the spread across its spheres as a near-zone error bar (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw}). Collapse, alone or in a merger, also bears on JWST's overmassive black holes at $z\gtrsim4$--$6$, which favour heavy seeds of $\clmDetIntroSeedLo$--$\clmDetIntroSeedHi\,M_\odot$ from formation channels that remain contested~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024,inayoshi2020}; Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} explores what a population of such throats would imply for heavy seeds and millihertz bursts, conditional on the epoch at which they form, which our evolutions do not fix.

Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:setup} give the model and numerics; Secs.~\ref{sec:single}--\ref{sec:spiral} follow the lone wormhole, two throats at rest, and the head-on and orbital mergers; Sec.~\ref{sec:gw} treats the radiation of every channel and Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} confronts it with LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data; Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} draws the consequences and Sec.~\ref{sec:repro} gives reproducibility and cost. Appendix~\ref{app:health} holds the code-health record and Appendix~\ref{app:supp} supplementary measurements. \section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Field equations}
\label{sec:model:field}

The matter is a single real, massless phantom scalar: gravity couples to \emph{minus} the canonical stress tensor,
\begin{equation}
T_{ab} = -\Bigl(\nabla_a\phi\,\nabla_b\phi - \tfrac12 g_{ab}\,\nabla_c\phi\nabla^c\phi\Bigr),
\qquad V(\phi)\equiv 0,
\label{eq:phantom}
\end{equation}
so the energy density seen by normal observers, $\rho=-\tfrac12\bigl(\Pi^2+\gamma^{ij}\partial_i\phi\,\partial_j\phi\bigr)$ with $\Pi=(\partial_t\phi-\beta^i\partial_i\phi)/\alpha$, is non-positive everywhere, and the null energy condition fails wherever $\phi$ varies---the exotic stress that holds a throat open. The field itself obeys the canonical wave equation $\Box\phi=0$, evolved in first-order form in $\phi$ and $\Pi$.

\subsection{Drainhole initial data}
\label{sec:model:drainhole}

Each body is a massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973}. In isotropic radius $r$ about the throat centre, with
\begin{equation}
X = \frac{r - a^2/4r}{a},\quad
\Omega = 1 + \frac{a^2}{4r^2},\quad
u = \frac{m}{a}\Bigl(\arctan X - \frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the exact static solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom} is
\begin{equation}
\alpha = e^{u},\quad
\gamma_{ij} = e^{-2u}\,\Omega^2\,\delta_{ij},\quad
\phi = C\arctan X,
\label{eq:drainhole}
\end{equation}
with $4\pi a^2C^2 = a^2+m^2$, $K_{ij}=0$, $\Pi=0$ and zero shift; in the evolved conformal variables $\chi = e^{2u}\Omega^{-2}$ and $h_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$. Both constraints hold identically at $t=0$. Here $a$ is the throat scale and $m$ the ADM mass, carried by the lapse rather than the conformal factor, so $\chi$ at the throat stays in $[\clmChiThroatLo,\clmChiThroatHi]$ for $m/a\le1$ and mass costs no resolution. The minimal surface sits at $r_t=\tfrac12\bigl(m+\sqrt{m^2+a^2}\bigr)$, where $X=m/a$, so its areal radius is analytic: $R_{\min}=e^{-u(r_t)}\sqrt{a^2+m^2}=\sqrt{a^2+m^2}\,\exp\!\bigl[(m/a)\arctan(a/m)\bigr]$, which is $a$ at $m=0$. The production throat $a=\clmThroatA$, $m=\clmThroatM$ has $R_{\min}=\sqrt5\,e^{\frac12\arctan2}=\clmRstar$ at $r_t=\tfrac12(1+\sqrt5)=\clmThroatCoordRadius$. The point $r\to0$ is the far side's spatial infinity, compactified ($\chi\sim r^4$). At $m=0$ the throat is ultrastatic ($\alpha\equiv1$, $M_{\rm ADM}=0$).

\subsection{Two throats in one slice}
\label{sec:model:binary}

Two throats at separation $d$ (one-body radii $r_A$, $r_B$) start from their combined fields,
\begin{equation}
u = u_A + u_B,\qquad
\psi = 1 + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_A}-1\bigr) + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_B}-1\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\chi = e^{2u}\psi^{-4}$, $\alpha = e^{u}$ and $\phi = \phi_A + \sigma\,\phi_B$, $\sigma=\pm1$; the constant tail of the $\arctan$ profiles is subtracted so that $\phi\to0$ at the boundary. This is the starting point of the constraint solve of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:solve}, which turns it into initial data. Momentum is carried by the exact boost of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:boost}: the throats sit at $x=\mp d/2$ with $P^i=(0,\pm p,0)$, momenta of magnitude $p$ in units of the one-body mass, tangential to the separation and opposite on the two throats, so the pair carries no net momentum and orbital angular momentum $J=pd$; $p=0$ is a release from rest. The relative sign $\sigma$ is the pair's one discrete parameter. Far from its throat each body is a mass $m$ carrying the scalar charge $q=Ca$ ($\phi_A\simeq-q/r_A$ once the constant tail is subtracted), and a phantom mediator \emph{repels} like charges and \emph{attracts} opposite ones, so two throats at rest a large distance $d$ apart attract with
\begin{equation}
F=\frac{m^2}{d^2}\,\bigl(1-\sigma Q\bigr),\qquad
Q\equiv\Bigl|\frac{F_\phi}{F_{\rm grav}}\Bigr|=\frac{4\pi q^2}{m^2}=\frac{a^2+m^2}{m^2}>1
\label{eq:force}
\end{equation}
always ($Q=\clmScalarQ$ for the production throat): a like-signed pair repels and never merges under its own gravity, and $\sigma=\clmMergerSign$ is the merger channel.

\subsection{Constraint-solved data and matched placement}
\label{sec:model:solve}

With $\Pi=0$, $K=0$ and $\gamma_{ij}=\Psi^4\delta_{ij}$ the Hamiltonian constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom} reads, in flat operators,
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2\Psi-\pi|\nabla\phi|^2\,\Psi+\tfrac18\hat A_{ij}\hat A^{ij}\,\Psi^{-7}=0 .
\label{eq:hamsolve}
\end{equation}
The single drainhole solves it exactly. For a pair we solve it for a correction $w$ to the starting $\Psi$ on the whole initial mesh hierarchy with AMReX's multigrid~\cite{amrex}, holding $\phi$, $\Pi$ and the lapse fixed (a Robin outer boundary); a pair with momentum solves both constraints together (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:boost}).

\emph{Which throat is each mouth?} Near its centre $\Psi=c/r+d_c+O(r)$, and the inversion $r'=c^2/r$ turns this into an asymptotically flat end---the mouth's own far universe---with ADM mass and scalar charge
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm far}=2c\,d_c,\qquad Q_{\rm far}=\frac{4Cc^2}{a},
\label{eq:farside}
\end{equation}
both frozen in time, like a puncture's individual mass. The isolated drainhole has $M_{\rm far}=-m\,e^{\pi m/a}=\clmFarMassIso$ and $Q_{\rm far}=\sqrt{a^2+m^2}\,e^{\pi m/a}/\sqrt{4\pi}=\clmFarChargeIso$. A pair $(M_{\rm far},Q_{\rm far})$ names one isolated drainhole, and its near-side mass $m'$ is the mouth's one-body mass.

A second throat in the slice rescales each mouth: near $A$ the companion's constants multiply the throat's own field, $\Psi\to e^{-u_B(x_A)/2}\,\Psi$, and because $\phi$ fixes a static throat's coordinate size, the puncture coefficient $c$ alone cannot undo this. The \emph{matched placement} gives each throat a coordinate scale $\lambda$ ($a\to\lambda a$ and $m\to\lambda m$ in $\phi$, $u$ and $\Omega$; $C$ is scale-free), sets $\lambda=(c/c_{\rm iso})^2$ with $c_{\rm iso}=\tfrac a2e^{\pi m/2a}$, which holds $Q_{\rm far}$ at its isolated value, and iterates $c$ until $M_{\rm far}$ is isolated too. Each mouth is then locally the isolated static drainhole (Table~\ref{tab:placement}): its $R_{\min}$ lands within $\clmMatchedRminDev\%$ of $R_\star$ without being imposed, and $m'=1$. With momentum the iteration converges the same way ($d=12$, $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$: $R_{\min}$ within $\clmMatchedRminDevOrbit\%$ of the lone boosted throat's).

\emph{The pair's mass.} With $\Psi\to1+M_{\rm ADM}/2r$, Gauss's theorem on Eq.~\eqref{eq:hamsolve}, whose punctures contribute $-4\pi c_X\delta_X$, gives
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm ADM}=2\sum_X c_X-\frac1{2\pi}\int\Bigl(\pi|\nabla\phi|^2\Psi-\tfrac18\hat A_{ij}\hat A^{ij}\Psi^{-7}\Bigr)d^3x ,
\label{eq:madmvol}
\end{equation}
which returns the exact throat's $M=1$ to $\clmVolumeIdentityExact\%$ and, unlike a fit to the monopole tail, does not read the outer boundary's offset of $w$ as mass. For matched pairs at rest (Table~\ref{tab:energy}) the excess over two isolated throats follows
\begin{equation}
E_b\equiv M_{\rm ADM}-2m=-\lambda^2\,\frac{m^2}{d}\,\bigl(1+\sigma Q\bigr)
\label{eq:ebind}
\end{equation}
for both signs, to $\clmEnergyLawDevNear\%$ at $d=8$ and $\clmEnergyLawDevFar\%$ at $d=48$. It is the Newtonian binding $-\lambda^2m^2/d$ plus the phantom cross energy $-\sigma\!\int\nabla\phi_A\!\cdot\!\nabla\phi_B\,d^3x=-\sigma\lambda^2(a^2+m^2)/d$ of two charges $\lambda q$, with $\lambda\to1$ as $d\to\infty$; the solve adds nothing else. In the merger channel $\sigma=-1$ the scalar term is positive and wins: the attracting pair weighs \emph{more} than its parts, $E_b\,d\to\clmEnergyLimitAttract$, and the repelling pair less, $E_b\,d\to\clmEnergyLimitRepel$. Read at fixed charges, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ebind} would make the $\sigma=-1$ pair repel. Equation~\eqref{eq:force} follows instead if the scalar part acts at fixed potential, $F_\phi=+\partial E_\phi/\partial d$, as between conductors held at fixed voltage: the value of $\phi$ at each throat's far infinity cannot change, while the flux through the throat can. The evolution decides for fixed potential: rest pairs on matched data pull and push in the ratio $\clmMatchedSignRatio\pm\clmMatchedSignRatioErr$ that Eq.~\eqref{eq:force} predicts (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:sign}).

\emph{Units of this paper.} Masses and times are in units of the drainhole parameter $m=1$. \subsection{Throats with momentum}
\label{sec:model:boost}

A throat with ADM momentum $p$ is the exact drainhole boosted to $v=p/\sqrt{m^2+p^2}$ and cut at $t=0$: the metric, $K_{ij}$, $\phi$ and $\Pi$ together, with the boosted lapse and shift, so that the slice is an exact solution of both constraints and the scalar moves with its throat ($\Pi\neq0$). On the slice every $\chi$ contour, the throat's included, is an ellipsoid contracted along the motion by $1/\gamma=m/\sqrt{m^2+p^2}$, as a boosted black hole's horizon is; the measured axis ratios follow this law to $\clmBoostAxisResidual$ at every $p$ up to $\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:boost_contraction}).

A pair is built from two boosted throats as in Sec.~\ref{sec:model:solve}, with the companion's $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ windowed out inside each throat, and the solve then corrects both constraints, for $w$ and a vector potential $W$ ($\hat A\to\hat A+\mathbb{L}W$). Three checks anchor it: on a lone boosted throat the solve is idle ($\max|w|\le\clmBoostSolveW$, $\max|W|\le\clmBoostSolveWvec$, the far side isolated to $\clmBoostSolveFar$); on the rest pair it reproduces the scalar solve's $c$, $\sigma$ and $w_0$ to $\clmBoostRestRepro$; and on the flipped $d=12$, $p=\pm\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ pair on the production grid the far sides come out the isolated throat's to $\clmBoostPairFar$, each mouth's $R_{\min}$ lands $\clmBoostPairRmin\%$ from the isolated value, and the solved throat-shell Hamiltonian is at most $\clmBoostPairShellHam$ rms across the refinement levels. The pair's mass is the boosted background's own ADM energy---each throat's $\gamma\lambda_X m$ and its puncture shift, by the full surface integral of the closed forms---plus the monopole of $w$ on the box faces, since Eq.~\eqref{eq:madmvol} needs conformally flat data with $K=\Pi=0$; where both apply, the face value reads $\clmBoostFaceBias\%$ below it.

\subsection{Declared seeds}
\label{sec:model:seeds}

The instability is probed with declared perturbations of the conformal factor only: a Gaussian shell on the minimal surface,
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon\, g(r)\bigr],\qquad
g = e^{-(r-r_t)^2/w^2},\quad w = a/4,
\label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
which changes the throat's areal radius by a fraction ${\approx}\clmSeedArealFactor\varepsilon$, and a quadrupolar factor
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon_2\, g(r)\,P_2(\cos\theta)\bigr]
\end{equation}
about the $z$ axis through the throat. Neither seed is constraint-solved. $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ stay untouched, so the momentum constraint holds at $t=0$; but the drainhole solves the linear equation $\nabla^2\Psi=\pi|\nabla\phi|^2\Psi$ ($\Psi^4=\chi^{-1}$, flat operators), so the spherical seed leaves exactly $\mathcal H=-8\varepsilon\,\Psi^{-4}(1+\varepsilon g)^{-5}(\nabla^2g+2\nabla\ln\Psi\cdot\nabla g)$---confined to the shell and, the shell being narrow, not small there: its peak is $\clmSeedDefectPeak$--$\clmSeedDefectPeakHi\times16\pi|\rho|$ at $\varepsilon=\pm10^{-2}$. The quadrupolar seed's defect is pure $\ell=2$ at first order. CCZ4 feeds the defect into $\Theta$ and, within the first steps, into $\mathcal M$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:constraints}(b)). Above $\varepsilon\simeq\clmSeedBagThreshold$ the spherical seed turns the minimal surface into a maximum between two minima. The spherical seed selects the branch but cannot radiate: a spherical collapse has no $\ell\ge2$ moment. The quadrupolar seed, with no $\ell=0$ part at first order, leaves the branch to it and gives a collapsing throat a quadrupole to shed---a lone throat's only gravitational-wave signal (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:gw}).

\subsection{Evolution scheme}
\label{sec:setup:scheme}

We evolve CCZ4~\cite{alic2012} with damping $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=(\clmCczKappaOne,0,\clmCczKappaThree)$, $1{+}\log$ slicing~\cite{bona1995} and a Gamma-driver shift~\cite{alcubierre2003gauge,campanelli2006,baker2006} with $\eta=\clmShiftEta$, both started from the initial data's lapse and shift, fourth-order stencils, RK4 and Kreiss--Oliger dissipation $\sigma=\clmKOsigma$. The time step is $\Delta t=\clmTimeStepFactor\,\Delta x$, an order of magnitude below the usual CCZ4 Courant factor, because larger steps fail: a multiplier of $\clmControlDtMedium$ departs from the production run at $t\approx\clmControlDtMediumDeparture$, and $\clmControlDtLarge$ dies at $t=\clmControlDtLargeDeath$. Every resolution statement below is at fixed $\Delta t/\Delta x$.

\subsection{Grid, boundaries and regularisation}
\label{sec:setup:grid}

The domain is a cube of side $L=\clmBoxL$ centred on the system, with a $\clmBaseN^3$ base grid ($\Delta x=\clmDxBase$). Nested boxes, halving per level and centred on the tracked throats (half-width $\clmFinestHalfWidth$ per throat at the production finest level), are re-drawn every $\clmRegridSteps$ steps. Production runs use \clmProdLevels{} refinement levels (finest $\Delta x=\clmFinestDx$); resolution arms go to levels $\clmResArmsLevelLo$--$\clmResArmsLevelHi$ where stated, and every binary deeper than level 3 and two lone-throat arms use $L=\clmBigBoxL$: the same cells in a box twice as wide. Level $\ell$ thus means a finest cell $\Delta x=\clmDxBase/2^\ell$. The one exception is the octant of an $L=\clmFourBoxL$ box that carries the inflating throat (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:fate}): its base spacing is $\clmFourDxBase$, so its \clmFourLevel{} levels give $\Delta x=\clmFourFinestDx$, the resolution of our level-3 arms, and we quote it by that $\Delta x$. Outer boundaries are Sommerfeld, backed by a spherical sponge about the box centre over the outer quarter of the half-width---$r=\clmSpongeInner$--$\clmSpongeOuter$ at $L=\clmBoxL$ and $\clmSpongeInnerBig$--$\clmSpongeOuterBig$ at $L=\clmBigBoxL$---in which extra dissipation ramps to $\sigma_{\rm sp}=\clmSpongeStrength$ as the \clmSpongePower{} power of the depth; every extraction sphere lies inside its inner radius. The sponge is Kreiss--Oliger dissipation only: it damps grid-scale noise but is transparent to long-wavelength gauge and physical waves, which reflect from the box faces (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}). The vacuum controls (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:bbh}) instead run \texttt{GRTeclyn}'s binary-black-hole example with its own numerical settings and no sponge.

Each throat carries a compactified second infinity at its centre, where $\chi\sim r^4$, so the evolved variables are floored at $\chi\ge\clmChiFloor$ and $\alpha\ge\clmLapseFloor$ (a variant floors $\chi$ at $\clmChiFloorVariant$ and applies $\clmChiRhsFloor$ only where $1/\chi$ is evaluated); $\chi$-floor twins at $\clmChiFloor$ and $\clmChiFloorVariant$ re-converge to \clmControlChiDigits{} digits after touching the floor. Every boosted evolution except the $t{=}0$ contraction runs and the $d=12$, $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$ arm switches the $1{+}\log$ source off within coordinate radius $\clmFreezeRadiusFull$ of each tracked throat centre, the far side's compactified infinity, restoring it by $\clmFreezeRadiusStart$ (the throat sits at $r_t=\clmThroatCoordRadius$), so that the moving puncture does not drive the slicing. The $d=6$ chain and the tangential $d=12$ arms at $p\ge\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$, bar two undamped controls, damp the scalar exponentially wherever the lapse has collapsed below $\clmDampLapseStart$: inside the merged cores, and between the scatterers' mouths after the approach.

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:setup:diagnostics}

Every coarse step the code writes the volume-averaged norms $\|\mathcal H\|=(\int\mathcal H^2\,\mathrm{d}V/L^3)^{1/2}$ and $\|\mathcal M\|=(\int\mathcal M_i\mathcal M_i\,\mathrm{d}V/L^3)^{1/2}$, evaluated with fourth-order differences on the base grid over the whole domain after the finer levels are restricted onto it: they do not resolve the throats, monitor code health rather than local accuracy, and compare only within one box. Both throat centres are tracked, and about each the areal radius $R(r)$ is scanned on coordinate spheres out to a fixed edge; its minimum is the throat, and an inflating throat that outgrows the scan is bounded from above by the edge reading. On the same spheres the null expansions $\theta_\pm$ are computed with the outward direction fixed by \emph{increasing areal radius}, so that a dynamic throat is marginally \emph{anti}-trapped~\cite{hochberg_visser1998,hayward1999}; the in-code monitor orients by coordinate radius, which points the wrong way beyond a throat, and serves only as a live monitor.

The star-shaped scan reports the outermost coordinate sphere that is trapped everywhere ($\max\theta_+=0$, $\theta_-<0$). A trapped sphere lies inside the apparent horizon, so this sphere---``the scan's MOTS'' below---bounds the marginally outer trapped surface from inside. On its spheres, as on the finder's surfaces of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:mots}, we evaluate the areal radius $R$ and the Misner--Sharp mass~\cite{misner_sharp1964},
\begin{equation}
\frac{2M_{\rm MS}}{R} \;=\; 1 + \frac{R^{2}\,\langle\theta_+\theta_-\rangle}{4},
\label{eq:misner_sharp}
\end{equation}
with the product averaged over the sphere and $\theta_\pm$ computed for the null normals $k^a=n^a+s^a$ and $l^a=n^a-s^a$ ($n^a$ the slice's unit normal, $s^a$ the sphere's outward unit normal, so $k\cdot l=-2$); with the normalisation $k\cdot l=-1$ the factor $1/4$ becomes $1/2$. On an exact MOTS $M_{\rm MS}=R/2$; on the scan's spheres $M_{\rm MS}$ exceeds $R/2$ by at most $\clmShapeSystLone\%$ about a lone collapsed throat, which stays nearly round.

Radiation is read from $\Psi_4$ on spheres at $R=\clmExtractRadiusTen$, $\clmExtractRadiusFourteen$, $\clmExtractRadiusEighteen$ (and $\clmExtractRadiusTwentyTwo$ on the single-throat wave arms) on the single throats, and at $R=\clmExtractRadiusTwenty$, $\clmExtractRadiusTwentyEight$, $\clmExtractRadiusThirtySix$, $\clmExtractRadiusFortyFour$ on every $L=\clmBigBoxL$ binary, the head-on adding the single throats' three, decomposed in spin-weight $-2$ harmonics about $z$, the orbital axis. The head-ons fall along $x$: there the $z$-based $(2,0)$ is $-h'_{20}/2$ of the quadrupole $h'_{20}$ about the collision axis and carries a quarter of its power, the $(2,\pm2)$ the rest ($|h_{22}/h_{20}|=\sqrt{3/2}$ to $\clmHeadonAxisRatioDev\%$ on every sphere of both head-ons), so their $(2,0)$ is quoted as $h'_{20}$. Every amplitude quoted is $r\Psi_4$; on the head-on the in-code and offline pipelines agree at the burst peak to $\clmPsiPipelines\%$, while on the single throats the in-code stream is dominated by its grid-scale floor and only the offline one is used. A scalar stream is read on the single throats' spheres and, on the binaries, at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusInner$, $20$, $\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$ and $44$ (the head-on adding $10$ and $18$), where the offline pipeline also reads $\Psi_4$: $\phi$ and $\Pi$ in spin-weight-$0$ harmonics, $A_{\ell m}=R\,\phi_{\ell m}$, and the kinematic flux $F_{\rm kin}=-R^2\oint\Pi\,\partial_r\phi\,\mathrm{d}\Omega$, positive for an outgoing \emph{canonical} wave (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}). An opt-in per-cell autopsy records the location and one-step history of any floating-point failure.

\subsection{Marginally outer trapped surfaces}
\label{sec:setup:mots}

A marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS) is a closed two-surface of the slice on which the outgoing null
expansion vanishes, $\theta_+=0$, while the ingoing one is negative, $\theta_-<0$; the outermost MOTS is the
apparent horizon. A MOTS is read off a single slice, unlike the event horizon, and since the phantom violates
the null energy condition it need not lie inside one: every ``horizon'', ``censored'' and ``black hole'' in this
paper is a statement about MOTSs on the evolved slices.

The finder runs in post-processing on every archived slice, so horizon coverage matches the plot cadence. It
expands the surface about the tracked centre as $r=h(\theta,\phi)$ in real spherical harmonics up to degree
$\ell_{\max}=\clmFlowFinderLmax$ on the head-on slices and $\ell_{\max}=\clmFlowFinderLmaxAlt$ on the orbital
ones, and solves $\theta_+[h]=0$ by Newton iteration on metric and extrinsic-curvature data interpolated from
level 3 on the head-on and level 2 on the orbits, whatever the run's finest level, with the areal orientation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}. The first surface of a run is found by the damped fast
flow~\cite{gundlach1998}; each later slice warm-starts from the previous surface and converges in a few
iterations until every projected harmonic of $\theta_+$ is below the solver tolerance of $10^{-6}$. From the converged surface
we quote the areal radius $R=\sqrt{A/4\pi}$, the Misner--Sharp mass of Eq.~\eqref{eq:misner_sharp}, and the
harmonic content beyond $\ell=0$ as the deformation. Its flow stage recovers Kerr--Schild Schwarzschild
($R=2M$ to $\clmFlowFinderSchw\%$, $M_{\rm MS}=M$ to $\clmFlowFinderSchwMass\%$) and finds no trapped surface
outside the time-symmetric Ellis throat. Every binary horizon number in this paper---formation times, $R$,
$M_{\rm MS}$---is read from these surfaces, the lone throats' nearly round collapse horizons from the star-shaped
scan (within $\clmShapeSystLone\%$, Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}). On a binary the scan is only an inner bound
and a live monitor: a fully trapped round sphere lies strictly inside a deformed MOTS, so the scan under-reads a
forming horizon, or misses it.

\subsection{The static throat as a fixed point}
\label{sec:single:fixedpoint}

Evolved without a declared perturbation, the exact drainhole holds $R_\star=\clmRstar$ to $\clmHoldTolLevelThree\%$ for $\clmHoldTimeLevelThree$ time units at level 3 and to $\clmHoldTolOnePct\%$ until $t\simeq\clmHoldTimeOnePct$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a)); at level 4 it is exact to \clmLevelFourDigits{} digits at $t=30$. The departure moves \emph{later} by $\clmOnsetShiftLo$--$\clmOnsetShiftHi$ units per halving of the cell---a truncation-noise seed of fixed growth rate handed a smaller amplitude. Level 2 dies of an origin failure at $t=\clmLevelTwoDeath$ with the throat still exact to $\clmLevelTwoDev\%$. The hold scales with the mass: at level 3 the $m=\clmHalfMassM$ throat holds $\clmHalfMassHoldTol\%$ only to $t\approx\clmHalfMassHoldTime$, against $\clmHoldTimeLevelThree$ at $m=\clmThroatM$, and dies at its origin at $t=\clmHalfMassDeath$.

\subsection{The unstable mode}
\label{sec:single:instability}

The departure is a single exponential mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b)): fits below $\clmTauFitDev\%$ deviation give $\tau=\clmTauLevelThree$ (level 3) and $\clmTauLevelFour$ (level 4), i.e.\ $T\equiv\tau\,\alpha_{\rm th}/R_\star=\clmProperEfoldThree$ and $\clmProperEfoldFour$, where $\alpha_{\rm th}=e^{u(r_t)}=\clmThroatLapse$ is the static lapse at the throat, so that $T$ is the proper-time e-fold in units of the throat's light-crossing time. Our drainhole is the $\gamma_1=m/a=\clmThroatGammaOne$ member of the static family of Gonz\'alez, Guzm\'an and Sarbach (their metric with $b=a$, $\gamma_0=-\pi\gamma_1/2$), for which their linear analysis gives $T=\clmGgsMatched$; their Table~I spans $T=\clmGgsMassless$ at the massless end to $\clmGgsMassive$~\cite{gonzalez2009lin}. Solving the linearised field equations of this solution ourselves---their regularised master equation and, independently, the reduction in the gauge $\delta\phi=0$---gives $T=\clmLinT$, i.e.\ $\tau_{\rm lin}=\clmLinTau\,M$. The measured e-fold matches it at both resolutions, to $\clmGgsAgreement\%$ at level 3 and $\clmGgsAgreementFour\%$ at level 4; larger departures grow more slowly. The seed is truncation error, and its sign flips with resolution---level 3 collapses, level 4 inflates---confirming from exact massive data the two branches found for the massless throat~\cite{shirokov2026}.

\subsection{Branching: collapse versus inflation}
\label{sec:single:branching}

A declared kick, Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed}, overrides the truncation seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c)). At $\varepsilon=\pm10^{-2}$ and $\pm10^{-3}$ the fate is always opposite to the push, each $\pm$ pair crosses at the $\varepsilon$-independent $t_\times\simeq\clmCrossingMean$, and a tenfold smaller kick arrives $\clmMatchedDelayLo$--$\clmMatchedDelayHi$ units later at matched horizon radius---linear response on the same mode. The branching survives refinement: at level 4, whose own seed inflates the unkicked throat, $+10^{-2}$ still forms a horizon at $t=\clmHorizonTimeLevelFour$ and $-10^{-2}$ still inflates, the twins crossing at $t=\clmCrossingLevelFour$ against level 3's $\clmCrossingLevelThree$. The branch point is the throat's, not the grid's or the kick's. The kick also sets the clock: horizons form at $t=\clmHorizonTimeEpsTwo$ ($\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$) and $t=\clmHorizonTimeEpsThree$ ($+10^{-3}$), against $t\le\clmHorizonTimeNoise$ for the unkicked throat (its first scanned slice after departure already holds a MOTS). At $\varepsilon=\pm0.1$ the data are no longer perturbative (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c)): $+0.1$ turns the minimal surface into a maximum between two minima and is trapped from $t=\clmCeilingPlusMotsTime$, while $-0.1$, which narrows the throat to $R_{\min}=\clmCeilingMinusRzero$, re-expands to $\clmCeilingMinusRmax$ by $t=\clmCeilingMinusRmaxTime$ and then collapses as well, trapped from $t=\clmCeilingMinusMotsTime$---against the sign rule. Both die at the compactified origin ($t=\clmCeilingDeathPlus$ and $\clmCeilingDeathMinus$, NaN in $h_{11}$), the failure that also ends the $+10^{-3}$ arm at $t=\clmKickThreeDeath$, before the lapse has collapsed anywhere. Their Hamiltonian defect is $\clmCeilingHamLo$--$\clmCeilingHamHi\times$ that of the $1\%$ arms in the box norm and $\clmCeilingDefectLo$--$\clmCeilingDefectHi\times16\pi|\rho|$ on the shell; the usable range ends at $10^{-2}$.

\subsection{The throat in motion}
\label{sec:single:moving}

A uniformly moving throat keeps the resting throat's fate. The exact-boost $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ throat (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:boost}; level 3, $v=\clmBoostSpeed$) rides $R_\star$, rising at most $\clmBoostMovingHoldMax\%$, until $t=\clmBoostMovingOnePct$, where it falls $1\%$ below its start (the resting level-3 throat: $t\simeq\clmHoldTimeOnePct$), and then collapses on the resting throat's own mode: $\tau=\clmBoostMovingTauLo$--$\clmBoostMovingTauHi$ against $\clmTauLevelThree$ at rest, on the same branch---level 3 collapses---and it dies at the same numerical endpoint ($t=\clmBoostMovingDeath$, Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(d)). The instability is the throat's, carried with the motion; the momentum changes neither the branch nor the rate.

\subsection{The collapse endpoint}
\label{sec:single:collapse}

The collapse branch ends in a black hole that consumes its supporting field. On the unkicked level-3 arm the horizon's areal radius falls $\clmUnkickedHorizonRStart\to\clmUnkickedHorizonREnd$ and $M_{\rm MS}$ $\clmUnkickedHorizonMStart\to\clmUnkickedHorizonMEnd$ over $\clmUnkickedHorizonSpan$ units, with the origin lapse at $\clmUnkickedOriginLapse$; the kicked $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ arm runs clean to $t=\clmKickedRunsClean$ behind its horizon. Unlike the massless throat of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026}, which bounced at $t\approx\clmMasslessBounce{}M$ and shed its horizon by $\clmMasslessShed{}M$, no arm here sheds a horizon. That collapse was driven with the phantom's stress-energy halved in the Einstein equations; the unmodified evolutions here supersede it where they differ. The pure-quadrupole arm ($\varepsilon_2=\clmPureQuadAmp$, no radial kick) is fully instrumented (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse}): a MOTS from $t=\clmPureQuadMotsTime$ at $R=\clmPureQuadMotsRStart$, shrinking to $\clmPureQuadMotsREnd$ by $t=\clmPureQuadTrust$ ($M_{\rm MS}$ $\clmPureQuadMotsMStart\to\clmPureQuadMotsMEnd$), while $|K|$ grows inward and the lapse pinches to zero at the centre.

The shrinking is physical. In spherical symmetry a massless phantom can only shrink a horizon: with the double-null metric $-2e^{-f}\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}v+R^2\mathrm{d}\Omega^2$, $T_{uv}=0$ and $T_{vv}=-(\partial_v\phi)^2$, the Misner--Sharp mass on a marginally trapped tube obeys $\partial_v m=4\pi R^2e^{f}(\partial_v\phi)^2\partial_uR\le0$ and $\partial_u m=0$, and the tube is timelike or null~\cite{hayward1996,hayward1994}, so $R=2m$ can only fall, as phantom accretion requires~\cite{babichev2004,gao2008}. The arms kicked with $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ shrink by $\clmRegrowthShrinkPct\%$ in areal radius to a floor at $t\approx\clmRegrowthFloorTime$; the regrowth their scans show after it tracks the unconstrained seed's Hamiltonian defect (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}) reaching the horizon, and is absent from the pure-quadrupole arm, whose seed has no $\ell=0$ defect (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse}(a)). The throat becomes a black hole that swallows its own phantom support and loses $\clmRegrowthShrinkPct\%$ of its horizon radius doing so, in minutes even at $10^{6}\,M_\odot$ (Table~\ref{tab:scales}): Hawking's area law assumes the null energy condition~\cite{hawking1971,ashtekar_krishnan2004}, and this horizon runs it backwards.

\subsection{The inflation branch}
\label{sec:single:fate}

The inflation branch forms no trapped surface. The $\varepsilon=-10^{-2}$ throat, evolved on an octant of an $L=\clmFourBoxL$ box with finest $\Delta x=\clmFourFinestDx$ (the resolution of our $L=\clmBoxL$ level-3 arms; Fig.~\ref{fig:single_inflation}), keeps growing to the end of the record, $\times\clmFourRRatio$ by $t=\clmFourWall$, and stays anti-trapped~\cite{hayward1999}: the $\theta_l=0$ horizon sits on the neck and $\theta_k=0$ runs outward, to $R\approx\clmFourHorizonR$ by $t=\clmFourHorizonTime$. Until $t\approx\clmFourSHWinHi$ the neck grows exponentially in its own proper time with $HR_0=\clmFourSHRate$ (locally $\clmFourSHLocalLo$--$\clmFourSHLocalHi$; $R_0=\clmFourRStart$, the kicked neck), $\clmFourLinearGap\%$ below our linear mode's $\clmFourLinearRate$, which its local rate reaches. Spherical evolutions find the same proper-time exponential: $Ha\approx\clmSHMassless$ for the massless throat~\cite{shinkai2002} and exponential growth for massive ones~\cite{gonzalez2009}. After that the $1{+}\log$ slicing freezes the lapse at the neck ($\clmFourLapseStart\to\clmFourLapseEnd$), only $\approx\clmFourTauLate$ units of its proper time pass by $t=\clmFourWall$, and its growth per unit $t$ slows---a property of the slicing, not of the throat.

The throat is shedding its own support: the monopole scalar flux through every sphere grows quasi-exponentially over the quotable window (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), drawing on a store that outside the static throat is a Misner--Sharp deficit of $R_\star/2-m=\clmInfStoreDeficit$; while the exterior slicing is still quiet the arms of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship} shed $\clmInfShedQuiet$ of it through $R=18$, a few percent. By $t=\clmFourWall$ neither a downturn of $R_{\min}(t)$ nor a trapped surface occurs: the growth holds at every sample, and the $\theta_k=0$ surface keeps moving outward.

\subsection{A type-I critical solution}
\label{sec:single:critical}

In the language of critical collapse the static throat is a type-I critical solution~\cite{gundlach2007}: a static solution with a single unstable mode (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability}), sitting on the threshold between two end states---collapse and inflation, where the standard problem has collapse and dispersal. The measurements above carry both type-I signatures. The first is time scaling: data a distance $\varepsilon$ from threshold stay near the critical solution for a time $\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)$ set by its one e-fold, which is the lifetime law of Table~\ref{tab:scales}, $t_{\rm BH}\simeq\clmHorizonTimeEpsTwo\,M+\tau\ln(10^{-2}/\varepsilon)$, and both branches leave along the same mode, each $\pm\varepsilon$ pair crossing at the same $t_\times$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}). The second is a mass gap: black-hole formation turns on at finite mass, as at the static Bartnik--McKinnon solution of Yang--Mills collapse~\cite{choptuik1996}, not at the vanishing mass of type-II scaling. The $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ and $+10^{-3}$ horizons form at $R=\clmHorizonRadiusEpsTwo$ and $\clmHorizonRadiusEpsThree$, both close to the throat's own $R_\star$: a tenfold smaller kick leaves the black hole's mass at formation almost unchanged, where type-II scaling would shrink it as a power of $\varepsilon$. Shinkai and Hayward found evidence, in spherical evolutions of the massless throat, for critical solutions with a definite black-hole mass~\cite{shinkai2002}; here the critical solution is the static throat itself, its one unstable mode measured in 3+1 dimensions.

\subsection{The sign rule}
\label{sec:pair:sign}

Two identical throats released from rest push apart; reversing one throat's scalar, $\sigma=+1\to-1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}, turns the push into a pull (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b)). Both forces share one radial law, so their ratio $Q$ is separation-independent: no gap, mass or approach speed lets a like-signed pair fall together, and $\sigma=\clmMergerSign$ is the only gravity-driven merger channel. The flip is not a tuning: $\phi\to-\phi$ leaves $T_{ab}$, and with it the one-body geometry, unchanged. The measured pull-to-push ratio at $a=\clmThroatA$ is $\clmMatchedSignRatio\pm\clmMatchedSignRatioErr$ (scatter over $15$ slices) against the point-charge, large-separation $(Q+1)/(Q-1)=\clmScalarQRatio$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}, and the throat mass is equal in all arms, so the asymmetry is the scalar charge's.

\subsection{The force law}
\label{sec:pair:force}

How the push falls with distance is read off a ladder (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c)). \clmCountLikeSignedPairs{} like-signed pairs, identical but for the gap $d=12/14/16/18$, are released from rest, and by a common $t=11.5$ their separations have grown by $\delta d=\clmDLadderTwelve/\clmDLadderFourteen/\clmDLadderSixteen/\clmDLadderEighteen$. For displacements this small, $\delta d$ at a fixed time is proportional to the force, so under the inverse-square law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:force} $\delta d\,d^2$ would be the same on every rung. It is not: it rises monotonically, $\clmFdSquaredLo\to\clmFdSquaredHi$, across the ladder, which excludes inverse-square in the coordinate separation. Instead $F\propto(d+\delta)^{-2}$ with $\delta\approx\clmOffsetDeltaLo$--$\clmOffsetDeltaHi$ describes it: $\delta$ fitted on $d=12$--$16$ alone predicts $\clmOffsetPrediction$ at $d=18$, where inverse-square gives $\clmInverseSquarePrediction$ and the measurement is $\clmDLadderEighteen$; the full-ladder fit gives $\delta=\clmMatchedOffsetDelta$. Since $\delta$ is a sizable fraction of the mouth radius $R_\star=\clmRstarShort$, the force is inverse-square in an \emph{effective} separation exceeding the coordinate one by most of a throat radius. Across widths, at $d=12$, the push grows at every rung (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(d)): $\delta d=\clmALadderOne/\clmALadderOneHalf/\clmALadderTwo/\clmALadderThree$ by $t=11.5$ for $a=1/1.5/2/3$, a power law $\delta d\propto a^n$ with $n=\clmWidthExponentLo$--$\clmWidthExponentHi$ when fitted at $t=8$--$11.5$, where point charges, whose net push $(Q-1)\,m^2/d^2$ is $a^2/d^2$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}, give $n=2$.

\subsection{Past the interior failure to a black hole}
\label{sec:headon:wall}

The merged core is the grid's hard part, not the spacetime's: $\clmHeadonCsmWallLead$ units after the horizon forms the level-5 leg dies of the censored interior NaN of Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall} ($t=\clmHeadonCsmNanTime$), a level-6 leg walks through the failure window, and a level-4 leg carries the remnant to $t=100$ with no NaN. \section{The orbital channel}

\subsection{Merger or fly-by}
\label{sec:spiral:capture}

Varying the tangential momentum $p$ at $d=12$ on boosted, solved data separates scattering from plunge (Fig.~\ref{fig:orbits} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}). The two middle arms scatter: at $p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ the pair passes at $\clmFlybyLbfClosest$ ($t\approx\clmFlybyLbfClosestTime$) and has receded to $\clmFlybyLbfFar$ by $t=\clmFlybyLbfTrust$; at $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ it passes closer, $\clmPFortyFiveClosest$ at $t\approx\clmPFortyFiveClosestTime$, and recedes to $\clmPFortyFiveFar$ by $t=\clmPFortyFiveTrust$. Both reach their stop times with no NaN---the $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ arm at stock settings to $t=100$, so scattering meets no wall of its own---and the finder, run through each pass, sees no trapped surface on either. The slowest arm, $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$, reaches contact (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:race}), and the two faster arms plunge: $p=\clmEgwPSixty$ reaches contact at $t\approx\clmPSixtyContactTime$ (pit separation $\clmPSixtyContactSep$ at its first leg's stop) and $p=\clmEgwPNinety$ just past its $t=40$ leg ($\clmPNinetyContactSep$ and still falling), each collapsing to a merged core on which no common MOTS converges (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}). The plunge boundary therefore sits between $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ and $\clmEgwPSixty$: $\clmPFortyFiveCircFrac$--$\clmPSixtyCircFrac\%$ of the circular momentum under the combined point-charge pull, $1+Q$ times gravity's (Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}), bracketing the circular value itself. That pull is central: a pair that misses contact keeps its angular momentum and swings past. But from $p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ up the approach deepens with speed---closest approach falls from $\clmFlybyLbfClosest$ through $\clmPFortyFiveClosest$ to contact, against the Newtonian expectation of a pericentre growing with $J=pd$---so capture here is contact, not an innermost orbit. Nor do the scatterers escape: both start below the circular momentum and remain bound in Newtonian terms, and what follows a pass is set by the throats, which inflate (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:race}).

The recession after the turn is not that of two compact throats: on both scatterers the mouths inflate, and the constraint norm crosses the campaign's $2.5\times10^{-2}$ criterion as they do, at $t=\clmFlybyLbfTrust$ ($p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$) and $\clmPFortyFiveTrust$ ($p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$)---the trust windows past which nothing is quoted, from the runs or their waves (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:flyby}).

\subsection{The race between contact and inflation}
\label{sec:spiral:race}

At $d=12$ the finder holds no common horizon at any momentum. The slowest tangential arm, $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$, reaches contact at $t\approx\clmPTwelveContactTime$ and inflates, and its finder, run from $t=\clmPTwelveMotsFrom$ to the arm's trust window ($t=\clmPTwelveTrust$), holds no common MOTS. In an inward-momentum scout ($p=\clmDTwelveInwardP$) the mouths inflate faster than the orbit closes---$R_{\min}$ grows $+\clmDTwelveInflEight\%$ by $t=8$ and $+\clmDTwelveInflTwentyFour\%$ by $t=24$ while its infall stalls near separation $\clmDTwelveStallSep$---and the finder holds no common MOTS on any of its plotfiles.

The mechanism is the branch selection of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}, run by the companion. A lone throat's fate is opposite to a declared kick: pushed outward it collapses, squeezed it inflates. A companion squeezes: each mouth starts in the isolated throat's shape (Table~\ref{tab:placement}), yet grows as a lone throat kicked inward on the seed ladder of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}, at $\varepsilon_{\rm eff}\approx-\clmRaceKickTwelve\%$ for $d=12$ at rest and $-\clmRaceKickEight\%$ for $d=8$, so every pair's mouths start on the inflation branch, at any momentum, and grow on the throat's own e-fold time $\tau\approx\clmTauLevelThree$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability}). That the solve keeps a declared seed is checked directly: the $\varepsilon=+0.1$ kicked single, run through the constraint solve, is born trapped (a MOTS on its $t=0$ slice, $R=\clmSeedSolvedMotsR$), the fate its kick dictates. A merger is therefore a race against the inflation it starts: the head-on's horizon arrives $\clmRaceFoldsHeadon$ e-folds in and wins; the $d=6$ orbit's arrives at $\clmRaceFoldsSix$ e-folds and wins (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}); the $d=12$ approach, $\clmRaceFoldsTwelve$ e-folds to its closest pass, always loses. The design rule for an orbital merger follows: short separation and small tangential momentum.

\subsection{Interior failure after horizon formation}
\label{sec:spiral:wall}

Two failure modes stand between a merged core and its settled remnant, and each has a measured cure. The first is a blow-up of the metric component $h_{11}$ at the merged core's centre: the head-on's level-5 leg at $t=\clmHeadonCsmNanTime$, the $d=6$ chain's restarts at $t=\clmMergerLvlSevenDeath$--$\clmMergerSigDeath$, the $p=\clmEgwPSixty$ plunge at $t=\clmPSixtyExtDeath$---in every case with the global constraint norms clean to the last step. It is not under-resolution: on the $d=6$ chain the level-6 restart dies at $t=\clmMergerLvlSixDeath$ and the level-7 restart dies \emph{earlier}, at $t=\clmMergerLvlSevenDeath$ with $\max|K|$ still climbing---a finer grid tracks the core's collapse further and meets the failure sooner---and raising the dissipation to $\sigma_{\rm KO}=1.0$ does not cure it. What cures it is the $\chi$ floor: with $\min\chi$ capped at $10^{-4}$ the $1/\chi$ steepness runaway cannot complete, and both the $d=6$ chain and the $p=\clmEgwPSixty$ plunge cross their death points and run to $t=100$, the $d=6$ settle leg then running floor-free. The second mode is the $K$ wall: $\max|K|$ itself running away at the merging core, $\clmPNinetyKLo\to\clmPNinetyKHi$ in one step on the $p=\clmEgwPNinety$ arm at $t=\clmPNinetyDeath$. On the $d=6$ chain level 6 cleared the runaway that stopped level 5; on the $p=\clmEgwPNinety$ arm a level-5 continuation removed the runaway but died at the same instant ($t=\clmPNinetyLvlFiveDeath$).

Where a horizon forms, the failure is censored. The core fails only after a common MOTS encloses it: the $d=6$ cell NaNs arrive $\clmMergerWallLeadLo$--$\clmMergerWallLeadHi$ units after its horizon, the head-on's $\clmHeadonCsmWallLead$ after its, and lone collapses die behind their horizons at level 3 too ($\varepsilon=+10^{-3}$ at $t=\clmKickThreeDeath$, $\clmKickThreeLead$ units after its horizon forms; $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ dressed with $\varepsilon_2=10^{-2}$ at $t=\clmDressedDeathLevelThree$, $\clmDressedLeadLevelThree$ after), while no level-4 lone collapse dies. The failure is local, the exterior is already set, and the finder's surface rides through every crossing unmoved: whatever diverges is the coordinate representation of a merged interior, behind the horizon.

The record thus tests weak cosmic censorship outside the physically reasonable matter it is posed for~\cite{penrose1969,wald1997}: in every collapse resolved here---the head-on across its legs, the $d=6$ chain at levels 5--7, and the lone collapses---the evolution fails, if at all, only inside a MOTS. This is evidence for censorship under null-energy violation, not a proof---the failure is a coordinate blow-up read on the slice, not a located curvature singularity---and the plunges whose horizon never converges (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:capture}) leave their failures uncensored on the slice, at the edge of what the grid resolves.

\subsection{Scalar channel and its sign}
\label{sec:gw:scalar}

$\Psi_4$ is blind to a spin-$0$ wave. Two opposite scalar charges---every merging pair here (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair})---carry a dipole, where a vacuum binary's leading moment is the quadrupole. On every binary the scalar stream rides the offline $\Psi_4$ spheres (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}), so the two channels compare sphere by sphere (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}). The recorded kinematic flux $F_{\rm kin}$ approximates the scalar energy crossing $S_R$ per coordinate time: the flux, through the world tube of $S_R$, of the canonical energy current $J^\mu\equiv-T^{(\phi)\mu}{}_{\nu}\,t^\nu$ with $t^\mu=\alpha n^\mu+\beta^\mu$, conserved where $t^\mu$ is Killing and nearly so in the quasi-stationary exterior the spheres sample~\cite{clough2021,gourgoulhon2012},
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
F^{\rm geo}_\phi&=\oint_{S_R}\alpha\,s_iJ^i\sqrt{\sigma}\;\mathrm{d}^2x\\
&=-\oint_{S_R}\partial_t\phi\,\bigl(\alpha\,s^j\partial_j\phi+\beta_js^j\,\Pi\bigr)\sqrt{\sigma}\;\mathrm{d}^2x,
\end{split}
\label{eq:scalarflux}
\end{equation}
positive for an outgoing canonical wave ($s^i$ the sphere's outward unit normal, $\sqrt{\sigma}$ its area element, $\partial_t\phi=\alpha\Pi+\beta^i\partial_i\phi$). On a conformally flat sphere with uniform $\alpha$ and $\chi$ and no shift, $F^{\rm geo}_\phi=\alpha^2\chi^{-1/2}F_{\rm kin}$; at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$ the initial data's area mean of $\alpha^2\chi^{-1/2}$ is $\approx\clmGwScalarFluxFactor$, so energies built from $F_{\rm kin}$ overstate $|F^{\rm geo}_\phi|$ by about $\clmGwScalarFluxCorr\%$; the shift terms are neglected.

The channel is dipole-dominated and comparable to the gravitational one. On the fly-by at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$, $\ell=1$ carries the scalar power $\sum_{\ell m}|\dot A_{\ell m}|^2$ to one part in $10^{\clmGwScalarDipoleShare}$, and the dipole leaves as a single arch that crests at $t=\clmGwScalarCrestInner$ on $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusInner$ before $t=\clmGwScalarCrestOuter$ on $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$---an outward pulse, and one this run holds complete: the outer sphere's trusted record ($t-R\le\clmFlybyTrustRetarded$) ends only after the arch has passed (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}(a)). Over that whole record $|E_\phi|=\clmGwScalarEnergyFlyby\,M$, $\clmGwScalarRatioFull$ times the gravitational energy that crosses the sphere with it, and the estimator stops mattering: the band-limited energy of Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(d) gives $\clmGwScalarBandFull$ and the wave-zone mode sum $\clmGwScalarRatioWaveZone$. Part of that energy is not radiation: before the arch $F_{\rm kin}$ rises on every sphere at once, ahead of any signal from the throats, and falls with radius as $R^{-\clmScalarNearSlope}$---the near-zone field of the moving charges, not a propagating wave. It supplies $\clmScalarPrearchShare\%$ of $|E_\phi|$, and the arch alone gives $|E_\phi|/E_{\rm GW}=\clmScalarArchRatio$. Mid-burst, at $t=\clmGwScalarCut$, the same three read $\clmGwScalarRatioSixty$, $\clmGwScalarBandSixty$ and $\clmGwScalarRatioWaveZoneSixty$, and the inner sphere $\clmGwScalarRatioInner$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}(c)), so downstream we use only the channel's multipole, direction, sign and order of magnitude.

Gravity couples to minus the canonical stress tensor, Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}, so the physical energy flux is $-F_{\rm kin}$: the outgoing $\ell=1$ wave---on the fly-by and, once their horizons form, on the head-on and the orbital merger---carries negative energy. If the ADM energy balance holds with this term, a bound pair emitting it gains energy and radiation reaction is \emph{anti-damped}: the classical face of the standard objection to ghosts, whose quantum vacuum decays into ghosts and positive-energy quanta together at a rate kept finite only by cutting the effective theory off~\cite{carroll2003,cline2004}. Classically these binaries make the same trade, and the head-on remnant settles near the pair's Bondi mass, a little above $M_{\rm ADM}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}).

The dipole returns angular momentum too: on the fly-by, through both spheres and over its trusted record, the scalar's physical angular-momentum flux is opposite to the gravitational waves', which carry off angular momentum of the orbit's sign. Nor does the negative energy invert the nonlinear memory~\cite{christodoulou1991,thorne1992,favata2009}: per unit energy an equatorial dipole projects onto the $(2,0)$ memory with the opposite sign to a quadrupole, so the ghost's negative flux adds to the gravitational memory, by $\clmMemoryRatioLo$--$\clmMemoryRatioHi$ of it on the fly-by at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$ across the scalar estimators.

\subsection{Horizon censorship of the scalar channel}
\label{sec:gw:censorship}

The head-on's chain carries the scalar stream end to end, and its horizon switches the channel off (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_censorship} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}). After the common MOTS closes ($t=\clmHeadonCsmMotsTime$), the mouths' remaining hair leaves as a single $\ell=1$ pulse along the collision axis, cresting later at each sphere in causal order ($t=\clmGwCensCrestMid$ and $\clmGwCensCrestOuter$ at $R=\clmGwHeadonRadiusB$ and $\clmGwHeadonRadiusC$), and the envelope then decays: log-linear fits over $t=\clmGwCensFitLo$--$\clmGwCensFitHi$ give $\tau=\clmGwCensTauInner/\clmGwCensTauMid/\clmGwCensTauOuter$ at $R=\clmGwHeadonRadiusA/\clmGwHeadonRadiusB/\clmGwHeadonRadiusC$. The decay is the remnant ringing down its hair: fitted from $t=40$ as one damped oscillation, the dipole at $R=\clmGwHeadonRadiusA$ rings at $\omega=\clmScalarQnmOmegaR$ and decays at $\clmScalarQnmOmegaI$, against $\clmScalarQnmPredR$ and $\clmScalarQnmPredI$ for the $\ell=1$ scalar quasinormal mode of a Schwarzschild hole of the final MOTS mass ($M\omega=\clmScalarQnmLitRe-\clmScalarQnmLitIm\,i$~\cite{berti2009}): the frequency agrees to $\clmScalarQnmFreqDev\%$ for fits started anywhere over $t=35$--$45$, and the damping is $\clmScalarQnmDampDev\%$ slower. The post-horizon energies, integrated from the MOTS, keep the sign of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}, $E_\phi=\clmGwCensEnergyInner/\clmGwCensEnergyMid/\clmGwCensEnergyOuter$ in units of $m$. Only that window is integrated: before $t=\clmGwCensIngoing$ the flux at $R=\clmGwHeadonRadiusA$ runs canonically ingoing, the two open mouths' static hair overlapping and draining inward.

The orbital merger, the one other encounter with a horizon (common MOTS from $t=\clmMergerMotsTime$; Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}), sheds the same way on its own glued record: its dipole crests at $t=\clmGwCensMergerCrest$ on $R=\clmGwCensMergerRadius$ and falls exponentially, $\tau=\clmGwCensMergerTau$ (fit over $t=\clmGwCensMergerFitLo$--$\clmGwCensFitHi$), \clmGwCensMergerDrop$\times$ down at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusInner$ by the record's end. Without a horizon there is nothing to switch the channel off: the fly-by radiates its arch as the mouths swing past one another, and what ends that emission is the encounter itself---the mouths leave intact, still carrying the charges that source the dipole.

The lone throat shows the same contrast. Re-run at level \clmGwKickedLevel{} with the scalar stream, the $\varepsilon=\clmGwKickedEps$ throat collapses on schedule---a permanent MOTS from $t=\clmGwKickedMotsTime$, $R$ falling $\clmGwKickedRadiusStart\to\clmGwKickedRadiusFloor$ at its floor---and its scalar envelope, after the burst, falls across the spheres by $t=\clmGwKickedDropTime$, with late e-fold times of $\clmGwKickedEfoldLo$--$\clmGwKickedEfoldHi$. The unkicked level-\clmGwSingleLevel{} throat, which inflates with no trapped surface by scan or flow finder, instead grows quasi-exponentially in the $\ell=0$ monopole (e-fold ${\approx}\clmGwSingleEfold$, shortening across $t=\clmGwSingleWindowLo$--$\clmGwSingleWindowHi$), as in a box-doubled ($L=\clmGwSingleBox$) copy, to $\clmGwSingleAgreePct\%$ over $t=\clmGwSingleAgreeFrom$--$\clmGwSingleAgreeTime$, so the growth is the throat's and not the boundary's or the sponge's; beyond $t\approx\clmGwSingleConstraintTime$ the constraint norms grow, and only $t\lesssim\clmGwSingleCountTime$ is counted. The contrast is confounded with the fate: the horizonless arms are also those whose mouths inflate toward the spheres. What is measured is that the scalar channel shuts off behind a horizon and grows while a throat inflates.

\section{Pattern matching against detector data}
\label{sec:ligo}

In $\clmDetLivetime$ h of coincident Advanced LIGO O3b data~\cite{gwosc}, a matched-filter search~\cite{allen2012,pycbc} with $\clmDetBankSize$ templates---the waveforms of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw} rescaled as $h\propto M/D$, $f\propto1/M$---finds \emph{no candidate at zero lag}, and none was expected. No static throat of astrophysical mass survives (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime}), so LIGO could catch only the burst of an encounter or collapse at formation, never a wormhole in residence; and a population formed at $z_e\gtrsim\clmDetZConv$ would convert there, far beyond the search's reach (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds}). At single-detector SNR $\clmDetHorizonSnr$ and optimal orientation the median horizon over each mass ladder is $\clmDetHorizonFlyby$ Gpc (fly-by), $\clmDetHorizonHeadon$ (head-on), $\clmDetHorizonSpiral$ (merger), $\clmDetHorizonPlunge$ (plunge, to its trust window), $\clmDetHorizonTwin$ (vacuum twin) and $\clmDetHorizonThroat$ (collapsing throat); the first four are upper bounds: their strain ends at $\clmDetPedestalLo$--$\clmDetPedestalHi$ of peak (fixed-frequency integration~\cite{reisswig2011}; the plunge's trust cut). This short search demonstrates the pipeline; much stronger limits on short bursts follow from the LVK O3 all-sky burst search~\cite{lvk2021burst}, whose rate-density limits against characteristic frequency apply directly to the energies of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst}.

The null result is not a blind spot. Cut to the signal's duration, binary-black-hole templates (\texttt{IMRPhenomD}) match the bursts with fitting factors of $\clmDetFFWindowLo$--$\clmDetFFWindowHi$, as they match the vacuum control ($\clmDetFFTwinWindowLo$--$\clmDetFFTwinWindowHi$), so burst morphology alone cannot tell the two apart. All $\clmDetInjCount$ injections into O3b strain at optimal SNR $\clmDetInjSnr$, each midway between two bank rungs, are recovered at $\clmDetInjEffLo$--$\clmDetInjEffHi\%$ of optimal within $\clmDetInjDt$ ms, and the $\chi^2$ test vetoes none; the highest is a plunge recovered by a heavier rung, whose lower corner also collects the injected strain below the plunge record's own.

\subsection{The frequency track}
\label{sec:ligo:ringdown}

The frequency track separates a drainhole burst from a binary black hole by what comes before the ringdown (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(c)). The vacuum twin peaks at $fM=\clmGwTwinPeakFM$ and climbs the point-mass track before turning toward the equal-mass Kerr mode at $fM=\clmDetKerrFM$~\cite{berti2006,berti2009}: a monotone rise, then a fixed ringdown frequency. No drainhole channel follows it. The head-on's frequency \emph{falls}, by $\times\clmDetHeadonFreqRatio$, from contact onto the $\ell=2$ quasinormal mode of a Schwarzschild hole of its final MOTS mass, $\clmHeadonQnmHz$ Hz at this calibration, which it reaches to $\clmHeadonQnmDev\%$ at the track's minimum, before a late upturn: the remnant does not spin, and the shrinking of its horizon would raise that mode, not lower it. The fly-by's frequency falls as the pair separates; the merger's rises after contact; the lone collapse rings at its own Schwarzschild mode (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:gw}), $\clmDetThroatOverKerr$ of the equal-mass Kerr one. Wherever a remnant's ringdown is resolved---the lone throat's, the head-on's and the head-on's scalar dipole (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship})---it is the ordinary quasinormal spectrum of the black hole left behind, as in any catalogue event~\cite{gwtc3}; the wormhole is in the burst before it.

\subsection{Energy per event}
\label{sec:ligo:burst}

The detector-independent deliverable is the energy per event in units of the source's ADM mass $M$, in the dominant multipole about each source's axis (sphere spread in parentheses): fly-by $\clmGwEnergyFlyby\,M$ ($\clmGwSpreadFlyby\%$), merger $\clmGwEnergySpiral\,M$ ($\clmDetESpiralSpread\%$), head-on $\clmDetEHeadon\,M$ ($\clmDetEHeadonSpread\%$), collapsing throat $\clmGwEnergyThroat\,M$ ($\clmDetEThroatSpread\%$), vacuum twin $\clmGwEnergyTwin\,M$ ($\clmDetETwinSpread\%$). Since $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ scales as event density times energy per event, these convert any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$~\cite{lvk2021,pagano2016} into a bound on the abundance of primordial drainhole encounters, provided the ghost scalar exists at that epoch. The scalar channel adds comparable energy of opposite sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

All four fates of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} occur, each with a measured selection rule: the relative scalar sign separates scattering from merger (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}), the seed's sign separates collapse from inflation (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}), and the orbital momentum separates plunge from fly-by at $\clmSpiralBoundaryLow$--$\clmSpiralBoundaryHigh\%$ of circular (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}). For detection, three results combine: no inspiral exists (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), the wormhole phase is a burst with no chirp ahead of it, its frequency falling or drifting until the remnant's ordinary ringdown takes over (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}), and the scalar counterpart decays once a horizon forms (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}). A surviving wormhole signature is a drifting-frequency burst with a scalar counterpart; a settled ringdown without one is already the descendant.

\subsection{No wormhole inspiral}
\label{sec:discussion:noinspiral}

A drainhole binary cannot radiate an inspiral. Each throat's unstable mode gives it a lifetime $t\approx\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, with $\tau=\clmTauLevelFourRounded$--$\clmTauRounded$ in isolation. An inspiral needs many orbits. Under the combined pull of Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}, six times gravity at large separation and softened as $(d+\delta)^{-2}$ at the separations evolved (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:force}), a circular orbit at $d=12$ has period $P\approx\clmDetOrbitPeriodLo$--$\clmDetOrbitPeriodHi$. No pair starts quiet enough to wait that long: the companion itself plants the seed---the effective inward kick of $\clmRaceKickTwelve\%$ at $d=12$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:race})---an $\varepsilon$ worth $t\approx\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)\approx\clmDetCompanionLifeLo$--$\clmDetCompanionLifeHi$ units---under half a period. Even $\varepsilon=\clmDetSeedExampleC$ buys only $\clmDetLifePeriodsLo$--$\clmDetLifePeriodsHi$ periods, and each further factor of ten adds only $\tau\ln10\approx\clmDetDecadeUnitsLo$ units. The orbit's demand grows with separation as $P\propto d^{3/2}$, while the throat's endurance grows only as $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, and the companion's kick is part of the binary itself.

Radiation reaction cannot close the gap either: the leading radiative moment, the scalar dipole, carries negative energy comparable to the gravitational channel's (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}), which, if the ADM balance holds, pumps the orbit rather than draining it.

The wormhole-specific signal is therefore a short transient: within a few tens of $M$ each mouth collapses or inflates, and the plunge, burst and conversion of Secs.~\ref{sec:headon}--\ref{sec:gw} are all that this source class emits \emph{as wormholes}; any long chirp belongs to the objects the throats become. The argument holds while the throat's unstable mode exists, for the minimally coupled ghost scalar without spin of the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach analysis. Rotation can remove that mode---in five dimensions above a critical angular momentum~\cite{dzhunushaliev2013}, in four as perturbative analyses indicate~\cite{azad2024plb,azad2024prd,khoo2024}, with spinning Ellis wormholes existing up to an extremal limit~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014}---and other supports are radially stable~\cite{poisson_visser1995,kanti2011}, so a spinning or differently supported pair that outlives an inspiral is not excluded here. The same lifetimes rule out a static ghost-supported throat as a long-lived black-hole mimicker of the kind ringdown and echo tests target~\cite{cardoso2016,bueno2018}.

\subsection{Heavy seeds and LISA bursts}
\label{sec:discussion:seeds}

The lifetimes of Table~\ref{tab:scales} forbid any throat from surviving from the early universe, so what follows is conditional on the epoch $z_e$ at which throats of mass $M$ would form, which our evolutions do not fix. The known mechanisms---bubbles and domain walls~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017,deng_vilenkin2017,gouttenoire2024}---form them at horizon re-entry, $t\sim\clmDetReentryTime$~s for $\clmDetSeedMassMid\,M_\odot$ ($z\sim\clmDetReentryZLo$--$\clmDetReentryZHi$), where these bursts would be redshifted out of every band; we know of none forming $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$ throats as late as $z_e\approx\clmDetZConv$.

If such throats existed, collapse---alone or in a merger---would seed black holes. Wormholes pinching off at the mouths of inflating bubbles or domain walls leave black holes that can seed supermassive ones~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017,deng_vilenkin2017,gouttenoire2024}; here the wormhole is traversable, and its conversion is driven by its own instability or by a merger, in seconds to minutes (Table~\ref{tab:scales}), at a remnant mass of order its progenitors': the head-on and orbital remnants end at $R=\clmHeadonCsmEndRadius$ and $\clmMergerEndRadius$ from pairs of ADM mass $\clmHeadonCsmAdmMass$ and $\clmMergerAdmMass$. The scalar hair leaves on a ${\sim}\clmGwShedClock\,M$ clock (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), and the remnants then grow as ordinary black holes: from $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassMid\,M_\odot$ at $z\simeq\clmDetZConv$, Eddington-limited growth (Salpeter e-fold ${\simeq}\clmDetSalpeter$ Myr) reaches $\clmDetGrowthTarget\,M_\odot$ in $\clmDetGrowthTimeLo$--$\clmDetGrowthTimeHi$ Myr, inside the ${\sim}\clmDetGrowthWindow$ Myr available by $z\simeq\clmDetGrowthZ$---a heavy seed~\cite{inayoshi2020} (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(a)) that needs no atomic-cooling halo, Lyman--Werner flux or baryonic environment, and that, predating structure, would make UHZ1's overmassive morphology generic~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024}. One seed per present-day massive galaxy is $n\simeq\clmDetNMin$--$\clmDetNMax\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ comoving, a dark-matter fraction $\clmDetDMFracLo$--$\clmDetDMFracHi$; the FIRAS $\mu$-distortion bound~\cite{nakama2018} on curvature-perturbation black holes of $\clmDetFirasLo$--$\clmDetFirasHi\,M_\odot$ does not apply, but only because the formation mechanism is left unspecified.

The encounters would radiate in LISA's band (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(b)): with the measured $(fM)_{\rm peak}=\clmDetFMPeakLo$--$\clmDetFMPeakHi$, $f_{\rm obs}=(fM)_{\rm peak}/[M(1+z_e)]$ spans $\clmDetBandLo$--$\clmDetBandHi$ mHz across $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$ at $z_e\simeq\clmDetZConv$ (luminosity distance ${\approx}\clmDetLumDist$~Gpc~\cite{planck2018}). Against the analytic LISA noise~\cite{robson2019}, sky and inclination averaged and counting only each record's resolved band, the fly-by and the $d=6$ merger reach signal-to-noise ratios of $\clmDetLoudSnrLo$--$\clmDetBurstSnrHi$ at $\clmDetSeedMassMid$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$ and the head-on $\clmDetBurstSnrLo$--$\clmDetHeadonSnrHi$; all three stay above $8$ from $\clmDetDetectMassLo$ to $\clmDetDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$. Heavier events are louder but redder: the fly-by and the head-on hold $8$ to $\clmDetFlybyDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$, and at $\clmDetConvMassLo$--$\clmDetConvMassHi\,M_\odot$ only the plunge still reaches it, to at least $\clmDetPlungeDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$ (its record is cut at its trust window). A lone collapse, radiating only $\clmGwEnergyThroat\,M$, stays below detection, at a signal-to-noise ratio of at most ${\approx}\clmDetCollapseSnr$. The merger and head-on bursts each announce a seed; the fly-by, the loudest, announces none. What limits detection is occurrence: every seed converts once, so LISA records $\clmDetBurstsPerN\,(n/\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3})$ bursts in four years, ${\sim}\clmDetRateLo$--$\clmDetRateHi$ per year for $n=\clmDetNMin$--$\clmDetNMax\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$---resolved bursts, never a stochastic background, although their time average, $\Omega_{\rm GW}\sim\clmDetOmegaLo$--$\clmDetOmegaHi$ at ${\sim}\clmDetOmegaFreq$ mHz, would lie above LISA's power-law-integrated sensitivity~\cite{thrane2013} for $n\gtrsim(\clmDetPlsNLo$--$\clmDetPlsNHi)\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ at $\clmDetSeedMassMid$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(c)). The spectrum is a pure burst population with no $f^{2/3}$ inspiral ramp (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}).

The negative-energy scalar radiation dilutes like radiation ($w=1/3$) and has the wrong sign to act as dark energy; within the dark-matter budget its present density stays below $\clmDetDepositCeiling$ of critical. Nothing in the astrophysical mass range survives (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime}), so every observed supermassive black hole is at most a descendant of this channel, not a surviving wormhole mouth~\cite{kardashev2007,bambi2013}. Whether it is \emph{the} channel behind the JWST population is a question of abundance, and the abundance is cheap: one conversion per little red dot, $n\simeq(\clmDetLrdNLo$--$\clmDetLrdNHi)\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$~\cite{greene2024,matthee2024}, is a fraction $\clmDetLrdFracLo$--$\clmDetLrdFracHi$ of the dark matter, and LISA would record $\clmDetLrdBurstsLo$--$\clmDetLrdBurstsHi$ such bursts in four years: it tests the channel only if conversions outnumber the little red dots at least tenfold.

\subsection{The inflating half of the population}
\label{sec:discussion:inflating}

Seeding is half the census. The branch is chosen by the perturbation's sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}), so a sign-symmetric seed distribution converts about half of a population of lone throats and sets the other half inflating. A companion tilts the census toward inflation: its squeeze starts both mouths on the inflation branch (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:race}), and only pairs whose common horizon wins the race convert---here the head-on and the $d=6$ orbit, no $d=12$ pair. An inflating throat creates, from regular data, an expanding anti-trapped region (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:fate}), which under the null energy condition would need an initial singularity~\cite{farhi_guth1987,blau1987}; unlike the false-vacuum bubbles of those references and the domain-wall wormholes of Ref.~\cite{deng2017}, whose inflating interiors pinch off behind a horizon, it lies on our side of the throat and grows into the surrounding space, as the massless throat's explosion does~\cite{shinkai2002}. Over its record the neck accumulates $\clmInfEfoldNeck$ areal e-folds and the boundary $\clmInfEfoldBoundary$, against the ${\sim}\clmInfEfoldsNeeded$ of cosmic inflation~\cite{liddle2003}: the record shows the mechanism, not the magnitude, and the seed channel of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} requires either that such regions stall or that the inflating half is rare.

\subsection{Topological censorship}
\label{sec:discussion:topology}

Topological censorship forbids an observer at infinity from probing the topology of a globally hyperbolic, asymptotically flat spacetime that satisfies the null energy condition~\cite{friedman1993}. A traversable wormhole evades it only by violating that condition, and the two branches end the evasion in opposite ways. On the collapse branch, alone or in a merger, the throats end inside a MOTS while the phantom outside is swallowed or radiated away (Secs.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}, \ref{sec:headon:wall} and~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}): the wormholes are not removed---no classical evolution changes the number of asymptotic ends~\cite{geroch1967}---but end hidden behind a horizon, as the theorem would have them. On the inflation branch no trapped surface forms and the throat stays open (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:fate}).

\subsection{Scope}
\label{sec:discussion:open}

The results hold for one matter model and one family of data: a single minimally coupled ghost scalar, equal masses, one throat compactness ($m/a=1/2$) and no spin, on constraint-solved, matched initial data whose momentum is the exact boost (Secs.~\ref{sec:model:solve} and~\ref{sec:model:boost}). Horizons here are MOTSs on the evolved slices, which for matter violating the null energy condition need not lie inside event horizons (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:mots}). The cosmology of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} takes the birth abundance $n(M)$ and the conversion epoch $z_e$ as inputs---Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(c) maps any choice onto its signals---and requires the ghost scalar to exist at that epoch.

\section{Reproducibility and cost}
\label{sec:repro}
\label{sec:cost}

Every evolution in Table~\ref{tab:matrix} is one line in a registry shipped with the results pack---its single knob against a named reference, its outcome, its caveats---and every figure is regenerated by a script in the pack from the archived diagnostic streams. The code, the analysis and the results pack are in the \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn} fork at \url{https://github.com/Nikchik-coder/GRTeclyn} (branch \texttt{develop}; results pack at \url{https://github.com/Nikchik-coder/GRTeclyn/tree/develop/results/merger}): the drainhole initial data with its constraint solve and exact boost, the seeds of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}, the scalar damping and the in-code diagnostics in the evolution code; the spectral MOTS finder, the orientation-corrected surface scan and the figure scripts in the analysis layer; and the parameter files and thinned diagnostic streams of every run in the pack. A table in the pack lists the binaries the runs name and, where known, each one's source commit. The evolutions are bit-reproducible on the production hardware: a lone-throat arm rerun with checkpoint output gives byte-identical physics streams over $\clmControlByteRows$ output rows.

Every evolution ran on one NVIDIA H100 \clmDetMemDevice\,GB device, with \texttt{GRTeclyn} built CUDA-native against AMReX~\clmDetAmrexVersion. At $\Delta t=\clmTimeStepFactor\,\Delta x$ a production arm ($L=\clmBoxL$, $\clmBaseN^3$ base, \clmProdLevels{} refinement levels) advances $\clmDetSpeedProdLo$--$\clmDetSpeedProdHi$ code units per hour, and a $t=100$ single-throat evolution takes $\clmDetCostTHundredLo$--$\clmDetCostTHundredHi$ h. Depth is the expense: level-5 arms run at $\clmDetSpeedLevelFiveLo$--$\clmDetSpeedLevelFiveHi$ units per hour on the full box (the octant $L=\clmFourBoxL$ arm, an eighth of the cells, at $\clmDetSpeedFourLo$--$\clmDetSpeedFourHi$, $\clmDetCostFour$ h in all)---$\clmDetCostHeadonChain$ h for the head-on's three-leg $t=0\to100$ chain, $\clmDetCostFlybyLevelFive$ h for the $p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ fly-by at level 5, which peaks at $\clmDetMemFlybyPeak$\,GiB of the $\clmDetMemDevice$\,GB device, and $\clmDetCostMergerChain$ h for the $d=6$ merger chain. The $\clmDetRunsTotal$ evolutions total $\clmDetGpuHours$ GPU-hours, each run's cost recomputed from its packed logs: the logged speed and time span, a restarted leg dated from its checkpoint.
