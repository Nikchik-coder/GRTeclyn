\begin{abstract}
We present the first numerical-relativity evolutions of wormhole binaries: pairs of massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainholes held open by a ghost scalar field, evolved in 3+1 dimensions at equal masses, one throat compactness and zero spin. Each throat is an unstable fixed point whose e-fold time approaches the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach linear rate with resolution, to $\clmGgsAgreementFour\%$ at our finest level, so its lifetime grows only as the logarithm of its initial perturbation. Two throats obey a charge-sign rule: like scalar charges repel and opposite charges attract, with a measured attraction-to-repulsion ratio of $\clmMatchedSignRatio\pm\clmMatchedSignRatioErr$ against the predicted $\clmScalarQRatio$, so only opposite-signed pairs merge. They do not fuse into one wormhole. A head-on collision forms a common marginally outer trapped surface around both still-open throats, born with their combined area; its irreducible mass exceeds the ADM mass, so the Penrose inequality fails for this matter. The horizon then loses more than \clmHeadonShrink{} of its area as it swallows the phantom field and settles near the Schwarzschild radius of the pair's mass: a black hole with two wormholes inside it. An orbital plunge ends in a remnant of the same size. With more than $\clmSpiralBoundaryLow$--$\clmSpiralBoundaryHigh\%$ of the circular momentum the pair passes without merging; the fly-by forms no horizon, and both mouths inflate. Gravitational waves are measured from both conversions. A lone collapsing throat sheds its seeded quadrupole as a burst ringing at $\clmDetThroatOverKerr$ of the equal-mass Kerr frequency---following the first gravitational-wave signal reported from a wormhole collapse, the massless throat's~\cite{shirokov2026}---and every encounter radiates its own burst of gravitational waves and a scalar dipole of comparable energy and negative sign, which a horizon switches off on the timescale on which the remnant's mass settles. A throat survives at most a few orbital periods for any plausible perturbation, and the negative-energy dipole would pump rather than drain the orbit, so these binaries have no inspiral. Their signal is a single burst whose frequency falls or drifts where a Kerr ringdown would hold; a search of $\clmDetLivetime$\,h of LIGO O3b data finds none. If such throats formed as late as $z\approx\clmDetZConv$, their collapse would supply halo-independent heavy black-hole seeds and millihertz bursts resolvable by LISA. The throats that inflate would create expanding anti-trapped regions from regular data, which the null energy condition otherwise forbids.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Wheeler's spacetime foam is a sea of handles---wormholes that open and close on the Planck scale~\cite{wheeler1955,wheeler1957,misner_wheeler1957}. Several mechanisms promote them to macroscopic size: inflation stretches a Planck-scale Lorentzian wormhole with the background~\cite{roman1993}; bubble and domain-wall cosmologies produce post-inflationary regions joined through wormholes~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017,deng_vilenkin2017,gouttenoire2024}; and a gas of relic wormholes has been studied as a cosmology in its own right~\cite{kirillov2008,kirillov2011}. The bubble and wall wormholes are not traversable and pinch off into black holes~\cite{deng2017,deng_vilenkin2017}; none of this work evolves a traversable relic---what one does, and what two of them do to each other. That is the subject of this paper.

Two classical facts govern the dynamics. First, the simplest evolvable traversable wormhole---the Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole held open by a ghost scalar~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973,morris_thorne1988,visser1995}---is unstable~\cite{gonzalez2009lin,bronnikov2011,cremona2019}: depending on the sign of the perturbation, the throat collapses to a black hole or inflates, on a timescale set by the throat radius~\cite{hayward1999,shinkai2002,gonzalez2009,doroshkevich2009,hayward2009,novikov2019}, as 3+1 evolutions of the massless throat confirm nonlinearly~\cite{shirokov2026}. A ghost field is a classical model here: as a quantum field coupled to gravity it destabilises the vacuum unless its effective theory is cut off at low energy with Lorentz violation~\cite{cline2004}, and we treat it as a classical phenomenological model throughout. Second, Geroch's theorem forbids classical topology change~\cite{geroch1967,tipler1977}: two wormholes cannot fuse into one, and none can be created classically. Creation is quantum~\cite{hawking1988}, so our evolutions start at $t=0$ from the newborn state---the stipulated initial data of every numerical binary, here with a physical origin attached.

Together these facts set selection rules. No astrophysical assembly channel exists: a throat decays faster than dynamical friction or radiation reaction could pair it. A primordial pair born from foam needs no assembly---birth and collision coincide, at a separation comparable to the objects---and its evolution is a race between orbital collision and throat decay, both rates fixed by the classical solution. The scalar adds a second rule: a phantom field repels like charges and attracts opposite ones, so like-signed pairs scatter while opposite-signed pairs fall together; and the sign of a lone throat's perturbation selects collapse---a wormhole-to-primordial-black-hole channel beside the standard ones~\cite{zeldovich1967,carr_hawking1974,carr2021}---or inflation. A foam population thus splits into four fates---merger, scattering, collapse, inflation---across birth separation, perturbation and relative sign, the space mapped here. The binary evolved has three asymptotic ends: two independent wormholes, each leading to its own far universe. A foam handle differs---its two mouths belong to one wormhole and carry opposite scalar charge by construction---so our opposite-signed pairs reproduce a handle's charges but not its topology. What merges is not two wormholes into one but two wormholes into one black hole containing both throats.

The observable is not a chirp but a population of bursts from the merger epoch, redshifted into whatever band the mass scale selects~\cite{caprini2018,nanograv2023,lisa2017,lvk2021}. The epoch-independent deliverable is the energy radiated per event and its spectrum in throat-mass units (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst}). Every waveform behind it is extracted in the near zone, on spheres at $R=\clmExtractRadiusTen$--$\clmExtractRadiusFortyFour$, and none is extrapolated to null infinity: each radiated energy carries the spread across its spheres as a near-zone error bar (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw}). Collapse, alone or in a merger, also bears on JWST's overmassive black holes at $z\gtrsim4$--$6$, which favour heavy seeds of $\clmDetIntroSeedLo$--$\clmDetIntroSeedHi\,M_\odot$ from formation channels that remain contested~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024,inayoshi2020}; Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} explores what a population of such throats would imply for heavy seeds and millihertz bursts, conditional on the epoch at which they form, which our evolutions do not fix.

Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:setup} give the model and numerics. Sections~\ref{sec:single}--\ref{sec:spiral} follow the campaign from one throat to two: the lone wormhole, two throats at rest, and the head-on and spiral mergers. Section~\ref{sec:gw} treats the radiation of every channel, and Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} confronts the rescaled waveforms with LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} draws the consequences and states the scope; Sec.~\ref{sec:repro} gives reproducibility and cost. Appendix~\ref{app:health} collects the code-health record---constraint evolution, the interior failure under refinement and gauge, and device checks---and Appendix~\ref{app:supp} supplementary measurements. All evolutions use \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}, the AMReX-based~\cite{amrex} GPU port of \texttt{GRChombo}~\cite{grchombo}, continuing Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026b}. We use $G=c=1$.

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Field equations}
\label{sec:model:field}

The matter is a single real, massless phantom scalar: gravity couples to \emph{minus} the canonical stress tensor,
\begin{equation}
T_{ab} = -\Bigl(\nabla_a\phi\,\nabla_b\phi - \tfrac12 g_{ab}\,\nabla_c\phi\nabla^c\phi\Bigr),
\qquad V(\phi)\equiv 0,
\label{eq:phantom}
\end{equation}
so the energy density seen by normal observers, $\rho=-\tfrac12\bigl(\Pi^2+\gamma^{ij}\partial_i\phi\,\partial_j\phi\bigr)$ with $\Pi=(\partial_t\phi-\beta^i\partial_i\phi)/\alpha$, is non-positive everywhere, and the null energy condition fails wherever $\phi$ varies---the exotic stress that holds a throat open. The field itself obeys the canonical wave equation $\Box\phi=0$, evolved in first-order form in $\phi$ and $\Pi$.

\subsection{Drainhole initial data}
\label{sec:model:drainhole}

Each body is a massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973}. In isotropic radius $r$ about the throat centre, with
\begin{equation}
X = \frac{r - a^2/4r}{a},\quad
\Omega = 1 + \frac{a^2}{4r^2},\quad
u = \frac{m}{a}\Bigl(\arctan X - \frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the exact static solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom} is
\begin{equation}
\alpha = e^{u},\quad
\gamma_{ij} = e^{-2u}\,\Omega^2\,\delta_{ij},\quad
\phi = C\arctan X,
\label{eq:drainhole}
\end{equation}
with $4\pi a^2C^2 = a^2+m^2$, $K_{ij}=0$, $\Pi=0$ and zero shift; in the evolved conformal variables $\chi = e^{2u}\Omega^{-2}$ and $h_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$. Both constraints hold identically at $t=0$. Here $a$ is the throat scale and $m$ the ADM mass, carried by the lapse rather than the conformal factor, so $\chi$ at the throat stays in $[\clmChiThroatLo,\clmChiThroatHi]$ for $m/a\le1$ and mass costs no resolution. The minimal surface sits at $r_t=\tfrac12\bigl(m+\sqrt{m^2+a^2}\bigr)$, where $X=m/a$, so its areal radius is analytic: $R_{\min}=e^{-u(r_t)}\sqrt{a^2+m^2}=\sqrt{a^2+m^2}\,\exp\!\bigl[(m/a)\arctan(a/m)\bigr]$, which is $a$ at $m=0$. The production throat $a=\clmThroatA$, $m=\clmThroatM$ has $R_{\min}=\sqrt5\,e^{\frac12\arctan2}=\clmRstar$ at $r_t=\tfrac12(1+\sqrt5)=\clmThroatCoordRadius$. The point $r\to0$ is the far side's spatial infinity, compactified ($\chi\sim r^4$). At $m=0$ the throat is ultrastatic ($\alpha\equiv1$, $M_{\rm ADM}=0$) and two such throats do not attract.

\subsection{The binary seed}
\label{sec:model:binary}

Two throats at separation $d$ (one-body radii $r_A$, $r_B$) are superposed in excess form,
\begin{equation}
u = u_A + u_B,\qquad
\psi = 1 + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_A}-1\bigr) + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_B}-1\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\chi = e^{2u}\psi^{-4}$, $\alpha = e^{u}$ and $\phi = \phi_A + \sigma\,\phi_B$, $\sigma=\pm1$; the constant tail of the $\arctan$ profiles is subtracted so that $\phi\to0$ at the boundary. Momentum is carried by the exact boost of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:boost}: the throats sit at $x=\mp d/2$ with $P^i=(0,\pm p,0)$, momenta of magnitude $p$ in units of the one-body mass, tangential to the separation and opposite on the two throats, so the pair carries no net momentum and orbital angular momentum $J=pd$; $p=0$ is a release from rest. The superposition violates the Hamiltonian constraint by $O(a^2/d^2)+O(m/d)$, which the solve of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:solve} removes. The relative sign $\sigma$ is the pair's one discrete parameter. Far from its throat each body is a mass $m$ carrying the scalar charge $q=Ca$ ($\phi_A\simeq-q/r_A$ once the constant tail is subtracted), and a phantom mediator \emph{repels} like charges and \emph{attracts} opposite ones, so two throats at rest a large distance $d$ apart attract with
\begin{equation}
F=\frac{m^2}{d^2}\,\bigl(1-\sigma Q\bigr),\qquad
Q\equiv\Bigl|\frac{F_\phi}{F_{\rm grav}}\Bigr|=\frac{4\pi q^2}{m^2}=\frac{a^2+m^2}{m^2}>1
\label{eq:force}
\end{equation}
always ($Q=\clmScalarQ$ for the production throat): a like-signed pair repels and never merges under its own gravity, and $\sigma=\clmMergerSign$ is the merger channel. The superposition itself is never evolved: it seeds the constraint solve of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:solve}, which removes the defect and fixes which throats the pair is made of.

\subsection{Constraint-solved data and matched placement}
\label{sec:model:solve}

With $\Pi=0$, $K=0$ and $\gamma_{ij}=\Psi^4\delta_{ij}$ the Hamiltonian constraint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom} reads, in flat operators,
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2\Psi-\pi|\nabla\phi|^2\,\Psi+\tfrac18\hat A_{ij}\hat A^{ij}\,\Psi^{-7}=0 .
\label{eq:hamsolve}
\end{equation}
The single drainhole solves it exactly; the superposition does not. We solve it for a correction $w$ to the superposed $\Psi$ on the whole initial mesh hierarchy with AMReX's multigrid~\cite{amrex}, holding $\phi$, $\Pi$ and the lapse fixed (a Robin outer boundary); a pair with momentum superposes the boosted slices of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:boost} and solves both constraints together, for $w$ and a vector correction $W^i$, with the companion's $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ cut inside each throat. On the $d=8$ head-on the Hamiltonian on the finest level's throat shell falls from $\clmSolveShellHamSup$ to $\clmSolveShellHamSolved$ rms, the exact throat's discretisation floor.

\emph{Which throat is each mouth?} Near its centre $\Psi=c/r+d_c+O(r)$, and the inversion $r'=c^2/r$ turns this into an asymptotically flat end---the mouth's own far universe---with ADM mass and scalar charge
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm far}=2c\,d_c,\qquad Q_{\rm far}=\frac{4Cc^2}{a},
\label{eq:farside}
\end{equation}
both frozen in time, like a puncture's individual mass. The isolated drainhole has $M_{\rm far}=-m\,e^{\pi m/a}=\clmFarMassIso$ and $Q_{\rm far}=\sqrt{a^2+m^2}\,e^{\pi m/a}/\sqrt{4\pi}=\clmFarChargeIso$. A pair $(M_{\rm far},Q_{\rm far})$ names one isolated drainhole, and its near-side mass $m'$ is the mouth's one-body mass.

The superposition does not place isolated throats (Table~\ref{tab:placement}). Each throat inherits the companion's constants, $\Psi\to e^{-u_B(x_A)/2}\,\Psi$ near $A$, and is rescaled: at $d=8$ each mouth has one-body mass $m'=\clmPlaceOneBodyEight$ and $R_{\min}=\clmPlaceRminEight$, $\clmPlaceRminExcessEight\%$ above $R_\star$, and at $d=12$ it has $m'=\clmPlaceOneBodyTwelve$. Solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:hamsolve} at the superposition's puncture coefficients keeps these mouths---$M_{\rm far}$ moves by $\clmSolveKeepsMfar\%$ and $R_{\min}$ by $\clmSolveKeepsRmin\%$---so a solved run and its superposed twin differ only in the data. Because $\phi$ fixes a static throat's coordinate size, $c$ alone cannot remove the rescaling. The \emph{matched placement} gives each throat a coordinate scale $\lambda$ ($a\to\lambda a$ and $m\to\lambda m$ in $\phi$, $u$ and $\Omega$; $C$ is scale-free), sets $\lambda=(c/c_{\rm iso})^2$ with $c_{\rm iso}=\tfrac a2e^{\pi m/2a}$, which holds $Q_{\rm far}$ at its isolated value, and iterates $c$ until $M_{\rm far}$ is isolated too. Each mouth is then locally the isolated static drainhole: its $R_{\min}$ lands within $\clmMatchedRminDev\%$ of $R_\star$ without being imposed, and $m'=1$, so that $p$ is in units of the true one-body mass. With momentum the iteration converges the same way ($d=12$, $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$: $R_{\min}$ within $\clmMatchedRminDevOrbit\%$ of $R_\star$).

\emph{The pair's mass.} With $\Psi\to1+M_{\rm ADM}/2r$, Gauss's theorem on Eq.~\eqref{eq:hamsolve}, whose punctures contribute $-4\pi c_X\delta_X$, gives
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm ADM}=2\sum_X c_X-\frac1{2\pi}\int\Bigl(\pi|\nabla\phi|^2\Psi-\tfrac18\hat A_{ij}\hat A^{ij}\Psi^{-7}\Bigr)d^3x ,
\label{eq:madmvol}
\end{equation}
which returns the exact throat's $M=1$ to $\clmVolumeIdentityExact\%$ and, unlike a fit to the monopole tail, does not read the outer boundary's offset of $w$ as mass. For matched pairs at rest (Table~\ref{tab:energy}) the excess over two isolated throats follows
\begin{equation}
E_b\equiv M_{\rm ADM}-2m=-\lambda^2\,\frac{m^2}{d}\,\bigl(1+\sigma Q\bigr)
\label{eq:ebind}
\end{equation}
for both signs, to $\clmEnergyLawDevNear\%$ at $d=8$ and $\clmEnergyLawDevFar\%$ at $d=48$. It is the Newtonian binding $-\lambda^2m^2/d$ plus the phantom cross energy $-\sigma\!\int\nabla\phi_A\!\cdot\!\nabla\phi_B\,d^3x=-\sigma\lambda^2(a^2+m^2)/d$ of two charges $\lambda q$, with $\lambda\to1$ as $d\to\infty$; the solve adds nothing else. In the merger channel $\sigma=-1$ the scalar term is positive and wins: the attracting pair weighs \emph{more} than its parts, $E_b\,d\to\clmEnergyLimitAttract$, and the repelling pair less, $E_b\,d\to\clmEnergyLimitRepel$. Read at fixed charges, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ebind} would make the $\sigma=-1$ pair repel. Equation~\eqref{eq:force} and the measured pull (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:sign}) follow instead if the scalar part acts at fixed potential, $F_\phi=+\partial E_\phi/\partial d$, as between conductors held at fixed voltage: the value of $\phi$ at each throat's far infinity cannot change, while the flux through the throat can. The evolution has now answered: rest pairs rerun on matched data pull and push in the ratio $\clmMatchedSignRatio\pm\clmMatchedSignRatioErr$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:sign}), the fixed-potential prediction, while fixed charges would reverse the sign rule itself.

\subsection{Throats with momentum}
\label{sec:model:boost}

A throat with ADM momentum $p$ is the exact drainhole boosted to $v=p/\sqrt{m^2+p^2}$ and cut at $t=0$: the metric, $K_{ij}$, $\phi$ and $\Pi$ together, with the boosted lapse and shift, so that the slice is an exact solution of both constraints and the scalar moves with its throat ($\Pi\neq0$). On the slice every $\chi$ contour, the throat's included, is an ellipsoid contracted along the motion by $1/\gamma=m/\sqrt{m^2+p^2}$, as a boosted black hole's horizon is; the measured axis ratios follow this law to $\clmBoostAxisResidual$ at every $p$ up to $\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:boost_contraction}). The alternative---Bowen--York momentum~\cite{bowen_york1980} on round, conformally flat data with the scalar at rest, the black-hole recipe---is not a moving wormhole: at $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ it inflates the throat by $10\%$ in $R_{\min}$ by $t=\clmBoostByTenPct$, where the boosted throat holds its shape-fitted radius to $\clmBoostHoldsTol\%$ through $t=\clmBoostHoldsTime$.

A pair is built from two boosted throats as in Sec.~\ref{sec:model:solve}, with the companion's $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ windowed out inside each throat, and the solve then corrects both constraints, for $w$ and a vector potential $W$ ($\hat A\to\hat A+\mathbb{L}W$). Three checks anchor it: on a lone boosted throat the solve is idle ($\max|w|\le\clmBoostSolveW$, $\max|W|\le\clmBoostSolveWvec$, the far side isolated to $\clmBoostSolveFar$); on the rest pair it reproduces the scalar solve's $c$, $\sigma$ and $w_0$ to $\clmBoostRestRepro$; and on the flipped $d=12$, $p=\pm\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ pair on the production grid the far sides come out the isolated throat's to $\clmBoostPairFar$, each mouth's $R_{\min}$ lands $\clmBoostPairRmin\%$ from the isolated value, and the throat-shell Hamiltonian falls from $\clmBoostPairShellHamUnsolved$ to at most $\clmBoostPairShellHam$ rms across the refinement levels.

\subsection{Declared seeds}
\label{sec:model:seeds}

The instability is probed with declared perturbations of the conformal factor only: a Gaussian shell on the minimal surface,
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon\, g(r)\bigr],\qquad
g = e^{-(r-r_t)^2/w^2},\quad w = a/4,
\label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
which changes the throat's areal radius by a fraction ${\approx}\clmSeedArealFactor\varepsilon$, and a quadrupolar factor
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon_2\, g(r)\,P_2(\cos\theta)\bigr]
\end{equation}
about the $z$ axis through the throat. Neither seed is constraint-solved. $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ stay untouched, so the momentum constraint holds at $t=0$; but the drainhole solves the linear equation $\nabla^2\Psi=\pi|\nabla\phi|^2\Psi$ ($\Psi^4=\chi^{-1}$, flat operators), so the spherical seed leaves exactly $\mathcal H=-8\varepsilon\,\Psi^{-4}(1+\varepsilon g)^{-5}(\nabla^2g+2\nabla\ln\Psi\cdot\nabla g)$---linear in $\varepsilon$, confined to the shell and, the shell being narrow, not small there: its peak is $\clmSeedDefectPeak\times16\pi|\rho|$ at $|\varepsilon|=10^{-2}$. The quadrupolar seed's defect is pure $\ell=2$ at first order. CCZ4 feeds the defect into $\Theta$ and, within the first steps, into $\mathcal M$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:constraints}(b)). Above $\varepsilon\simeq\clmSeedBagThreshold$ the spherical seed turns the minimal surface into a maximum between two minima. The spherical seed selects the branch but cannot make waves: a spherical collapse has no $\ell\ge2$ moment and radiates no gravitational waves. The quadrupolar seed is there for that reason. It has no $\ell=0$ part at first order, so it does not compete with the spherical seed for the branch, and it gives a collapsing throat a quadrupole to shed---the only gravitational-wave signal a lone throat has (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:gw}).

\subsection{Evolution scheme}
\label{sec:setup:scheme}

We evolve CCZ4~\cite{alic2012} with damping $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=(\clmCczKappaOne,0,\clmCczKappaThree)$, $1{+}\log$ slicing~\cite{bona1995} started from the exact static lapse of Eq.~\eqref{eq:drainhole}, a Gamma-driver shift~\cite{alcubierre2003gauge,campanelli2006,baker2006} with $\eta=\clmShiftEta$ started from zero, fourth-order stencils, RK4 and Kreiss--Oliger dissipation $\sigma=\clmKOsigma$. The time step is $\Delta t=\clmTimeStepFactor\,\Delta x$, an order of magnitude below the usual CCZ4 Courant factor, because larger steps fail: a multiplier of $\clmControlDtMedium$ departs from the production run at $t\approx\clmControlDtMediumDeparture$, and $\clmControlDtLarge$ dies at $t=\clmControlDtLargeDeath$. A halved step was run only on the spiral's interior failure (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}); elsewhere time-discretisation error is not separately bounded, and every other resolution statement is at fixed $\Delta t/\Delta x$.

\subsection{Grid, boundaries and regularisation}
\label{sec:setup:grid}

The domain is a cube of side $L=\clmBoxL$ centred on the system, with a $\clmBaseN^3$ base grid ($\Delta x=\clmDxBase$). Nested boxes, halving per level and centred on the tracked throats (half-width $\clmFinestHalfWidth$ per throat at the production finest level), are re-drawn every $\clmRegridSteps$ steps. Production runs use \clmProdLevels{} refinement levels (finest $\Delta x=\clmFinestDx$); resolution arms go to levels $\clmResArmsLevelLo$--$\clmResArmsLevelHi$ where stated, and the production spiral, the level-5 fly-by and two lone-throat arms use $L=\clmBigBoxL$: the same cells in a box twice as wide. Level $\ell$ thus means a finest cell $\Delta x=\clmDxBase/2^\ell$. The one exception is the octant of an $L=\clmFourBoxL$ box that carries the inflating throat (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:fate}): its base spacing is $\clmFourDxBase$, so its \clmFourLevel{} levels give $\Delta x=\clmFourFinestDx$, the resolution of our level-3 arms, and we quote it by that $\Delta x$. Outer boundaries are Sommerfeld, backed by a spherical sponge about the box centre over the outer quarter of the half-width---$r=\clmSpongeInner$--$\clmSpongeOuter$ at $L=\clmBoxL$ and $\clmSpongeInnerBig$--$\clmSpongeOuterBig$ at $L=\clmBigBoxL$---in which extra dissipation ramps to $\sigma_{\rm sp}=\clmSpongeStrength$ as the \clmSpongePower{} power of the depth; every extraction sphere lies inside its inner radius. The sponge is Kreiss--Oliger dissipation only: it damps grid-scale noise but is transparent to long-wavelength gauge and physical waves, which reflect from the box faces (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}). The vacuum controls (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:bbh}) instead run \texttt{GRTeclyn}'s binary-black-hole example with its own numerical settings and no sponge.

Each throat carries a compactified second infinity at its centre, where $\chi\sim r^4$, so the evolved variables are floored at $\chi\ge\clmChiFloor$ and $\alpha\ge\clmLapseFloor$ (a variant floors $\chi$ at $\clmChiFloorVariant$ and applies $\clmChiRhsFloor$ only where $1/\chi$ is evaluated). Two further devices act only inside regions already trapped: exponential damping of the scalar where the lapse has collapsed, and an interior freeze that multiplies every right-hand side by $1-W(r)$, with $W$ a $C^2$ quintic step between radii $r_{\rm full}$ and $r_{\rm start}$. All three are numerical devices, each measured by a twin: $\chi$-floor twins at $\clmChiFloor$ and $\clmChiFloorVariant$ re-converge to \clmControlChiDigits{} digits after touching the floor, the scalar damping moves the spiral's interior failure by less than a unit (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}), and the freeze leaves the exterior unchanged (Secs.~\ref{sec:headon:wall} and~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}).

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:setup:diagnostics}

Every coarse step the code writes the volume-averaged norms $\|\mathcal H\|=(\int\mathcal H^2\,\mathrm{d}V/L^3)^{1/2}$ and $\|\mathcal M\|=(\int\mathcal M_i\mathcal M_i\,\mathrm{d}V/L^3)^{1/2}$, evaluated with fourth-order differences on the base grid over the whole domain---sponge and throat interiors included---after the finer levels have been restricted onto it; they depend on the box and are compared only within one. These base-grid norms do not resolve the throats; they monitor code health, not local accuracy. Both throat centres are tracked, and about each the areal radius $R(r)$ is scanned on coordinate spheres; its minimum is the throat. The spheres run out to $r=\clmFlybyScanEdge$, a fixed setting of the offline instrument: wide enough for the static throat and the collapse horizons, but an inflating throat can pass it, after which the edge reading bounds the throat's areal radius from above. On the same spheres the null expansions $\theta_\pm$ are computed with the outward direction fixed by \emph{increasing areal radius}, so that a dynamic throat is marginally \emph{anti}-trapped~\cite{hochberg_visser1998,hayward1999}. The orientation matters: the in-code monitor fixes the outward direction by increasing \emph{coordinate} radius, which points the wrong way beyond a throat and manufactures trapped verdicts wherever $R(r)$ falls. Every horizon statement here is therefore read offline from archived plotfiles with the areal orientation; the in-code columns serve only as a live monitor.

The star-shaped scan reports the outermost coordinate sphere that is trapped everywhere ($\max\theta_+=0$, $\theta_-<0$). A trapped sphere lies inside the apparent horizon, so this sphere---``the scan's MOTS'' below---bounds the marginally outer trapped surface from inside. We quote its areal radius $R$ and its Misner--Sharp mass~\cite{misner_sharp1964},
\begin{equation}
\frac{2M_{\rm MS}}{R} \;=\; 1 + \frac{R^{2}\,\langle\theta_+\theta_-\rangle}{4},
\label{eq:misner_sharp}
\end{equation}
with the product averaged over the sphere and $\theta_\pm$ computed for the null normals $k^a=n^a+s^a$ and $l^a=n^a-s^a$ ($n^a$ the slice's unit normal, $s^a$ the sphere's outward unit normal, so $k\cdot l=-2$); with the normalisation $k\cdot l=-1$ the factor $1/4$ becomes $1/2$. On an exact MOTS $M_{\rm MS}=R/2$; on the scan's spheres $M_{\rm MS}$ exceeds $R/2$ by at most $\clmShapeSystLone\%$ about a lone collapsed throat, but about a merged remnant by up to $\clmShapeSystForming\%$ while the surface forms ($\clmShapeSystFormingLevelThree\%$ at level 3), falling to $\clmShapeSystRounded\%$ as it rounds. This is the shape systematic of star-shaped scanning, and it also affects whether a surface is found at all. Because the phantom violates the null energy condition, a trapped surface need not lie inside an event horizon; ``horizon'', ``censored'' and ``black hole'' below refer to MOTSs on the evolved slices. A deformable instrument backs the scan: a spectral flow finder expands the trial surface as $r=h(\theta,\phi)$ in real harmonics up to degree $\ell_{\max}=\clmFlowFinderLmax$ ($\clmFlowFinderLmaxAlt$ where stated) and drives its expansion to zero by a damped fast flow~\cite{gundlach1998}, with the same orientation rule. It recovers Kerr--Schild Schwarzschild ($R=2M$ to $\clmFlowFinderSchw\%$ and $M_{\rm MS}=M$ to $\clmFlowFinderSchwMass\%$ from spherical and dented seeds), finds no trapped surface outside the time-symmetric Ellis throat (whose minimal surface has $\theta_+=\theta_-=0$), and reproduces the star-shaped scan's surface on the head-on remnant.

Radiation is read from $\Psi_4$ on spheres at $R=\clmExtractRadiusTen$, $\clmExtractRadiusFourteen$, $\clmExtractRadiusEighteen$ (and $\clmExtractRadiusTwentyTwo$ on the single-throat wave arms) on the head-on and the single throats, whose $L=\clmBigBoxL$ arms keep these spheres, and at $R=\clmExtractRadiusTwenty$, $\clmExtractRadiusTwentyEight$, $\clmExtractRadiusThirtySix$, $\clmExtractRadiusFortyFour$ on the $L=\clmBigBoxL$ spiral and fly-by, decomposed in spin-weight $-2$ harmonics. Every amplitude quoted is $r\Psi_4$; on the head-on the in-code and offline pipelines agree at the burst peak to $\clmPsiPipelines\%$, while on the single throats the in-code stream is dominated by its grid-scale floor and only the offline one is used. A scalar stream is read on the same spheres, and at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusInner$ and $\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$ on the $L=\clmBigBoxL$ spiral and fly-by: $\phi$ and $\Pi$ in spin-weight-$0$ harmonics, $A_{\ell m}=R\,\phi_{\ell m}$, and the kinematic flux $F_{\rm kin}=-R^2\oint\Pi\,\partial_r\phi\,\mathrm{d}\Omega$, positive for an outgoing \emph{canonical} wave (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}). A per-cell autopsy records the location and one-step history of any floating-point failure.

\subsection{Marginally outer trapped surfaces}
\label{sec:setup:mots}

A marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS) is a closed two-surface of the slice on which the outgoing null
expansion vanishes, $\theta_+=0$, while the ingoing one is negative, $\theta_-<0$; the outermost MOTS is the
apparent horizon. Unlike the event horizon, which is defined only once the entire future of the spacetime is
known, a MOTS is quasi-local---it is read off a single slice---which makes it the operational horizon of
numerical relativity. Because the phantom violates the null energy condition, a MOTS here need not lie inside
an event horizon (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}); every ``horizon'', ``censored'' and ``black hole'' in this
paper is a statement about MOTSs on the evolved slices.

The evolution code does not locate horizons; finding a MOTS is an elliptic problem on a single slice and is
solved in post-processing, on every archived slice, so horizon coverage matches the plot cadence. The finder
expands the surface about the tracked centre as $r=h(\theta,\phi)$ in real spherical harmonics up to degree
$\ell_{\max}=\clmFlowFinderLmax$ on the head-on slices and $\ell_{\max}=\clmFlowFinderLmaxAlt$ on the orbital
ones, and solves $\theta_+[h]=0$ by Newton iteration on metric and extrinsic-curvature data interpolated from
the finest refinement level covering the surface, with the areal orientation of
Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}. The first surface of a run is found by the damped fast
flow~\cite{gundlach1998}; each later slice warm-starts from the previous surface and converges in a few
iterations to a root-mean-square residual below the solver tolerance of $10^{-6}$. From the converged surface
we quote the areal radius $R=\sqrt{A/4\pi}$, the Misner--Sharp mass of Eq.~\eqref{eq:misner_sharp}, and the
harmonic content beyond $\ell=0$ as the deformation. The instrument recovers Kerr--Schild Schwarzschild
($R=2M$ to $\clmFlowFinderSchw\%$, $M_{\rm MS}=M$ to $\clmFlowFinderSchwMass\%$) and finds no trapped surface
outside the time-symmetric Ellis throat. Every horizon number in this paper---formation times, $R$,
$M_{\rm MS}$---is read from these surfaces. The star-shaped scan of
Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics} is retained only as an inner bound and a live monitor: a fully trapped round
sphere lies strictly inside a deformed MOTS, so while the surface is forming the scan under-reads it by the
shape systematic quoted there, and can miss it altogether.

\subsection{The static throat as a fixed point}
\label{sec:single:fixedpoint}

Evolved without a declared perturbation, the exact drainhole holds $R_\star=\clmRstar$ to $\clmHoldTolLevelThree\%$ for $\clmHoldTimeLevelThree$ time units at level 3 and to $\clmHoldTolOnePct\%$ until $t\simeq\clmHoldTimeOnePct$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a)); at level 4 it is exact to \clmLevelFourDigits{} digits at $t=30$. The departure moves \emph{later} by $\clmOnsetShiftLo$--$\clmOnsetShiftHi$ units per halving of the cell---a truncation-noise seed of fixed growth rate handed a smaller amplitude. Level 2 dies of an origin failure at $t=\clmLevelTwoDeath$ with the throat still exact to $\clmLevelTwoDev\%$. A lighter throat is less stable: at level 3 the $m=\clmHalfMassM$ throat holds $\clmHalfMassHoldTol\%$ only to $t\approx\clmHalfMassHoldTime$, against $\clmHoldTimeLevelThree$ at $m=\clmThroatM$, and dies at its origin at $t=\clmHalfMassDeath$.

\subsection{The unstable mode}
\label{sec:single:instability}

The departure is a single exponential mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b)): plateau fits give $\tau=\clmTauLevelThree$ (level 3) and $\clmTauLevelFour$ (level 4), i.e.\ $T\equiv\tau\,\alpha_{\rm th}/R_\star=\clmProperEfoldThree$ and $\clmProperEfoldFour$, where $\alpha_{\rm th}=e^{u(r_t)}=\clmThroatLapse$ is the static lapse at the throat, so that $T$ is the proper-time e-fold in units of the throat's light-crossing time. Our drainhole is the $\gamma_1=m/a=\clmThroatGammaOne$ member of the static family of Gonz\'alez, Guzm\'an and Sarbach (their metric with $b=a$, $\gamma_0=-\pi\gamma_1/2$), for which their linear analysis gives $T=\clmGgsMatched$; their Table~I spans $T=\clmGgsMassless$ at the massless end to $\clmGgsMassive$~\cite{gonzalez2009lin}. Solving the linearised field equations of this solution ourselves---their regularised master equation and, independently, the reduction in the gauge $\delta\phi=0$---gives $T=\clmLinT$, i.e.\ $\tau_{\rm lin}=\clmLinTau\,M$. The measured e-fold approaches it with resolution: it is $\clmGgsAgreementFour\%$ longer at level 4 and $\clmGgsAgreement\%$ longer at level 3; with two resolutions we quote no convergence order. The seed is truncation error, and its sign flips with resolution---level 3 collapses, level 4 inflates---confirming from exact massive data the two branches found for the massless throat~\cite{shirokov2026}.

\subsection{Branching: collapse versus inflation}
\label{sec:single:branching}

A declared kick, Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed}, overrides the truncation seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c)). At $\varepsilon=\pm10^{-2}$ and $\pm10^{-3}$ the fate is always opposite to the push, each $\pm$ pair crosses at the $\varepsilon$-independent $t_\times\simeq\clmCrossingMean$, and a tenfold smaller kick arrives $\clmMatchedDelayLo$--$\clmMatchedDelayHi$ units later at matched horizon radius---linear response on the same mode. The branching survives refinement: at level 4, whose own seed inflates the unkicked throat, $+10^{-2}$ still forms a horizon at $t=\clmHorizonTimeLevelFour$ and $-10^{-2}$ still inflates, the twins crossing at $t=\clmCrossingLevelFour$ against level 3's $\clmCrossingLevelThree$. The branch point is the throat's, not the grid's or the kick's. The kick also sets the clock: horizons form at $t=\clmHorizonTimeEpsTwo$ ($\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$) and $t=\clmHorizonTimeEpsThree$ ($+10^{-3}$), against $t\le\clmHorizonTimeNoise$ for the unkicked throat (its first scanned slice after departure already holds a MOTS). At $\varepsilon=\pm0.1$ the data are no longer perturbative (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c)): $+0.1$ turns the minimal surface into a maximum between two minima and is trapped from the first scan ($t=\clmCeilingPlusMotsTime$), while $-0.1$, which narrows the throat to $R_{\min}=\clmCeilingMinusRzero$, re-expands to $\clmCeilingMinusRmax$ by $t=\clmCeilingMinusRmaxTime$ and then collapses as well, trapped from $t=\clmCeilingMinusMotsTime$---against the sign rule. Both die at the compactified origin ($t=\clmCeilingDeathPlus$ and $\clmCeilingDeathMinus$, NaN in $h_{11}$), the failure that also ends the $+10^{-3}$ arm at $t=\clmKickThreeDeath$, before the lapse has collapsed anywhere. Their Hamiltonian defect is $\clmCeilingHamLo$--$\clmCeilingHamHi\times$ that of the $1\%$ arms in the box norm and $\clmCeilingDefectLo$--$\clmCeilingDefectHi\times16\pi|\rho|$ on the shell; the usable range ends at $10^{-2}$.

\subsection{The throat in motion}
\label{sec:single:moving}

A uniformly moving throat keeps the resting throat's fate. The exact-boost $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ throat (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:boost}; level 3, $v=\clmBoostSpeed$) rides $R_\star$, rising at most $\clmBoostMovingHoldMax\%$, until $t=\clmBoostMovingOnePct$, where it falls $1\%$ below its start (the resting level-3 throat: $t\simeq\clmHoldTimeOnePct$), and then collapses on the resting throat's own mode: $\tau=\clmBoostMovingTauLo$--$\clmBoostMovingTauHi$ against $\clmTauLevelThree$ at rest, on the same branch---level 3 collapses---and it dies at the same numerical endpoint ($t=\clmBoostMovingDeath$, Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a)). The instability is the throat's, carried with the motion; the momentum changes neither the branch nor the rate.

\subsection{The collapse endpoint}
\label{sec:single:collapse}

The collapse branch ends in a black hole that consumes its supporting field. On the unkicked level-3 arm the horizon's areal radius falls $\clmUnkickedHorizonRStart\to\clmUnkickedHorizonREnd$ and $M_{\rm MS}$ $\clmUnkickedHorizonMStart\to\clmUnkickedHorizonMEnd$ over $\clmUnkickedHorizonSpan$ units, with the origin lapse at $\clmUnkickedOriginLapse$; the kicked $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ arm runs clean to $t=\clmKickedRunsClean$ behind its horizon. Unlike the massless throat of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026}, which bounced at $t\approx\clmMasslessBounce{}M$ and shed its horizon by $\clmMasslessShed{}M$, no arm here sheds a horizon. Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026} drove that collapse by rescaling the phantom's source term, halving its stress-energy in the Einstein equations; where the present unmodified evolutions differ---the bounce and the horizon shedding---they supersede it. The pure-quadrupole arm ($\varepsilon_2=\clmPureQuadAmp$, no radial kick) is fully instrumented (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse}): a MOTS from $t=\clmPureQuadMotsTime$ at $R=\clmPureQuadMotsRStart$, shrinking to $\clmPureQuadMotsREnd$ ($M_{\rm MS}$ $\clmPureQuadMotsMStart\to\clmPureQuadMotsMEnd$), while $|K|$ grows inward and the lapse pinches to zero at the centre.

The shrinking is physical; the regrowth that follows it in the scans is not. In spherical symmetry a massless phantom can only shrink a horizon: with the double-null metric $-2e^{-f}\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}v+R^2\mathrm{d}\Omega^2$, $T_{uv}=0$ and $T_{vv}=-(\partial_v\phi)^2$, the Misner--Sharp mass on a marginally trapped tube obeys $\partial_v m=4\pi R^2e^{f}(\partial_v\phi)^2\partial_uR\le0$ and $\partial_u m=0$, and the tube is timelike or null~\cite{hayward1996,hayward1994}, so $R=2m$ can only fall, as phantom accretion requires~\cite{babichev2004,gao2008}. In the three arms kicked with $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$---at level 3, and at level 4 dressed with $\varepsilon_2=\clmDressedQuadSmall$ and $\clmDressedQuadLarge$---the horizon falls by $\clmRegrowthShrinkPct\%$ in areal radius to a floor at $t\approx\clmRegrowthFloorTime$ ($R=\clmRegrowthFloorR$), and the scans then show it growing back by $\clmRegrowthRadiusLo$--$\clmRegrowthRadiusHi\%$. That regrowth is not a measurement. It tracks a Hamiltonian-constraint violation that reaches the horizon at the floor time, it is the same in a purely spherical level-4 arm, and levels 3 and 4 agree on the horizon's radius to a median $\clmRegrowthLevelAgreement\%$ over the record, so it is not truncation error but most plausibly the evolved defect of our unconstrained seed (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}). The pure-quadrupole arm, whose seed has no $\ell=0$ defect, only shrinks (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse}(a)). What the collapse branch establishes is the shrink: the throat becomes a black hole that swallows its own phantom support and loses $\clmRegrowthShrinkPct\%$ of its horizon radius doing so, in minutes even at $10^{6}\,M_\odot$ (Table~\ref{tab:scales}). Hawking's area law assumes the null energy condition~\cite{hawking1971,ashtekar_krishnan2004}, which the phantom violates; this horizon runs it backwards.

\subsection{The inflation branch}
\label{sec:single:fate}

The inflation branch forms no trapped surface. The $\varepsilon=-10^{-2}$ throat, evolved on an octant of an $L=\clmFourBoxL$ box with finest $\Delta x=\clmFourFinestDx$ (the resolution of our $L=\clmBoxL$ level-3 arms; Fig.~\ref{fig:single_inflation}), keeps growing to the end of the record, $\times\clmFourRRatio$ by $t=\clmFourWall$, and stays anti-trapped~\cite{hayward1999}: the $\theta_l=0$ horizon sits on the neck and $\theta_k=0$ runs outward, to $R\approx\clmFourHorizonR$ by $t=\clmFourHorizonTime$. Until $t\approx\clmFourSHWinHi$ the neck grows exponentially in its own proper time with $HR_0=\clmFourSHRate$ (locally $\clmFourSHLocalLo$--$\clmFourSHLocalHi$; $R_0=\clmFourRStart$, the kicked neck), $\clmFourLinearGap\%$ below our linear mode's $\clmFourLinearRate$, consistent with this arm's level-3-class resolution. Spherical evolutions find the same proper-time exponential: $Ha\approx\clmSHMassless$ for the massless throat~\cite{shinkai2002} and exponential growth for massive ones~\cite{gonzalez2009}. After that the $1{+}\log$ slicing freezes the lapse at the neck ($\clmFourLapseStart\to\clmFourLapseEnd$), only $\approx\clmFourTauLate$ units of its proper time pass by $t=\clmFourWall$, and its growth per unit $t$ slows---a property of the slicing, not of the throat.

Its end state is open, and the throat is shedding its own support: the monopole scalar flux through every sphere grows quasi-exponentially over the quotable window (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}). Three endings remain: saturation at a larger radius, disfavoured because static drainholes are unstable fixed points; exponential inflation in proper time, which spherical evolutions find~\cite{shinkai2002,gonzalez2009} and our onset matches; or a turnover into collapse once too little phantom support remains, which a flux still growing when its reservoir is spent would force. That reservoir now has a number: outside the static throat the field carries a Misner--Sharp deficit of $R_\star/2-m=\clmInfStoreDeficit$. Its spending is only bracketed. Over the window in which the exterior slicing is still quiet, the arms of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship} shed $\clmInfShedQuiet$ through $R=18$---a few percent of the store---while on this arm the $1{+}\log$ front reaches every sphere before the monopole grows, and the energy through the coordinate-$60$ sphere by $t=\clmFourWall$ reads $\clmInfShedKin$ kinematically but $\clmInfShedGeo$ with the geometric factor of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar} applied---either side of the store. A downturn of $R_{\min}(t)$ followed by a trapped surface would decide the ending; none occurs by $t=\clmFourWall$, the growth holds at every sample, and the $\theta_k=0$ surface keeps moving outward, with no slowing over the last fifty units; its rate in areal radius per unit coordinate time, ${\approx}\clmInfBoundarySpeed$, depends on the slicing and is not a propagation speed.

The lifetime is a logarithm of the seed: the horizon times of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching} set a decade's cost at $\clmDecadeCost\,M\simeq\tau\ln10$, and the unkicked throat's places the level-3 truncation seed at $\varepsilon\gtrsim\clmNoiseSeed$. Table~\ref{tab:scales} converts these to physical units. No static throat of astrophysical mass survives: a stellar-mass drainhole is gone in milliseconds, a supermassive one in minutes, and only at $10^9\,M_\odot$ does the lifetime reach days. \subsection{A type-I critical solution}
\label{sec:single:critical}

In the language of critical collapse the static throat is a type-I critical solution~\cite{gundlach2007}: a static solution with a single unstable mode (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability}), sitting on the threshold between two end states---collapse and inflation, where the standard problem has collapse and dispersal. The measurements above carry both type-I signatures. The first is time scaling: data a distance $\varepsilon$ from threshold stay near the critical solution for a time $\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)$ set by its one e-fold, which is the lifetime law of Table~\ref{tab:scales}, $t_{\rm BH}\simeq\clmHorizonTimeEpsTwo\,M+\tau\ln(10^{-2}/\varepsilon)$, and both branches leave along the same mode, each $\pm\varepsilon$ pair crossing at the same $t_\times$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}). The second is a mass gap: black-hole formation turns on at finite mass, as at the static Bartnik--McKinnon solution of Yang--Mills collapse~\cite{choptuik1996}, not at the vanishing mass of type-II scaling. The $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ and $+10^{-3}$ horizons form at $R=\clmHorizonRadiusEpsTwo$ and $\clmHorizonRadiusEpsThree$, both close to the throat's own $R_\star$: a tenfold smaller kick leaves the black hole's mass at formation almost unchanged, where type-II scaling would shrink it as a power of $\varepsilon$. Shinkai and Hayward found evidence, in spherical evolutions of the massless throat, for critical solutions with a definite black-hole mass and for a collapse time tied to the input energy by an apparently universal exponent~\cite{shinkai2002}. The measurements here identify the critical solution as the static throat itself, whose one unstable mode is measured in 3+1 dimensions and approaches the linear rate with resolution (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability}).

\subsection{The sign rule}
\label{sec:pair:sign}

Two identical throats released from rest push apart; reversing one throat's scalar, $\sigma=+1\to-1$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}, turns the push into a pull (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b)). Both forces share one radial law, so their ratio $Q$ is separation-independent: no gap, mass or approach speed lets a like-signed pair fall together, and $\sigma=\clmMergerSign$ is the only gravity-driven merger channel. The flip is not a tuning: $\phi\to-\phi$ leaves $T_{ab}$, and with it the one-body geometry, unchanged. The measured pull-to-push ratio at $a=\clmThroatA$ is $\clmMatchedSignRatio\pm\clmMatchedSignRatioErr$ (scatter over $15$ slices) against the $(Q+1)/(Q-1)=\clmScalarQRatio$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}, and the mass is equal in all arms, so the asymmetry is the scalar charge's.

\subsection{The force law}
\label{sec:pair:force}

How the push falls with distance is read off a ladder (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c)). \clmCountLikeSignedPairs{} like-signed pairs, identical but for the gap $d=12/14/16/18$, are released from rest, and by a common $t=11.5$ their separations have grown by $\delta d=\clmDLadderTwelve/\clmDLadderFourteen/\clmDLadderSixteen/\clmDLadderEighteen$. For displacements this small, $\delta d$ at a fixed time is proportional to the force, so under the inverse-square law of Eq.~\eqref{eq:force} $\delta d\,d^2$ would be the same on every rung. It is not: it rises monotonically, $\clmFdSquaredLo\to\clmFdSquaredHi$, across the ladder, which excludes inverse-square in the coordinate separation. Instead $F\propto(d+\delta)^{-2}$ with $\delta\approx\clmOffsetDeltaLo$--$\clmOffsetDeltaHi$ describes it: $\delta$ fitted on $d=12$--$16$ alone predicts $\clmOffsetPrediction$ at $d=18$, where inverse-square gives $\clmInverseSquarePrediction$ and the measurement is $\clmDLadderEighteen$; the full-ladder fit gives $\delta=\clmMatchedOffsetDelta$. Since $\delta$ is a sizable fraction of the mouth radius $R_\star=\clmRstarShort$, the force is inverse-square in an \emph{effective} separation exceeding the coordinate one by most of a throat radius. Across widths, at $d=12$, the push grows at every rung (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(d)): $\delta d=\clmALadderOne/\clmALadderOneHalf/\clmALadderTwo/\clmALadderThree$ by $t=11.5$ for $a=1/1.5/2/3$, a power law $\delta d\propto a^n$ with $n=\clmWidthExponentLo$--$\clmWidthExponentHi$ when fitted at $t=8$--$11.5$, where point charges, whose net push $(Q-1)\,m^2/d^2$ is $a^2/d^2$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}, give $n=2$.

\label{sec:spiral:capture}

Varying the tangential momentum $p$ at $d=12$ on boosted, solved data separates scattering from plunge (Fig.~\ref{fig:orbits} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}). The two slower arms scatter: at $p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ the pair passes at $\clmFlybyLbfClosest$ ($t\approx\clmFlybyLbfClosestTime$) and recedes to $\clmFlybyLbfFar$ by $t=100$; at $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ it passes closer, $\clmPFortyFiveClosest$ at $t\approx\clmPFortyFiveClosestTime$, and recedes to $\clmPFortyFiveFar$. Both reach their stop times with no NaN---the $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ arm at stock settings to $t=100$, so the channel has no wall of its own below the plunge boundary---and the finder sees no trapped surface on any plotfile of either. The two faster arms plunge: $p=\clmEgwPSixty$ reaches contact at $t\approx\clmPSixtyContactTime$ (pit separation $\clmPSixtyContactSep$ at its first leg's stop) and $p=\clmEgwPNinety$ just past its $t=40$ leg ($\clmPNinetyContactSep$ and still falling), each collapsing to a merged core (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}). The plunge boundary therefore sits between $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ and $\clmEgwPSixty$: $\clmPFortyFiveCircFrac$--$\clmPSixtyCircFrac\%$ of the circular momentum under the combined pull, bracketing the circular value itself. That pull, $1+Q$ times gravity's (Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}), is central: a pair that misses contact keeps its angular momentum and swings past. But the approach deepens with speed---closest approach falls from $\clmFlybyLbfClosest$ at $p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ through $\clmPFortyFiveClosest$ at $\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ to contact at $\clmEgwPSixty$, against the Newtonian expectation of a pericentre growing with $J=pd$---so capture here is contact, not an innermost orbit. Nor do the scatterers escape: both start below the circular momentum and remain bound in Newtonian terms; the vacuum innermost stable circular orbit does not apply to a pair bound by a pull six times gravity's, and Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} shows the throats' own decay would end any wider orbit within a few periods. What follows a pass is set by the throats, which inflate (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:race}).

The $p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ fly-by ($L=128$, level 5 to $t=100$) is the production scattering pass. The turn at closest approach is orbital, but the recession after it is not that of two compact throats: both mouths inflate, the constraint norm crosses the campaign's $2.5\times10^{-2}$ criterion at $t=\clmFlybyLbfTrust$ as they do, and nothing is quoted from the run after that time, nor from its waves after the matching retarded time (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:flyby}). By then the encounter is an expanding cloud of the field that supported the throats, not two receding wormholes.

\subsection{The race between contact and inflation}
\label{sec:spiral:race}

At $d=12$ no arm merges at any momentum. The exact-boost spiral, its Bowen--York-momentum twin (solved data, the momentum ansatz the knob), and an inward-momentum scout ($p=\clmDTwelveInwardP$ aimed straight at the companion) all end the same way: the mouths inflate faster than the orbit closes---the scout's $R_{\min}$ grows $+\clmDTwelveInflEight\%$ by $t=8$ and $+\clmDTwelveInflTwentyFour\%$ by $t=24$ while its infall stalls near separation $\clmDTwelveStallSep$---and the finder holds no common MOTS on any plotfile of the three (the boosted arm dies of the interior failure at $t=\clmSpiralLbfDeath$ having never trapped within its search window).

The mechanism is the branch selection of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}, run by the companion. A lone throat's fate is opposite to a declared kick: pushed outward it collapses, squeezed it inflates. A companion squeezes: solving the constraints with the second throat present compresses each mouth, by $\clmRaceKickTwelve\%$ at $d=12$ and $\clmRaceKickEight\%$ at $d=8$---the physical interaction, not a data defect---so every pair's mouths start on the inflation branch, at any momentum, and grow on the throat's own e-fold time $\tau\approx\clmTauLevelThree$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability}). That the solve keeps a declared seed is checked directly: the $\varepsilon=+0.1$ kicked single, run through the constraint solve, is born trapped (a MOTS on its $t=0$ slice, $R=\clmSeedSolvedMotsR$), the fate its kick dictates. A merger is therefore a race against the inflation it starts: the head-on's horizon arrives $\clmRaceFoldsHeadon$ e-folds in and wins; the $d=6$ orbit's arrives at $\clmRaceFoldsSix$ e-folds and wins (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}); the $d=12$ approach, $\clmRaceFoldsTwelve$ e-folds to its closest pass, always loses. The design rule for an orbital merger follows: short separation and small tangential momentum.

\subsection{The $d=6$ merger}
\label{sec:spiral:inspiral}

The design point that wins the race is $d=6$ with a small tangential twist, $p=\clmDSixP$ per mouth. A level-3 scout merges, and the production chain repeats it on the $L=128$ box (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_collapse}): the spectral finder holds a common MOTS around both mouths from $t=\clmMergerMotsTime$---$\clmRaceFoldsSix$ e-folds into the inflation, the pits still $\clmMergerBirthSep$ apart---born with $R=\clmMergerMotsRadius$ ($M_{\rm MS}=\clmMergerMotsMass$, deformation $\clmMergerBornDeform$) and shrinking monotonically ($\le\clmMergerRowWobble\%$ per row over the finder's $\clmMergerRows$ rows) by $\clmMergerShrinkR\%$ to $R=\clmMergerEndRadius$, $M_{\rm MS}=\clmMergerEndMass$, deformation $\clmMergerEndDeform$ at $t=100$: a settled black hole within $\clmMergerRemnantAgree\%$ of the head-on remnant's radius, reached through an orbit. The chain is the head-on's pattern stretched over a harder wall (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}): level 5 from $t=0$, stopped at $t=\clmMergerLegOneStop$ as the merged core's $K$ ran away; a $\sigma_{\rm KO}=1.0$ leg through the $K$ wall to $t=\clmMergerSigDeath$; the $\chi$-floor leg ($\min\chi$ raised to $10^{-4}$), which sails through the single-cell wall, past every earlier death, to $t=\clmMergerChiStop$; and a level-4 settle leg, the wall knobs reverted, to $t=100$ with no NaN---the cured interior running at its own $\chi_{\min}\sim\clmMergerSettleChi$, below the crossing leg's floor, with no cap needed. The floor is a crossing device, confined to $t=\clmMergerSeamTwo$--$\clmMergerChiStop$ inside the horizon; everything quoted here comes from the finder's surface outside it.

\subsection{Interior failure after horizon formation}
\label{sec:spiral:wall}

Two failure modes stand between a merged core and its settled remnant, and each now has a measured cure. The first is a single-cell blow-up of the metric component $h_{11}$ at the merged core's centre: the head-on's level-5 leg at $t=\clmHeadonCsmNanTime$, the $d=6$ chain's restarts at $t=\clmMergerLvlSevenDeath$--$\clmMergerSigDeath$, the $p=\clmEgwPSixty$ plunge at $t=\clmPSixtyExtDeath$---in every case with the global constraint norms clean to the last step. It is not under-resolution: on the $d=6$ chain the level-6 restart dies at $t=\clmMergerLvlSixDeath$ and the level-7 restart dies \emph{earlier}, at $t=\clmMergerLvlSevenDeath$ with $\max|K|$ still climbing---a finer grid tracks the core's collapse further and meets the failure sooner---and raising the dissipation to $\sigma_{\rm KO}=1.0$ delays the cell by a unit without curing it. What cures it is the $\chi$ floor: with $\min\chi$ capped at $10^{-4}$ the $1/\chi$ steepness runaway cannot complete, and both the $d=6$ chain and the $p=\clmEgwPSixty$ plunge cross their death points and run to $t=100$, the $d=6$ settle leg then running floor-free. The second mode is the $K$ wall: $\max|K|$ itself running away at the merging core, $\clmPNinetyKLo\to\clmPNinetyKHi$ in one step on the $p=\clmEgwPNinety$ arm at $t=\clmPNinetyDeath$ with $\chi$ nowhere near its floor ($\chi_{\min}=\clmPNinetyChiMin$)---the floor does not touch it. Its knob is resolution: the $d=6$ chain's level-6 restart cleared the same runaway that stopped its level-5 leg.

Both failures are censored. The core fails only after a common MOTS encloses it: the $d=6$ cell NaNs arrive $\clmMergerWallLeadLo$--$\clmMergerWallLeadHi$ units after its horizon, the head-on's $\clmHeadonCsmWallLead$ after its, and lone collapses die behind their horizons at level 3 too ($\varepsilon=+10^{-3}$ at $t=\clmKickThreeDeath$, $\clmKickThreeLead$ units after its horizon forms; $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ dressed with $\varepsilon_2=10^{-2}$ at $t=\clmDressedDeathLevelThree$, $\clmDressedLeadLevelThree$ after), while no level-4 lone collapse dies. The failure occupies one cell (the $K$ wall, a few), the exterior is already set, and the finder's surface rides through every crossing unmoved: whatever diverges is the coordinate representation of a merged interior, behind the horizon.

The record also tests weak cosmic censorship outside the matter it is posed for: censorship is conjectured for physically reasonable matter~\cite{penrose1969,wald1997}, and the phantom violates the null energy condition. In every collapse resolved here---the head-on across its legs, the $d=6$ chain at levels 5--7, and the lone collapses---the evolution fails, if at all, only inside a MOTS. We have not determined whether a curvature singularity reaches the slice at the failure, so this is evidence for censorship under null-energy violation, not a proof of it; the plunges whose horizon never converges (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:capture}) leave their failures uncensored on the slice, at the edge of what the grid resolves. Every exterior quantity we quote is taken before its run's trust window closes.

\section{Gravitational waves}
\label{sec:gw}

Every channel radiates, and at our extraction radii the most energetic is the fly-by at the capture boundary, which forms no horizon and escapes. The peak $|r\Psi_4|$ of the dominant mode at the innermost sphere is $\clmGwPeakFlyby$ for the fly-by, $\clmGwPeakPlunge$ for the plunge, $\clmGwPeakHeadon$ for the head-on, $\clmGwPeakSpiral$ for the $d=6$ merger, $\clmGwPeakTwin$ for the vacuum reference and $\clmGwPeakThroat$ for the collapsing throat (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}). Each record propagates outward at $v/c=\clmGwSpeedLow$--$\clmGwSpeedHigh$ between neighbouring spheres, the correlated-lag measurement of Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:bbh} (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}, right). No sphere is asymptotic: the dominant periods are comparable to the extraction radii, and the peak $r\Psi_4$ falls across the spheres by $\clmGwPeakFallHeadon\%$ on the head-on and $\clmGwPeakFallFlyby\%$ on the fly-by. We therefore do not extrapolate, and carry the spread over spheres as the near-zone error bar of every energy (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(d)).

The records are also clean of initial-data junk radiation. Every binary evolves constraint-solved data (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:solve}), so there is no $t=0$ defect to radiate: a defect would cross an extraction sphere within one light-crossing, $t\approx R$, while every quoted burst arrives with its physical trigger, tens of units later, and the per-sphere envelopes rise with the burst, not at $t-R\approx0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}, right). The seeded singles carry a declared defect by design (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}), measured where it is largest: the spherically kicked throat, whose shell defect peaks at $\clmSeedDefectPeak\times16\pi|\rho|$, radiates nothing above the $\ell=2$ numerical floor (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:gw}), and the quadrupolar arms' bursts are linear in $\varepsilon_2$ and unchanged when the radial kick is removed (Fig.~\ref{fig:seed_linearity})---the throat's wave, not the shell's transient.

In the dominant multipole the fly-by radiates $\clmGwEnergyFlybyLo\times10^{-2}\,M$ through the outermost sphere ($\clmGwEnergyFlybyHi\times10^{-2}\,M$ through the innermost, where near-zone content is largest) and the $d=6$ merger $\clmGwEnergySpiral\,M$. For scale, the closest non-merging black-hole scatterings computed in vacuum radiate ${\approx}\clmGwLitScatter$ of their total mass in all multipoles~\cite{damour2014}; the drainhole pair is pulled six times harder. The chirping vacuum reference---a binary black hole at $d=12$, $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$, equal masses, no scalar (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:bbh})---radiates $\clmGwEnergyTwin\,M$, $\clmGwEnergyRatioSpiral\times$ less than the merger; the fly-by's own control is a vacuum binary at $d=\clmGwVacPassSep$, $p=\clmGwVacPassMom$. In vacuum that momentum is unbound (circular $\clmGwVacPassCirc$, parabolic $\clmGwVacPassPar$), so the black holes start at periapsis and coast apart, radiating $\clmGwEnergyVacPass\,M$ in a single cycle as they swing away (grey in Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}(d): peak $|r\Psi_4|=\clmGwPeakVacPass$ at the fly-by's sphere, $\clmGwVacPassPeakRatio\times$ below the fly-by's), whereas the drainhole pair falls from $12$ to $\clmPFortyFiveClosest$ under a pull six times gravity's. The fly-by thus outradiates a vacuum binary of its own parameters by $\clmGwVacPassRatioLo$--$\clmGwVacPassRatioHi\times$. The vacuum controls use the settings of \texttt{GRTeclyn}'s black-hole example (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:grid}), so these ratios include a numerical systematic we have not quantified.

The closest positive-energy comparison is a boson-star binary, likewise horizonless and held up by a scalar field. Its mergers end in a boson star or a black hole~\cite{palenzuela2017,bezares2017,bezares2022}, unless the pair is a boson--antiboson one, which all but annihilates~\cite{bezares2017}, and head-on outcomes and wave output depend on the stars' relative phase~\cite{palenzuela2007}. The negative-energy support changes how a horizonless encounter ends: the fly-by leaves no star to relax and ring down but two mouths that inflate (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:capture}), while the field that held them open radiates energy of the opposite sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}).

\subsection{Collapsing throat}
\label{sec:single:gw}

A lone throat radiates only if its collapse is not spherical. The spherically kicked arm emits nothing above the $\ell=2$ numerical floor, while the same kick with an $\varepsilon_2=\clmGwSeedStrong$ quadrupole produces a burst of peak $|r\Psi_4|=\clmGwPeakThroat$, $\clmGwThroatOverControl\times$ the floor of the matched level-4 control at $R=\clmExtractRadiusFourteen$, carrying $\clmGwEnergyThroat\,M$ (integrated to $t=\clmGwThroatEnergyCut$, before the numerical floor dominates). The burst arrives in radius order at $\clmGwThroatSpeedLo$--$\clmGwThroatSpeedHi\,c$, carries the same $r\Psi_4$ to four spheres within $\clmGwThroatSphereSpread\%$, and is linear in the seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:seed_linearity} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}): $\clmGwThroatFiveFold\times$ for a $\clmGwThroatSeedRatio\times$ kick, ratios $\clmGwHalvedRatioA/\clmGwHalvedRatioB/\clmGwHalvedRatioC$ at $R=10/14/\clmGwSeedRadiusC$ for a halved seed, linear to $\clmGwLinDevLo$--$\clmGwLinDevHi\%$ across $\varepsilon_2=\clmGwSeedWeak$--$\clmGwSeedStrong$. The pure quadrupole---$\varepsilon_2=\clmGwSeedPure$ with the radial kick removed---radiates the same amplitude to $\clmGwPureVsKicked\%$, its waveform leading the kicked arm's by $\clmGwPureLeadLo$--$\clmGwPureLeadHi\,M$: the deformation sets the wave, the kick only its clock. These seed amplitudes follow the wave, not a decade grid: $\varepsilon_2=\clmGwSeedKicked$ matches the radial kick, whose usable range ends at $10^{-2}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}); $\clmGwSeedStrong$ was added when the $\clmGwSeedKicked$ burst stood only ${\approx}\clmGwSeedOldFloorRatio\times$ above the spherical control's floor, too little to read a ringdown; and $\clmGwSeedWeak$ halves the headline seed. A $10^{-3}$ arm would sit below that floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:seed_linearity}(b)), and a $10^{-1}$ arm would deform the throat's poles by ${\approx}\clmGwSeedTenthDeform\%$, as much as the $\varepsilon=\pm0.1$ radial seeds that do not survive. The remnant rings at period $\clmGwThroatPeriod\,M$ (dashed in Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}(a); an independent least-squares fit gives $\clmGwThroatPeriodFit\pm\clmGwThroatPeriodFitErr$), with no drift across the window (half-periods $\clmGwThroatHalfPeriodA$, $\clmGwThroatHalfPeriodB$ and $\clmGwThroatHalfPeriodC\,M$), consistent with the Schwarzschild fundamental of a black hole of mass $\clmGwThroatMassFloor$--$\clmGwThroatMassEnd$ (period $\clmGwThroatSchwLo$--$\clmGwThroatSchwHi\,M$~\cite{berti2009}), close to the horizon mass near its minimum. After that minimum our horizon masses are not reliable (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}), so we claim no more than this consistency. It does not follow the larger mass the horizon carries at the window's start ($\clmGwThroatMassStart$, whose fundamental period is $\clmGwThroatPeriodStartMass\,M$). The decay time is not measurable on this record.

\subsection{Head-on ringdown}
\label{sec:headon:wave}

The head-on's signal is a bell with no chirp: the $(2,0)$ breathing mode at $R=\clmGwHeadonRadiusA$ swings through $\clmGwHeadonSwingA$, $\clmGwHeadonSwingB$, $\clmGwHeadonSwingC$ at $t=\clmGwHeadonSwingTimeA$, $\clmGwHeadonSwingTimeB$, $\clmGwHeadonSwingTimeC$---the half-period stretching from $\clmGwHeadonHalfLo$ to $\clmGwHeadonHalfHi$, the amplitude falling $\times\clmGwHeadonDecayLo$--$\clmGwHeadonDecayHi$ per half-swing---and arrives ${\sim}\clmGwHeadonLag$ units later per $\clmGwHeadonSpacing$ units of extraction radius ($\clmGwHeadonSpeed\,c$), outgoing across all three spheres. On the detector track it is the bell that slows from $\clmGwHeadonFreqHi$ to $\clmGwHeadonFreqLo$ Hz in Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(c). Band-integrated about its peak, the bell carries $E_{\rm GW}/M=\clmHeadonCsmGwEnergy$ through $R=14$, falling from $\clmHeadonCsmGwEnergyInner$ to $\clmHeadonCsmGwEnergyOuter$ across $R=10$--$18$, the bell's own near-zone spread. The vacuum control---two equal black holes released from rest at the same $d=\clmHeadonSeparation$---rings the same $(2,0)$ bell at the same sphere $\clmBbhHeadonGwRatio\times$ quieter ($E_{\rm GW}/M=\clmBbhHeadonGwEnergy$) and $\clmBbhHeadonPeakLag$ units later (its crest at $t=\clmBbhHeadonPeakT$ against $\clmHeadonCsmPeakT$): without the scalar's pull the holes fall slower and hit softer.

\subsection{Merger burst}
\label{sec:spiral:burst}

The $d=6$ merger's $(2,2)$ burst peaks at $|r\Psi_4|=\clmGwPeakSpiral$ and folds onto one curve across $R=\clmGwSpiralRadiusIn$--$\clmGwSpiralRadiusOut$ at $v/c=\clmGwSpeedSpiralA/\clmGwSpeedSpiralB/\clmGwSpeedSpiralC$. The burst is launched at contact---the common MOTS of $t=\clmMergerMotsTime$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral})---peaks at $R=\clmGwSpiralRadiusIn$ at $t\approx\clmGwSpiralPeakTime$, and the remnant's ringdown carries the record, clean, to $t=100$ on the settle leg (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}(c)).

\subsection{Scatter and plunge bursts}
\label{sec:gw:flyby}

\subsection{Vacuum twin}
\label{sec:controls:bbh}

The chirping reference---a vacuum binary at $d=\clmPairSeparation$, $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$, equal masses, no scalar---behaves as a binary black hole must: it merges, climbs the point-mass chirp $\clmGwTwinFreqLo\to\clmGwTwinFreqHi$ Hz and turns toward the equal-mass Kerr mode, its $\Psi_4$ peak at the textbook $fM=\clmGwTwinPeakFM$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(c)); no wormhole channel does either. It also calibrates the wavefront method: its flat-topped merger envelope defeats peak timing, which reads $\clmGwSpeedTwinPeak{}c$ on it against the $\clmGwSpeedTwin{}c$ of its whole-waveform lag, so every speed here is a correlated lag.

\subsection{Scalar channel and its sign}
\label{sec:gw:scalar}

$\Psi_4$ is blind to a spin-$0$ wave. Two opposite scalar charges---every merging pair here (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair})---carry a dipole, where a vacuum binary's leading moment is the quadrupole. The scalar stream rides the same spheres as $\Psi_4$ on the spiral and fly-by, so the two channels compare sphere by sphere (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}). The recorded kinematic flux $F_{\rm kin}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}) approximates the scalar energy crossing $S_R$ per coordinate time. That energy is the flux, through the world tube that $S_R$ sweeps out, of the matter energy current $J^\mu\equiv-T^{(\phi)\mu}{}_{\nu}\,t^\nu$ of the time vector $t^\mu=\alpha n^\mu+\beta^\mu$, with $T^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}$ the canonical stress tensor (the negative of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}). Since $\nabla_\mu J^\mu=-\tfrac12T^{(\phi)\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_t g_{\mu\nu}$, the current is conserved where $t^\mu$ is a Killing vector and nearly so in the quasi-stationary exterior that the spheres sample, and Gauss's theorem on the world tube gives its flux in 3+1 form~\cite{clough2021,gourgoulhon2012},
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
F^{\rm geo}_\phi&=\oint_{S_R}\alpha\,s_iJ^i\sqrt{\sigma}\;\mathrm{d}^2x\\
&=-\oint_{S_R}\partial_t\phi\,\bigl(\alpha\,s^j\partial_j\phi+\beta_js^j\,\Pi\bigr)\sqrt{\sigma}\;\mathrm{d}^2x,
\end{split}
\label{eq:scalarflux}
\end{equation}
positive for an outgoing canonical wave, with $s^i$ the sphere's outward unit normal, $\sqrt{\sigma}$ its area element, $J^i$ the spatial components of the four-vector (not $\gamma^{ij}J_j$) and $\partial_t\phi=\alpha\Pi+\beta^i\partial_i\phi$. On a conformally flat sphere with uniform $\alpha$ and $\chi$ and no shift, $F^{\rm geo}_\phi=\alpha^2\chi^{-1/2}F_{\rm kin}$; the shift adds an advective term ${\propto}(\beta^i\partial_i\phi)(s^j\partial_j\phi)$ and a term ${\propto}\beta_js^j\,\Pi\,\partial_t\phi$ from its normal component. In the initial data the sphere at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$ has $\alpha\approx\clmGwScalarLapseInit$ and $\chi\approx\clmGwScalarChiInit$, and the area mean of $\alpha^2\chi^{-1/2}$ is $\approx\clmGwScalarFluxFactor$, so energies built from $F_{\rm kin}$ overstate $|F^{\rm geo}_\phi|$ by about $\clmGwScalarFluxCorr\%$; the shift terms are neglected.

The channel is dipole-dominated and comparable to the gravitational one. On the fly-by at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$, $\ell=1$ carries the scalar power $\sum_{\ell m}|\dot A_{\ell m}|^2$ to one part in $10^{\clmGwScalarDipoleShare}$, the $\ell=1$ signal travels outward at $c$ from $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusInner$, and the time-integrated flux to $t=\clmGwScalarCut$ is $|E_\phi|=\clmGwScalarEnergyFlyby\,M$---$\clmGwScalarRatioSixty$ times the gravitational energy that has crossed the sphere by then (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}(a)). The ratio depends on the cut and on how $E_{\rm GW}$ is integrated. With the running integral of Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}(a) it falls to $\clmGwScalarRatioSeventy$ by $t=\clmGwScalarCutSeventy$ and $\clmGwScalarRatioEighty$ by $t=\clmGwScalarCutEighty$, but that integral keeps growing after the burst has passed, accumulating the drift of the time-integrated $\Psi_4$; the band-limited energy of Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(d) levels off instead ($\clmGwScalarBandSixty$, $\clmGwScalarBandSeventy$ and $\clmGwScalarBandEighty$ at $t=60$, $70$ and $80$). The wave-zone mode sum gives $\clmGwScalarRatioWaveZone$, and the inner sphere returns $\clmGwScalarRatioInner$ for the same window. We therefore use only the channel's multipole, direction, sign and order of magnitude downstream.

Gravity couples to minus the canonical stress tensor, Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}, so the physical energy flux is $-F_{\rm kin}$ and an outgoing scalar wave carries negative energy; what the data establish is that the $\ell=1$ wave is outgoing (on the fly-by, and on the head-on after its horizon forms) and of the size above. We have not closed the ADM energy balance. If it holds with this term, a bound pair emitting it gains energy: the scalar channel would pump the orbit where gravitational radiation damps it, and radiation reaction would be \emph{anti-damped}. This is the classical face of the standard objection to ghosts. Quantum mechanically a ghost's vacuum decays into ghosts and positive-energy quanta together, at a rate kept finite only by cutting the effective theory off~\cite{carroll2003,cline2004}; classically these binaries make the same trade, emitting negative-energy scalar waves together with positive-energy gravitational waves, and the head-on remnant settles, within the accuracy of the scalar-flux estimate, near the pair's Bondi mass, a little above $2M_{\rm ADM}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}). The same bookkeeping appears at horizons, where swallowing the phantom shrinks the head-on remnant (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}) and the single-throat remnant (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}).

The dipole carries angular momentum with the same sign. On the fly-by, through both spheres and over its trusted record, the scalar's physical angular-momentum flux is opposite to the gravitational waves', which carry off angular momentum of the orbit's sign: the channel that returns energy to the orbit returns angular momentum too. It also feeds the nonlinear memory, which the energy flux of any massless radiation sources~\cite{christodoulou1991,thorne1992,favata2009}. The scalar's flux enters with its negative sign, yet it adds to the gravitational $(2,0)$ memory rather than cancelling it: about the source's axis, a dipole's flux pattern projects onto that harmonic with the opposite sign to a quadrupole's, and the negative energy turns it back. The fly-by and the head-on show the same sign on every sphere and window; the scalar's share of the memory carries the near-zone uncertainty of $E_\phi$ above.

\subsection{Horizon censorship of the scalar channel}
\label{sec:gw:censorship}

The head-on, re-run at level \clmGwCensHeadonLevel{} from $t=\clmGwCensHeadonStart$ with the scalar stream, is the one encounter that forms a horizon early, and the horizon switches the channel off (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_censorship} in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}). After the common MOTS closes ($t=\clmGwCensMotsTime$ on this arm's live scan), the mouths' remaining hair leaves as a single $\ell=1$, $m=0$ pulse, cresting later at each sphere in causal order, and the envelope then decays: log-linear fits over $t=\clmGwCensFitLo$--$\clmGwCensFitHi$ give $\tau=19/\clmGwCensTauMid/\clmGwCensTauOuter$ at $R=10/14/\clmGwHeadonRadiusC$. The post-horizon energies keep the sign of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}, $E_\phi=\clmGwCensEnergyInner/\clmGwCensEnergyMid/\clmGwCensEnergyOuter\,M$. Only this window is integrated: before $t=\clmGwCensIngoing$ the flux at $R=\clmGwHeadonRadiusA$ runs canonically ingoing, the two open mouths' static hair superposing.

Without a horizon the channel does not shut. The fly-by's dipole flux is still growing where its trusted record ends ($t-R=\clmFlybyTrustRetarded$). The spiral's horizon, present from $t=\clmSpiralFlowBirthTime$, is only \clmSpiralFlowLeadWord{} units old at its interior failure, against a shedding clock of ${\sim}\clmGwShedClock$, so it is too young to show the shut-off.

The lone throat shows the same contrast. Re-run at level \clmGwKickedLevel{} with the scalar stream, the $\varepsilon=\clmGwKickedEps$ throat collapses on schedule---a permanent MOTS from $t=\clmGwKickedMotsTime$, $R$ settling $\clmGwKickedRadiusStart\to\clmGwKickedRadiusEnd$---and its scalar envelope, after the burst, falls by $\clmGwKickedDropLo$--$\clmGwKickedDropHi\times$ across the spheres by $t=\clmGwKickedDropTime$, with late e-fold times of $\clmGwKickedEfoldLo$--$\clmGwKickedEfoldHi$. The unkicked level-\clmGwSingleLevel{} throat, which inflates with no trapped surface by scan or flow finder, instead grows quasi-exponentially in the $\ell=0$ monopole (e-fold ${\approx}\clmGwSingleEfold$, shortening across $t=\clmGwSingleWindowLo$--$\clmGwSingleWindowHi$), identically in a box-doubled ($L=\clmGwSingleBox$) copy through $t\approx\clmGwSingleAgreeTime$, so the growth is the throat's and not the boundary's or the sponge's; beyond $t\approx\clmGwSingleConstraintTime$ the constraint norms grow, and only $t\lesssim\clmGwSingleCountTime$ is counted. The contrast is confounded with the fate: the horizonless arms are also those whose mouths inflate toward the spheres. What is measured is that the scalar channel shuts off behind a horizon and grows while a throat inflates.

\section{Pattern matching against detector data}
\label{sec:ligo}

In $\clmDetLivetime$ h of coincident Advanced LIGO O3b data~\cite{gwosc}, a matched-filter search~\cite{allen2012,pycbc} with $\clmDetBankSize$ templates---the waveforms of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw} rescaled as $h\propto M/D$, $f\propto1/M$---finds \emph{no candidate at zero lag}, and none was expected. No static throat of astrophysical mass survives (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime}), so LIGO could catch only the burst of an encounter or collapse at formation, never a wormhole in residence; and a population formed at $z_e\gtrsim\clmDetZConv$ would convert there, far beyond the search's reach (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds}). At single-detector SNR $\clmDetHorizonSnr$ and optimal orientation the median horizon over each mass ladder is $\clmDetHorizonSpiral$ Gpc (spiral), $\clmDetHorizonFlyby$ (fly-by), $\clmDetHorizonHeadon$ (head-on), $\clmDetHorizonTwin$ (vacuum twin) and $\clmDetHorizonThroat$ (collapsing throat); the first three are upper bounds, since their strain, from fixed-frequency integration~\cite{reisswig2011}, ends at $\clmDetPedestalLo$--$\clmDetPedestalHi$ of peak. This short search demonstrates the pipeline; much stronger limits on short bursts follow from the LVK O3 all-sky burst search~\cite{lvk2021burst}, whose rate-density limits against characteristic frequency apply directly to the energies of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst}.

The null result is not a blind spot. Cut to the signal's duration, binary-black-hole templates (\texttt{IMRPhenomD}) match the bursts with fitting factors of $\clmDetFFWindowLo$--$\clmDetFFWindowHi$, as they match the vacuum control ($\clmDetFFTwinWindowLo$--$\clmDetFFTwinWindowHi$), so burst morphology alone cannot tell the two apart. All $\clmDetInjCount$ injections into O3b strain at optimal SNR $\clmDetInjSnr$ are recovered at $\clmDetInjEffLo$--$\clmDetInjEffHi\%$ of optimal within $\clmDetInjDt$ ms; only the lightest fly-by is demoted, by the $\chi^2$ veto.

\subsection{The frequency track}
\label{sec:ligo:ringdown}

The frequency track separates a drainhole burst from a binary black hole (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(c)). The vacuum twin peaks at $fM=\clmGwTwinPeakFM$ and climbs the point-mass track before turning toward the equal-mass Kerr mode at $fM=\clmDetKerrFM$~\cite{berti2006,berti2009}: a monotone rise, then a fixed ringdown frequency. No drainhole channel does this. The head-on's frequency \emph{falls}, and mass loss cannot explain it: a black hole shrinking as its remnant does rings faster, by $\times\clmDetKerrRise$ over the same window (with the area-based mass $R/2$), while the measurement is $\times\clmDetHeadonFreqRatio$. The fly-by's frequency falls as the pair separates; the spiral's late record is carried by a frozen core and climbs without settling; the lone collapse rings at $\clmDetThroatOverKerr$ of the equal-mass Kerr mode. A catalogue event~\cite{gwtc3} whose post-merger frequency holds within a few percent of a Kerr value is therefore inconsistent with the head-on channel at that mass.

\subsection{Energy per event}
\label{sec:ligo:burst}

The detector-independent deliverable is the energy per event in throat-mass units, in the dominant multipole (sphere spread in parentheses): fly-by $\clmGwEnergyFlybyOuter\,M$ at the outermost sphere ($\clmGwEnergyFlyby$ at the innermost), spiral $\clmGwEnergySpiral\,M$ ($\clmDetESpiralSpread\%$), head-on $\clmDetEHeadon\,M$ ($\clmDetEHeadonSpread\%$), collapsing throat $\clmGwEnergyThroat\,M$ ($\clmDetEThroatSpread\%$), vacuum control $\clmGwEnergyTwin\,M$ ($\clmDetETwinSpread\%$). Since $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ scales as event density times energy per event, these convert any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$~\cite{lvk2021,pagano2016} into a bound on the abundance of primordial drainhole encounters, provided the ghost scalar exists at that epoch. The scalar channel adds comparable energy of opposite sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

All four fates of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} occur, each with a measured selection rule: the relative scalar sign separates scattering from merger (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}), the seed's sign separates collapse from inflation (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}), and the orbital momentum separates plunge from fly-by at $\clmSpiralBoundaryLow$--$\clmSpiralBoundaryHigh\%$ of circular (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}). For detection, three results combine: no inspiral exists (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), the wormhole phase is a burst whose frequency falls or drifts where a Kerr remnant's would hold (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}), and the scalar counterpart decays once a horizon forms (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}). A surviving wormhole signature is a drifting-frequency burst with a scalar counterpart; a settled ringdown without one is already the descendant.

\subsection{No wormhole inspiral}
\label{sec:discussion:noinspiral}

A drainhole binary cannot radiate an inspiral. Each throat's unstable mode gives it a lifetime $t\approx\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, with $\tau=\clmTauLevelFourRounded$--$\clmTauRounded$ in isolation. An inspiral needs many orbits. Under the combined pull of Eq.~\eqref{eq:force}, six times gravity at large separation and softened as $(d+\delta)^{-2}$ at the separations evolved (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:force}), a circular orbit at $d=12$ has period $P\approx\clmDetOrbitPeriodLo$--$\clmDetOrbitPeriodHi$. No pair starts quiet enough to wait that long: the companion itself plants the seed---its solve-time compression of each mouth, $\clmRaceKickTwelve\%$ at $d=12$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral})---an $\varepsilon$ worth $t\approx\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)\approx\clmDetCompanionLifeLo$--$\clmDetCompanionLifeHi$ units---under half a period. Even $\varepsilon=\clmDetSeedExampleC$ buys only $\clmDetLifePeriodsLo$--$\clmDetLifePeriodsHi$ periods, and each further factor of ten adds only $\tau\ln10\approx\clmDetDecadeUnitsLo$--$\clmDetDecadeUnitsHi$ units. The orbit's demand grows with separation as $P\propto d^{3/2}$, while the throat's endurance grows only as $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$---and the companion's compression is physics, not data error: no solve can remove it.

Radiation reaction cannot close the gap either. The leading radiative moment is a scalar dipole carrying energy comparable to the gravitational channel's, with negative sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}). If the ADM balance holds, it pumps the orbit rather than draining it, so there is no quasi-circular decay to compute, and the vacuum inspiral time does not apply.

The wormhole-specific signal is therefore a short transient: within a few tens of $M$ each mouth collapses or inflates, and the plunge, burst and conversion of Secs.~\ref{sec:headon}--\ref{sec:gw} are all that this source class emits \emph{as wormholes}. Three qualifications apply. The argument covers a minimally coupled ghost scalar without spin, the matter model of the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach analysis, and holds only while the throat's unstable mode exists. Rotation can remove that mode: Ellis wormholes set spinning exist up to an extremal-Kerr limit~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014}; in five dimensions, with equal angular momenta, the static throat's unstable mode disappears above a critical angular momentum~\cite{dzhunushaliev2013}; in four, radial perturbations to second order in the rotation indicate the same~\cite{azad2024plb,azad2024prd}, and the non-axisymmetric modes computed at rapid rotation show no instability~\cite{khoo2024}. Other supports are untouched by the argument and include radially stable throats---thin shells for suitable equations of state~\cite{poisson_visser1995} and dilatonic Einstein--Gauss--Bonnet wormholes, which need no exotic matter~\cite{kanti2011}---so a spinning or differently supported pair that outlives an inspiral is not excluded here. The same lifetimes rule out a static ghost-supported throat as one of the long-lived black-hole mimickers that ringdown and echo tests target, since those tests presuppose a wormhole that persists~\cite{cardoso2016,bueno2018}; only the rotating or differently supported throats above remain candidates. ``No inspiral'' means no \emph{wormhole} inspiral: any long chirp belongs to the objects the throats become. And the seeds here are physical, not numerical: the companion's compression of each mouth is measured directly on solved data (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}), just below the declared-kick decade over which the throat's response is measured linear (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}).

\subsection{Heavy seeds and LISA bursts}
\label{sec:discussion:seeds}

The lifetimes of Table~\ref{tab:scales} forbid any throat from surviving from the early universe, so everything below depends on the epoch $z_e$ at which throats of mass $M$ would form, which our evolutions do not fix. The mechanisms known to make macroscopic wormholes in the early universe---bubbles and domain walls~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017,deng_vilenkin2017,gouttenoire2024}---do so at horizon re-entry, $t\sim\clmDetReentryTime$~s for $\clmDetSeedMassMid\,M_\odot$ ($z\sim\clmDetReentryZLo$--$\clmDetReentryZHi$), where these bursts would be redshifted out of every band. We know of no mechanism forming $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$ throats as late as $z_e\approx\clmDetZConv$, so we present the consequences of such a population conditionally.

If such throats existed at $z_e\approx\clmDetZConv$, collapse---alone or in a merger---would be a seeding mechanism. Black holes descended from wormholes have been proposed before. The wormholes that join the inflating interiors of supercritical vacuum bubbles or domain walls to the exterior pinch off and leave black holes at their mouths~\cite{deng2017,deng_vilenkin2017,gouttenoire2024}, which for some parameter values can seed supermassive black holes~\cite{garriga2016,deng_vilenkin2017}; and some galactic nuclei have been proposed as former wormhole entrances~\cite{kardashev2007}. Here the wormhole is traversable and ghost-supported, and its conversion is driven by its own measured instability or by a merger, at a remnant mass comparable to its own. A throat converts to a black hole in seconds at $10^{4}$--$10^{5}\,M_\odot$ and in minutes at $10^{6}\,M_\odot$ (Table~\ref{tab:scales}), and the remnant keeps a mass of order its progenitors': the head-on and spiral remnants both end at $R\simeq\clmRemnantRadiusMean$ (irreducible mass $\clmSpiralRemnantMass$) from pairs of ADM mass $\clmHeadonADMMass$, and the lone collapse at $R\approx\clmLoneRemnantLo$ from a throat of mass $\clmThroatM$ (Secs.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}, \ref{sec:headon:wall}, \ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}). The scalar hair leaves on a ${\sim}\clmGwShedClock\,M$ clock after horizon formation (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), so the remnants then accrete as ordinary black holes. From $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassMid\,M_\odot$ at $z\simeq\clmDetZConv$, Eddington-limited growth (Salpeter e-fold ${\simeq}\clmDetSalpeter$ Myr) reaches $\clmDetGrowthTarget\,M_\odot$ in $\clmDetGrowthEfoldsLo$--$\clmDetGrowthEfoldsHi$ e-folds, $\clmDetGrowthTimeLo$--$\clmDetGrowthTimeHi$ Myr, inside the ${\sim}\clmDetGrowthWindow$ Myr available by $z\simeq\clmDetGrowthZ$: no super-Eddington phase is needed, which is what defines a heavy seed~\cite{inayoshi2020} (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(a)). The channel would then seed JWST's overmassive black holes and little red dots~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024} without an atomic-cooling halo, a critical Lyman--Werner flux, or any baryonic environment.

Halo independence would itself be a signature: the seeds would predate baryonic structure, so $M_{\rm BH}/M_*$ would start effectively infinite and relax downward, which would make UHZ1's overmassive morphology the generic expectation rather than a tuning. The abundance required is small: one seed per present-day massive galaxy is $n\simeq\clmDetNMin$--$\clmDetNMax\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ comoving, a dark-matter fraction $f=nM/\rho_{\rm DM}\simeq\clmDetDMFracLo$--$\clmDetDMFracHi$ at $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$. The FIRAS $\mu$-distortion bound~\cite{nakama2018}, which excludes primordial black holes of $\clmDetFirasLo$--$\clmDetFirasHi\,M_\odot$ at any cosmologically relevant abundance, applies to black holes formed from Gaussian curvature perturbations; a wormhole-collapse channel is not subject to it, but only because its formation mechanism is left unspecified, as for the bubble and domain-wall channels~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017}.

For $z_e\approx\clmDetZConv$ the encounters would radiate in LISA's band (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(b)). Each burst carries the energies of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst} at $f_{\rm obs}=(fM)_{\rm peak}/[M(1+z_e)]$; with the measured $(fM)_{\rm peak}=\clmDetFMPeakLo$--$\clmDetFMPeakHi$ the band is $\clmDetBandLo$--$\clmDetBandHi$ mHz across $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$ at $z_e\simeq\clmDetZConv$: LISA's band, not LIGO's. One at a time the bursts would be loud. Rescaled with the strain of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} to $z_e=\clmDetZConv$ in a Planck cosmology~\cite{planck2018} (detector-frame mass $(1+z_e)M$, luminosity distance ${\approx}\clmDetLumDist$~Gpc) and filtered against the analytic LISA noise~\cite{robson2019}, the fly-by and the spiral merger, the two loudest channels of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw}, reach optimal signal-to-noise ratios of $\clmDetLoudSnrLo$--$\clmDetBurstSnrHi$ at $\clmDetSeedMassMid$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$, and the head-on $\clmDetBurstSnrLo$--$\clmDetHeadonSnrHi$. All three stay above $8$ from $\clmDetDetectMassLo$ to $\clmDetDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$ with sky position and inclination averaged, the galactic confusion noise added and only each record's resolved band counted, and carry the near-zone uncertainty of the energies (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw}); the strain pedestal of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} matters only at the light end. Heavier events are louder but redder, and LISA's low-frequency noise rises faster than their strain (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(b)): at $\clmDetConvMassLo$--$\clmDetConvMassHi\,M_\odot$, where the bursts peak at $\clmDetLowBandLo$--$\clmDetLowBandHi\,\mu$Hz, only the fly-by, whose spectrum extends furthest above its peak, still reaches $8$, up to $\clmDetFlybyDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$. The spiral and head-on bursts each announce a seed, since each ends in a black hole; the fly-by, the loudest, leaves two inflating mouths, announces none, and occurs at a rate not tied to the seeds'. A lone collapse, radiating only $\clmGwEnergyThroat\,M$ (a background of $\clmDetCollapseOmegaLo$--$\clmDetCollapseOmegaHi$), stays below detection on the same count, at a signal-to-noise ratio of at most ${\approx}\clmDetCollapseSnr$ (${\approx}\clmDetCollapseSnrOpt$ only at optimal orientation). What limits detection is occurrence: every seed converts once, so LISA records $4\pi D_c^2(z_e)\,c\,T\,n\simeq\clmDetBurstsPerN\,(n/\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3})$ bursts in $T=4$ yr, ${\sim}\clmDetRateLo$--$\clmDetRateHi$ per year for $n=\clmDetNMin$--$\clmDetNMax\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, each ${\sim}\clmDetBurstDuration\,(M/10^{5}M_\odot)$ s long. Averaged over time the conversions give $\Omega_{\rm GW}\simeq n\,E_{\rm rad}/[\rho_c(1+z_e)]$: with $E_{\rm rad}/M=\clmDetEHeadon$ (head-on) to $\clmGwEnergySpiral$ (spiral) at $M=\clmDetSeedMassMid\,M_\odot$, $n=\clmDetNMin$--$\clmDetNMax\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ gives $\Omega_{\rm GW}\sim\clmDetOmegaLo$--$\clmDetOmegaHi$ at ${\sim}\clmDetOmegaFreq$ mHz, above LISA's four-year power-law-integrated sensitivity~\cite{thrane2013} ($\Omega\sim\clmDetLisaLo$--$\clmDetLisaHi$ near millihertz~\cite{lisa2017,caprini2018}) for $n\gtrsim(\clmDetPlsNLo$--$\clmDetPlsNHi)\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ at $\clmDetSeedMassHi$--$\clmDetSeedMassMid\,M_\odot$ if every conversion radiates like the spiral (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(c)); fly-bys, which convert nothing, add up to $\clmGwEnergyFlyby\,M$ each at a rate the evolutions do not fix. At these rates, though, the signal is resolved bursts, never a stochastic background. The channel would therefore be detectable by LISA for merger conversions of $\clmDetDetectMassLo$--$\clmDetDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$ with $n\gtrsim\clmDetOneBurstN\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, where at least one burst falls in four years, and not for lone collapses, for $\clmDetSeedMassLo\,M_\odot$ seeds or for $n\lesssim\clmDetNMin\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$. The level and band scale as $1/(1+z_e)$, and $z_e\simeq\clmDetZConv$ is about the latest conversion epoch the seed arithmetic allows; a population converting at horizon re-entry would lie far below any band. Its spectrum is a pure burst population with no $f^{2/3}$ inspiral ramp (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), unlike any direct-collapse scenario, whose seeds form hydrodynamically.

The negative-energy scalar radiation dilutes like radiation ($w=1/3$) and has the wrong sign to act as dark energy; within the dark-matter budget its present density stays below $\clmDetDepositCeiling$ of critical.

Nothing in the astrophysical mass range survives (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime}), so every observed supermassive black hole is at most a descendant of this channel, not a surviving wormhole mouth~\cite{kardashev2007,bambi2013}, and fits of a static Ellis--Bronnikov metric to the Sgr~A$^*$ or M87$^*$ shadows are excluded within this model. The claim is conditional on $z_e$: collapse, alone or in a merger, would be a heavy-seed channel with one measured property---prompt conversion at comparable mass---two that would follow from an early birth---halo independence and freedom from the $\mu$-distortion bound on curvature-perturbation black holes---and one testable signature: the encounter bursts, which LISA would resolve one by one. Whether it is \emph{the} channel behind the JWST population is a question of abundance, and the abundance is cheap: one conversion per little red dot, $n\simeq(\clmDetLrdNLo$--$\clmDetLrdNHi)\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ (the number densities of Refs.~\cite{greene2024,matthee2024}, summed over their bins), is a mass density $nM\simeq\clmDetLrdRhoLo$--$\clmDetLrdRhoHi\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ at $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$, a fraction $\clmDetLrdFracLo$--$\clmDetLrdFracHi$ of the dark matter (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(c)). LISA would then record $\clmDetLrdBurstsLo$--$\clmDetLrdBurstsHi$ bursts in four years, so it tests the channel only if conversions outnumber the little red dots at least tenfold. The evolutions map a birth abundance $n(M)$ onto these observables; they do not fix it (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}).

\subsection{The inflating half of the population}
\label{sec:discussion:inflating}

Seeding is half the census. The branch is chosen by the perturbation's sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}), so a sign-symmetric primordial seed distribution converts about half of a population of lone throats into black holes and sets the other half inflating; only large seeds skew the count, and toward collapse---at $\varepsilon=\pm0.1$ both signs collapse (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}). Pairs born close enough to collide (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime}) shift the count through the selection rules. Let a fraction $f_<$ of them be born below the capture threshold, at $\clmSpiralBoundaryLow$--$\clmSpiralBoundaryHigh\%$ of the momentum of a circular orbit (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:capture}). With the relative sign random, the like-signed half scatter, and each of their throats then meets a lone throat's fate if its own seed decides it; the opposite-signed half merge below the threshold and fly by above it, both mouths inflating. Per throat, a fraction $f_</2$ then converts by merger, a quarter by lone collapse, and $3/4-f_</2$ inflates, so between a quarter and three quarters of a paired population ends in black holes, set by where its birth momenta fall about the threshold. An inflating throat creates, from regular data, an expanding anti-trapped region inside an asymptotically flat spacetime (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:fate}). Farhi and Guth showed that under the null energy condition such a region cannot be created classically without an initial singularity~\cite{farhi_guth1987,blau1987}; the phantom violates that condition. Unlike the false-vacuum bubbles of Refs.~\cite{farhi_guth1987,blau1987} and the domain-wall wormholes of Ref.~\cite{deng2017}, whose inflating interiors lie behind a black-hole horizon and pinch off, this region lies on our side of the throat and grows into the surrounding space, as the massless throat's explosion does~\cite{shinkai2002}. We therefore do not call it a baby universe. The record demonstrates the mechanism, not the magnitude: by $t=\clmFourWall$ the neck accumulates $\clmInfEfoldNeck$ areal e-folds and the boundary $\clmInfEfoldBoundary$, against the ${\sim}\clmInfEfoldsNeeded$ of cosmic inflation~\cite{liddle2003}.

Whether such regions keep growing matters for any population: if half the throats inflate (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}) and their anti-trapped regions grew without bound, they would eventually overlap. Our record cannot decide this. The outward motion of the $\theta_k=0$ surface is measured in a slicing that freezes the neck, spans $\clmFourWall\,M$, and is evolved in an asymptotically flat rather than expanding background. The seed channel of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} therefore requires either that the growth stalls or that the inflating half is rare; an evolution in a slicing that does not freeze the neck is needed to decide. In the foam limit the mouths overlap by construction, and a predominantly inflating foam would be a candidate pre-inflationary expansion phase with a built-in exit, since growing perturbations widen the collapse basin; the equal-mass, two-throat evolutions here neither model such a foam nor fix its abundance.

\subsection{Topological censorship}
\label{sec:discussion:topology}

Topological censorship forbids an observer at infinity from probing the topology of a globally hyperbolic, asymptotically flat spacetime that satisfies the null energy condition: any topological structure collapses too quickly for light to traverse it~\cite{friedman1993}. A traversable wormhole evades the theorem only by violating that condition, and the two branches end the evasion in opposite ways. On the collapse branch, alone or in a merger, the throats end inside a MOTS while the phantom outside is swallowed or radiated away (Secs.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}, \ref{sec:headon:wall} and~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}). The wormholes are not removed---no classical evolution changes the number of asymptotic ends~\cite{geroch1967}, and both pits stay distinct inside the spiral's remnant (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral})---but they end behind a horizon, hidden from outside as the theorem would have them. On the inflation branch no trapped surface forms and the phantom is not swallowed: the throat stays open while its anti-trapped region grows (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:fate}), and nothing restores the condition. The sign of the seed, or the encounter, decides which way a throat goes.

\subsection{Scope}
\label{sec:discussion:open}

The results hold for one matter model and one family of data: a single minimally coupled ghost scalar, equal masses, one throat compactness ($m/a=1/2$) and no spin, on constraint-solved, matched initial data whose momentum is the exact boost (Secs.~\ref{sec:model:solve} and~\ref{sec:model:boost}). Horizons here are MOTSs on the evolved slices, which for matter violating the null energy condition need not lie inside event horizons (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:mots}). The ghost field is treated classically~\cite{cline2004} (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}). The evolved binary has three asymptotic ends, not a handle's two mouths. Time-step convergence is not tested, the growth rates rest on two resolutions, waveforms are extracted in the near zone without extrapolation, and the ADM energy balance including the scalar channel is not closed. The cosmology of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} takes the birth abundance $n(M)$ and the conversion epoch $z_e$ as inputs---Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(c) maps any choice onto its signals---and requires the ghost scalar to exist at that epoch.

\section{Reproducibility and cost}
\label{sec:repro}
\label{sec:cost}

Every evolution in Table~\ref{tab:matrix} is one line in a registry shipped with the results pack---its single knob against a named reference, its outcome, its caveats---and every figure is regenerated by a script in the pack from the archived diagnostic streams. The code, the analysis and the results pack are in the \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn} fork at \url{https://github.com/Nikchik-coder/GRTeclyn} (branch \texttt{feature/merger}): the drainhole initial data, the seeds of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}, the interior freeze and the in-code diagnostics in the evolution code; the flow finder, the orientation-corrected surface scan, the figure scripts and the claims ledger that recomputes every number quoted here in the analysis layer; and the parameter files and thinned diagnostic streams of every run in the pack. Each run's manifest names the binary it ran, and a table in the pack maps every binary to its source commit; the campaign binary from 24 September 2026 on is commit \texttt{7166787a}, which it carries stamped. The evolutions are bit-reproducible on the production hardware: two builds differing only in a passive diagnostic give byte-identical physics streams over $\clmControlByteRows$ output rows.

Every evolution ran on one NVIDIA H100 \clmDetMemDevice\,GB device, with \texttt{GRTeclyn} built CUDA-native against AMReX~\clmDetAmrexVersion. At $\Delta t=\clmTimeStepFactor\,\Delta x$ a production arm ($L=\clmBoxL$, $\clmBaseN^3$ base, \clmProdLevels{} refinement levels) advances $\clmDetSpeedProdLo$--$\clmDetSpeedProdHi$ code units per hour, and a $t=100$ single-throat or head-on evolution takes $\clmDetCostTHundredLo$--$\clmDetCostTHundredHi$ h. Depth is the expense: level-5 arms run at $\clmDetSpeedLevelFiveLo$--$\clmDetSpeedLevelFiveHi$ units per hour on the full box (the octant $L=\clmFourBoxL$ arm, an eighth of the cells, at $\clmDetSpeedFourLo$--$\clmDetSpeedFourHi$, $\clmDetCostFour$ h in all)---$\clmDetCostHeadonChain$ h for the head-on's three-leg $t=0\to100$ chain, $\clmDetCostFlybyLevelFive$ h for the $p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ fly-by at level 5, which peaks at $\clmDetMemFlybyPeak$ of the $\clmDetMemDevice$ GB, and $\clmDetCostMergerChain$ h for the $d=6$ merger chain. The $\clmDetRunsTotal$ evolutions total $\clmDetGpuHours$ GPU-hours, each run's cost recomputed from its packed logs: the logged speed and time span, a restarted leg dated from its checkpoint.
