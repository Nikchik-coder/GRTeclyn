\begin{abstract}
% Headline results only; every number is frozen in results/merger (2026-09-18).
We evolve pairs of massive drainhole wormholes---phantom-scalar throats---in full numerical relativity: one throat compactness, equal masses, no spin. A lone throat is an unstable fixed point (e-fold time $\tau=5.9$, within $15\%$ of the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach rate); a declared kick deterministically selects collapse or inflation. Like signs repel, so a merger requires opposite scalar signs. The head-on pair collides with both mouths open and forms a black hole---the trapped surface born enclosing both mouths, never either alone---which loses mass to the phantom it swallows ($M_{\rm MS}$ $3.0\to2.2$) and rings without a chirp. The spiral merger stalls: no horizon anywhere on its measured record, the curvature wall rides the still-open throat, and a $16\times$ refinement ladder saturates short of the merger's end. Three agreeing measurements of the unstable mode forbid a wormhole inspiral outright: an orbit costs $(d/M)^4$ e-folds while a throat endures $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, so one orbit from the innermost stable circular orbit needs a seed below $10^{-20}$---every encounter is a plunge. The loudest gravitational channel is the horizonless fly-by, $7.4\times10^{-2}\,M$ in the dominant multipole ($43\%$ sphere spread): $29\times$ this campaign's vacuum merger control and $70\times$ a vacuum pass matched to the fly-by's own momentum. The ghost scalar radiates $2.3$ times more again, in the $\ell=1$ dipole a vacuum binary cannot have, with negative energy: the system is pumped by its own dominant emission---and the channel is switched off by horizon formation, the hair shed in one dipole pulse on the remnant's own mass-settling clock. At detector scale the bursts match binary black holes at fixed duration (fitting factors $0.82$--$0.98$ against a vacuum control's $0.94$--$0.95$); the discriminator is the frequency track, which falls or drifts where a remnant's would hold at the Kerr value. A $127$-template matched-filter search of Advanced LIGO O3b strain returns no candidate at zero lag, at a measured single-detector reach of $0.15$--$4.8$ Gpc. Finally, since collapse converts a throat of any mass into a black hole of comparable mass within days at the outside, a primordial drainhole population is a heavy-seed channel for the earliest supermassive black holes---halo-free, evading the $\mu$-distortion bound that excludes supermassive primordial black holes from curvature perturbations---testable as a burst-shaped millihertz background, $\Omega_{\rm GW}\sim10^{-13}$--$10^{-11}$ at one seed per massive galaxy, straddling LISA's stochastic reach.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Wheeler's spacetime foam is a sea of handles---wormholes that open and close on the Planck scale, both mouths in the same space~\cite{wheeler1955,wheeler1957,misner_wheeler1957}. More than one mechanism promotes them to macroscopic size: a Planck-scale Lorentzian wormhole caught in an inflating background is stretched with it and emerges macroscopic~\cite{roman1993}; bubble and domain-wall cosmologies produce, without tuning, post-inflationary regions joined through wormholes~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017}; and a gas of relic wormholes has been run as a cosmology in its own right~\cite{kirillov2008,kirillov2011}. What none of this work evolves is the aftermath: what a relic wormhole does once it is macroscopic and classical, and what two of them do to each other. That aftermath is this paper's subject.

The classical theory fixes its two clocks. First, the simplest evolvable traversable wormhole---the Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole held open by a ghost scalar~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973,morris_thorne1988,visser1995}---is unstable: perturbed one way the throat collapses to a black hole, the other way it inflates, on a timescale set by the throat radius~\cite{shinkai2002,gonzalez2009}, as a 3+1 evolution of the massless throat confirms nonlinearly~\cite{shirokov2026}. Every constituent destroys itself on its own schedule. Second, Geroch's theorem forbids classical topology change~\cite{geroch1967,tipler1977}: two wormholes cannot fuse into one, and none can be born. Birth is quantum~\cite{hawking1988}, so a simulation takes over at $t=0$ with the newborn state as its initial data---the stipulation every numerical-relativity binary makes, here with a physical story attached.

Together the two facts turn the standing objection to wormhole binaries into a selection rule. A binary has no astrophysical assembly channel---each throat dies faster than capture could pair two---but a foam-born pair needs none: creation and collision are the same event, at a birth separation of order the objects themselves. The race is collision against decay, and both rates belong to the classical solution. The scalar's sign adds a second rule at no cost: a phantom field repels like charges and attracts opposite ones, so like-signed pairs scatter while opposite-signed pairs fall together; and the sign of a lone throat's perturbation selects collapse---a wormhole-to-primordial-black-hole channel beside the standard routes~\cite{zeldovich1967,carr_hawking1974,carr2021}---or inflation. A foam population thus fans out into four fates---merge, scatter, collapse, inflate---across the three-parameter space of birth separation, perturbation and relative sign, and that space is what a family of evolutions maps. The binary evolved here is the exterior approximation of a pair of handles (three asymptotic ends, the far sides passive); what merges is not two wormholes into one, which Geroch forbids, but two wormholes into one black hole with both throats inside it---the horizon does the work that topology change cannot.

The observable is not a chirp but a stochastic background from the merger epoch, redshifted to whichever band the mass scale puts it in~\cite{caprini2018,nanograv2023,lisa2017,lvk2021}. The clean deliverable is therefore dimensionless and epoch-independent: the energy radiated per merger and its spectral shape in throat-mass units, which converts any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$---including the nucleosynthesis cap that holds outside every detector band~\cite{pagano2016}---into a bound on the comoving abundance of primordial wormhole mergers. The same conversion runs forward: JWST has uncovered an unexpectedly abundant population of overmassive black holes at $z\gtrsim4$--$6$---compact ``little red dots'' and hosts with $M_{\rm BH}/M_*\gtrsim10^{-2}$, UHZ1 the flagship---whose demographics favour heavy seeds of $10^4$--$10^6\,M_\odot$ over Eddington-grown light ones, while every proposed heavy-seed formation channel remains contested~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024,inayoshi2020}. The collapse branch measured here is such a channel---prompt, full-mass, independent of any baryonic environment---and Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} develops it into a falsifiable millihertz prediction. Two caveats travel with the statement: the ghost scalar must exist at that epoch to hold the newborn throats open, and the merger's scalar channel---measured here at $2.3$ times the gravitational energy and negative (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar})---has a cosmological consequence that is genuinely open.

The paper is organised as follows. Sec.~\ref{sec:model} sets out the model: the Einstein--phantom-scalar system, the drainhole, the binary superposition with its sign rule, and the declared seeds. Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} gives the numerical setup and the run matrix. The results then follow the campaign from one throat to two: the lone wormhole---fixed point, unstable mode, branching and collapse (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}); two throats at rest and the force between them (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}); the head-on merger to a black hole (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon}); the spiral merger and its capture boundary (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}); the gravitational waves of every channel---collapse, head-on, spiral, fly-by---gathered in one chapter with the vacuum twin (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw}); the numerical controls and systematics (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}). Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} confronts the rescaled waveforms with the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data products---template banks, matched-filter search, ringdown, burst searches---and Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} collects the findings, including the e-fold budget that forbids a wormhole inspiral (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), the heavy-seed channel it leaves open (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds}) and the list of what is not established (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}). Secs.~\ref{sec:repro} and~\ref{sec:cost} give the reproducibility pack and the computational cost. Every evolution runs on GPUs with \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}, the AMReX-based~\cite{amrex} port of \texttt{GRChombo}~\cite{grchombo}, continuing the campaign of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026b}. Throughout, $G=c=1$.

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Field equations}
\label{sec:model:field}

The matter is a single real, massless phantom scalar: gravity couples to \emph{minus} the canonical stress tensor,
\begin{equation}
T_{ab} = -\Bigl(\nabla_a\phi\,\nabla_b\phi - \tfrac12 g_{ab}\,\nabla_c\phi\nabla^c\phi\Bigr),
\qquad V(\phi)\equiv 0,
\label{eq:phantom}
\end{equation}
so $\rho<0$ where the field's gradient dominates---the exotic stress that holds a throat open. With $V=0$ this is exactly a ghost field. The sign flip lives entirely in the coupling to geometry: the scalar itself obeys the canonical wave equation $\Box\phi=0$, evolved first order in $\phi$ and $\Pi=(\partial_t\phi-\beta^i\partial_i\phi)/\alpha$.

\subsection{Drainhole initial data}
\label{sec:model:drainhole}

Each body is a massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973}. In isotropic radius $r$ about the throat centre, with
\begin{equation}
X = \frac{r - a^2/4r}{a},\quad
\Omega = 1 + \frac{a^2}{4r^2},\quad
u = \frac{m}{a}\Bigl(\arctan X - \frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the exact static solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom} is
\begin{equation}
\alpha = e^{u},\quad
\gamma_{ij} = e^{-2u}\,\Omega^2\,\delta_{ij},\quad
\phi = C\arctan X,
\label{eq:drainhole}
\end{equation}
with $4\pi a^2C^2 = a^2+m^2$ (so $C\to1/\sqrt{4\pi}$ as $m\to0$), $K_{ij}=0$, $\Pi=0$ and zero shift; in the evolved conformal variables $\chi = e^{2u}\Omega^{-2}$ and $h_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$. Both constraints hold identically at $t=0$. Here $a$ is the throat scale and $m$ the ADM mass, carried by the \emph{lapse} rather than the conformal factor, so $\chi$ at the throat stays in $[0.15,0.25]$ for $m/a\le1$ and mass costs no grid resolution. The minimal surface sits at $r_t=\tfrac12\bigl(m+\sqrt{m^2+a^2}\bigr)$ with areal radius $R_{\min}=e^{-u(r_t)}\sqrt{a^2+m^2}$; the production throat $a=2$, $m=1$ has $R_{\min}=3.8895$ at $r_t=1.618$. The point $r\to0$ is the far universe's spatial infinity compactified ($\chi\sim r^4$). The mass matters: at $m=0$ the throat is ultrastatic ($\alpha\equiv1$, $M_{\rm ADM}=0$) and two of them do not attract at all.

\subsection{The binary superposition}
\label{sec:model:binary}

Two throats at separation $d$ (one-body radii $r_A$, $r_B$) are superposed in excess form,
\begin{equation}
u = u_A + u_B,\qquad
\psi = 1 + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_A}-1\bigr) + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_B}-1\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\chi = e^{2u}\psi^{-4}$, $\alpha = e^{u}$ and $\phi = \phi_A + \sigma\,\phi_B$, $\sigma=\pm1$; the constant tail of the $\arctan$ profiles is subtracted so $\phi\to0$ at the boundary, which is free for a massless field. Momentum enters through one Bowen--York term~\cite{bowen_york1980} per throat,
\begin{equation}
\hat A_{ij} = \frac{3}{2r^2}\bigl[P_i n_j + P_j n_i - (\delta_{ij}-n_i n_j)\,P{\cdot}n\bigr],
\end{equation}
converted by $\tilde A_{ij} = \chi^{3/2}\hat A_{ij}$, with $K=0$. Since $\Pi=0$ the matter momentum density vanishes and $\hat A_{ij}$ is flat-space divergence free, so the momentum constraint is satisfied \emph{exactly} at $t=0$; only the Hamiltonian constraint carries the superposition defect, $O(a^2/d^2)+O(m/d)$. The relative sign $\sigma$ is the pair's one discrete parameter: $\phi\to-\phi$ is exact for a single body, but a phantom mediator \emph{repels} like scalar charges and \emph{attracts} opposite ones, with $|F_\phi/F_{\rm grav}| = (a^2+m^2)/m^2>1$ always ($=5$ for the production throat)---so like-signed pairs can never merge under their own gravity, and $\sigma=-1$ is the merger channel. A windowed one-body correction of the Helfer type~\cite{helfer2022} is implemented but off in production (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Declared seeds}
\label{sec:model:seeds}

The instability is probed with declared perturbations of the conformal factor only: a Gaussian shell on the minimal surface,
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon\, g(r)\bigr],\qquad
g = e^{-(r-r_t)^2/w^2},\quad w = a/4,
\label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
which moves the throat's areal radius by ${\approx}2\varepsilon$ with the sign of $\varepsilon$, and a quadrupolar factor
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon_2\, g(r)\,P_2(\cos\theta)\bigr]
\end{equation}
about the $z$ axis through the throat. $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ are untouched, so the momentum constraint stays exact. The spherical seed selects the branch; the quadrupolar seed has no $\ell=0$ part---it gives a collapsing throat a quadrupole to shed---and with both on the cross term is $O(\varepsilon\varepsilon_2)$.

The campaign is one-knob: every run differs from a named reference in a single setting, and every run has one line---its difference, its outcome, its caveats---in a registry shipped with the results pack (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}). \subsection{Evolution scheme}
\label{sec:setup:scheme}

Evolutions use \texttt{GRTeclyn}: CCZ4~\cite{alic2012} with damping $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=(3,0,1)$, $1{+}\log$ slicing~\cite{bona1995} started from the exact static lapse $\alpha=e^{u}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:drainhole}, a Gamma-driver shift~\cite{campanelli2006,baker2006} with $\eta=1$ started from zero, fourth-order stencils, RK4, and Kreiss--Oliger dissipation $\sigma=0.1$. The time step is $\Delta t=0.02\,\Delta x$, an order of magnitude below the usual CCZ4 Courant factor; the bracket in $\Delta t$ that makes it necessary is a control (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Grid, boundaries and regularisation}
\label{sec:setup:grid}

The domain is a cube of side $L=64$ with the system at its centre; the base grid is $128^3$ ($\Delta x=0.5$). Refinement is by moving nested boxes centred on the tracked throats, shrinking by a factor of two per level (half-width $4$ per throat at the production finest level) and re-drawn every $16$ steps. Production runs use three refinement levels (finest $\Delta x=1/16$); resolution arms raise this to levels $4$--$7$ where stated, and the orbital production chain and the fly-by double the box to $L=128$, carrying the sponge and the extraction spheres outward with it. Outer boundaries are Sommerfeld, backed by an absorbing sponge over $r=24$--$32$ that ramps extra dissipation up to $\sigma_{\rm sp}=4$ as the fourth power of the depth into the layer; everything dynamical sits inside $r=24$.

Each throat carries a compactified second infinity at its own centre, where $\chi\sim r^4$, so the evolved variables are floored: $\chi\ge10^{-8}$ and $\alpha\ge10^{-10}$ (a variant keeps $\chi$ unfloored down to $10^{-20}$ and applies the $10^{-8}$ floor only where $1/\chi$ is evaluated). Two further devices act only inside regions that are already trapped: an exponential damping of the scalar where the lapse has collapsed, and a smooth interior freeze that multiplies every right-hand side by $1-W(r)$, with $W$ a $C^2$ quintic step between an inner radius $r_{\rm full}$ and an outer $r_{\rm start}$. All three are numerical devices, not physics; whether any of them is load-bearing is itself measured by twin runs (Secs.~\ref{sec:headon:wall} and~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:setup:diagnostics}

$L^2$ norms of the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints over the box are written continuously. Both throat centres are tracked, and about each centre the areal radius $R(r)$ is scanned on coordinate spheres; its minimum is the throat radius. On the same spheres the two null expansions $\theta_\pm$ are computed with the outward direction fixed by \emph{increasing areal radius}: a marginally outer trapped surface is an outermost zero of $\theta_+$ with $\theta_-<0$ (crossings at extrema of $R(r)$ are rejected), while a dynamic wormhole throat is marginally \emph{anti}-trapped~\cite{hochberg_visser1998}. That orientation is not a refinement but a correction: the in-code $\theta_\pm$ stream fixes the outward direction by increasing \emph{coordinate} radius, which on the far side of a throat points the wrong way and manufactures trapped verdicts wherever $R(r)$ is falling. Every horizon statement in this paper---formation, absence, radius, mass---is therefore read from an offline scan over the archived plotfiles with the areal orientation applied, and the in-code columns are used only as a live monitor and are never plotted. The correction is not cosmetic: on the naive orientation the spiral remnant reports a common horizon that the corrected scan does not find (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}). Masses of such surfaces are Misner--Sharp~\cite{misner_sharp1964},
\begin{equation}
\frac{2M_{\rm MS}}{R} \;=\; 1 + \frac{R^{2}\,\theta_+\theta_-}{4},
\label{eq:misner_sharp}
\end{equation}
with $\theta_\pm$ carrying dimensions of inverse length so that the second term is dimensionless; the scan uses coordinate spheres rather than a deformable finder, and quoted masses---and the presence or absence of a surface, not only its mass---carry that shape systematic. Radiation is read from $\Psi_4$ interpolated onto spheres at $R=10$, $14$, $18$ (and $22$ on the single-throat wave arms) on the $L=64$ box, and at $R=20$, $28$, $36$, $44$ on the doubled one, decomposed in spin-weight $-2$ harmonics. \emph{Every} amplitude in this paper, in text and in every figure, is $r\Psi_4$: both pipelines multiply by the sphere radius before the harmonic projection, so neither stream is a bare $\Psi_4$ that a reader---or a plotting script---should rescale. The two agree to $0.1\%$ where they overlap, which is what makes the convention checkable rather than declared. The same spheres also carry a scalar stream: $\phi$ and $\Pi$ projected on spin-weight-$0$ harmonics with the same radial scaling, $A_{\ell m}=R\,\phi_{\ell m}$, and the kinematic flux $F_{\rm kin}=-R^2\oint\Pi\,\partial_r\phi\,\mathrm{d}\Omega$ signed so that an outgoing \emph{canonical} wave gives a positive number (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}). A per-cell autopsy reports the location and one-step history of any floating-point failure.

\subsection{The static throat as a fixed point}
\label{sec:single:fixedpoint}
% Exact data holds the areal throat radius; departure time vs resolution;
% what the constraint norms do and do not certify.

Evolved with no declared perturbation, the exact drainhole holds its areal throat radius $R_\star=3.8895$ to better than $0.1\%$ for $35$ time units at level 3 and to $1\%$ until $t\simeq44$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a)); at level 4 the radius is still exact to four digits at $t=30$. The static throat is a fixed point of the discrete system as well as of the continuum one. What ends the quiet phase is not a failure of the data: the departure time moves \emph{later} by $5.6$--$8.7$ units per halving of the cell, the signature of a truncation-noise seed of fixed growth rate being handed a smaller initial amplitude. Level 2 never reaches the branch at all---it dies of an origin failure at $t=24.2$ with the throat still exact to $0.12\%$.

\subsection{The unstable mode}
\label{sec:single:instability}
% Growth rate / e-fold time at levels 3 and 4; comparison to the GGS band;
% the undeclared (truncation) seed and its level-dependent sign.
% Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b).

The departure is a single exponential mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b)): plateau fits give e-fold times $\tau=5.88$ (level 3) and $5.26$ (level 4)---the same mode to $11\%$---at $T=\tau\,\alpha_{\rm th}/R_\star=0.87$ and $0.78$. Gonz\'alez, Guzm\'an and Sarbach's perturbative growth times for the Ellis throat run from $T=0.846$ at their massless end to $0.590$ in the opposite limit of their one-parameter family~\cite{gonzalez2009}; the level-3 value sits $3\%$ above that massless endpoint and the level-4 value $8\%$ below it. We do not claim a mass-resolved comparison: the map from this paper's $(a,m)$ to their parameters has not been checked term by term, so what is established is agreement with the published rate to ${\sim}15\%$, not placement within a band (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}). The instability is the throat's; only its seed is the grid's, and the seed's \emph{sign} flips with resolution: level 3 collapses, level 4 inflates. This confirms, from exact massive-drainhole data, the unstable behaviour and the two-branch structure found for the massless Ellis--Bronnikov throat in Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026}, where ambient noise drove inflation and a compressive perturbation drove collapse: the branches are the wormhole's, not an artefact of either code.

\subsection{Branching: collapse versus inflation}
\label{sec:single:branching}
% Declared seed scan eps = +/-1e-2, +/-1e-3: fate opposite to the push,
% linear response, twin crossing time, tenfold-smaller kick = fixed delay;
% amplitude ceiling (eps = 0.1 is not a perturbation).  Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c).

A declared kick removes the grid's vote (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c)). At $\varepsilon=\pm10^{-2}$ and $\pm10^{-3}$ the fate is always opposite to the push, each $\pm$ pair crosses itself at the $\varepsilon$-independent $t_\times\simeq13.0$, and a tenfold smaller kick arrives later by a fixed delay of $11$--$12$ units, read at matched horizon radius; markers as far apart as the twins' separation and first detection bracket it at $8$--$14$, and the quoted figure is the matched-radius one throughout---linear response riding the same unstable mode. The branching survives refinement: at level 4, the grid whose own truncation seed \emph{inflates} the unkicked throat, $+10^{-2}$ still forms its horizon at $t=11$ (areal $3.883$ against level 3's $3.88$) and $-10^{-2}$ still inflates, the level-4 twins crossing at $t=12.99$ against level 3's $13.01$. The branch point is thus fixed to $0.2\%$ against both knobs---$13.01$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ against $13.04$ at $10^{-3}$, and level 4 against level 3---which is what makes it the throat's number rather than the grid's or the kick's. The kick also sets the clock: the collapse branch forms its horizon at $t=11$ for $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ and $t=25$ for $+10^{-3}$, against $t\simeq61$ for the unkicked throat left to truncation noise---a driven branching an order of magnitude faster than the undeclared one. The dial has a ceiling: $\varepsilon=\pm0.1$ is not a perturbation---both signs collapse and die ($t=15.2/14.1$) with a Hamiltonian violation $3$--$5\times$ the $1\%$ arms' and growing---so the usable amplitude ends at $10^{-2}$.

\subsection{The collapse endpoint}
\label{sec:single:collapse}
% MOTS formation, horizon mass, clean evolution to t = 100 on the collapse
% branch; the inflation branch: anti-trapped shells, no horizon.
% The half-mass throat: lighter is less stable.

The collapse branch ends in a black hole that eats its own support: behind the marginally trapped surface the areal radius falls $3.23\to2.37$ and the Misner--Sharp mass $1.62\to1.23$ over 40 units---phantom infall \emph{removes} mass---with the origin lapse at $0.016$. (The massless throat of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026} bounced at $t\approx4M$ and shed its horizon by $18.5M$; no arm here ever sheds a horizon, and the partial re-expansion below is a different thing.) The kicked $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ arm runs clean to $t=100$ behind its horizon, and the pure-quadrupole arm gives the endpoint its full instrumentation (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse}): a MOTS from $t=33$ at areal radius $3.80$ settling to $2.41$ with $M_{\rm MS}$ $1.90\to1.24$, while the radial shells watch $|K|$ grow inward and the lapse pinch to zero at the centre---the first single-throat collapse recorded by the in-code radial profile from beginning to end.

The shrinking does not, however, continue. In the three kicked arms, which reach the floor $45$ units earlier than the pure-quadrupole one and so have a record beyond it, the horizon bottoms out and then \emph{grows back}: areal radius $2.00\to2.34$ ($+17.0\%$, floor at $t=48$) in the undamped $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ arm, $2.27\to2.48$ ($+9.4\%$, floor at $t=47$) and $2.11\to2.42$ ($+15.1\%$, floor at $t=43$) in the two scalar-damped ones, with $M_{\rm MS}$ recovering $+10.6$ to $+12.5\%$ in all three and the curves smooth and saturating rather than turning over. Three arms differing in the damping device agree, so the turnaround is not the device. The interior is meanwhile quasi-static---$\min\alpha$ still creeping down, $10^{-2}\to10^{-3}$, $\max|K|$ flat at $0.23$, $\min\chi$ flat---so this is a horizon gaining mass around a frozen core, and it is the local counterpart of the negative-energy flux measured at infinity in Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}: the remnant first loses mass to the phantom field it swallows and then regains a tenth of it. The pure-quadrupole arm of Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse} reaches its own floor only at $t=92$ and therefore shows no turnaround, which is the whole of why this record has to be read across arms. The inflation branch forms no horizon at all, and with the kick declared it can be watched to the end of a record rather than inferred from snapshots. A $\varepsilon=-10^{-2}$ arm at level 4 carrying the collapse arms' full instrument set (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_inflation}) grows $3.81\to11.39$ in areal radius, a factor $3.0$, by $t=100$ with no marginally trapped surface anywhere about the throat; what it does carry is that surface's mirror, an anti-trapped shell ($\theta_+>0$ and $\theta_->0$) present at every scan centre from $t=1$ to $35$---the positive signature for which the earlier arms could report only an absence. Measured against its unkicked level-4 twin, the grid whose own truncation noise inflates the throat to $+122\%$, the kicked arm departs by $10\%$ at $t=25$, the time by which the $+10^{-2}$ twin on this same level has already formed its horizon: the two signs leave together and end apart, and the departure rides the same mode ($\tau=5.47$ from the plateau fit, between the levels' $5.88$ and $5.26$). It also does not stay exponential. The \emph{deviation} grows at the mode's own $0.190\pm0.002$ per unit while it is small, but once the radius itself has doubled the growth decays---in the unkicked arm $\mathrm{d}\ln R/\mathrm{d}t$ peaks at $0.031$ over $t=70$--$80$ and falls to $0.0093$ over $t=90$--$100$, a doubling time lengthening from $22$ to $74$ units, and the kicked arm does the same thing earlier and harder, peaking at $0.036$ over $t=30$--$40$ and reaching $0.0023$ over $t=90$--$100$ (doubling time $19\to298$). The two branches are not mirror images: the collapse runs away to a horizon, the inflation coasts. Mass sets the stability margin from below: the lone $m=0.5$ throat tracks its exact solution only to $t\approx17$ at the $0.1\%$ level (the $m=1$ twin holds to $t\approx35$) and dies at its origin at $t=26.3$ with no horizon---the lighter throat is the less stable one.

\subsection{Lifetime and physical scale}
\label{sec:single:lifetime}

% times M, nothing extrapolated.

At fixed $m/a$ the solution is scale-free, so every time above is a multiple of the throat's own light-crossing time: the measured e-fold is $\tau\alpha_{\rm th}=0.78$--$0.87\,R_\star$ in proper time at the minimal surface, one crossing of the throat to within the resolution spread. The lifetime is then a logarithm of the seed. The two declared kicks fix it directly---a horizon at $11\,M$ for $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ and $25\,M$ for $10^{-3}$, a decade costing $14\,M\simeq\tau\ln10$---and the unkicked throat's $61\,M$ places the level-3 truncation seed at $\varepsilon\simeq2\times10^{-6}$ on the same line. The logarithm is what makes this robust rather than a statement about our grids: ten further decades of quiet initial data extend a $30\,M_\odot$ throat from $1.6$ to $22$ ms. This is the single-throat form of the budget that forbids an inspiral in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}, and it is why a foam-born pair must collide as it is born or not at all.

\subsection{The sign rule}
\label{sec:pair:sign}
% Like-signed throats repel, opposite-signed attract; measured pull/push
% ratio vs the (a^2+m^2)/m^2 prediction.  Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b).

Two identical throats released from rest push apart; reversing one throat's scalar field turns the push into a pull (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b)). That one sign is the only gravity-driven route to a merger in this model, and every merging binary in this paper carries it. The exclusion is complete rather than marginal: both forces fall as $1/d^{2}$, so their ratio $(a^2+m^2)/m^2>1$ is separation-independent and holds for \emph{every} massive drainhole---no gap, no mass, no approach speed lets a like-signed pair fall together under its own gravity, and $\sigma=+1$ is simply not a merger channel. Nor is the flip a tuning put in to get one: $\phi\to-\phi$ leaves $T_{ab}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}, and with it the whole one-body geometry, untouched, so the $\sigma=-1$ partner is the same wormhole carrying the opposite scalar charge. The merger channel costs nothing at the level of the constituents. The measured pull-to-push ratio at $a=2$ is $1.518\pm0.021$ over $15$ slices against the predicted $3/2$, and the force follows the throat \emph{width}---the scalar charge---not the mass, which is equal in all arms.

\subsection{The force law}
\label{sec:pair:force}
% Distance ladder d = 12..18: 1/d^2 excluded, effective separation
% F ~ 1/(d+delta)^2 with delta ~ throat radius; blind d = 18 test;
% width ladder.

Four like-signed pairs, byte-identical but for the gap, give displacements at a common $t=11.5$ of $0.4696/0.3699/0.2980/0.2438$ for $d=12/14/16/18$. Pure $1/d^2$ is excluded---$F\,d^2$ rises monotonically $67.6\to79.0$ across the ladder, a $15.4\%$ spread---but $F\propto(d+\delta)^{-2}$ with $\delta\simeq3.5$ fits every rung pair, and it was a blind prediction: $\delta$ fitted on $d=12$--$16$ alone predicts $0.243$ at $d=18$ where inverse-square predicts $0.209$, and the measurement is $0.2438$. Since $\delta$ is comparable to the measured mouth radius of the placed pair ($4.29$ at $a=2$, $d=12$; $R_\star=3.89$ isolated, Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:placement}), the force is inverse-square in an \emph{effective} separation exceeding the coordinate one by about a throat radius---the deviation lives in the distance label, not in the law. Across widths the push grows at every rung ($0.147/0.283/0.416/0.615$ for $a=1/1.5/2/3$) with a measured exponent ${\approx}1.36$ and falling; the point-charge $a^2$ scaling is not confirmed.

\subsection{Placement systematics}
\label{sec:pair:placement}
% Superposition offset of the placed throat vs d; early squeeze of the
% mouths.  Data: 04_binary_headon/placement_curve.  Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c,d).

The superposition itself is on the ruler: freshly placed pairs read $+1.8\%$ ($d=48$) to $+20.5\%$ ($d=6$) wider than the isolated throat, falling as $d^{-1.16}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c,d)), so every same-$d$ comparison in this paper is made against that curve rather than against the lone throat. Read that way, the released head-on pair's mouths are genuinely squeezed, $-0.6\%$ at $t=7$ growing to $-4.0\%$ by $t=13$---an interaction effect, not placement, since the lone throat is exact until $t=35$.

\subsection{Through the wall to a black hole}
\label{sec:headon:wall}
% Level-3 death anatomy; the seamless level-5 arm clean from t = 0 to 100;
% the remnant is trapped with no throat left, and settles to a finite mass;
% seam-, down-step- and device-independence of the wave.

Five units after the horizon forms, the level-3 scout dies at $t=26.91$ with the classic anatomy---$\chi$ floored at the midpoint, $\max|K|$ running away to $18$, a one-step overflow---but the failure is the grid's, not the spacetime's: at $t=27.05$, where the scout died, the level-5 arm reads a smooth, monotone midpoint (lapse $1.6\times10^{-2}$, $\chi=1.2\times10^{-6}$, $\max|K|=0.11$) and runs to $t=100$ with the constraint norms \emph{falling} the whole way. The arm quoted here carries level 5 from $t=0$: no restart, no mesh seam, no interior fill anywhere on the record. It supersedes the level-3$\to$5 chain used in earlier drafts, and reproduces it---the two arms' $(2,0)$ waveforms agree to $2.9$--$5.4\%$ of peak over the burst window $t=23$--$70$ at $R=10/14/18$, and their radiated energies to $3$--$8\%$; only the post-burst tail, where both amplitudes are below $4\times10^{-4}$ and both arms are noisy, diverges (up to $62\%$ at $R=18$). The seam was therefore never carrying the result. The exterior is grid- and device-independent on the same evidence: the down-step arm agrees on the horizon mass to $1.3\%$ and on the wave to $0.05$--$0.33\%$ of peak over $t=45$--$100$, and the fill twins agree with the no-fill arms at the $0.01\%$-of-peak level.

\subsection{The half-mass pair}
\label{sec:headon:halfmass}

Halving the throat mass does not avoid the horizon; it moves everything earlier. At $m=0.5$, $d=6$, the lapse falls from $0.44$ to $0.036$ over $t=7.5$--$13$, the tracker merges the mouths at $t=11.1$, the offline scan confirms a common MOTS at $t=12$ (areal $R=3.83$, $M_{\rm MS}=2.16$), and the run dies at $t=14.0$. The death belongs to the pair: the lone $m=0.5$ control is exact to $0.02\%$ through $t=14$ and only fails, with no horizon of its own, at $t=26.3$.

\subsection{Capture and scattering}
\label{sec:spiral:capture}
% Momentum scan p = 0.12..0.45: fuse / hover / escape; the capture boundary;
% flyby arm.  Data: 05_binary_spiral + 06_binary_flyby.

Dialling the tangential momentum at $d=12$ splits the outcomes three ways: $p=0.12$ and $0.15$ fuse ($p=0.15$ reproduces $p=0.12$'s endgame signature---a plateau at pit separation $0.816$ against $0.815$, then the dive with the core lapse rising); $p=0.20$ and $0.25$ are captured into bound orbits; $p=0.35$ and $0.45$ escape. The capture boundary therefore sits between $p=0.25$ and $0.35$---$50$--$70\%$ of the circular value---and the fusing boundary between $0.15$ and $0.20$. The $p=0.45$ fly-by is its own result, and every number quoted for it in this paper comes from one arm: the $L=128$, level-5 record that also supplies its waveform. On that arm the pair reaches closest approach $4.82$ at $t=40$ and is back out at $9.6$ by $t=100$, still separating---a scattering pass, not a bound one. (An earlier $L=64$, level-3 arm read $3.95$ at $t=40$ and came back in; it is superseded, and its number should not be read beside the level-5 one.) After the pass \emph{no horizon ever forms}---the corrected scan reads zero surfaces on the whole record---and both mouths swell $\times7.8$ in areal radius, that expansion, not any wall, driving the late constraint growth ($\mathrm{corr}(\log R,\log\|\mathcal{H}\|)=0.92$). Past $t\approx60$ the mouths are large enough that ``separation'' is no longer a clean observable at all: the areal radius reaches $33$ by $t=97$, so the tracked pit centres, not the throats, are what the number describes.

The swelling is the constituents' own unstable mode with the companion driving it, and the honest measure of the driving is the mode's e-fold time, not the growth rate of the radius. Fitted on the growth \emph{excess} $R/R_0-1$, which is the quantity that grows exponentially, the mode runs at $\tau=5.9$ in isolation (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability}), $4.39$ in this fly-by and $3.70$ in the arm that merges (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}): a companion shortens the throat's clock by $25$--$37\%$. The much larger factors one reads off $\mathrm{d}\ln R/\mathrm{d}t$ itself---$399$ units early, $52$ through the approach, $32$ after closest passage---are mostly not tidal driving at all: a mode of \emph{fixed} $\tau$ accelerates the radius automatically as its amplitude climbs, and only the residual $25$--$37\%$ is the companion. It is the $\tau$ that the lifetime budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} runs on.

\subsection{Plunge and collapse}
\label{sec:spiral:inspiral}
% Orbit, whirl, double core, central collapse; resolution cross-check.
% Fig. \ref{fig:spiral_collapse} is the headline collapse page: level-5
% window t = 36-60.44 of the p012_paper production series, every panel
% validated against the pack streams 2026-09-18 (plot_spiral_collapse.py).

Every ``spiral'' in this campaign is a plunge, not an inspiral: with $M_{\rm ADM}\simeq1$ per throat, $d=12$ is $d/M=6$---the innermost-stable-circular-orbit separation---and the throat tracks of the momentum scan complete only $0.16$--$0.23$ of a revolution before closest approach ($p=0.20$--$0.35$). Whether a wider pair could inspiral instead is answered, in the negative, by the lifetime budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}. The production spiral ($p=0.12$, $L=128$) falls from $d=11.9$ to a single merged pit and then collapses at the centre while the throat stays open (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_collapse}). The merger's constraint transient at $t=41.2$ is resolved, not resolution---the level-3 leg reproduces the level-5 norms to $3.5\%/0.7\%$ over the shared window---and everything after it is a clean gauge-and-curvature collapse: the lapse to $2.0\times10^{-3}$, $\chi$ to its floor at $t=58.4$, $\max|K|$ up $25\times$ over $t=50$--$58$ alone (and $\times700$, $0.0084\to6.06$, across the whole level-5 window) on a wall that lands on the throat. Unlike the head-on, the corrected scans find \emph{no} marginally outer-trapped surface at any of $t=55$--$60$, at either resolution, while the throat's areal radius shrinks only $5.7\%$: at its death this remnant is still a wormhole.

The throats are not passive while the orbit closes. A stage-1 re-run of the production arm carrying the per-mouth oriented scan---the same initial data, level 3 to $t=50$---measures each mouth growing from areal radius $4.2385$ to $4.7567$, $+12.2\%$, by $t=28$, the last sample at which the two scan spheres are disjoint and each reading is still about one throat, while the separation closes $11.9\to3.6$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mouth_growth}). Fitted over $t=8$--$25$, the window used on the fly-by, the growth excess is exponential with e-fold time $3.70$ against the fly-by's $4.39$: the two arms share their initial separation and their throats and differ in tangential momentum, one reaching contact and the other missing, yet their mouths inflate on the same clock to $19\%$---the instability belongs to the throat, not to the encounter, which is the premise the budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} runs on. The two arms differ in refinement as well as in momentum (levels 3 and 5), so this is agreement across two grids rather than a single-knob comparison; what makes it usable is that the spiral's own wave and scalar streams are level-independent to $0.005\%$ over their shared window (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}). Two mouths track each other to $2\times10^{-4}$ in areal radius, so this is the radial mode and not an orbital asymmetry, and no marginally trapped or anti-trapped surface appears on any of the three scan centres through $t=50$. The burst of Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:burst} is therefore radiated by throats already more than a tenth larger than the initial data's by the time the pair has halved its separation.

\subsection{The wall}
\label{sec:spiral:wall}
% The late-time failure is gauge + resolution, deterministic, saturating
% with level; the freeze-fill carries the evolution to t = 100.
% Fig. \ref{fig:spiral_ladder}: refinement_ladder.dat, plot_spiral_ladder.py.

No grid gets the spiral through its merger. Restarting the $p=0.12$ run from the same $t=50$ checkpoint at max\_level $3$--$7$, every arm dies on the same $h_{11}$ NaN, and the gains saturate: $+1.03$, $+2.51$, $+0.53$, $+0.06$ units per doubling (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_ladder}). Sixteen times the resolution buys $4.13$ units and never reaches the merger's end, and a halved time step buys $+0.36$ on its own level-5 twin---most of the level $5\to6$ spatial gain---so the top of the ladder is saturation evidence, not a converged limit. The wall is insensitive to the matter device (the damped family dies on the same clock), and to starting refined---the two arms that carry level 5 from $t=0$ are $p=0.20$ and $p=0.25$, and both die at the level-3 wall ($t=52.07$ and $52.79$), so the gain per level belongs to the \emph{restart} recipe and not to depth; the matching cell for $p=0.12$ at $L=128$, level 5 from birth, has not been run---and it moves only modestly when the whole problem is re-based: the production chain---base grid doubled, restarted three times earlier at $t=36$---buys $4.2$ units past the entire ladder and still dies, at $t=60.445$. The interior freeze then carries the exterior to $t=100$; it is a numerical device, engaged only after the burst is causally clear of the extraction spheres (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:burst}).

Why refinement rescues one merger and not the other has a one-line answer that every arm in Table~\ref{tab:matrix} obeys: \emph{resolution rescues a curvature wall only when a horizon censors it}. The head-on's wall kills level 3 at $t=26.91$, but a marginally outer-trapped surface has enclosed the site since $t=22$, and level 5 walks through it and runs to $t=100$ with the constraints falling (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}). The single throat's collapse wall is likewise behind a MOTS from $t=33$, and that branch runs clean to $t=100$ at level 3 alone. The spiral's wall has no horizon anywhere near it---the corrected scan finds no trapped surface at any time, on any centre, in any arm it was run over, sampled at levels 3 and 5 (the level-6 and level-7 rungs, which die on the same clock, were never scanned)---and no grid survives it: sixteen times the resolution buys $4.13$ units, and re-basing the whole problem buys $4.2$ more and still dies. A last arm removes the remaining escape, that the wall might be the grid's own truncation noise rather than the spacetime's: run from the initial data to the wall at level 5 throughout---no restart, no seam, and therefore a seed two refinement levels smaller than the arm that inherits one from a level-3 checkpoint---the merger dies at $t=59.94$ against that arm's $60.45$, the same wall to $0.8\%$ and, if anything, marginally sooner. Refinement applied earlier buys nothing, so the wall is not seeded by the grid. The pattern is not a numerical curiosity. A censored wall is a coordinate problem behind a surface whose exterior is causally insulated from it, so a finer grid has only to postpone a gauge failure the exterior never feels; a naked one is the exterior's own geometry, and no cell size makes it integrable. On this reading the spiral's death is a statement about the spacetime and the head-on's is a statement about the grid---which is exactly how the two are reported here. One arm is untested against the rule rather than consistent with it: the half-mass head-on forms its MOTS at $t=12$ and still dies at $t=14.0$, but it was never re-run at level 5, so whether refinement would have carried it is unknown.

\section{Gravitational waves}
\label{sec:gw}
% Every run's radiation in ONE chapter: what each channel emits (the gallery),

% the ARMS table of plot_psi4_gallery.py; both figures read the same table.

Every channel radiates, and the loudest is the one that never makes a horizon. At the innermost sphere the peak $|r\Psi_4|$ of the dominant mode reads: fly-by $4.1\times10^{-2}$, spiral $2.9\times10^{-2}$, head-on $2.3\times10^{-2}$, vacuum twin $1.1\times10^{-2}$, collapsing throat $2.9\times10^{-3}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery})---the scattering pass outradiates every merger, by $1.4\times$ over the spiral and $3.8\times$ over the equal-parameter vacuum binary. Each record propagates at $c$ within the output cadence and falls as $1/r$ across its spheres, and rescaled to a detector the ordering survives: at each record's own resolved spectral peak the strain amplitude spectral density reads $1.2\times10^{-21}\,\mathrm{Hz}^{-1/2}$ for the fly-by ($193$ Hz), $1.1\times10^{-21}$ for the head-on and $9.3\times10^{-22}$ for the spiral (both $406$ Hz), $3.0\times10^{-22}$ for the vacuum twin ($450$ Hz) and $1.5\times10^{-22}$ for the lone throat ($286$ Hz) (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}). These are quoted at a stated frequency and not as maxima of the curve, because these bursts have no resolved strain maximum: after the $1/f^4$ weight every one of them still rises toward low frequency, so the maximum sits wherever the record's own low-frequency corner stops it---a statement about record length rather than about the source.

The energy budget makes the same point without a detector: in the dominant multipole the fly-by radiates $7.4\times10^{-2}\,M$ and the spiral $2.2\times10^{-2}\,M$, against $2.6\times10^{-3}\,M$ for this campaign's vacuum merger control---$29\times$ and $8.5\times$ more. That control matches the \emph{spiral} in every parameter, $d=12$, $p=0.12$, equal masses, and is therefore the wrong denominator for the fly-by. The right one was run: a vacuum binary with the fly-by's own $d$ and $p=0.45$ and the same bare masses. In vacuum that momentum is unbound---the Newtonian circular value at $d=12$ is $0.204$ and the parabolic one $0.289$---so where the drainhole pair fell from $12$ to $4.8$ on a pull six times gravity, the black holes start at periapsis and coast apart, sweeping $96^\circ$ while their separation opens $12\to21$. They radiate $1.05\times10^{-3}\,M$, a factor $2.3$ \emph{below} the merger control on the sphere where both are in the wave zone. The fly-by therefore outradiates a vacuum binary of its own parameters by ${\simeq}70\times$, not $29$; the published $29$ is the value against a vacuum \emph{merger} and is retained above because it is the comparison the literature's quasi-circular numbers invite. The fly-by also exceeds the $4.84\times10^{-2}\,M$ a quasi-circular vacuum merger of the same mass radiates from infinity. That bound brackets vacuum binaries because it is a statement about a positive-energy system with a finite reservoir; a ghost field is unbounded below, so no such bracket applies here and the exceedance is a signature of the energy condition being violated rather than a symptom of a contaminated measurement---the sphere spread, quoted below, is the instrument for the latter.

The loudest signal in the campaign comes from the encounter that makes nothing: the $p=0.45$ pass emits a single $(2,2)$ arch of peak $|r\Psi_4|=4.1\times10^{-2}$ with no ringdown---there is no remnant to ring---propagating at $v/c=1.00$ across every sphere pair. \subsection{The scalar channel, and its sign}
\label{sec:gw:scalar}
% The second radiation channel, measured on the same spheres as Psi4 from the
% scalar stream of Sec. III.C.  Dipole-dominated, comparable in size to the
% gravitational channel, and NEGATIVE.  Fig. \ref{fig:scalar_channel}.

Every energy above is a $\Psi_4$ energy, and $\Psi_4$ is spin-$2$: it cannot see a spin-$0$ wave. That blindness is not a detail for this matter model, because the throats are held open by a field that carries charge, and a charge that is shaken radiates. Two opposite scalar charges---the sign rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:pair} makes every merging pair opposite---have a dipole moment, where a vacuum binary's leading gravitational moment is the quadrupole. The scalar stream of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics} rides the same extraction spheres as $\Psi_4$ on the spiral and fly-by arms, so the two channels can be compared sphere for sphere with no cross-pipeline normalisation in between (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}).

It is the dipole, and it is large. At $R=30$, over the window in which both spheres still lie outside the inflating mouths, the scalar sector's outgoing power $\sum_{\ell m}|\dot A_{\ell m}|^2$ is carried by $\ell=1$ to within one part in $10^{7}$---the monopole and the quadrupole together are $10^{-7}$ of it---and the time-integrated kinematic flux reaches $2.3$ times the gravitational-wave energy through the same sphere for the fly-by and $2.4$ times for the spiral. The two arms agree although one merges and one misses; the two estimators of the scalar energy, the kinematic flux and the wave-zone mode sum, agree to a factor $1.7$; and for the fly-by the inner sphere returns $3.0$ against the outer sphere's $2.3$, a $30\%$ spread narrower than the $43\%$ its own $\Psi_4$ energy carries. The spiral's inner sphere is not quoted: at $R=14$ it sits only two units outside an orbit of initial diameter $12$, and there both channels are near-zone---its $\Psi_4$ energy is $5\times$ the outer sphere's and its scalar integral has not yet changed sign. The dominant radiation of a drainhole encounter is therefore not gravitational.

Its sign is the result. The stream is signed so that a \emph{canonical} outgoing wave gives a positive flux; gravity here couples to minus that field's stress tensor (Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}), so the physical energy flux is the negative of it, and what these arms radiate in $\ell=1$ is negative energy. A bound system that emits negative energy gains: the dominant channel pumps the orbit where gravitational radiation damps it. This is the same bookkeeping, read at infinity, that the head-on remnant shows at its horizon, where swallowing the phantom field \emph{removes} mass ($M_{\rm MS}$ $3.0\to2.2$, Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:contact}), and that the single-throat remnant shows in reverse when its horizon re-expands (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}).

Three limits are declared with it. The flux is a coordinate flux---no lapse or shift factors are applied, which is justified at $R=30$ where $\alpha$ and $\chi$ are within a percent of unity, and not elsewhere. Both spheres are inside the near zone for part of every record, which is why the number is quoted with its sphere spread and not to three digits. And the balance itself, $\dot M_{\rm ADM}=-F_{\rm GW}-F_\phi$, is \emph{not} closed here: this campaign carries no surface-integral ADM mass, so what is established is the size, the multipole and the sign of the missing term, not that the books balance (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}). The single-throat arms ran before the scalar stream existed and their plotfiles are not retained, so that channel has no number here; the head-on has since been re-run with the stream, and is the subject of the next section.

\section{Controls and systematics}

\subsection{Numerical systematics}
\label{sec:controls:systematics}
% chi-floor twins, fill on/off, damping, gauge, determinism, timestep,
% boundary/sponge checks; the Helfer correction and why it is rejected.

Each numerical device carries a twin that measures it. The $\chi$ clamp is not load-bearing: twins at $10^{-8}$ against $10^{-20}$ re-converge to four digits after touching the floor, and the level-4 pair is byte-identical. The interior fill is not load-bearing for anything outside: head-on fill and no-fill arms agree to $0.01\%$ of the wave peak. The spiral's interior freeze is certified the same way, and directly rather than by proxy: over the $3.4$ units in which the frozen-core arm and the no-fill profiled arm both exist, $t=57.0$--$60.4$, the two agree in the $(2,0)$ and $(2,2)$ modes to $0.013\%$ of peak on the innermost sphere, to one part in $10^{13}$ on the next, and \emph{bit for bit} on the outer two---so what the device does not reach is not inferred from a window shift but read off an overlap. Over that same window the spiral's burst is also resolution-independent: the level-5 leg reproduces the level-3 leg's $\Psi_4$ envelope to $0.005\%$ of peak at both consumer spheres, and its scalar flux to $0.005\%$, which is the convergence test the spiral's waveform had otherwise been missing. The level-5 leg restarts from the level-3 checkpoint at $t=36$, so this bounds the effect of adding two refinement levels to an existing solution rather than of evolving from $t=0$ at each resolution. The diagnostics are provably non-invasive: two different binaries evolving the same spiral checkpoint produce byte-for-byte identical physics streams over $2384$ shared output rows---which also certifies bit-reproducibility on this hardware. The time step is bracketed ($\mathrm{d}t$ multiplier $0.05$ is a different solution from $t\approx33$; $0.1$ dies at $t=16$; $0.02$ is used throughout), grid independence after the head-on merger is $0.05$--$0.33\%$ of peak across three restarted arms, and the placement systematic of Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:placement} is subtracted by construction. The Helfer superposition correction is rejected on measurement: better initial data, worse evolution. It trades the constraint defect at the throats for one in the fade zone between them, and the orbit then stalls at separation $4.7$ instead of plunging---at levels 3, 4 and 5 alike, so converged rather than under-resolved, and worse ($5.1$) when the window is halved; on a fly-by, where nothing merges, the corrected arm's Hamiltonian norm grows $3.2\times10^{-3}\to1.6\times10^{-2}$ in seven units against a flat $2.2\times10^{-3}$ for its plain twin. These arms do not die---they stall, and were stopped by hand---so production superposes plainly and accepts the initial mouth-size excess of Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:placement}. Constraint-solved data was attempted and is not used either: a \texttt{GRTresna} solve reproduces the drainhole once the outer condition on $\psi_{\rm reg}$ is Robin rather than unity ($\psi$ error $4.9\%\to0.7\%$), but the bridge that carries the solved grid into the evolution loses the throat, dying at $t=5.3$---so every binary here is superposed, with the defect of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:binary} declared rather than removed. Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} shows that removing it would buy a fifth of an orbit. Matter damping shapes nothing it was hoped to shape. Finally, the late $\Psi_4$ floor growth of the single-throat family is sphere-local, not a boundary reflection: doubling the box leaves the onset at $t\simeq41$, and in the spherical control---which cannot radiate a quadrupole---the $R=14$ sphere climbs four orders of magnitude while the same run's $R=30$ sphere never leaves $1.1\times10^{-3}$. Every wave window in this paper is therefore gated per sphere, ahead of its own onset.

\section{Pattern matching against detector data}
\label{sec:ligo}

The simulated waveforms are scale-free: with the throat mass $M$ as the unit, $r\Psi_4$ rescales to detector strain by $h \propto M/D$ and $f \propto 1/M$, so one numerical waveform covers every candidate mass at once. This section confronts the rescaled $(2,2)$ and $(2,0)$ waveforms with the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data products on four fronts: their fitting factor against the template families the catalogues are built from~\cite{gwtc3}, a matched-filter search of the open data itself~\cite{allen2012}, the ringdown, and the unmodelled searches.

One pipeline produces every number in this section, and it is validated before it is believed. The $\Psi_4$ records are integrated to strain by fixed-frequency integration~\cite{reisswig2011}, with the divisor clamped below a per-arm corner---the lowest instantaneous frequency the record resolves, never below one cycle per record---without which the fly-by, whose frequency genuinely falls below its own $1/T$, is reconstructed a hundred times too loud. The residual pedestal $|h|(\text{ends})/|h|(\text{peak})$ is $0.02$--$0.07$ for the collapsing throat and the vacuum twin, but $0.31$--$0.42$ for the head-on, spiral and fly-by, whose low-frequency strain is therefore an upper bound and not a measurement; each template is filtered only from its own corner. The end-to-end test that can fail is the campaign's vacuum binary-black-hole twin, a black-hole merger evolved with the same code, grid and extraction: through this pipeline it matches \texttt{IMRPhenomD} at $0.902$--$0.938$ over $60$--$300\,M_\odot$ and recovers its total mass to $0$--$5\%$ at $60$--$200\,M_\odot$, the missing few per cent accounted for by its eccentricity, its $75\,M$ record and its finite extraction radius. Every fitting factor below is read against that $0.90$--$0.94$ ceiling, never against unity.

\subsection{Fitting factor against the binary-black-hole bank}
\label{sec:ligo:match}

Against a quasi-circular \texttt{IMRPhenomD} bank spanning $20$--$500\,M_\odot$ in total mass, mass ratios $1$--$4$ and aligned effective spins $\pm0.9$, at Advanced LIGO design sensitivity, the channels return fitting factors of $0.49$--$0.82$: collapsing throat $0.54$--$0.79$, head-on $0.49$--$0.75$, spiral $0.54$--$0.80$, fly-by $0.76$--$0.82$. Taken alone those numbers would say a modelled search recovers $12$--$56\%$ of the optimal detection volume. The control forbids that reading: the vacuum twin---an ordinary black-hole merger---scores $0.52$--$0.74$ on the same bank, inside the wormhole range. What the full-bank fitting factor measures here is not the exoticism of the source but the \emph{length of the record}: a bank template that spends seconds in band climbing its inspiral has most of its power with nothing to align against, and the recovered masses say the same ($0.11$--$1.84$ of the true value; the control's own at $0.11$--$0.53$). No claim that a wormhole merger would be missed by the modelled searches follows from these records---and none can, because the shortness is the physics rather than a truncation: this matter model cannot produce an inspiral at all (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), so the transient is the whole of the wormhole-phase signal, and what a modelled search would meet at late times is the chirp of whatever the throats convert into, outside these simulations.

What the records do settle is morphology at fixed duration. Cutting each bank template to the signal's own window removes the duration deficit and leaves the shape: the channels return $0.82$--$0.98$ against the control's $0.94$--$0.95$---at this duration, burst morphology alone does not separate a drainhole channel from a binary black hole. The discriminator that does is the frequency track, Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}.

\subsection{A matched-filter search of O3b open data}
\label{sec:ligo:search}

The rescaled waveforms were also run as an actual search of public Advanced LIGO strain~\cite{gwosc}. The bank is the five channels times a mass ladder placed so that neighbouring rungs match at $0.97$ against the live noise curve---$127$ templates, total mass the only bank dimension, because the records are scale-free---and the pipeline is the standard one~\cite{allen2012,pycbc}: matched filter, Allen's $\chi^2$ bin test, re-weighted SNR, Hanford--Livingston coincidence, background from $5000$ time slides. Its sensitivity is measured, not assumed: all $15$ injections into conditioned O3b strain at optimal SNR $20$ are recovered at $93$--$139\%$ of optimal (median $103\%$), within $3$ ms, with $\chi^2_r$ near unity. The one weakness is the bank's shortest template ($5$ ms, four $\chi^2$ bins), where the veto demotes SNR $27.8$ to $14.2$---a cost in sensitivity at the lightest fly-by rungs, not in false triggers.

Searching $2.26$ h of coincident CBC\_CAT3 science time yields \emph{no candidate at zero lag}. The background---$13\,920$ accidental coincidences over $471$ days of slid livetime, loudest $60.4$---caps the smallest reportable false-alarm rate at one per $471$ days; its loudest event is an SNR $758$ Hanford glitch that the veto re-weights to $60.4$, raising the bar rather than entering the foreground.

The useful output of a null result is reach. At single-detector SNR $8$ and optimal orientation, the median horizon over each channel's mass ladder is $4.8$ Gpc (spiral), $2.5$ (fly-by), $1.8$ (head-on), $1.3$ (vacuum twin) and $0.15$ (collapsing throat); divide by the standard sky-and-orientation factor $\langle\mathrm{SNR}\rangle^{-1}=2.26$ (numerically close to the coincident livetime above, and unrelated to it) for the all-sky average, and read the three pedestal-limited channels as upper bounds. The ordering is \emph{not} the energies' ordering---the spiral reaches furthest although the fly-by radiates $3.4\times$ more---and the reason is that reach is set by where a channel's power lands in the noise curve, not by how much of it there is: each channel is scanned over its own mass ladder, which moves its spectrum bodily in frequency, and the fly-by's is the one whose low-frequency content is most nearly all pedestal (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}). What survives is the weaker statement the null result actually supports: the horizonless encounters are the ones a detector reaches furthest for, both of them beyond the vacuum control.

\subsection{Ringdown discriminators}
\label{sec:ligo:ringdown}

Where the burst's shape does not separate these channels, the way its frequency \emph{moves} does. The reference is fixed by the control: the vacuum twin's peak sits at $fM=0.065$, the textbook equal-mass merger value, and its instantaneous frequency climbs $299\to473$ Hz along the Newtonian point-mass track at $30\,M_\odot$ before peeling off at merger, heading toward the $\ell=m=2$ Kerr quasi-normal mode of the equal-mass remnant at $fM=0.0885$ ($599$ Hz). That is what a binary black hole does: monotone rise, then a fixed ringdown frequency.

No drainhole channel does it (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(c)). The head-on's signal is an $(\ell,m)=(2,0)$ bell, and the Kerr reference for it is the axisymmetric $(2,0,0)$ mode rather than the $(2,2,0)$ track quoted for the twin; against either, its frequency \emph{falls}, $486\to342$ Hz, over a sequence of swings of lengthening period ($\sim31$--$39$ in units of $M$) with the amplitude dropping only $\times0.6$--$0.8$ per half-swing---a slowly decaying oscillation, not a ringdown, and it falls where a remnant's would sit fixed. Mass loss cannot rescue the fit, and this is the sharpest form of the discriminator: over the same window the remnant's Misner--Sharp mass \emph{also} falls, $2.99\to2.17$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:contact}), and a black hole's quasi-normal frequency scales as $1/M$, so a shrinking remnant must ring \emph{faster}---by $\times1.38$ here. The measurement is $\times0.70$. The frequency drifts the wrong way by a factor of two against its own mass track, which no choice of remnant parameters can absorb. The fly-by falls further and faster, $227\to115$ Hz, as the pair separates; this one is expected, because no horizon ever forms and there is no remnant to ring. The collapsing throat rings at a single frequency, $335$ Hz at its peak ($fM=0.050$, the $20.6\,M$ period of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:gw}), whose period is $21\%$ \emph{short} of the Schwarzschild fundamental for its Misner--Sharp mass $M_{\rm MS}=1.56$ ($26\,M$)---it rings fast for its mass, at $0.55$ of the equal-mass Kerr mode.

The practical statement is therefore narrow and defensible. A ringdown-only analysis that fits a damped sinusoid and asks whether its frequency and decay lie on the Kerr $(M_f, a_f)$ track would reject every one of these channels---the head-on because its frequency drifts downward through the fit window instead of holding, the throat because it rings $27\%$ fast for its mass, the fly-by because it is not a ringdown at all. Equivalently, a catalogue event whose post-merger frequency is measured to hold within a few per cent at the Kerr value excludes the head-on channel at that mass. Two limits travel with this. The spiral's late record is transported by a frozen core from $t=57$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:burst}) and its frequency climbs $151\to679$ Hz without settling, so this campaign has not measured a spiral ringdown and quotes none. And the head-on's bell is followed only over four swings on a record that ends at $t=100$, so its decay constant is bounded, not fitted.

\subsection{Burst searches and the stochastic background}
\label{sec:ligo:burst}

An unmodelled search is the right instrument for these signals: a burst pipeline asks only for excess power, coherent between detectors, in a time--frequency region, so the missing inspiral---the feature that costs the modelled bank most of its match, and which this source class cannot supply---costs it nothing. The channels are short and loud in exactly its regime: $6$--$125$ ms across the bank, the energy concentrated within a factor of three in frequency.

The clean, detector-independent deliverable promised in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} is the energy per event in throat-mass units, and this campaign measures it in the dominant multipole, with the spread over extraction spheres as its error bar: fly-by $7.4\times10^{-2}\,M$ ($43\%$), spiral $2.2\times10^{-2}\,M$ ($21\%$), head-on $3.3\times10^{-3}\,M$ ($13\%$), collapsing throat $3.2\times10^{-5}\,M$ ($18\%$), against the equal-parameter vacuum binary's $2.6\times10^{-3}\,M$ ($5\%$). Since $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ from a population scales as the product of the comoving event density and the energy released per event, these numbers convert any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$---the observing-run limits, or the nucleosynthesis cap that holds outside every detector band~\cite{lvk2021,pagano2016}---into a bound on the comoving abundance of primordial drainhole encounters, one that is a factor of ${\simeq}70$ tighter for the horizonless fly-by than for an equal-mass black-hole binary of the same parameters, and $29$ tighter than for a vacuum \emph{merger} of the same masses and separation. Three caveats travel with it. The ghost scalar must exist at the epoch in question to hold the newborn throats open. The fly-by's $43\%$ sphere spread is the widest in the campaign, because the expansion of its open mouths is near-zone content that the outer spheres see less of. And these are gravitational energies only: the scalar channel carries $2.3$ times as much again, in the opposite sign, so a bound derived from $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ constrains a population through the smaller of a merger's two channels---conservatively for the abundance, but it means the quantity a detector limits is not the quantity these events mostly emit (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The four fates promised in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} are all realised, each with a measured selection rule: the relative scalar sign separates scattering from merger (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}), the seed's sign separates collapse from inflation (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}), and the orbital momentum separates fusing, capture and escape at $50$--$70\%$ of circular (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}). What a foam-born population radiates is bursts---the energies of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst} convert any $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ bound into an abundance bound---and the budget below shows why no member of the population chirps. Three measurements assemble into one detection philosophy: no inspiral exists (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), the wormhole phase is a burst whose frequency falls or drifts where a Kerr remnant's would hold (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}), and the scalar counterpart dies at horizon formation (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship})---so a surviving wormhole signature is a drifting-frequency burst with a scalar counterpart, and anything with a settled ringdown and no counterpart is already the descendant.

\subsection{No wormhole inspiral}
\label{sec:discussion:noinspiral}

A drainhole binary cannot radiate an inspiral, and the obstruction is a logarithm. Three independent measurements fix the e-fold time of the mouths' unstable mode at $\tau\simeq4.4$ code units: an exponential fit to the fly-by's mouth growth (back-extrapolated seed $7\times10^{-4}$, the superposition defect) gives $4.39$; the eight-unit shift in departure time between refinement levels 3 and 4, divided by $\ln16$, the fourth-order truncation ratio, gives ${\sim}2.9$; and the $11$--$12$-unit delay per decade of declared kick (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}) gives ${\sim}5.0$. In proper time at the minimal surface, where the static lapse is $\alpha_{\rm th}=e^{u(r_t)}=0.575$, that is $1.7$--$2.9$, i.e.\ $T=\tau\alpha_{\rm th}/R_\star=0.43$--$0.74$. These are the \emph{driven} throats of a binary, and they run faster than the isolated mode of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability} ($T=0.87$) rather than bracketing it; the comparison with Ref.~\cite{gonzalez2009} is made there, on the isolated throat its perturbation theory describes, and is not repeated here.

The budget follows. A quasi-circular pair at separation $d$ needs $t_{\rm insp}=\tfrac{5}{256}(d/M)^4\,M/\eta$ to merge by radiation reaction ($M$ the total mass, $\eta=1/4$): $202$ units from the innermost stable circular orbit $d/M=6$ and $640$ units from $d/M=8$---$1.1$ and $2.25$ orbits, counting orbits at the initial separation ($T_{\rm orb}=2\pi\sqrt{d^3/M}$), which understates the cycles actually completed and so states the budget conservatively. The instability is thus handed $t_{\rm insp}/\tau=46$ e-folds at the ISCO and $146$ for a two-orbit inspiral, so the mouths arrive at merger unchanged only if the initial perturbation is below $e^{-46}\simeq10^{-20}$, respectively $10^{-63}$. Turned around, the lifetime is $\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)$: seeds of $10^{-6}$, $10^{-10}$, $10^{-15}$ buy $0.33$, $0.55$, $0.82$ of one orbit---not one, for any nameable seed. The asymmetry is the whole argument: the orbit's demand grows as $(d/M)^4$ while the throat's endurance grows as $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, so no finer grid, quieter data or better solver closes the gap---constraint-solved initial data, cutting the seed from the superposition defect to the truncation floor, is worth $\tau\ln(2\times10^{3})\simeq33$ units, a fifth of one orbit. A companion only sharpens the verdict: a tidal field drives the mode, shortening its e-fold time $5.9\to4.4\to3.7$ from the isolated throat to the fly-by to the merging arm, a $25$--$37\%$ tightening of the clock the budget is spent against (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:capture}). That the budget's $\tau$ is a property of the throat and not of one particular encounter is measured rather than assumed: the arm that merges and the arm that misses, sharing an initial separation and their throats, return $3.70$ and $4.39$ from the same fitted window (Fig.~\ref{fig:mouth_growth}).

Two corrections to the budget's own machinery both point the same way, and neither is small. The inspiral time above is the vacuum, quadrupole, quasi-circular result, and this binary satisfies none of its premises. Its constituents carry opposite scalar charges, so their leading radiative moment is a \emph{dipole}, not a quadrupole, and the ratio of scalar to gravitational emission measured here is $2.3$ on the same sphere (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar})---so the dominant channel is one the formula does not contain. And that channel's energy is negative, so it pumps the orbit rather than draining it. Under the ordinary sign, a radiation reaction a factor of a few stronger than assumed only shortens $t_{\rm insp}$ and hands the instability fewer e-folds while it still needs $\ln(1/\varepsilon)\gtrsim46$: the verdict is unchanged and reached sooner. Under the sign this matter actually has, the orbit is driven outward by its own emission and a quasi-circular decay does not exist to be computed. A circularised wormhole inspiral therefore fails twice over---once on the throat's clock and once on the orbit's---and the arithmetic above, which uses the vacuum formula, is the conservative of the two arguments rather than the fragile one.

The wormhole-specific signal is therefore a short transient: within a few tens of $M$ each mouth has collapsed---forming a horizon at $t=11$ and $25$ for kicks of $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}$---or inflated, measured to $\times7.8$ in the fly-by, and the plunge, burst and conversion of Secs.~\ref{sec:headon}--\ref{sec:gw} are the whole of what this source class emits \emph{as wormholes}. Three qualifications are mandatory. The statement covers the matter model the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach theorem covers, a minimally coupled ghost scalar; throats held open by rotation, additional fields, thin shells or higher-curvature terms are untouched by it. The system converts rather than falls silent, so ``no inspiral'' means no \emph{wormhole} inspiral: any long chirp belongs to the objects the throats become. And the seeds measured here are numerical---superposition defect and truncation noise---which the argument tolerates only because its dependence on the seed is logarithmic; no physical environment supplies a perturbation below $10^{-20}$.

\subsection{Wormhole collapse as a heavy-seed channel}
\label{sec:discussion:seeds}
% Arithmetic on measured numbers only (Table II, Sec. IX B energies, fM peaks
% of Sec. X C); the abundance n(M) is stipulated, and item 10 of XI C says so.

The collapse branch is a seeding mechanism, and Table~\ref{tab:scales} is already the seeding table. A throat of mass $M$ converts to a black hole in $54\,\mathrm{s}$ to $5$ minutes at $10^{6}\,M_\odot$ (seconds at $10^{4}$--$10^{5}$), and the remnant holds a mass of order the constituent's own: the head-on remnant keeps $M_{\rm MS}=2.2$ of a pair born at $3.0$, the lone collapse $1.24$ of $1.90$ at horizon formation and then regains a tenth (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}). The seed therefore exists \emph{at full mass essentially at birth}---no accretion phase is needed to reach seed mass---and it is born \emph{clean}: the scalar hair leaves in a single dipole pulse decaying on the remnant's own mass-settling clock, e-fold ${\sim}20\,M$ from horizon formation (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), so the channel delivers ordinary accreting black holes rather than hairy ones. From $10^{4}$--$10^{5}\,M_\odot$ at $z\simeq20$, growth at the plain Eddington rate (Salpeter e-fold ${\simeq}45$ Myr) reaches $10^{9}\,M_\odot$ in $9$--$12$ e-folds, $420$--$550$ Myr, inside the ${\sim}600$ Myr to $z\simeq7$: no super-Eddington episode is required, which is the arithmetic that defines a heavy seed~\cite{inayoshi2020} (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(a)). That is the population JWST's overmassive black holes and little red dots call for~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024}, by a route that needs no atomic-cooling halo, no critical Lyman--Werner flux and no baryons at all.

Halo independence is itself a signature. The seeds predate and ignore baryonic structure, so $M_{\rm BH}/M_*$ starts effectively infinite and relaxes \emph{downward}---the overmassive morphology of UHZ1 is the generic expectation of this channel rather than a tuning. The abundance asked of it is small: one seed per present-day massive galaxy is $n\simeq10^{-4}$--$10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ comoving, a dark-matter fraction $f=nM/\rho_{\rm DM}\simeq10^{-10}$--$3\times10^{-7}$ at $10^{4}$--$10^{6}\,M_\odot$. And the channel evades the bound that closes the conventional route: FIRAS $\mu$-distortion excludes primordial black holes of $10^{4}$--$10^{13}\,M_\odot$ formed from Gaussian curvature perturbations at any cosmologically relevant abundance~\cite{nakama2018}, but the black hole here forms by wormhole collapse, not from an enhanced curvature perturbation, the same evasion the bubble and domain-wall channels use~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017}---and Ref.~\cite{deng2017} is this paper's birth mechanism read forward: the walls that make the wormholes make the seeds.

The channel radiates, and that makes it falsifiable (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}). Each conversion emits the burst energies of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst}, so a population contributes $\Omega_{\rm GW}\simeq n\,E_{\rm rad}/[\rho_c(1+z_e)]$ at $f_{\rm obs}=(fM)_{\rm peak}/[M(1+z_e)]$; with the measured $(fM)_{\rm peak}=0.03$--$0.06$ the band is $0.3$--$30$ mHz across $10^{4}$--$10^{6}\,M_\odot$ at $z_e\simeq20$. Taking $E_{\rm rad}/M=2.2\times10^{-2}$ (spiral) to $7.4\times10^{-2}$ (fly-by---and a foam population scatters more often than it merges) at $M=10^{5}\,M_\odot$: $n=10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ gives $\Omega_{\rm GW}\sim10^{-11}$ at ${\sim}3$ mHz, $n=10^{-4}$ gives ${\sim}10^{-13}$. LISA's stochastic reach at millihertz is $\Omega\sim10^{-13}$--$10^{-12}$~\cite{lisa2017,caprini2018}, so the prediction band straddles the instrument: if the earliest supermassive black holes are drainhole relics at the one-per-galaxy level, LISA sees or excludes the conversion background. The spectral shape is distinctive twice over---a pure burst population with no $f^{2/3}$ inspiral ramp, because Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} forbids inspirals, and a scalar counterpart invisible to the strain channel---and no direct-collapse scenario radiates anything analogous, because its seed forms by hydrodynamics.

The same table carries a corollary about today's sky. Nothing in the mass range survives (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime}): within this matter model no drainhole reaches the present epoch at any mass, so every observed supermassive black hole is at most a \emph{descendant} of this channel, never the throat itself---and any fit of a static Ellis--Bronnikov metric to the Sgr A$^*$ or M87$^*$ shadows is excluded in-model, the fitted object having died within a day of its birth. The claim made here is deliberately bounded: the collapse branch is a heavy-seed channel with three measured properties---prompt full-mass conversion, halo independence, a $\mu$-distortion-free abundance---and one testable background; whether it is \emph{the} channel behind the JWST population is conditional on the stipulated birth statistics, and item~10 of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open} lists everything that conditionality contains.

\subsection{What is not established}
\label{sec:discussion:open}

Collected here so that no claim above has to carry its own retraction.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No horizon is measured for the spiral merger.} No trapped surface exists anywhere on its measured record, and the curvature wall that kills every grid rides the still-open throat; a $16\times$ refinement ladder saturates without reaching the merger's end (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}).
\item \textbf{No spiral ringdown is measured.} The late record is transported by a core frozen from $t=57$, so its swings are propagation, not a remnant ringing; the instantaneous frequency climbs $151\to679$ Hz without settling (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}).
\item \textbf{The converted pair's own signal is not simulated.} After the wormhole transient the constituents are black holes or inflating runaways (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}); the chirp of such a converted binary---what a modelled search would meet at late times---is outside these records, which bound only the wormhole-phase morphology (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:match}).
\item \textbf{The merging spiral's mouths are measured only to $t=50$ and only at level 3.} The instrumented re-run (Fig.~\ref{fig:mouth_growth}) gives the mouth sizes through the plunge, $+12.2\%$ by $t=28$, but its per-mouth reading ends where the two scan spheres meet, and the production record itself still carries no areal-radius stream: how far the throats had gone at the level-5 death is inferred from the clock, not read off.
\item \textbf{The low-frequency strain of three channels is an upper bound.} Head-on, spiral and fly-by reconstruct with $31$--$42\%$ of their peak strain still present at the record's ends, so their sub-corner content is integration pedestal rather than measurement (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo}); the collapsing throat and the vacuum control, at $2$--$7\%$, do not have this problem.
\item \textbf{The late constraint growth on both single-throat branches from $t\approx75$ is unexplained}, as is the grid-scale wobble that appears on the outermost head-on sphere after $t\approx80$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}).
\item \textbf{The energy balance is not closed.} Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar} establishes that the scalar channel is dipole-dominated, of order twice the gravitational energy and negative---but not that $\dot M_{\rm ADM}=-F_{\rm GW}-F_\phi$ holds, because no surface-integral ADM mass was recorded on any arm. The scalar flux itself is a coordinate flux with no lapse or shift factors, quoted with a $30\%$ sphere spread, and one of the five channels---the single throat---still has no scalar measurement at all: its arms predate the stream and their plotfiles are not retained, so closing it requires a re-run, not a re-read. The head-on was closed that way (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), and only its post-horizon window is usable: before the horizon the flux at the inner sphere runs ingoing, the two open mouths' static hair rather than radiation. For a system whose novelty is negative-energy matter this is the largest single gap in the campaign.
\item \textbf{The horizon scan is star-shaped about a chosen centre.} Every horizon statement---formation, absence, radius, mass---comes from coordinate spheres rather than a deformable finder (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}). The shape systematic therefore attaches not only to the quoted masses but to the \emph{absence} claims that carry the most weight: ``born common or not at all'' (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:contact}) and ``no trapped surface anywhere on the spiral's record'' (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}) are statements about star-shaped surfaces about the tracked pits and the box centre, not about all surfaces.
\item \textbf{The comparison with Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach is a rate comparison, not a parameter-matched one.} The map from this paper's $(a,m)$ drainhole to that work's perturbation parameters has not been verified term by term, so the agreement quoted in Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability} is ${\sim}15\%$ against the published growth times and no finer statement is supported.
\item \textbf{The heavy-seed channel of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} is conditional on stipulated birth statistics.} The birth mass function, epoch and abundance are not derived---birth is quantum, and $n(M)$ is an input---so ``seeds the earliest supermassive black holes'' is conditional on it. The collapse \emph{fraction} of a birth population is not computed: the $\pm$ selection rules are measured (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}) but the birth distribution over them is not, and ambient compression at a radiation-dominated epoch could bias the branch---unmeasured; a throat evolved in a compressive background is the one-arm experiment that would turn the conditional channel into a predicted fraction. The ghost scalar must exist at that epoch. Seed spin is unmeasured (this campaign is non-spinning) where supermassive black hole spins are observable. And each conversion deposits $2.3$ times its gravitational energy as \emph{negative} scalar energy into the early universe (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}); the cosmological back-reaction of that deposit is open (item~7) and must be weighed wherever the seeding claim is used.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every evolution in Table~\ref{tab:matrix} is one line in a registry shipped with the results pack---its single knob against a named reference, its outcome, its caveats---and every figure in this paper is regenerated by a script in the same pack from the archived diagnostic streams. The evolutions are bit-reproducible on the production hardware: two builds differing only in a passive diagnostic reproduce $2384$ shared output rows byte for byte (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}). The code is \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}; the drainhole initial data, the seeds of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}, the orientation-corrected surface scan and the parameter file of every run accompany the registry.

\section{Computational cost}
\label{sec:cost}

Every evolution ran on one NVIDIA H100 80\,GB device of a four-H100 node with two 32-core Xeon Platinum 8462Y+ processors---up to four arms concurrently while the cards were free---with \texttt{GRTeclyn} built CUDA-native against AMReX~26.02. At $\Delta t=0.02\,\Delta x$ a production arm ($L=64$, $128^3$ base, three refinement levels) advances $12$--$18$ code units per hour, so a $t=100$ evolution costs $6$--$8$ h. Depth is the expense: the level-5 arms run at $2$--$4$ units per hour---$28.2$ h for the head-on's seamless $t=0\to100$ (the level-3$\to$5 chain it replaced cost $18.6$ h for its $t=22\to100$ leg alone), $46.7$ h for the fly-by's full $t=0\to100$ on the doubled box, which peaks at $58$ of the $80$ GB with $26$-GB checkpoints against a rolling keep-list---and the production spiral's three-stage chain (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}) costs $23.5$ h end to end. The $121$ evolutions this paper draws on total $708$ GPU-hours, recomputed from each run's own logged speed and time span by a script shipped with the results pack; the archived shakedown stage adds $7$ h more, for $716$ h of packed evolution in all. Earlier drafts quoted a higher figure, which charged the campaign twice for ten runs the prune had left a second copy of; the script now drops copies and dead launches by name rather than counting directories.
