\begin{abstract}
% Headline results only; every number is frozen in results/merger (2026-09-18).
We evolve pairs of massive drainhole wormholes---phantom-scalar throats---in full numerical relativity and follow every outcome a pair can have. A lone throat is a fixed point with one unstable mode (e-fold time $\tau=5.9$, in the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach band) whose two branches, collapse and inflation, confirm the behaviour found for the massless throat; a declared kick picks the branch deterministically and accelerates it, forming a horizon at $t=11$ for $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ against $t\simeq61$ from truncation noise alone. Like-oriented throats repel---attraction requires flipping one scalar sign---with a force inverse-square in an effective separation one throat radius beyond the coordinate one. A head-on pair collides while both mouths are still open and makes a black hole, the trapped surface born enclosing both mouths and never either alone, which then loses mass to the phantom field it swallows ($M_{\rm MS}$ $3.0\to2.2$) and rings without a chirp. The spiral merger instead stalls: no horizon exists anywhere on its measured record while the curvature wall that kills every grid rides the still-open throat, and a $16\times$ refinement ladder saturates without reaching the merger's end. An e-fold budget built on three agreeing measurements of the unstable mode ($\tau\simeq4.4\,M$) forbids a wormhole inspiral outright: an orbit's cost grows as $(d/M)^4$ while a throat's endurance grows only as $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, so surviving even the one orbit from the innermost stable circular orbit needs a seed below $10^{-20}$---every encounter is a plunge, and any long chirp belongs to whatever the throats become. Capture ends between $50$ and $70\%$ of the circular momentum, and the loudest gravitational-wave channel of all is the horizonless fly-by, whose burst at $30\,M_\odot$ would stand clear of the Advanced LIGO design floor at $10$ Mpc.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Wheeler's spacetime foam is a sea of handles---wormholes that open and close on the Planck scale, both mouths in the same space~\cite{wheeler1955,wheeler1957,misner_wheeler1957}. More than one mechanism promotes them to macroscopic size: a Planck-scale Lorentzian wormhole caught in an inflating background is stretched with it and emerges macroscopic~\cite{roman1993}; bubble and domain-wall cosmologies produce, without tuning, post-inflationary regions joined through wormholes~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017}; and a gas of relic wormholes has been run as a cosmology in its own right~\cite{kirillov2008,kirillov2011}. What none of this work evolves is the aftermath: what a relic wormhole does once it is macroscopic and classical, and what two of them do to each other. That aftermath is this paper's subject.

The classical theory fixes its two clocks. First, the simplest evolvable traversable wormhole---the Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole held open by a ghost scalar~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973,morris_thorne1988,visser1995}---is unstable: perturbed one way the throat collapses to a black hole, the other way it inflates, on a timescale set by the throat radius~\cite{shinkai2002,gonzalez2009}. Every constituent destroys itself on its own schedule. Second, Geroch's theorem forbids classical topology change~\cite{geroch1967,tipler1977}: two wormholes cannot fuse into one, and none can be born. Birth is quantum~\cite{hawking1988}, so a simulation takes over at $t=0$ with the newborn state as its initial data---the stipulation every numerical-relativity binary makes, here with a physical story attached.

Together the two facts turn the standing objection to wormhole binaries into a selection rule. A binary has no astrophysical assembly channel---each throat dies faster than capture could pair two---but a foam-born pair needs none: creation and collision are the same event, at a birth separation of order the objects themselves. The race is collision against decay, and both rates belong to the classical solution. The scalar's sign adds a second rule at no cost: a phantom field repels like charges and attracts opposite ones, so like-signed pairs scatter while opposite-signed pairs fall together; and the sign of a lone throat's perturbation selects collapse---a wormhole-to-primordial-black-hole channel beside the standard routes~\cite{zeldovich1967,carr_hawking1974,carr2021}---or inflation. A foam population thus fans out into four fates---merge, scatter, collapse, inflate---across the three-parameter space of birth separation, perturbation and relative sign, and that space is what a family of evolutions maps. The binary evolved here is the exterior approximation of a pair of handles (three asymptotic ends, the far sides passive); what merges is not two wormholes into one, which Geroch forbids, but two wormholes into one black hole with both throats inside it---the horizon does the work that topology change cannot.

The observable is not a chirp but a stochastic background from the merger epoch, redshifted to whichever band the mass scale puts it in~\cite{caprini2018,nanograv2023,lisa2017,lvk2021}. The clean deliverable is therefore dimensionless and epoch-independent: the energy radiated per merger and its spectral shape in throat-mass units, which converts any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$---including the nucleosynthesis cap that holds outside every detector band~\cite{pagano2016}---into a bound on the comoving abundance of primordial wormhole mergers. Two caveats travel with the statement: the ghost scalar must exist at that epoch to hold the newborn throats open, and the merger's scalar channel radiates negative energy whose cosmological consequence is genuinely open.

The paper is organised as follows. Sec.~\ref{sec:model} sets out the model: the Einstein--phantom-scalar system, the drainhole, the binary superposition with its sign rule, and the declared seeds. Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} gives the numerical setup and the run matrix. The results then follow the campaign from one throat to two: the lone wormhole---fixed point, unstable mode, branching and collapse (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}); two throats at rest and the force between them (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}); the head-on merger to a black hole (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon}); the spiral merger and its capture boundary (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}); the gravitational waves of every channel---collapse, head-on, spiral, fly-by---gathered in one chapter with the vacuum twin (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw}); the numerical controls and systematics (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}). Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} confronts the rescaled waveforms with the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data products---template banks, ringdown, burst searches---and Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} collects the findings. Every evolution runs on GPUs with \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}, the AMReX-based~\cite{amrex} port of \texttt{GRChombo}~\cite{grchombo}, continuing the campaign of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026b}. Throughout, $G=c=1$.

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Field equations}
\label{sec:model:field}

The matter is a single real, massless phantom scalar: gravity couples to \emph{minus} the canonical stress tensor,
\begin{equation}
T_{ab} = -\Bigl(\nabla_a\phi\,\nabla_b\phi - \tfrac12 g_{ab}\,\nabla_c\phi\nabla^c\phi\Bigr),
\qquad V(\phi)\equiv 0,
\label{eq:phantom}
\end{equation}
so $\rho<0$ where the field's gradient dominates---the exotic stress that holds a throat open. With $V=0$ this is exactly a ghost field. The sign flip lives entirely in the coupling to geometry: the scalar itself obeys the canonical wave equation $\Box\phi=0$, evolved first order in $\phi$ and $\Pi=(\partial_t\phi-\beta^i\partial_i\phi)/\alpha$.

\subsection{Drainhole initial data}
\label{sec:model:drainhole}

Each body is a massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973}. In isotropic radius $r$ about the throat centre, with
\begin{equation}
X = \frac{r - a^2/4r}{a},\quad
\Omega = 1 + \frac{a^2}{4r^2},\quad
u = \frac{m}{a}\Bigl(\arctan X - \frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the exact static solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom} is
\begin{equation}
\alpha = e^{u},\quad
\gamma_{ij} = e^{-2u}\,\Omega^2\,\delta_{ij},\quad
\phi = C\arctan X,
\label{eq:drainhole}
\end{equation}
with $4\pi C^2 = a^2+m^2$, $K_{ij}=0$, $\Pi=0$ and zero shift; in the evolved conformal variables $\chi = e^{2u}\Omega^{-2}$ and $h_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$. Both constraints hold identically at $t=0$. Here $a$ is the throat scale and $m$ the ADM mass, carried by the \emph{lapse} rather than the conformal factor, so $\chi$ at the throat stays in $[0.15,0.25]$ for $m/a\le1$ and mass costs no grid resolution. The minimal surface sits at $r_t=\tfrac12\bigl(m+\sqrt{m^2+a^2}\bigr)$ with areal radius $R_{\min}=e^{-u(r_t)}\sqrt{a^2+m^2}$; the production throat $a=2$, $m=1$ has $R_{\min}=3.8895$ at $r_t=1.618$. The point $r\to0$ is the far universe's spatial infinity compactified ($\chi\sim r^4$). The mass matters: at $m=0$ the throat is ultrastatic ($\alpha\equiv1$, $M_{\rm ADM}=0$) and two of them do not attract at all.

\subsection{The binary superposition}
\label{sec:model:binary}

Two throats at separation $d$ (one-body radii $r_A$, $r_B$) are superposed in excess form,
\begin{equation}
u = u_A + u_B,\qquad
\psi = 1 + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_A}-1\bigr) + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_B}-1\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\chi = e^{2u}\psi^{-4}$, $\alpha = e^{u}$ and $\phi = \phi_A + \sigma\,\phi_B$, $\sigma=\pm1$; the constant tail of the $\arctan$ profiles is subtracted so $\phi\to0$ at the boundary, which is free for a massless field. Momentum enters through one Bowen--York term~\cite{bowen_york1980} per throat,
\begin{equation}
\hat A_{ij} = \frac{3}{2r^2}\bigl[P_i n_j + P_j n_i - (\delta_{ij}-n_i n_j)\,P{\cdot}n\bigr],
\end{equation}
converted by $\tilde A_{ij} = \chi^{3/2}\hat A_{ij}$, with $K=0$. Since $\Pi=0$ the matter momentum density vanishes and $\hat A_{ij}$ is flat-space divergence free, so the momentum constraint is satisfied \emph{exactly} at $t=0$; only the Hamiltonian constraint carries the superposition defect, $O(a^2/d^2)+O(m/d)$. The relative sign $\sigma$ is the pair's one discrete parameter: $\phi\to-\phi$ is exact for a single body, but a phantom mediator \emph{repels} like scalar charges and \emph{attracts} opposite ones, with $|F_\phi/F_{\rm grav}| = (a^2+m^2)/m^2>1$ always ($=5$ for the production throat)---so like-signed pairs can never merge under their own gravity, and $\sigma=-1$ is the merger channel. A windowed one-body correction of the Helfer type~\cite{helfer2022} is implemented but off in production (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Declared seeds}
\label{sec:model:seeds}

The instability is probed with declared perturbations of the conformal factor only: a Gaussian shell on the minimal surface,
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon\, g(r)\bigr],\qquad
g = e^{-(r-r_t)^2/w^2},\quad w = a/4,
\label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
which moves the throat's areal radius by ${\approx}2\varepsilon$ with the sign of $\varepsilon$, and a quadrupolar factor
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon_2\, g(r)\,P_2(\cos\theta)\bigr]
\end{equation}
about the $z$ axis through the throat. $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ are untouched, so the momentum constraint stays exact. The spherical seed selects the branch; the quadrupolar seed has no $\ell=0$ part---it gives a collapsing throat a quadrupole to shed---and with both on the cross term is $O(\varepsilon\varepsilon_2)$.

\section{Numerical setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Evolution scheme}
\label{sec:setup:scheme}

Evolutions use \texttt{GRTeclyn}: CCZ4~\cite{alic2012} with damping $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=(3,0,1)$, $1{+}\log$ slicing~\cite{bona1995} started from the exact static lapse $\alpha=e^{u}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:drainhole}, a Gamma-driver shift~\cite{campanelli2006,baker2006} with $\eta=1$ started from zero, fourth-order stencils, RK4, and Kreiss--Oliger dissipation $\sigma=0.1$. The time step is $\Delta t=0.02\,\Delta x$, an order of magnitude below the usual CCZ4 Courant factor; the bracket in $\Delta t$ that makes it necessary is a control (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Grid, boundaries and regularisation}
\label{sec:setup:grid}

The domain is a cube of side $L=64$ with the system at its centre; the base grid is $128^3$ ($\Delta x=0.5$). Refinement is by moving nested boxes centred on the tracked throats, shrinking by a factor of two per level (half-width $4$ per throat at the production finest level) and re-drawn every $16$ steps. Production runs use three refinement levels (finest $\Delta x=1/16$); resolution arms raise this to levels $4$--$7$ where stated, and one control doubles the box to $L=128$. Outer boundaries are Sommerfeld, backed by an absorbing sponge over $r=24$--$32$ that ramps extra dissipation up to $\sigma_{\rm sp}=4$ as the fourth power of the depth into the layer; everything dynamical sits inside $r=24$.

Each throat carries a compactified second infinity at its own centre, where $\chi\sim r^4$, so the evolved variables are floored: $\chi\ge10^{-8}$ and $\alpha\ge10^{-10}$ (a variant keeps $\chi$ unfloored down to $10^{-20}$ and applies the $10^{-8}$ floor only where $1/\chi$ is evaluated). Two further devices act only inside regions that are already trapped: an exponential damping of the scalar where the lapse has collapsed, and a smooth interior freeze that multiplies every right-hand side by $1-W(r)$, with $W$ a $C^2$ quintic step between an inner radius $r_{\rm full}$ and an outer $r_{\rm start}$. All three are numerical devices, not physics; whether any of them is load-bearing is itself measured by twin runs (Secs.~\ref{sec:headon:wall} and~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:setup:diagnostics}

$L^2$ norms of the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints over the box are written continuously. Both throat centres are tracked, and about each centre the areal radius $R(r)$ is scanned on coordinate spheres; its minimum is the throat radius. On the same spheres the two null expansions $\theta_\pm$ are computed with the outward direction fixed by \emph{increasing areal radius}: a marginally outer trapped surface is an outermost zero of $\theta_+$ with $\theta_-<0$ (crossings at extrema of $R(r)$ are rejected), while a dynamic wormhole throat is marginally \emph{anti}-trapped~\cite{hochberg_visser1998}. Masses of such surfaces are Misner--Sharp~\cite{misner_sharp1964}, $2M/R = 1 + \tfrac14 R^2\,\theta_+\theta_-$; the scan uses coordinate spheres rather than a deformable finder, and quoted masses carry that shape systematic. Radiation is read from $\Psi_4$ interpolated onto spheres at $R=10$, $14$, $18$ (and $22$ on the single-throat wave arms), decomposed in spin-weight $-2$ harmonics; all amplitudes are quoted as $r\Psi_4$. The in-code stream is cross-checked against an independent plotfile pipeline at the same radii. A per-cell autopsy reports the location and one-step history of any floating-point failure.

\subsection{The static throat as a fixed point}
\label{sec:single:fixedpoint}
% Exact data holds the areal throat radius; departure time vs resolution;
% what the constraint norms do and do not certify.

Evolved with no declared perturbation, the exact drainhole holds its areal throat radius $R_\star=3.8895$ to better than $0.1\%$ for $35$ time units at level 3 and to $1\%$ until $t\simeq44$; at level 4 the radius is still exact to four digits at $t=30$. The static throat is a fixed point of the discrete system as well as of the continuum one. What ends the quiet phase is not a failure of the data: the departure time moves \emph{later} by $5.6$--$8.7$ units per halving of the cell, the signature of a truncation-noise seed of fixed growth rate being handed a smaller initial amplitude. Level 2 never reaches the branch at all---it dies of an origin failure at $t=24.2$ with the throat still exact to $0.12\%$.

\subsection{The unstable mode}
\label{sec:single:instability}
% Growth rate / e-fold time at levels 3 and 4; comparison to the GGS band;
% the undeclared (truncation) seed and its level-dependent sign.
% Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b).

The departure is a single exponential mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b)): plateau fits give e-fold times $\tau=5.88$ (level 3) and $5.26$ (level 4)---the same mode to $9\%$---at $T=\tau\,\alpha_{\rm th}/R_\star=0.87$ and $0.78$ against the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach band $0.68$--$0.76$ for the massless limit~\cite{gonzalez2009}. The instability is the throat's; only its seed is the grid's, and the seed's \emph{sign} flips with resolution: level 3 collapses, level 4 inflates. This confirms, from exact massive-drainhole data, the unstable behaviour and the two-branch structure found for the massless Ellis--Bronnikov throat in Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026}, where ambient noise drove inflation and a compressive perturbation drove collapse: the branches are the wormhole's, not an artefact of either code.

\subsection{Branching: collapse versus inflation}
\label{sec:single:branching}
% Declared seed scan eps = +/-1e-2, +/-1e-3: fate opposite to the push,
% linear response, twin crossing time, tenfold-smaller kick = fixed delay;
% amplitude ceiling (eps = 0.1 is not a perturbation).  Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c).

A declared kick removes the grid's vote (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c)). At $\varepsilon=\pm10^{-2}$ and $\pm10^{-3}$ the fate is always opposite to the push, each $\pm$ pair crosses itself at the $\varepsilon$-independent $t_\times\simeq13.0$, and a tenfold smaller kick arrives $8$--$12$ units later---linear response riding the same unstable mode. The kick also sets the clock: the collapse branch forms its horizon at $t=11$ for $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ and $t=25$ for $+10^{-3}$, against $t\simeq61$ for the unkicked throat left to truncation noise---a driven branching an order of magnitude faster than the undeclared one. The dial has a ceiling: $\varepsilon=\pm0.1$ is not a perturbation---both signs collapse and die ($t=15.2/14.1$) with a Hamiltonian violation $3$--$5\times$ the $1\%$ arms' and growing---so the usable amplitude ends at $10^{-2}$.

\subsection{The collapse endpoint}
\label{sec:single:collapse}
% MOTS formation, horizon mass, clean evolution to t = 100 on the collapse
% branch; the inflation branch: anti-trapped shells, no horizon.
% The half-mass throat: lighter is less stable.

The collapse branch ends in a black hole that eats its own support: behind the marginally trapped surface the areal radius falls $3.23\to2.37$ and the Misner--Sharp mass $1.62\to1.23$ over 40 units---phantom infall \emph{removes} mass---with the origin lapse at $0.016$ and no bounce by $t=100$. (The massless throat of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026} bounced at $t\approx4M$ and shed its horizon by $18.5M$; the massive drainhole shows no bounce in 40 units.) The kicked $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ arm runs clean to $t=100$ behind its horizon, and the pure-quadrupole arm gives the endpoint its full instrumentation (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse}): a MOTS from $t=33$ at areal radius $3.80$ settling to $2.41$ with $M_{\rm MS}$ $1.90\to1.24$, while the radial shells watch $|K|$ grow inward and the lapse pinch to zero at the centre---the first single-throat collapse recorded by the in-code radial profile from beginning to end. The inflation branch forms no horizon at all: a growing anti-trapped shell, the throat $+122\%$ by $t=100$, the compactified inner sheet deforming. Mass sets the stability margin from below: the lone $m=0.5$ throat tracks its exact solution only to $t\approx17$ at the $0.1\%$ level (the $m=1$ twin holds to $t\approx35$) and dies at its origin at $t=26.3$ with no horizon---the lighter throat is the less stable one.

\subsection{The sign rule}
\label{sec:pair:sign}
% Like-signed throats repel, opposite-signed attract; measured pull/push
% ratio vs the (a^2+m^2)/m^2 prediction.  Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b).

Two identical throats released from rest push apart; reversing one throat's scalar field turns the push into a pull (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b)). That one sign is the only gravity-driven route to a merger in this model, and every merging binary in this paper carries it. The measured pull-to-push ratio at $a=2$ is $1.51\pm0.03$ against the predicted $3/2$, and the force follows the throat \emph{width}---the scalar charge---not the mass, which is equal in all arms.

\subsection{The force law}
\label{sec:pair:force}
% Distance ladder d = 12..18: 1/d^2 excluded, effective separation
% F ~ 1/(d+delta)^2 with delta ~ throat radius; blind d = 18 test;
% width ladder.

Four like-signed pairs, byte-identical but for the gap, give displacements at a common $t=11.5$ of $0.4696/0.3699/0.2980/0.2438$ for $d=12/14/16/18$. Pure $1/d^2$ is excluded---$F\,d^2$ rises monotonically by $15.4\%$ across the ladder---but $F\propto(d+\delta)^{-2}$ with $\delta\simeq3.5$ fits every rung pair, and it was a blind prediction: $\delta$ fitted on $d=12$--$16$ alone predicts $0.243$ at $d=18$ where inverse-square predicts $0.209$, and the measurement is $0.2438$. Since $\delta$ is comparable to the measured physical throat radius ($4.29$ at $a=2$), the force is inverse-square in an \emph{effective} separation exceeding the coordinate one by about a throat radius---the deviation lives in the distance label, not in the law. Across widths the push grows at every rung ($0.147/0.283/0.416/0.615$ for $a=1/1.5/2/3$) with a measured exponent ${\approx}1.36$ and falling; the point-charge $a^2$ scaling is not confirmed.

\subsection{Placement systematics}
\label{sec:pair:placement}
% Superposition offset of the placed throat vs d; early squeeze of the
% mouths.  Data: 04_binary_headon/placement_curve.  Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c,d).

The superposition itself is on the ruler: freshly placed pairs read $+1.8\%$ ($d=48$) to $+20.5\%$ ($d=6$) wider than the isolated throat, falling as $d^{-1.16}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c,d)), so every same-$d$ comparison in this paper is made against that curve rather than against the lone throat. Read that way, the released head-on pair's mouths are genuinely squeezed, $-0.6\%$ at $t=7$ growing to $-4.0\%$ by $t=13$---an interaction effect, not placement, since the lone throat is exact until $t=35$.

\subsection{Contact and the common horizon}
\label{sec:headon:contact}

Released from rest at $d=8$ with opposite scalar signs, the pair closes to contact at $t\approx20$ while both mouths are still open wormholes, and a common marginally outer-trapped surface encloses them from $t=22$: the offline corrected scan finds it at $r=3.17$, areal radius $R=5.56$, Misner--Sharp mass $2.99$, growing to $R=5.71$ by $t=24$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:headon_collapse}). The per-mouth scans never find a surface at either mouth at any time---the horizon is born common or not at all. The formation is robust: the frozen-interior ensemble, including both signs of the seed $\varepsilon$, finds the same surface at $t=22.0$ with its radius within half a percent across arms.

\subsection{Through the wall to a black hole}
\label{sec:headon:wall}
% Level-3 death anatomy; level-5 restart clean to t = 100; the remnant is
% trapped with no throat left; down-step and fill arms as evidence the wave
% is grid- and device-independent.

Five units after the horizon forms, the level-3 scout dies at $t=26.91$ with the classic anatomy---$\chi$ floored at the midpoint, $\max|K|$ running away to $18$, a one-step overflow---but the failure is the grid's, not the spacetime's: at $t=27.05$, where the scout died, the level-5 arm reads a smooth, monotone midpoint (lapse $1.6\times10^{-2}$, $\chi=1.2\times10^{-6}$, $\max|K|=0.11$) and runs to $t=100$ with the constraint norms \emph{falling} the whole way. The remnant is a trapped ball with no throat left, losing mass to the phantom field it swallows ($M_{\rm MS}$ $2.99\to2.17$, Fig.~\ref{fig:headon_collapse}). The exterior is grid- and device-independent: the down-step arm lands on the level-5 constraints to $0.08\%/1.5\%$ and on its wave to $0.05$--$0.33\%$ of peak over $t=45$--$100$, and the fill twins agree with the no-fill arms at the $0.01\%$-of-peak level.

\subsection{The half-mass pair}
\label{sec:headon:halfmass}
% m = 0.5, d = 6: earlier horizon, earlier death; mass sets the pull.

Halving the throat mass does not avoid the horizon; it moves everything earlier. At $m=0.5$, $d=6$, the lapse falls from $0.44$ to $0.036$ over $t=7.5$--$13$, the tracker merges the mouths at $t=11.1$, the offline scan confirms a common MOTS at $t=12$ (areal $R=3.83$, $M_{\rm MS}=2.16$), and the run dies at $t=14.0$. The death belongs to the pair: the lone $m=0.5$ control is exact to $0.02\%$ through $t=14$ and only fails, with no horizon of its own, at $t=26.3$.

\section{The spiral merger}
\label{sec:spiral}

\subsection{Capture and scattering}
\label{sec:spiral:capture}
% Momentum scan p = 0.12..0.45: fuse / hover / escape; the capture boundary;
% flyby arm.  Data: 05_binary_spiral + 06_binary_flyby.

Dialling the tangential momentum at $d=12$ splits the outcomes three ways: $p=0.12$ and $0.15$ fuse ($p=0.15$ reproduces $p=0.12$'s endgame signature---a plateau at pit separation $0.816$ against $0.815$, then the dive with the core lapse rising); $p=0.20$ and $0.25$ are captured into bound orbits; $p=0.35$ and $0.45$ escape. The capture boundary therefore sits between $p=0.25$ and $0.35$---$50$--$70\%$ of the circular value---and the fusing boundary between $0.15$ and $0.20$. The $p=0.45$ fly-by is its own result: a bound pass at closest approach $3.95$ after which \emph{no horizon ever forms}---the corrected scan reads zero surfaces on the whole record---and both mouths swell $\times7.8$ in areal radius, that expansion, not any wall, driving the late constraint growth ($\mathrm{corr}(\log R,\log\|\mathcal{H}\|)=0.92$). The swelling is the constituents' own unstable mode with the companion driving it: the mouths' areal-radius e-fold time collapses from $399$ units while the pair is far ($t=0$--$20$) to $52$ through the approach and $32$ after closest passage---the tidal acceleration the lifetime budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} runs on.

\subsection{Plunge and collapse}
\label{sec:spiral:inspiral}
% Orbit, whirl, double core, central collapse; resolution cross-check.
% Fig. \ref{fig:spiral_collapse} is the headline collapse page: level-5
% window t = 36-60.44 of the p012_paper production series, every panel
% validated against the pack streams 2026-09-18 (plot_spiral_collapse.py).

Every ``spiral'' in this campaign is a plunge, not an inspiral: with $M_{\rm ADM}\simeq1$ per throat, $d=12$ is $d/M=6$---the innermost-stable-circular-orbit separation---and the throat tracks of the momentum scan complete only $0.16$--$0.23$ of a revolution before closest approach ($p=0.20$--$0.35$). Whether a wider pair could inspiral instead is answered, in the negative, by the lifetime budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}. The production spiral ($p=0.12$, $L=128$) falls from $d=11.9$ to a single merged pit and then collapses at the centre while the throat stays open (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_collapse}). The merger's constraint transient at $t=41.2$ is resolved, not resolution---the level-3 leg reproduces the level-5 norms to $3.5\%/0.7\%$ over the shared window---and everything after it is a clean gauge-and-curvature collapse: the lapse to $2.0\times10^{-3}$, $\chi$ to its floor at $t=58.4$, $\max|K|$ up $25\times$ on a wall that lands on the throat. Unlike the head-on, the corrected scans find \emph{no} marginally outer-trapped surface at any of $t=55$--$60$, at either resolution, while the throat's areal radius shrinks only $5.7\%$: at its death this remnant is still a wormhole.

\subsection{The wall}
\label{sec:spiral:wall}
% The late-time failure is gauge + resolution, deterministic, saturating
% with level; the freeze-fill carries the evolution to t = 100.
% Fig. \ref{fig:spiral_ladder}: refinement_ladder.dat, plot_spiral_ladder.py.

No grid gets the spiral through its merger. Restarting the $p=0.12$ run from the same $t=50$ checkpoint at max\_level $3$--$7$, every arm dies on the same $h_{11}$ NaN, and the gains saturate: $+1.03$, $+2.51$, $+0.53$, $+0.06$ units per doubling (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_ladder}). Sixteen times the resolution buys $4.13$ units and never reaches the merger's end, and a halved time step buys $+0.36$ on its own level-5 twin---most of the level $5\to6$ spatial gain---so the top of the ladder is saturation evidence, not a converged limit. The wall is insensitive to the matter device (the damped family dies on the same clock), to starting refined (max\_level 5 from $t=0$ dies at $t=52.07$, indistinguishable from the restarted rung), and it moves only modestly when the whole problem is re-based: the production chain---base grid doubled, restarted three times earlier at $t=36$---buys $4.2$ units past the entire ladder and still dies, at $t=60.445$. The interior freeze then carries the exterior to $t=100$; it is a numerical device, engaged only after the burst is causally clear of the extraction spheres (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:burst}).

\section{Gravitational waves}
\label{sec:gw}
% Every run's radiation in ONE chapter: what each channel emits (the gallery),
% whether it is radiation at all (the wavefront speeds), and what it would put
% on a detector (the strain figure).  Streams, gates and innermost spheres are
% the ARMS table of plot_psi4_gallery.py; both figures read the same table.

Every channel radiates, and the loudest is the one that never makes a horizon. At the innermost sphere the peak $|r\Psi_4|$ of the dominant mode reads: fly-by $4.1\times10^{-2}$, spiral $2.9\times10^{-2}$, head-on $2.3\times10^{-2}$, vacuum twin $1.1\times10^{-2}$, collapsing throat $2.9\times10^{-3}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery})---the scattering pass outradiates every merger, by $1.4\times$ over the spiral and $3.8\times$ over the equal-parameter vacuum binary. Each record propagates at $c$ within the output cadence and falls as $1/r$ across its spheres, and rescaled to a detector the ordering survives: at each record's own resolved spectral peak the strain amplitude spectral density reads $1.2\times10^{-21}\,\mathrm{Hz}^{-1/2}$ for the fly-by ($193$ Hz), $1.1\times10^{-21}$ for the head-on and $9.3\times10^{-22}$ for the spiral (both $406$ Hz), $3.0\times10^{-22}$ for the vacuum twin ($450$ Hz) and $1.5\times10^{-22}$ for the lone throat ($286$ Hz) (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}). These are quoted at a stated frequency and not as maxima of the curve, because these bursts have no resolved strain maximum: after the $1/f^4$ weight every one of them still rises toward low frequency, so the maximum sits wherever the record's own low-frequency corner stops it---a statement about record length rather than about the source.

The energy budget makes the same point without a detector: in the dominant multipole the fly-by radiates $7.4\times10^{-2}\,M$ and the spiral $2.2\times10^{-2}\,M$, against $2.6\times10^{-3}\,M$ for the equal-parameter vacuum binary---$29\times$ and $8.5\times$ more, and in the fly-by's case more than the $4.84\times10^{-2}\,M$ a quasi-circular vacuum merger of the same mass radiates from infinity.

\subsection{The collapsing throat}
\label{sec:single:gw}
% Spherical collapse is silent (l = 2 floor); the quadrupolar seed radiates:

A lone throat radiates only if its collapse is not spherical. The spherically kicked arm emits nothing above the $\ell=2$ numerical floor---by symmetry---while the same kick dressed with an $\varepsilon_2=0.05$ quadrupole produces a burst of peak $|r\Psi_4|=2.9\times10^{-3}$, $35\times$ the no-quadrupole control, carrying $E_{\rm rad}=3.2\times10^{-5}\,M$. The burst arrives in radius order at $0.8$--$1.0\,c$, carries the same $r\Psi_4$ to four spheres within $7.2\%$, and scales by $4.85$ for a $5\times$ kick. The deformation, not the kick, is both the source of the wave and the selector of the fate. What is left rings at period $20.6\,M$, $21\%$ short of the Schwarzschild fundamental for the horizon mass; the decay time is not measurable on this record.

\subsection{The head-on ringdown}
\label{sec:headon:wave}

The head-on's signal is a bell with no chirp: the $(2,0)$ breathing mode at $R=10$ swings through $+0.23$, $-0.18$, $+0.11$, $-0.07$ at $t=28.2$, $43.6$, $63.0$, $81.7$---a period of ${\sim}31$--$38$, lengthening, with the amplitude falling $\times0.6$--$0.8$ per half-swing---and the same bell arrives $\sim4$ units later per $4$ units of extraction radius ($0.94\,c$), outgoing across all three spheres. \subsection{The fly-by burst}

The loudest signal in the campaign comes from the encounter that makes nothing: the $p=0.45$ pass emits a single $(2,2)$ arch of peak $|r\Psi_4|=4.1\times10^{-2}$ with no ringdown---there is no remnant to ring---propagating at $v/c=1.00$ across every sphere pair. The outer spheres are not a control here: in retarded time all four peak together at $t-R\simeq34.5$, as radiation must.

\subsection{The vacuum twin}
\label{sec:controls:bbh}

The vacuum control---same $d$, $p$ and masses, no scalar---does what black holes do: merges and settles onto a $227$ Hz ringdown shelf. Against it the wormhole channels are loud: the fly-by's peak $|r\Psi_4|$ is $3.8\times$ the twin's and the spiral's $2.7\times$. The twin also calibrates the wavefront method itself: its merger envelope is a ${\sim}15$-unit plateau at both spheres, where peak-to-peak timing reads a meaningless $0.57\,c$ and the full-waveform lag reads $0.82\,c$---which is why every speed in this chapter is a correlated lag, never a peak time.

\section{Controls and systematics}
\label{sec:controls}

\subsection{Numerical systematics}
\label{sec:controls:systematics}
% chi-floor twins, fill on/off, damping, gauge, determinism, timestep,
% boundary/sponge checks; the Helfer correction and why it is rejected.

Each numerical device carries a twin that measures it. The $\chi$ clamp is not load-bearing: twins at $10^{-8}$ against $10^{-20}$ re-converge to four digits after touching the floor, and the level-4 pair is byte-identical. The interior fill is not load-bearing for anything outside: head-on fill and no-fill arms agree to $0.01\%$ of the wave peak. The diagnostics are provably non-invasive: two different binaries evolving the same spiral checkpoint produce byte-for-byte identical physics streams over $2384$ shared output rows---which also certifies bit-reproducibility on this hardware. The time step is bracketed ($\mathrm{d}t$ multiplier $0.05$ is a different solution from $t\approx33$; $0.1$ dies at $t=16$; $0.02$ is used throughout), grid independence after the head-on merger is $0.05$--$0.33\%$ of peak across three restarted arms, and the placement systematic of Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:placement} is subtracted by construction. The Helfer superposition correction is rejected on measurement: better initial data, worse evolution. Matter damping shapes nothing it was hoped to shape. Finally, the late $\Psi_4$ floor growth of the single-throat family is sphere-local, not a boundary reflection: doubling the box leaves the onset at $t\simeq41$, and in the spherical control---which cannot radiate a quadrupole---the $R=14$ sphere climbs four orders of magnitude while the same run's $R=30$ sphere never leaves $1.1\times10^{-3}$. Every wave window in this paper is therefore gated per sphere, ahead of its own onset.

\section{Pattern matching against detector data}
\label{sec:ligo}

The simulated waveforms are scale-free: with the throat mass $M$ as the unit, $r\Psi_4$ rescales to detector strain by $h \propto M/D$ and $f \propto 1/M$, so one numerical waveform covers every candidate mass at once. This section confronts the rescaled $(2,2)$ and $(2,0)$ waveforms with the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data products on four fronts: their fitting factor against the template families the catalogues are built from~\cite{gwtc3}, a matched-filter search of the open data itself~\cite{allen2012}, the ringdown, and the unmodelled searches.

One pipeline produces every number in this section, and it is validated before it is believed. The $\Psi_4$ records are integrated to strain by fixed-frequency integration~\cite{reisswig2011}, with the divisor clamped below a per-arm corner---the lowest instantaneous frequency the record resolves, never below one cycle per record---without which the fly-by, whose frequency genuinely falls below its own $1/T$, is reconstructed a hundred times too loud. The residual pedestal $|h|(\text{ends})/|h|(\text{peak})$ is $0.02$--$0.07$ for the collapsing throat and the vacuum twin, but $0.31$--$0.42$ for the head-on, spiral and fly-by, whose low-frequency strain is therefore an upper bound and not a measurement; each template is filtered only from its own corner. The end-to-end test that can fail is the campaign's vacuum binary-black-hole twin, a black-hole merger evolved with the same code, grid and extraction: through this pipeline it matches \texttt{IMRPhenomD} at $0.902$--$0.938$ over $60$--$300\,M_\odot$ and recovers its total mass to $0$--$5\%$ at $60$--$200\,M_\odot$, the missing few per cent accounted for by its eccentricity, its $75\,M$ record and its finite extraction radius. Every fitting factor below is read against that $0.90$--$0.94$ ceiling, never against unity.

\subsection{Fitting factor against the binary-black-hole bank}
\label{sec:ligo:match}

Against a quasi-circular \texttt{IMRPhenomD} bank spanning $20$--$500\,M_\odot$ in total mass, mass ratios $1$--$4$ and aligned effective spins $\pm0.9$, at Advanced LIGO design sensitivity, the channels return fitting factors of $0.49$--$0.82$: collapsing throat $0.54$--$0.79$, head-on $0.49$--$0.75$, spiral $0.54$--$0.80$, fly-by $0.76$--$0.82$. Taken alone those numbers would say a modelled search recovers $12$--$56\%$ of the optimal detection volume. The control forbids that reading: the vacuum twin---an ordinary black-hole merger---scores $0.52$--$0.74$ on the same bank, inside the wormhole range. What the full-bank fitting factor measures here is not the exoticism of the source but the \emph{length of the record}: a bank template that spends seconds in band climbing its inspiral has most of its power with nothing to align against, and the recovered masses say the same ($0.11$--$1.84$ of the true value; the control's own at $0.11$--$0.53$). No claim that a wormhole merger would be missed by the modelled searches follows from these records---and none can, because the shortness is the physics rather than a truncation: this matter model cannot produce an inspiral at all (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), so the transient is the whole of the wormhole-phase signal, and what a modelled search would meet at late times is the chirp of whatever the throats convert into, outside these simulations.

What the records do settle is morphology at fixed duration. Cutting each bank template to the signal's own window removes the duration deficit and leaves the shape: the channels return $0.82$--$0.98$ against the control's $0.94$--$0.95$---at this duration, burst morphology alone does not separate a drainhole channel from a binary black hole. The discriminator that does is the frequency track, Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}.

\subsection{A matched-filter search of O3b open data}
\label{sec:ligo:search}

The rescaled waveforms were also run as an actual search of public Advanced LIGO strain~\cite{gwosc}. The bank is the five channels times a mass ladder placed so that neighbouring rungs match at $0.97$ against the live noise curve---$127$ templates, total mass the only bank dimension, because the records are scale-free---and the pipeline is the standard one~\cite{allen2012,pycbc}: matched filter, Allen's $\chi^2$ bin test, re-weighted SNR, Hanford--Livingston coincidence, background from $5000$ time slides. Its sensitivity is measured, not assumed: all $15$ injections into conditioned O3b strain at optimal SNR $20$ are recovered at $93$--$139\%$ of optimal (median $103\%$), within $3$ ms, with $\chi^2_r$ near unity. The one weakness is the bank's shortest template ($5$ ms, four $\chi^2$ bins), where the veto demotes SNR $27.8$ to $14.2$---a cost in sensitivity at the lightest fly-by rungs, not in false triggers.

Searching $2.26$ h of coincident CBC\_CAT3 science time yields \emph{no candidate at zero lag}. The background---$13\,920$ accidental coincidences over $471$ days of slid livetime, loudest $60.4$---caps the smallest reportable false-alarm rate at one per $471$ days; its loudest event is an SNR $758$ Hanford glitch that the veto re-weights to $60.4$, raising the bar rather than entering the foreground.

The useful output of a null result is reach. At single-detector SNR $8$ and optimal orientation, the median horizon over each channel's mass ladder is $4.8$ Gpc (spiral), $2.5$ (fly-by), $1.8$ (head-on), $1.3$ (vacuum twin) and $0.15$ (collapsing throat); divide by $2.26$ for the sky-and-orientation average, and read the three pedestal-limited channels as upper bounds. The ordering is the energies' ordering: the horizonless encounters are what a detector reaches furthest for.

\subsection{Ringdown discriminators}
\label{sec:ligo:ringdown}

Where the burst's shape does not separate these channels, the way its frequency \emph{moves} does. The reference is fixed by the control: the vacuum twin's peak sits at $fM=0.065$, the textbook equal-mass merger value, and its instantaneous frequency climbs $299\to473$ Hz along the Newtonian point-mass track at $30\,M_\odot$ before peeling off at merger, heading toward the $\ell=m=2$ Kerr quasi-normal mode of the equal-mass remnant at $fM=0.0885$ ($599$ Hz). That is what a binary black hole does: monotone rise, then a fixed ringdown frequency.

No drainhole channel does it (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(c)). The head-on's $(2,0)$ signal is a bell whose frequency \emph{falls}, $486\to342$ Hz, over a sequence of swings of lengthening period ($\sim31$--$38$ in units of $M$) with the amplitude dropping only $\times0.6$--$0.8$ per half-swing---a slowly decaying oscillation, not a ringdown, and it falls where a remnant's would sit fixed at $599$ Hz, a factor of $1.75$ above where the bell ends. The fly-by falls further and faster, $227\to115$ Hz, as the pair separates; this one is expected, because no horizon ever forms and there is no remnant to ring. The collapsing throat rings at a single frequency, $335$ Hz at its peak ($fM=0.050$, the $20.6\,M$ period of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:gw}), which is $-21\%$ against the Schwarzschild value for its Misner--Sharp mass and $0.56$ of the equal-mass Kerr mode.

The practical statement is therefore narrow and defensible. A ringdown-only analysis that fits a damped sinusoid and asks whether its frequency and decay lie on the Kerr $(M_f, a_f)$ track would reject every one of these channels---the head-on because its frequency drifts downward through the fit window instead of holding, the throat because it rings $21\%$ slow for its mass, the fly-by because it is not a ringdown at all. Equivalently, a catalogue event whose post-merger frequency is measured to hold within a few per cent at the Kerr value excludes the head-on channel at that mass. Two limits travel with this. The spiral's late record is transported by a frozen core from $t=57$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:burst}) and its frequency climbs $151\to679$ Hz without settling, so this campaign has not measured a spiral ringdown and quotes none. And the head-on's bell is followed only over four swings on a record that ends at $t=100$, so its decay constant is bounded, not fitted.

\subsection{Burst searches and the stochastic background}
\label{sec:ligo:burst}

An unmodelled search is the right instrument for these signals: a burst pipeline asks only for excess power, coherent between detectors, in a time--frequency region, so the missing inspiral---the feature that costs the modelled bank most of its match, and which this source class cannot supply---costs it nothing. The channels are short and loud in exactly its regime: $6$--$125$ ms across the bank, the energy concentrated within a factor of three in frequency.

The clean, detector-independent deliverable promised in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} is the energy per event in throat-mass units, and this campaign measures it in the dominant multipole, with the spread over extraction spheres as its error bar: fly-by $7.4\times10^{-2}\,M$ ($43\%$), spiral $2.2\times10^{-2}\,M$ ($21\%$), head-on $3.3\times10^{-3}\,M$ ($13\%$), collapsing throat $3.2\times10^{-5}\,M$ ($18\%$), against the equal-parameter vacuum binary's $2.6\times10^{-3}\,M$ ($5\%$). Since $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ from a population scales as the product of the comoving event density and the energy released per event, these numbers convert any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$---the observing-run limits, or the nucleosynthesis cap that holds outside every detector band~\cite{lvk2021,pagano2016}---into a bound on the comoving abundance of primordial drainhole encounters, one that is a factor of $29$ tighter for the horizonless fly-by than for an equal-mass black-hole binary of the same parameters. Three caveats travel with it, and none is removed by this work: the ghost scalar must exist at the epoch in question to hold the newborn throats open; the scalar channel radiates its own energy, of the opposite sign, which $\Psi_4$ does not see; and the fly-by's $43\%$ sphere spread is the widest in the campaign, because the expansion of its open mouths is near-zone content that the outer spheres see less of.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The four fates promised in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} are all realised, each with a measured selection rule: the relative scalar sign separates scattering from merger (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}), the seed's sign separates collapse from inflation (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}), and the orbital momentum separates fusing, capture and escape at $50$--$70\%$ of circular (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}). What a foam-born population radiates is bursts---the energies of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst} convert any $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ bound into an abundance bound---and the budget below shows why no member of the population chirps.

\subsection{No wormhole inspiral}
\label{sec:discussion:noinspiral}

A drainhole binary cannot radiate an inspiral, and the obstruction is a logarithm. Three independent measurements fix the e-fold time of the mouths' unstable mode at $\tau\simeq4.4$ code units: an exponential fit to the fly-by's mouth growth (back-extrapolated seed $7\times10^{-4}$, the superposition defect) gives $4.39$; the eight-unit shift in departure time between refinement levels 3 and 4, divided by $\ln16$, the fourth-order truncation ratio, gives ${\sim}2.9$; and the $11$--$12$-unit delay per decade of declared kick (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}) gives ${\sim}5.0$. In proper time at the throat, where the static lapse is $e^{-\pi m/2a}=0.456$, that is $1.3$--$2.3$---the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach mode again~\cite{gonzalez2009}, now timed in a 3+1 evolution.

The budget follows. A quasi-circular pair at separation $d$ needs $t_{\rm insp}=\tfrac{5}{256}(d/M)^4\,M/\eta$ to merge by radiation reaction ($M$ the total mass, $\eta=1/4$): $202$ units---$1.1$ orbits---from the innermost stable circular orbit $d/M=6$, and $640$ units, $2.25$ orbits, from $d/M=8$. The instability is thus handed $t_{\rm insp}/\tau=46$ e-folds at the ISCO and $146$ for a two-orbit inspiral, so the mouths arrive at merger unchanged only if the initial perturbation is below $e^{-46}\simeq10^{-20}$, respectively $10^{-63}$. Turned around, the lifetime is $\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)$: seeds of $10^{-6}$, $10^{-10}$, $10^{-15}$ buy $0.33$, $0.55$, $0.82$ of one orbit---not one, for any nameable seed. The asymmetry is the whole argument: the orbit's demand grows as $(d/M)^4$ while the throat's endurance grows as $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, so no finer grid, quieter data or better solver closes the gap---constraint-solved initial data, cutting the seed from the superposition defect to the truncation floor, is worth $\tau\ln(2\times10^{3})\simeq33$ units, a fifth of one orbit. A companion only sharpens the verdict: the fly-by's tidal field drives the mode, collapsing the mouths' e-fold from $399$ units to $32$ as the separation closes (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:capture}).

The wormhole-specific signal is therefore a short transient: within a few tens of $M$ each mouth has collapsed---forming a horizon at $t=11$ and $25$ for kicks of $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-3}$---or inflated, measured to $\times7.8$ in the fly-by, and the plunge, burst and conversion of Secs.~\ref{sec:headon}--\ref{sec:gw} are the whole of what this source class emits \emph{as wormholes}. Three qualifications are mandatory. The statement covers the matter model the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach theorem covers, a minimally coupled ghost scalar; throats held open by rotation, additional fields, thin shells or higher-curvature terms are untouched by it. The system converts rather than falls silent, so ``no inspiral'' means no \emph{wormhole} inspiral: any long chirp belongs to the objects the throats become. And the seeds measured here are numerical---superposition defect and truncation noise---which the argument tolerates only because its dependence on the seed is logarithmic; no physical environment supplies a perturbation below $10^{-20}$.

\subsection{What is not established}
\label{sec:discussion:open}

Collected here so that no claim above has to carry its own retraction.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No horizon is measured for the spiral merger.} No trapped surface exists anywhere on its measured record, and the curvature wall that kills every grid rides the still-open throat; a $16\times$ refinement ladder saturates without reaching the merger's end (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}).
\item \textbf{No spiral ringdown is measured.} The late record is transported by a core frozen from $t=57$, so its swings are propagation, not a remnant ringing; the instantaneous frequency climbs $151\to679$ Hz without settling (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}).
\item \textbf{The converted pair's own signal is not simulated.} After the wormhole transient the constituents are black holes or inflating runaways (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}); the chirp of such a converted binary---what a modelled search would meet at late times---is outside these records, which bound only the wormhole-phase morphology (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:match}).
\item \textbf{The merging spiral's mouth sizes are unmeasured.} The production spiral carried no per-mouth areal-radius stream, so how deformed the throats already were when the burst was emitted is unmeasured; the fly-by, identical data at the same separation, is $+56\%$ in areal radius by $t=40$, and the spiral's burst is emitted near $t=42$. Quantifying it needs the radius instrumented on a re-run.
\item \textbf{The low-frequency strain of three channels is an upper bound.} Head-on, spiral and fly-by reconstruct with $31$--$42\%$ of their peak strain still present at the record's ends, so their sub-corner content is integration pedestal rather than measurement (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo}); the collapsing throat and the vacuum control, at $2$--$7\%$, do not have this problem.
\item \textbf{The late constraint growth on both single-throat branches from $t\approx75$ is unexplained}, as is the grid-scale wobble that appears on the outermost head-on sphere after $t\approx80$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every evolution in Table~\ref{tab:matrix} is one line in a registry shipped with the results pack---its single knob against a named reference, its outcome, its caveats---and every figure in this paper is regenerated by a script in the same pack from the archived diagnostic streams. The evolutions are bit-reproducible on the production hardware: two builds differing only in a passive diagnostic reproduce $2384$ shared output rows byte for byte (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}). The code is \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}; the drainhole initial data, the seeds of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}, the orientation-corrected surface scan and the parameter file of every run accompany the registry.

\section{Computational cost}
\label{sec:cost}

Every evolution ran on one NVIDIA H100 80\,GB device of a four-H100 node with two 32-core Xeon Platinum 8462Y+ processors---up to four arms concurrently while the cards were free---with \texttt{GRTeclyn} built CUDA-native against AMReX~26.02. At $\Delta t=0.02\,\Delta x$ a production arm ($L=64$, $128^3$ base, three refinement levels) advances $12$--$18$ code units per hour, so a $t=100$ evolution costs $6$--$8$ h. Depth is the expense: the level-5 arms run at $2$--$4$ units per hour---$18.6$ h for the head-on's $t=22\to100$, $46.7$ h for the fly-by's full $t=0\to100$ on the doubled box, which peaks at $58$ of the $80$ GB with $26$-GB checkpoints against a rolling keep-list---and the production spiral's three-stage chain (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}) costs $23.5$ h end to end. The packed campaign totals $832$ GPU-hours of evolution, recomputed from each run's own logged speed and time span by a script shipped with the results pack.
