\begin{abstract}
% Headline results only; every number is frozen in results/merger (2026-09-18).
We evolve pairs of massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainholes---traversable wormholes supported by a phantom scalar field---in 3+1 numerical relativity, at equal masses and a single throat compactness, without spin. An isolated throat is an unstable fixed point: the sign of its perturbation selects collapse or inflation, with a measured e-fold time $\tau=5.9$ within $15\%$ of the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach rate. Like-signed scalar charges repel, so only opposite-signed pairs merge. The head-on binary forms a black hole containing both still-open mouths; the trapped surface is born common, never per mouth, and the remnant loses mass to the swallowed phantom field ($M_{\rm MS}$ $3.0\to2.2$) and rings down without a preceding chirp. The spiral merger reaches the same endpoint: a shape-free finder recovers its common apparent horizon, invisible to star-shaped scans ($M_{\rm MS}=2.41$ at formation, $23\%$ deformed, settling to $2.08$), five time units before the curvature wall that terminates a $16\times$ refinement ladder. No wormhole inspiral is possible: completing one orbit from the innermost stable circular orbit requires an initial perturbation below $10^{-20}$, so every encounter is a plunge. The loudest gravitational-wave emitter is the horizonless fly-by, which radiates $7.4\times10^{-2}\,M$ in the dominant multipole---$29$ times the vacuum merger control and $70$ times a vacuum pass at its own momentum. The ghost scalar radiates $2.3$ times more still, as a negative-energy $\ell=1$ dipole, and horizon formation switches that channel off. Rescaled to detector scale, the bursts match binary-black-hole templates at fixed duration (fitting factors $0.82$--$0.98$) while their frequency tracks fall or drift where a remnant's would hold at the Kerr value; a $127$-template matched-filter search of Advanced LIGO O3b strain returns no candidate at zero lag (reach $0.15$--$4.8$ Gpc). Finally, collapse converts any throat into a black hole of comparable mass within days at the outside. A primordial wormhole population would therefore convert to black holes essentially at birth, supplying heavy seeds for the earliest supermassive black holes that require no host halo and evade the $\mu$-distortion bound on supermassive primordial black holes. The conversions would leave a stochastic background in the millihertz band, $\Omega_{\rm GW}\sim10^{-13}$--$10^{-11}$ at one seed per massive galaxy, within LISA's reach to detect or exclude.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Wheeler's spacetime foam is a sea of handles---wormholes that open and close on the Planck scale, both mouths in the same space~\cite{wheeler1955,wheeler1957,misner_wheeler1957}. More than one mechanism promotes them to macroscopic size: a Planck-scale Lorentzian wormhole caught in an inflating background is stretched with it and emerges macroscopic~\cite{roman1993}; bubble and domain-wall cosmologies produce, without tuning, post-inflationary regions joined through wormholes~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017}; and a gas of relic wormholes has been run as a cosmology in its own right~\cite{kirillov2008,kirillov2011}. What none of this work evolves is the aftermath: what a relic wormhole does once it is macroscopic and classical, and what two of them do to each other. That aftermath is this paper's subject.

Two classical constraints govern the dynamics. First, the simplest evolvable traversable wormhole---the Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole held open by a ghost scalar~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973,morris_thorne1988,visser1995}---is unstable: depending on the sign of the perturbation, the throat either collapses to a black hole or inflates, on a timescale set by the throat radius~\cite{shinkai2002,gonzalez2009}, as a 3+1 evolution of the massless throat confirms nonlinearly~\cite{shirokov2026}. Every constituent is therefore destined to destroy itself. Second, Geroch's theorem forbids classical topology change~\cite{geroch1967,tipler1977}: two wormholes cannot fuse into one, and none can be created classically. Creation is a quantum process~\cite{hawking1988}, so the simulation begins at $t=0$ with the newborn state as its initial data---the stipulation every numerical-relativity binary makes, here with a physical origin attached.

Together these constraints convert the standing objection to wormhole binaries into a set of selection rules. An astrophysical assembly channel does not exist: each throat decays faster than dynamical friction or radiation reaction could pair two. A primordial pair born from spacetime foam, however, requires no assembly---creation and collision are the same event, at a birth separation comparable to the objects themselves---and its subsequent evolution is a race between orbital collision and radial decay, with both rates fixed by the classical solution. The scalar field supplies a second rule: a phantom field repels like charges and attracts opposite ones, so like-signed pairs scatter while opposite-signed pairs fall together; and the sign of a lone throat's perturbation selects collapse---a wormhole-to-primordial-black-hole channel beside the standard routes~\cite{zeldovich1967,carr_hawking1974,carr2021}---or inflation. A foam population therefore fans out into four fates---merger, scattering, collapse, inflation---across the three-parameter space of birth separation, perturbation and relative sign, and that space is what this family of evolutions maps. The binary evolved here is the exterior approximation of a pair of handles (three asymptotic ends, the far sides passive). What merges is not two wormholes into one, which Geroch's theorem forbids, but two wormholes into one black hole containing both throats: the horizon accomplishes what topology change cannot.

The observable is not a chirp but a stochastic background from the merger epoch, redshifted to whichever band the mass scale puts it in~\cite{caprini2018,nanograv2023,lisa2017,lvk2021}. The clean deliverable is therefore dimensionless and epoch-independent: the energy radiated per merger and its spectral shape in throat-mass units, which converts any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$---including the nucleosynthesis cap that holds outside every detector band~\cite{pagano2016}---into a bound on the comoving abundance of primordial wormhole mergers. The same conversion runs forward: JWST's overmassive black holes at $z\gtrsim4$--$6$ favour heavy seeds of $10^4$--$10^6\,M_\odot$ while every proposed formation channel remains contested~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024,inayoshi2020}; the collapse branch measured here is such a channel, and Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} develops it into a falsifiable millihertz prediction with its caveats.

The paper runs from model and setup (Secs.~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:setup}) through the campaign, one throat to two---the lone wormhole (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}), two throats at rest (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}), the head-on (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon}) and spiral (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}) mergers---to the gravitational waves of every channel (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw}) and the controls (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}). Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} confronts the rescaled waveforms with the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data products, and Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} collects the findings: the e-fold budget that forbids a wormhole inspiral (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), the heavy-seed channel (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds}) and what is not established (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}); Secs.~\ref{sec:repro} and~\ref{sec:cost} give the reproducibility pack and the cost. Every evolution runs on GPUs with \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}, the AMReX-based~\cite{amrex} port of \texttt{GRChombo}~\cite{grchombo}, continuing the campaign of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026b}. Throughout, $G=c=1$.

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Field equations}
\label{sec:model:field}

The matter is a single real, massless phantom scalar: gravity couples to \emph{minus} the canonical stress tensor,
\begin{equation}
T_{ab} = -\Bigl(\nabla_a\phi\,\nabla_b\phi - \tfrac12 g_{ab}\,\nabla_c\phi\nabla^c\phi\Bigr),
\qquad V(\phi)\equiv 0,
\label{eq:phantom}
\end{equation}
so $\rho<0$ where the field's gradient dominates---the exotic stress that holds a throat open. With $V=0$ this is exactly a ghost field. The sign flip lives entirely in the coupling to geometry: the scalar itself obeys the canonical wave equation $\Box\phi=0$, evolved first order in $\phi$ and $\Pi=(\partial_t\phi-\beta^i\partial_i\phi)/\alpha$.

\subsection{Drainhole initial data}
\label{sec:model:drainhole}

Each body is a massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973}. In isotropic radius $r$ about the throat centre, with
\begin{equation}
X = \frac{r - a^2/4r}{a},\quad
\Omega = 1 + \frac{a^2}{4r^2},\quad
u = \frac{m}{a}\Bigl(\arctan X - \frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the exact static solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom} is
\begin{equation}
\alpha = e^{u},\quad
\gamma_{ij} = e^{-2u}\,\Omega^2\,\delta_{ij},\quad
\phi = C\arctan X,
\label{eq:drainhole}
\end{equation}
with $4\pi a^2C^2 = a^2+m^2$ (so $C\to1/\sqrt{4\pi}$ as $m\to0$), $K_{ij}=0$, $\Pi=0$ and zero shift; in the evolved conformal variables $\chi = e^{2u}\Omega^{-2}$ and $h_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$. Both constraints hold identically at $t=0$. Here $a$ is the throat scale and $m$ the ADM mass, carried by the \emph{lapse} rather than the conformal factor, so $\chi$ at the throat stays in $[0.15,0.25]$ for $m/a\le1$ and mass costs no grid resolution. The minimal surface sits at $r_t=\tfrac12\bigl(m+\sqrt{m^2+a^2}\bigr)$ with areal radius $R_{\min}=e^{-u(r_t)}\sqrt{a^2+m^2}$; the production throat $a=2$, $m=1$ has $R_{\min}=3.8895$ at $r_t=1.618$. The point $r\to0$ is the far universe's spatial infinity compactified ($\chi\sim r^4$). The mass matters: at $m=0$ the throat is ultrastatic ($\alpha\equiv1$, $M_{\rm ADM}=0$) and two of them do not attract at all.

\subsection{The binary superposition}
\label{sec:model:binary}

Two throats at separation $d$ (one-body radii $r_A$, $r_B$) are superposed in excess form,
\begin{equation}
u = u_A + u_B,\qquad
\psi = 1 + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_A}-1\bigr) + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_B}-1\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\chi = e^{2u}\psi^{-4}$, $\alpha = e^{u}$ and $\phi = \phi_A + \sigma\,\phi_B$, $\sigma=\pm1$; the constant tail of the $\arctan$ profiles is subtracted so $\phi\to0$ at the boundary, which is free for a massless field. Momentum enters through one Bowen--York term~\cite{bowen_york1980} per throat,
\begin{equation}
\hat A_{ij} = \frac{3}{2r^2}\bigl[P_i n_j + P_j n_i - (\delta_{ij}-n_i n_j)\,P{\cdot}n\bigr],
\end{equation}
converted by $\tilde A_{ij} = \chi^{3/2}\hat A_{ij}$, with $K=0$. Since $\Pi=0$ the matter momentum density vanishes and $\hat A_{ij}$ is flat-space divergence free, so the momentum constraint is satisfied \emph{exactly} at $t=0$; only the Hamiltonian constraint carries the superposition defect, $O(a^2/d^2)+O(m/d)$. The relative sign $\sigma$ is the pair's one discrete parameter: $\phi\to-\phi$ is exact for a single body, but a phantom mediator \emph{repels} like scalar charges and \emph{attracts} opposite ones, with $|F_\phi/F_{\rm grav}| = (a^2+m^2)/m^2>1$ always ($=5$ for the production throat)---so like-signed pairs can never merge under their own gravity, and $\sigma=-1$ is the merger channel. A windowed one-body correction of the Helfer type~\cite{helfer2022} is implemented but off in production (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Declared seeds}
\label{sec:model:seeds}

The instability is probed with declared perturbations of the conformal factor only: a Gaussian shell on the minimal surface,
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon\, g(r)\bigr],\qquad
g = e^{-(r-r_t)^2/w^2},\quad w = a/4,
\label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
which moves the throat's areal radius by ${\approx}2\varepsilon$ with the sign of $\varepsilon$, and a quadrupolar factor
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon_2\, g(r)\,P_2(\cos\theta)\bigr]
\end{equation}
about the $z$ axis through the throat. $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ are untouched, so the momentum constraint stays exact. The spherical seed selects the branch; the quadrupolar seed has no $\ell=0$ part---it gives a collapsing throat a quadrupole to shed---and with both on the cross term is $O(\varepsilon\varepsilon_2)$.

The campaign is one-knob: every run differs from a named reference in a single setting, and every run has one line---its difference, its outcome, its caveats---in a registry shipped with the results pack (Sec.~\ref{sec:repro}). \subsection{Evolution scheme}
\label{sec:setup:scheme}

Evolutions use \texttt{GRTeclyn}: CCZ4~\cite{alic2012} with damping $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=(3,0,1)$, $1{+}\log$ slicing~\cite{bona1995} started from the exact static lapse $\alpha=e^{u}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:drainhole}, a Gamma-driver shift~\cite{campanelli2006,baker2006} with $\eta=1$ started from zero, fourth-order stencils, RK4, and Kreiss--Oliger dissipation $\sigma=0.1$. The time step is $\Delta t=0.02\,\Delta x$, an order of magnitude below the usual CCZ4 Courant factor; the bracket in $\Delta t$ that makes it necessary is a control (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Grid, boundaries and regularisation}
\label{sec:setup:grid}

The domain is a cube of side $L=64$ with the system at its centre; the base grid is $128^3$ ($\Delta x=0.5$). Refinement is by moving nested boxes centred on the tracked throats, shrinking by a factor of two per level (half-width $4$ per throat at the production finest level) and re-drawn every $16$ steps. Production runs use three refinement levels (finest $\Delta x=1/16$); resolution arms raise this to levels $4$--$7$ where stated, and the orbital production chain and the fly-by double the box to $L=128$, carrying the sponge and the extraction spheres outward with it. Outer boundaries are Sommerfeld, backed by an absorbing sponge over $r=24$--$32$ that ramps extra dissipation up to $\sigma_{\rm sp}=4$ as the fourth power of the depth into the layer; everything dynamical sits inside $r=24$.

Each throat carries a compactified second infinity at its own centre, where $\chi\sim r^4$, so the evolved variables are floored: $\chi\ge10^{-8}$ and $\alpha\ge10^{-10}$ (a variant keeps $\chi$ unfloored down to $10^{-20}$ and applies the $10^{-8}$ floor only where $1/\chi$ is evaluated). Two further devices act only inside regions that are already trapped: an exponential damping of the scalar where the lapse has collapsed, and a smooth interior freeze that multiplies every right-hand side by $1-W(r)$, with $W$ a $C^2$ quintic step between an inner radius $r_{\rm full}$ and an outer $r_{\rm start}$. All three are numerical devices, not physics; whether any of them is load-bearing is itself measured by twin runs (Secs.~\ref{sec:headon:wall} and~\ref{sec:controls:systematics}).

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:setup:diagnostics}

$L^2$ norms of the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints over the box are written continuously. Both throat centres are tracked, and about each centre the areal radius $R(r)$ is scanned on coordinate spheres; its minimum is the throat radius. On the same spheres the two null expansions $\theta_\pm$ are computed with the outward direction fixed by \emph{increasing areal radius}: a marginally outer trapped surface is an outermost zero of $\theta_+$ with $\theta_-<0$ (crossings at extrema of $R(r)$ are rejected), while a dynamic wormhole throat is marginally \emph{anti}-trapped~\cite{hochberg_visser1998}. That orientation is not a refinement but a correction: the in-code $\theta_\pm$ stream fixes the outward direction by increasing \emph{coordinate} radius, which on the far side of a throat points the wrong way and manufactures trapped verdicts wherever $R(r)$ is falling. Every horizon statement in this paper---formation, absence, radius, mass---is therefore read from an offline scan over the archived plotfiles with the areal orientation applied, and the in-code columns are used only as a live monitor and are never plotted. The correction is not cosmetic: on the naive orientation the spiral remnant reports a common horizon at the throat that the corrected scan does not find---and the common surface that does exist there, measured later by a shape-free finder, lies outside the throat and is invisible to coordinate spheres of either orientation (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}). Masses of such surfaces are Misner--Sharp~\cite{misner_sharp1964},
\begin{equation}
\frac{2M_{\rm MS}}{R} \;=\; 1 + \frac{R^{2}\,\theta_+\theta_-}{4},
\label{eq:misner_sharp}
\end{equation}
with $\theta_\pm$ carrying dimensions of inverse length so that the second term is dimensionless; the scan uses coordinate spheres, and quoted masses---and the presence or absence of a surface, not only its mass---carry that shape systematic. Where the sphere geometry fails, a deformable instrument backs it: a spectral flow finder expands the trial surface as $r=h(\theta,\phi)$ in real harmonics to $\ell=6$ and drives its level-set expansion to zero by a damped fast flow, the outward direction fixed by the same areal-radius rule. It is validated on Kerr--Schild Schwarzschild ($R=2M$ to $0.03\%$ from spherical and dented seeds), on the time-symmetric Ellis throat, which it must and does reject ($\theta_-\ge0$ there: marginally \emph{anti}-trapped), and on the head-on remnant, where it reproduces the star-shaped scan to $2\%$; its production deployment is the spiral's death window (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}). Radiation is read from $\Psi_4$ interpolated onto spheres at $R=10$, $14$, $18$ (and $22$ on the single-throat wave arms) on the $L=64$ box, and at $R=20$, $28$, $36$, $44$ on the doubled one, decomposed in spin-weight $-2$ harmonics. \emph{Every} amplitude in this paper, in text and figures, is $r\Psi_4$: both pipelines multiply by the sphere radius before the harmonic projection, and the two agree to $0.1\%$ where they overlap---the convention checkable rather than declared. The same spheres also carry a scalar stream: $\phi$ and $\Pi$ projected on spin-weight-$0$ harmonics with the same radial scaling, $A_{\ell m}=R\,\phi_{\ell m}$, and the kinematic flux $F_{\rm kin}=-R^2\oint\Pi\,\partial_r\phi\,\mathrm{d}\Omega$ signed so that an outgoing \emph{canonical} wave gives a positive number (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}). A per-cell autopsy reports the location and one-step history of any floating-point failure.

\subsection{The static throat as a fixed point}
\label{sec:single:fixedpoint}
% Exact data holds the areal throat radius; departure time vs resolution;
% what the constraint norms do and do not certify.

Evolved with no declared perturbation, the exact drainhole holds its areal throat radius $R_\star=3.8895$ to better than $0.1\%$ for $35$ time units at level 3 and to $1\%$ until $t\simeq44$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a)); at level 4 the radius is still exact to four digits at $t=30$. The static throat is a fixed point of the discrete system as well as of the continuum one. What ends the quiet phase is not a failure of the data: the departure time moves \emph{later} by $5.6$--$8.7$ units per halving of the cell, the signature of a truncation-noise seed of fixed growth rate being handed a smaller initial amplitude. Level 2 never reaches the branch at all---it dies of an origin failure at $t=24.2$ with the throat still exact to $0.12\%$.

\subsection{The unstable mode}
\label{sec:single:instability}
% Growth rate / e-fold time at levels 3 and 4; comparison to the GGS band;
% the undeclared (truncation) seed and its level-dependent sign.
% Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b).

The departure is a single exponential mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b)): plateau fits give e-fold times $\tau=5.88$ (level 3) and $5.26$ (level 4)---the same mode to $11\%$---at $T=\tau\,\alpha_{\rm th}/R_\star=0.87$ and $0.78$. Gonz\'alez, Guzm\'an and Sarbach's perturbative growth times run from $T=0.846$ at their massless end to $0.590$~\cite{gonzalez2009}; the level-3 value sits $3\%$ above the massless endpoint, the level-4 value $8\%$ below. What is established is agreement with the published rate to ${\sim}15\%$, not placement within the band (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}). The instability is the throat's; only its seed is the grid's, and the seed's \emph{sign} flips with resolution: level 3 collapses, level 4 inflates. This confirms, from exact massive-drainhole data, the two-branch structure found for the massless throat in Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026}: the branches are the wormhole's, not an artefact of either code.

\subsection{Branching: collapse versus inflation}
\label{sec:single:branching}
% Declared seed scan eps = +/-1e-2, +/-1e-3: fate opposite to the push,
% linear response, twin crossing time, tenfold-smaller kick = fixed delay;
% amplitude ceiling (eps = 0.1 is not a perturbation).  Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c).

A declared kick overrides the grid's truncation seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c)). At $\varepsilon=\pm10^{-2}$ and $\pm10^{-3}$ the fate is always opposite to the push, each $\pm$ pair crosses itself at the $\varepsilon$-independent $t_\times\simeq13.0$, and a tenfold smaller kick arrives later by a fixed delay of $11$--$12$ units, read at matched horizon radius (bracketed $8$--$14$ across markers)---linear response riding the same unstable mode. The branching survives refinement: at level 4, whose own truncation seed \emph{inflates} the unkicked throat, $+10^{-2}$ still forms its horizon at $t=11$ and $-10^{-2}$ still inflates, the twins crossing at $t=12.99$ against level 3's $13.01$; the branch point is fixed to $0.2\%$ against both knobs---the throat's number, not the grid's or the kick's. The kick also sets the clock: the collapse branch forms its horizon at $t=11$ for $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ and $t=25$ for $+10^{-3}$, against $t\simeq61$ for the unkicked throat left to truncation noise---a driven branching an order of magnitude faster than the undeclared one. The amplitude range has a ceiling: $\varepsilon=\pm0.1$ is no longer perturbative---both signs collapse and die ($t=15.2/14.1$) with a Hamiltonian violation $3$--$5\times$ the $1\%$ arms' and growing---so the usable amplitude ends at $10^{-2}$.

\subsection{The collapse endpoint}
\label{sec:single:collapse}
% MOTS formation, horizon mass, clean evolution to t = 100 on the collapse
% branch; the inflation branch: anti-trapped shells, no horizon.
% The half-mass throat: lighter is less stable.

The collapse branch ends in a black hole that consumes its own supporting field: behind the marginally trapped surface the areal radius falls $3.23\to2.37$ and the Misner--Sharp mass $1.62\to1.23$ over 40 units---phantom infall \emph{removes} mass---with the origin lapse at $0.016$. (The massless throat of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026} bounced at $t\approx4M$ and shed its horizon by $18.5M$; no arm here ever sheds a horizon, and the partial re-expansion below is a different thing.) The kicked $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ arm runs clean to $t=100$ behind its horizon, and the pure-quadrupole arm gives the endpoint its full instrumentation (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse}): a MOTS from $t=33$ at areal radius $3.80$ settling to $2.41$ with $M_{\rm MS}$ $1.90\to1.24$, while the radial shells watch $|K|$ grow inward and the lapse pinch to zero at the centre---the first single-throat collapse recorded by the in-code radial profile from beginning to end.

The shrinking does not, however, continue. In the three kicked arms, which reach the floor $45$ units earlier than the pure-quadrupole one and so have a record beyond it, the horizon bottoms out and then \emph{grows back}: areal radius $2.00\to2.34$ ($+17.0\%$, floor at $t=48$) in the undamped $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ arm, $2.27\to2.48$ ($+9.4\%$, floor at $t=47$) and $2.11\to2.42$ ($+15.1\%$, floor at $t=43$) in the two scalar-damped ones, with $M_{\rm MS}$ recovering $+10.6$ to $+12.5\%$ in all three and the curves smooth and saturating rather than turning over. Three arms differing in the damping device agree, so the turnaround is not the device. The interior is meanwhile quasi-static---$\min\alpha$ still creeping down, $10^{-2}\to10^{-3}$, $\max|K|$ flat at $0.23$, $\min\chi$ flat---so this is a horizon gaining mass around a frozen core, and it is the local counterpart of the negative-energy flux measured at infinity in Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}: the remnant first loses mass to the phantom field it swallows and then regains a tenth of it. The pure-quadrupole arm of Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse} reaches its own floor only at $t=92$ and so shows no turnaround---hence the cross-arm reading. The inflation branch forms no horizon at all: a $\varepsilon=-10^{-2}$ arm at level 4 with the collapse arms' full instrument set (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_inflation}) grows $3.81\to11.39$ in areal radius, a factor $3.0$, by $t=100$ with no marginally trapped surface anywhere about the throat---carrying instead that surface's mirror, an anti-trapped shell ($\theta_+>0$ and $\theta_->0$) at every scan centre from $t=1$ to $35$, the positive signature for which the earlier arms could report only an absence. Measured against its unkicked level-4 twin, the grid whose own truncation noise inflates the throat to $+122\%$, the kicked arm departs by $10\%$ at $t=25$, the time by which the $+10^{-2}$ twin on this same level has already formed its horizon: the two signs leave together and end apart, and the departure rides the same mode ($\tau=5.47$ from the plateau fit, between the levels' $5.88$ and $5.26$). Nor does it stay exponential: the \emph{deviation} grows at the mode's own $0.190\pm0.002$ per unit while small, but once the radius has doubled the growth decays---$\mathrm{d}\ln R/\mathrm{d}t$ peaking at $0.031$ and falling to $0.0093$ in the unkicked arm (doubling time $22\to74$), earlier and harder in the kicked one, $0.036\to0.0023$ ($19\to298$). The two branches are not mirror images: the collapse runs away to a horizon, the inflation coasts. Mass sets the stability margin from below: the lone $m=0.5$ throat tracks its exact solution only to $t\approx17$ at the $0.1\%$ level (the $m=1$ twin holds to $t\approx35$) and dies at its origin at $t=26.3$ with no horizon---the lighter throat is the less stable one.

\subsection{Lifetime and physical scale}
\label{sec:single:lifetime}
% tau = 5.9 M (Sec. IV B); horizon at t = 11 / 25 M for eps = 1e-2 / 1e-3 and
% t = 61 M on truncation noise (Sec. IV C).  Table II is those three numbers
% times M, nothing extrapolated.

At fixed $m/a$ the solution is scale-free, so every time above is a multiple of the throat's own light-crossing time: the measured e-fold is $\tau\alpha_{\rm th}=0.78$--$0.87\,R_\star$ in proper time at the minimal surface, one crossing of the throat to within the resolution spread. The lifetime is then a logarithm of the seed. The two declared kicks fix it directly---a horizon at $11\,M$ for $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ and $25\,M$ for $10^{-3}$, a decade costing $14\,M\simeq\tau\ln10$---and the unkicked throat's $61\,M$ places the level-3 truncation seed at $\varepsilon\simeq2\times10^{-6}$ on the same line. No throat in the astrophysical mass range survives. A stellar-mass drainhole is gone in milliseconds, a supermassive one in minutes, and only at $10^9\,M_\odot$ does the lifetime reach days---against a Hubble time in every case. The logarithm is what makes this robust rather than a statement about our grids: ten further decades of quiet initial data extend a $30\,M_\odot$ throat from $1.6$ to $22$ ms. This is the single-throat form of the budget that forbids an inspiral in Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}, and it is why a foam-born pair must collide as it is born or not at all.

\subsection{The sign rule}
\label{sec:pair:sign}
% Like-signed throats repel, opposite-signed attract; measured pull/push
% ratio vs the (a^2+m^2)/m^2 prediction.  Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b).

Two identical throats released from rest push apart; reversing one throat's scalar field turns the push into a pull (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b))---the only gravity-driven route to a merger in this model, and every merging binary here carries it. The exclusion is complete: both forces fall as $1/d^{2}$, so their ratio $(a^2+m^2)/m^2>1$ is separation-independent---no gap, mass or approach speed lets a like-signed pair fall together, and $\sigma=+1$ is not a merger channel. Nor is the flip a tuning: $\phi\to-\phi$ leaves $T_{ab}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}, and with it the whole one-body geometry, untouched, so the $\sigma=-1$ partner is the same wormhole carrying the opposite charge. The measured pull-to-push ratio at $a=2$ is $1.518\pm0.021$ over $15$ slices against the predicted $3/2$, and the force follows the throat \emph{width}---the scalar charge---not the mass, equal in all arms.

\subsection{The force law}
\label{sec:pair:force}
% Distance ladder d = 12..18: 1/d^2 excluded, effective separation
% F ~ 1/(d+delta)^2 with delta ~ throat radius; blind d = 18 test;
% width ladder.

Four like-signed pairs, byte-identical but for the gap, give displacements at a common $t=11.5$ of $0.4696/0.3699/0.2980/0.2438$ for $d=12/14/16/18$. Pure $1/d^2$ is excluded---$F\,d^2$ rises monotonically $67.6\to79.0$ across the ladder, a $15.4\%$ spread---but $F\propto(d+\delta)^{-2}$ with $\delta\simeq3.5$ fits every rung pair, and it was a blind prediction: $\delta$ fitted on $d=12$--$16$ alone predicts $0.243$ at $d=18$ where inverse-square predicts $0.209$, and the measurement is $0.2438$. Since $\delta$ is comparable to the measured mouth radius of the placed pair ($4.29$ at $a=2$, $d=12$; $R_\star=3.89$ isolated, Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:placement}), the force is inverse-square in an \emph{effective} separation exceeding the coordinate one by about a throat radius---the deviation lives in the distance label, not in the law. Across widths the push grows at every rung ($0.147/0.283/0.416/0.615$ for $a=1/1.5/2/3$) with a measured exponent ${\approx}1.36$ and falling; the point-charge $a^2$ scaling is not confirmed.

\subsection{Placement systematics}
\label{sec:pair:placement}
% Superposition offset of the placed throat vs d; early squeeze of the
% mouths.  Data: 04_binary_headon/placement_curve.  Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c,d).

The superposition itself is on the ruler: freshly placed pairs read $+1.8\%$ ($d=48$) to $+20.5\%$ ($d=6$) wider than the isolated throat, falling as $d^{-1.16}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c,d)), so every same-$d$ comparison in this paper is made against that curve rather than against the lone throat. Read that way, the released head-on pair's mouths are genuinely squeezed, $-0.6\%$ at $t=7$ growing to $-4.0\%$ by $t=13$---an interaction effect, not placement, since the lone throat is exact until $t=35$.

\subsection{Through the wall to a black hole}
\label{sec:headon:wall}
% Level-3 death anatomy; the seamless level-5 arm clean from t = 0 to 100;
% the remnant is trapped with no throat left, and settles to a finite mass;

Five units after the horizon forms, the level-3 scout dies at $t=26.91$ with the classic anatomy---$\chi$ floored at the midpoint, $\max|K|$ running away to $18$, a one-step overflow---but the failure is the grid's, not the spacetime's: at $t=27.05$, where the scout died, the level-5 arm reads a smooth, monotone midpoint (lapse $1.6\times10^{-2}$, $\chi=1.2\times10^{-6}$, $\max|K|=0.11$) and runs to $t=100$ with the constraint norms \emph{falling} the whole way. \subsection{The half-mass pair}
\label{sec:headon:halfmass}

Halving the throat mass does not avoid the horizon; it moves everything earlier. At $m=0.5$, $d=6$, the lapse falls from $0.44$ to $0.036$ over $t=7.5$--$13$, the tracker merges the mouths at $t=11.1$, the offline scan confirms a common MOTS at $t=12$ (areal $R=3.83$, $M_{\rm MS}=2.16$), and the run dies at $t=14.0$. The death belongs to the pair: the lone $m=0.5$ control is exact to $0.02\%$ through $t=14$ and only fails, with no horizon of its own, at $t=26.3$.

\section{The spiral merger}
\label{sec:spiral}

\subsection{Capture and scattering}
\label{sec:spiral:capture}
% Momentum scan p = 0.12..0.45: fuse / hover / escape; the capture boundary;
% flyby arm.  Data: 05_binary_spiral + 06_binary_flyby.

Dialling the tangential momentum at $d=12$ splits the outcomes three ways: $p=0.12$ and $0.15$ fuse ($p=0.15$ reproduces $p=0.12$'s endgame signature---a plateau at pit separation $0.816$ against $0.815$, then the dive with the core lapse rising); $p=0.20$ and $0.25$ are captured into bound orbits; $p=0.35$ and $0.45$ escape. The capture boundary therefore sits between $p=0.25$ and $0.35$---$50$--$70\%$ of the circular value---and the fusing boundary between $0.15$ and $0.20$. The $p=0.45$ fly-by is its own result, every number from one arm: the $L=128$, level-5 record that also supplies its waveform. Closest approach $4.82$ at $t=40$, back out at $9.6$ by $t=100$, still separating---a scattering pass, not a bound one (an earlier level-3 arm that read $3.95$ and came back in is superseded). After the pass \emph{no horizon ever forms} on the corrected scan, and both mouths swell $\times7.8$ in areal radius---that expansion, not any wall, driving the late constraint growth ($\mathrm{corr}(\log R,\log\|\mathcal{H}\|)=0.92$); past $t\approx60$ ``separation'' describes the tracked pit centres rather than the throats, the areal radius reaching $33$ by $t=97$.

The swelling is the constituents' own unstable mode with the companion driving it. Fitted on the growth \emph{excess} $R/R_0-1$, the quantity that grows exponentially, the mode runs at $\tau=5.9$ in isolation (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability}), $4.39$ in this fly-by and $3.70$ in the arm that merges (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}): a companion shortens the throat's clock by $25$--$37\%$---the driving itself, where the much larger factors read off $\mathrm{d}\ln R/\mathrm{d}t$ are what a mode of \emph{fixed} $\tau$ produces automatically as its amplitude climbs. It is this $\tau$ that the lifetime budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} runs on.

\subsection{Plunge and collapse}
\label{sec:spiral:inspiral}

Every ``spiral'' in this campaign is a plunge, not an inspiral: with $M_{\rm ADM}\simeq1$ per throat, $d=12$ is $d/M=6$---the innermost-stable-circular-orbit separation---and the throat tracks of the momentum scan complete only $0.16$--$0.23$ of a revolution before closest approach ($p=0.20$--$0.35$). Whether a wider pair could inspiral instead is answered, in the negative, by the lifetime budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}. The production spiral ($p=0.12$, $L=128$) falls from $d=11.9$ to a single merged pit and then collapses at the centre while the throat stays open (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_collapse}). The merger's constraint transient at $t=41.2$ is resolved, not resolution---the level-3 leg reproduces the level-5 norms to $3.5\%/0.7\%$ over the shared window---and everything after it is a clean gauge-and-curvature collapse: the lapse to $2.0\times10^{-3}$, $\chi$ to its floor at $t=58.4$, $\max|K|$ up $25\times$ over $t=50$--$58$ alone (and $\times700$, $0.0084\to6.06$, across the whole level-5 window) on a wall that lands on the throat. The corrected star-shaped scans find \emph{no} marginally outer-trapped surface at any of $t=55$--$60$, at either resolution, while the throat's areal radius shrinks only $5.7\%$---both readings correct, their apparent conclusion not. The spectral flow finder of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics} finds one on every death-window slice that survives: newly formed at $t=55.0$ ($R=4.83$, $M_{\rm MS}=2.412$, $\theta_-\simeq-0.05$, barely off zero; it needs $\ell_{\max}=8$, converging where $\ell_{\max}=6$ stalls just above tolerance), then $4.80/2.400$ at $56.0$, $4.77/2.387$ at $57.0$, and $R=4.72\pm0.01$, $M_{\rm MS}=2.361\pm0.001$ at $59.0$---$0.94$ before the NaN---each an inner- and outer-seed bracket, coordinate radius $2.3$--$3.0$ about the pit ($23\%$ peak-to-peak), $\theta_-$ deepening to $-0.18$, birth preceding every slice that survives. On the frozen-core arm's live exterior (the fill, $r\le1.9$, well inside the surface) the remnant at $t=98$--$100$ carries $R=4.15$ and $M_{\rm MS}=2.083\to2.075$, still draining at $0.2\%$ per unit, with both existence witnesses closed: a pointwise-trapped surface inside a pointwise-untrapped one, so a MOTS lies between them by construction, not by tolerance. The mass history $2.41\to2.08$ is phantom infall, the head-on's own signature; the still-open throat (areal radius $3.87$ at $t=59$) lies \emph{inside} the surface. The scans missed it for a measured reason: about the merged pit every coordinate sphere from $r=1.8$ to $3.4$ carries both signs of $\theta_+$ ($-0.2$ to $+0.15$), so no sphere is trapped or untrapped and the star-shaped bracket never closes---the absence was the instrument's, not the spacetime's (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}). At its death this remnant is a black hole with a wormhole inside: the head-on's anatomy, reached through a plunge instead of a collision.

The throats are not passive while the orbit closes. A stage-1 re-run of the production arm carrying the per-mouth oriented scan---the same initial data, level 3 to $t=50$---measures each mouth growing from areal radius $4.2385$ to $4.7567$, $+12.2\%$, by $t=28$, the last sample at which the two scan spheres are disjoint and each reading is still about one throat, while the separation closes $11.9\to3.6$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:mouth_growth}). Fitted over $t=8$--$25$, the window used on the fly-by, the growth excess is exponential with e-fold time $3.70$ against the fly-by's $4.39$: the two arms share their initial separation and their throats and differ in tangential momentum, one reaching contact and the other missing, yet their mouths inflate on the same clock to $19\%$---the instability belongs to the throat, not to the encounter, which is the premise the budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} runs on (grid caveat in Fig.~\ref{fig:mouth_growth}). Two mouths track each other to $2\times10^{-4}$ in areal radius, so this is the radial mode and not an orbital asymmetry, and no marginally trapped or anti-trapped surface appears on any of the three star-shaped scan centres through $t=50$. The burst of Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:burst} is therefore radiated by throats already more than a tenth larger than the initial data's by the time the pair has halved its separation.

\subsection{The curvature wall and grid failure}
\label{sec:spiral:wall}
% The late-time failure is gauge + resolution, deterministic, saturating
% with level; the freeze-fill carries the evolution to t = 100.
% Fig. \ref{fig:spiral_ladder}: refinement_ladder.dat, plot_spiral_ladder.py.

No tested grid configuration carries the spiral through its merger. Restarted from the same $t=50$ checkpoint at max\_level $3$--$7$, every arm of the $p=0.12$ run dies on the same $h_{11}$ NaN, and the gains saturate: $+1.03$, $+2.51$, $+0.53$, $+0.06$ units per doubling (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_ladder}). A sixteenfold increase in resolution buys $4.13$ units and never reaches the merger's end, and halving the time step buys $+0.36$ on the level-5 twin---most of the level $5\to6$ spatial gain---so the top of the ladder is saturation evidence, not a converged limit. The failure is insensitive to the matter device (the damped family dies on the same clock) and to starting refined: the two arms that carry level 5 from $t=0$, $p=0.20$ and $p=0.25$, both die at the level-3 wall ($t=52.07$ and $52.79$), so the gain per level belongs to the \emph{restart} recipe rather than to depth ($p=0.12$'s own level-5-from-birth cell is the seam-free arm below). Re-basing the whole problem moves it only modestly: the production chain, with base grid doubled and a restart three times earlier at $t=36$, survives $4.2$ units past the entire ladder and still dies, at $t=60.445$. The interior freeze then carries the exterior to $t=100$; it is a numerical device, engaged only after the burst is causally clear of the extraction spheres (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:burst}).

Why refinement rescues one merger and not the other requires care. The working hypothesis through most of this campaign was that resolution rescues a curvature wall only when a horizon censors it, and no horizon had been found near the spiral's. The evidence for the first half is direct. The head-on's wall kills level 3 at $t=26.91$, but a marginally outer-trapped surface has enclosed the site since $t=22$, and level 5 passes through and runs to $t=100$ with the constraints falling (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}); the single throat's collapse wall likewise lies behind a MOTS from $t=33$, and that branch runs cleanly to $t=100$ at level 3 alone. The spiral, however, is not a counterexample of the naked kind. The star-shaped scan that reported its wall uncensored---no trapped surface at any time, on any centre, in any arm---is structurally blind about the merged pit (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}), where the shape-free finder recovers a common MOTS on every surviving death-window slice, the latest $0.94$ before the seam-free arm's NaN. The wall is therefore censored, and the grid fails nevertheless (the ladder and re-based chain above). Truncation seeding is excluded as well: the arm evolved at level 5 from the initial data---no restart, no seam, a truncation seed two refinement levels smaller than a restarted arm inherits---dies at $t=59.94$ against that arm's $60.45$, the same wall to $0.8\%$ and, if anything, marginally sooner. Refinement applied earlier buys nothing, so the wall is not seeded by the grid. What survives is censorship as a \emph{necessary} condition for rescue, together with a measured counterexample to its converse. Why a censored wall defeats every grid on one encounter and none on the other remains open, and the obvious asymmetry is measured away: the spiral's horizon exists by $t=55.0$, at least five units before the NaN---the head-on's own lead over its level-3 wall---and is permanent, settling to $M_{\rm MS}=2.08$. Presence, lead time and permanence are matched between the rescued merger and the unrescued one; whatever distinguishes their walls, it is not the horizon. One arm is untested against the rule rather than consistent with it: the half-mass head-on forms its MOTS at $t=12$ and still dies at $t=14.0$, but it was never re-run at level 5, so whether refinement would have carried it is unknown.

The mechanism question is thereby sharpened rather than closed. The expectation behind the dichotomy was causal: a censored wall is a coordinate pathology behind a surface whose exterior is insulated from it, so a finer grid need only postpone a gauge failure the exterior never feels---the head-on's history---whereas a naked wall belongs to the exterior geometry itself. The spiral strains that expectation: its wall is censored yet cannot be postponed. The slicing itself is the natural suspect. Every arm in Table~\ref{tab:matrix} evolves the same moving-puncture gauge---$1{+}\log$ slicing with a Gamma-driver shift---a family tailored to punctures and horizons, whose slicings can develop coordinate shocks of their own~\cite{alcubierre2003}, failures that a finer grid resolves rather than cures. A shock forming \emph{at or outside} a young, strongly deformed horizon would terminate every grid, censored or not, and the refinement ladder cannot by itself distinguish such a shock from a physical obstruction. Five gauge arms---three on the slicing, two on the shift---bear on this without deciding it. Halving the $1{+}\log$ coefficient (the $-2\alpha K$ driver to $-\alpha K$) on the $L=64$ chain leaves the plunge and the merged remnant intact while moving the wall from $t=52.1$ to $43.6$. Leaving the family outright---the harmonic-class driver $-2\alpha^{2}K$---does not rescue the spiral either: its wall arrives at $t=49.0$, three units early, with the same failure anatomy. The same driver on the head-on control (the level-3 scout re-run) moves that death from $t=26.9$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon}) to $25.2$, and there the horizon's witness goes with it: the star-shaped scan that reports the common apparent horizon from $t=22$ in the standard gauge reports no closed surface before the failure, only scattered trapped points from $t=24.9$, and the shape-free flow finder of Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}, run on the last surviving slice, finds none either---so the standard gauge's five-unit horizon lead is not gauge-robust. The shift sector moves clocks without killing anything: with the Gamma-driver damping quadrupled ($\eta=1\to4$), the head-on control reaches its stop time still un-merged, and the spiral merges and collapses ${\sim}10$ units late, passing the standard wall's clock time by eight units with no failure---but its record ends $4.4$ units after its own delayed collapse, inside the standard arm's $7.9$-unit gap between the conformal factor reaching its floor and the wall, and the flow finder on its final slice finds no common horizon yet, so whether that wall is removed or merely rides the delayed clock is unresolved at this stop time. The failure \emph{time} is thus manifestly gauge-dependent wherever it was measured---$52.1/43.6/49.0$, and beyond $60$ under the quadrupled shift damping, on the spiral; $26.9/25.2$ on the control---while the wall itself has survived every slicing tried, including the one exit from the $1{+}\log$ family; both slicings tried are Bona--Masso drivers, and a shock-avoiding member or maximal slicing remains untried. What the ladder and the twins establish is therefore grid-, seed- and device-independence \emph{within the gauges tried}: no slicing tried removes the wall, only its clock, and whether one exists that does remains open; the untested gauges and the diagnostics' slicing-dependence are collected in item~1 of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}.

The fly-by also exceeds the $4.84\times10^{-2}\,M$ a quasi-circular vacuum merger radiates from infinity---a bound that brackets positive-energy systems with a finite reservoir; a ghost field is unbounded below, so the exceedance is a signature of the energy condition being violated, not of a contaminated measurement, for which the sphere spread is the instrument.

\subsection{The spiral's carried burst}
\label{sec:spiral:burst}
% Peak (2,2)/(2,0) amplitudes, fill-free by causality; envelopes fold onto
% one curve at v/c = 1.00/1.00/1.00 across R = 20--44; the flow finder's
% t = 59 horizon postdates the burst's emission window on surviving slices.

The spiral's $(2,2)$ burst peaks at $|r\Psi_4|=2.9\times10^{-2}$ and folds onto one curve across $R=20$--$44$ at $v/c=0.95/1.00/1.00$. The burst is fill-free by causality: the interior freeze that carries the late record is armed at $t=53$ with its skin at $r\ge1.8$, so the earliest it can influence any extraction sphere is after the burst has passed it---and the head-on's fill twins bound the device's imprint at the $0.01\%$-of-peak level. The production arm carries its own certificate (Fig.~\ref{fig:fill_insensitivity}): a twin with only the fill window moved shifts the $(2,2)$ waveform by at most $0.385\%$ of peak, the residual fronts arriving on the fill's own causal clock---the fill is a device, not a source, and the carried burst survives having its container reshaped. The nature of the burst remains half-measured. It was drafted here as the wave of a merger that stalls as a wormhole, the scans finding no horizon; the shape-free finder has since recovered the common horizon, newly formed, at $t=55.0$---the earliest surviving slice---and nothing surviving reaches back toward the merger transient at $t=41.2$ that launches the burst (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}). The remnant half is therefore measured---the object left behind is a black hole---while the source half is open: how much of the burst left before the horizon closed is on no surviving record. It is the wave of a black hole's formation, not of its ringdown.

\subsection{The scalar channel, and its sign}
\label{sec:gw:scalar}
% The second radiation channel, measured on the same spheres as Psi4 from the
% scalar stream of Sec. III.C.  Dipole-dominated, comparable in size to the
% gravitational channel, and NEGATIVE.  Fig. \ref{fig:scalar_channel}.

Every energy above is a $\Psi_4$ energy, and $\Psi_4$ is spin-$2$: it cannot see a spin-$0$ wave. Two opposite scalar charges---the sign rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:pair} makes every merging pair opposite---have a dipole moment, where a vacuum binary's leading gravitational moment is the quadrupole. The scalar stream rides the same extraction spheres as $\Psi_4$ on the spiral and fly-by arms, so the two channels compare sphere for sphere with no cross-pipeline normalisation (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}).

The channel is dipole-dominated, and it is large. At $R=30$, over the window in which both spheres still lie outside the inflating mouths, the scalar sector's outgoing power $\sum_{\ell m}|\dot A_{\ell m}|^2$ is carried by $\ell=1$ to within one part in $10^{7}$, and the time-integrated kinematic flux reaches $2.3$ times the gravitational-wave energy through the same sphere for the fly-by and $2.4$ times for the spiral. The two arms agree although one merges and one misses; the two estimators of the scalar energy, the kinematic flux and the wave-zone mode sum, agree to a factor $1.7$; and for the fly-by the inner sphere returns $3.0$ against the outer sphere's $2.3$, a $30\%$ spread narrower than the $43\%$ its own $\Psi_4$ energy carries. The spiral's inner sphere is not quoted: at $R=14$ it sits only two units outside an orbit of initial diameter $12$, and there both channels are near-zone---its $\Psi_4$ energy is $5\times$ the outer sphere's and its scalar integral has not yet changed sign. The dominant radiation of a drainhole encounter is therefore not gravitational.

The sign of the flux is the central result. The stream is signed so that a \emph{canonical} outgoing wave gives a positive flux; gravity here couples to minus that field's stress tensor (Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}), so the physical energy flux is the negative of it, and what these arms radiate in $\ell=1$ is negative energy. A bound system that emits negative energy gains: the dominant channel pumps the orbit where gravitational radiation damps it. This is the same bookkeeping, read at infinity, that the head-on remnant shows at its horizon, where swallowing the phantom \emph{removes} mass (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:contact}), and the single-throat remnant shows in reverse when its horizon re-expands (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}).

Three limits are declared with it. The flux is a coordinate flux---no lapse, shift or induced-metric factors are applied---and at $R=30$ the omission is bounded rather than assumed: $\alpha$ and $\chi$ are measured within a percent of unity on that sphere, so the geometric flux's multiplicative factors are percent-level corrections there, an order below the sphere spread every number carries; the advective $\beta^i$ term is not measured, and the same bound does not hold at the inner spheres, whose readings enter only as the error bar above. That is why the scalar energies are quoted to one significant figure, and why nothing downstream uses more than the channel's sign, its multipole and its order of magnitude. Both spheres are inside the near zone for part of every record. And the balance itself, $\dot M_{\rm ADM}=-F_{\rm GW}-F_\phi$, is \emph{not} closed here: this campaign carries no surface-integral ADM mass, so what is established is the size, the multipole and the sign of the missing term, not that the books balance (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}). The single-throat arms ran before the scalar stream existed and their plotfiles are not retained, so that channel has no number here; the head-on has since been re-run with the stream, and is the subject of the next section.

\subsection{Numerical systematics}
\label{sec:controls:systematics}
% chi-floor twins, fill on/off, damping, gauge, determinism, timestep,
% boundary/sponge checks; the Helfer correction and why it is rejected.

Each numerical device carries a twin that measures it. The $\chi$ clamp is not load-bearing: twins at $10^{-8}$ against $10^{-20}$ re-converge to four digits after touching the floor, and the level-4 pair is byte-identical. The interior fill is not load-bearing for anything outside: head-on fill and no-fill arms agree to $0.01\%$ of the wave peak. The spiral's interior freeze is certified the same way, and directly rather than by proxy: over the $3.4$ units in which the frozen-core arm and the no-fill profiled arm both exist, $t=57.0$--$60.4$, the two agree in the $(2,0)$ and $(2,2)$ modes to $0.013\%$ of peak on the innermost sphere, to one part in $10^{13}$ on the next, and \emph{bit for bit} on the outer two---read off an overlap, not inferred from a window shift. Over that same window the spiral's burst is also resolution-independent: the level-5 leg reproduces the level-3 leg's $\Psi_4$ envelope and scalar flux to $0.005\%$ of peak---the spiral waveform's otherwise-missing convergence test, bounding the effect of adding two levels to an existing solution (the leg restarts from the $t=36$ checkpoint). The diagnostics are provably non-invasive: two different binaries evolving the same spiral checkpoint produce byte-for-byte identical physics streams over $2384$ shared output rows---which also certifies bit-reproducibility on this hardware. The time step is bracketed ($\mathrm{d}t$ multiplier $0.05$ is a different solution from $t\approx33$; $0.1$ dies at $t=16$; $0.02$ is used throughout), grid independence after the head-on merger is $0.05$--$0.33\%$ of peak across three restarted arms, and the placement systematic of Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:placement} is subtracted by construction. The Helfer superposition correction is rejected on measurement: better initial data, worse evolution---it trades the constraint defect at the throats for one in the fade zone between them, and the orbit stalls at separation $4.7$ instead of plunging, at levels 3, 4 and 5 alike (worse, $5.1$, with the window halved), while on a fly-by the corrected arm's Hamiltonian norm grows $3.2\times10^{-3}\to1.6\times10^{-2}$ in seven units against a flat plain twin. Production therefore superposes plainly and accepts the mouth-size excess of Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:placement}. Constraint-solved data was attempted and is not used either: a \texttt{GRTresna} solve reproduces the drainhole once the outer condition on $\psi_{\rm reg}$ is Robin rather than unity ($\psi$ error $4.9\%\to0.7\%$), but the bridge into the evolution loses the throat at $t=5.3$---so every binary is superposed, the defect of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:binary} declared rather than removed (removing it would buy a fifth of an orbit, Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}). Matter damping shapes nothing it was hoped to shape. Finally, the late $\Psi_4$ floor growth of the single-throat family is sphere-local, not a boundary reflection---doubling the box leaves the onset at $t\simeq41$, and the spherical control's $R=14$ sphere climbs four orders of magnitude while its $R=30$ sphere never leaves $1.1\times10^{-3}$---so every wave window is gated per sphere, ahead of its own onset.

\section{Pattern matching against detector data}
\label{sec:ligo}

The simulated waveforms are scale-free: with the throat mass $M$ as the unit, $r\Psi_4$ rescales to detector strain by $h \propto M/D$ and $f \propto 1/M$, so one numerical waveform covers every candidate mass at once. This section confronts the rescaled $(2,2)$ and $(2,0)$ waveforms with the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data products on four fronts: their fitting factor against the template families the catalogues are built from~\cite{gwtc3}, a matched-filter search of the open data itself~\cite{allen2012}, the ringdown, and the unmodelled searches.

One validated pipeline produces every number here. The $\Psi_4$ records are integrated to strain by fixed-frequency integration~\cite{reisswig2011}, the divisor clamped below a per-arm corner (the lowest instantaneous frequency the record resolves, never below one cycle per record). The residual pedestal $|h|(\text{ends})/|h|(\text{peak})$ is $0.02$--$0.07$ for the collapsing throat and the vacuum twin but $0.31$--$0.42$ for the head-on, spiral and fly-by, whose low-frequency strain is therefore an upper bound, not a measurement; each template is filtered only from its own corner. The end-to-end test that can fail is the vacuum twin: through this pipeline it matches \texttt{IMRPhenomD} at $0.902$--$0.938$ over $60$--$300\,M_\odot$ and recovers its total mass to $0$--$5\%$, so every fitting factor below is read against that $0.90$--$0.94$ ceiling, never against unity.

\subsection{Fitting factor against the binary-black-hole bank}
\label{sec:ligo:match}

Against a quasi-circular \texttt{IMRPhenomD} bank spanning $20$--$500\,M_\odot$ in total mass, mass ratios $1$--$4$ and aligned effective spins $\pm0.9$, at Advanced LIGO design sensitivity, the channels return fitting factors of $0.49$--$0.82$: collapsing throat $0.54$--$0.79$, head-on $0.49$--$0.75$, spiral $0.54$--$0.80$, fly-by $0.76$--$0.82$. The control forbids reading that as exoticism: the vacuum twin scores $0.52$--$0.74$ on the same bank, inside the wormhole range---the full-bank fitting factor measures the record's length, not its source, and the recovered masses agree ($0.11$--$1.84$ of the true value; the control's own $0.11$--$0.53$). No claim that a modelled search would miss these sources follows, and none can: the shortness is the physics (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), the transient is the whole wormhole-phase signal, and what a modelled search meets at late times is the chirp of whatever the throats convert into.

What the records do settle is morphology at fixed duration. Cutting each bank template to the signal's own window removes the duration deficit and leaves the shape: the channels return $0.82$--$0.98$ against the control's $0.94$--$0.95$---at this duration, burst morphology alone does not separate a drainhole channel from a binary black hole. The discriminator that does is the frequency track, Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}.

\subsection{A matched-filter search of O3b open data}
\label{sec:ligo:search}

The rescaled waveforms were also run as an actual search of public Advanced LIGO strain~\cite{gwosc}: five channels times a mass ladder with neighbouring rungs matching at $0.97$ against the live noise curve---$127$ templates, total mass the only bank dimension---through the standard pipeline~\cite{allen2012,pycbc} (matched filter, Allen's $\chi^2$ bin test, re-weighted SNR, Hanford--Livingston coincidence, background from $5000$ time slides). Sensitivity is measured: all $15$ injections into conditioned O3b strain at optimal SNR $20$ are recovered at $93$--$139\%$ of optimal (median $103\%$), within $3$ ms, with $\chi^2_r$ near unity; the one weakness is the bank's shortest template ($5$ ms, four $\chi^2$ bins), where the veto demotes SNR $27.8$ to $14.2$---a sensitivity cost at the lightest fly-by rungs, not false triggers.

Searching $2.26$ h of coincident CBC\_CAT3 science time yields \emph{no candidate at zero lag}. The background---$13\,920$ accidental coincidences over $471$ days of slid livetime, loudest $60.4$---caps the smallest reportable false-alarm rate at one per $471$ days; its loudest event is an SNR $758$ Hanford glitch that the veto re-weights to $60.4$, raising the bar rather than entering the foreground.

The useful output of a null result is reach. At single-detector SNR $8$ and optimal orientation, the median horizon over each channel's mass ladder is $4.8$ Gpc (spiral), $2.5$ (fly-by), $1.8$ (head-on), $1.3$ (vacuum twin) and $0.15$ (collapsing throat); divide by the standard sky-and-orientation factor $2.26$ for the all-sky average, and read the three pedestal-limited channels as upper bounds. The ordering is not the energies' ordering: reach is set by where a channel's power lands in the noise curve, and the fly-by's low-frequency content is the most nearly all pedestal (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}). What survives is the statement the null result actually supports: the horizonless encounters are the ones a detector reaches furthest for, both beyond the vacuum control.

\subsection{Ringdown discriminators}
\label{sec:ligo:ringdown}

Where the burst's shape does not separate these channels, the way its frequency \emph{moves} does. The control fixes the reference: the vacuum twin peaks at the textbook $fM=0.065$ and climbs the point-mass track before peeling off toward the equal-mass Kerr mode at $fM=0.0885$---monotone rise, then a fixed ringdown frequency. No drainhole channel does it (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(c)). The head-on's frequency \emph{falls}, $486\to342$ Hz, against its axisymmetric $(2,0,0)$ Kerr reference, where a remnant's would sit fixed---and mass loss cannot rescue the fit: over the same window the remnant's Misner--Sharp mass also falls, and a shrinking black hole must ring \emph{faster}, by $\times1.38$ here; the measurement is $\times0.70$. The frequency drifts the wrong way by a factor of two against its own mass track, which no choice of remnant parameters can absorb. The fly-by falls further and faster, $227\to115$ Hz, as the pair separates---expected, with no remnant to ring. The collapsing throat rings at a single $335$ Hz ($fM=0.050$), $21\%$ short of the Schwarzschild fundamental for its $M_{\rm MS}=1.56$---fast for its mass, at $0.55$ of the equal-mass Kerr mode.

The practical statement is narrow and defensible: a ringdown-only analysis fitting a damped sinusoid against the Kerr $(M_f,a_f)$ track would reject every one of these channels, and a catalogue event whose post-merger frequency holds within a few per cent at the Kerr value excludes the head-on channel at that mass. Two limits travel with this. The spiral's late record is transported by a frozen core from $t=57$ and climbs $151\to679$ Hz without settling, so no spiral ringdown is measured or quoted. And the head-on's bell is followed over only four swings, so its decay constant is bounded, not fitted.

\subsection{Burst searches and the stochastic background}
\label{sec:ligo:burst}

An unmodelled search is the right instrument for these signals: a burst pipeline asks only for coherent excess power in a time--frequency region, so the missing inspiral costs it nothing, and the channels are short and loud in exactly its regime---$6$--$125$ ms across the bank, the energy concentrated within a factor of three in frequency.

The clean, detector-independent deliverable promised in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} is the energy per event in throat-mass units, measured in the dominant multipole with the sphere spread as its error bar: fly-by $7.4\times10^{-2}\,M$ ($43\%$), spiral $2.2\times10^{-2}\,M$ ($21\%$), head-on $3.3\times10^{-3}\,M$ ($13\%$), collapsing throat $3.2\times10^{-5}\,M$ ($18\%$), against the vacuum control's $2.6\times10^{-3}\,M$ ($5\%$). Since $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ from a population scales as event density times energy per event, these numbers convert any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$---the observing-run limits, or the nucleosynthesis cap that holds outside every detector band~\cite{lvk2021,pagano2016}---into a bound on the comoving abundance of primordial drainhole encounters. Three caveats travel with it: the ghost scalar must exist at that epoch; the fly-by's spread is the campaign's widest because its open mouths' expansion is near-zone content; and these are gravitational energies only---the scalar channel carries $2.3$ times as much again in the opposite sign, so an $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ bound constrains the population through the smaller of a merger's two channels (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The four fates promised in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} are all realised, each with a measured selection rule: the relative scalar sign separates scattering from merger (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}), the seed's sign separates collapse from inflation (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}), and the orbital momentum separates fusing, capture and escape at $50$--$70\%$ of circular (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}). Three measurements assemble into one detection philosophy: no inspiral exists (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), the wormhole phase is a burst whose frequency falls or drifts where a Kerr remnant's would hold (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}), and the scalar counterpart dies at horizon formation (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship})---so a surviving wormhole signature is a drifting-frequency burst with a scalar counterpart, and anything with a settled ringdown and no counterpart is already the descendant.

\subsection{No wormhole inspiral}
\label{sec:discussion:noinspiral}

A drainhole binary cannot radiate an inspiral, and the obstruction is a logarithm. Three independent measurements fix the e-fold time of the mouths' unstable mode at $\tau\simeq4.4$ code units: an exponential fit to the fly-by's mouth growth (back-extrapolated seed $7\times10^{-4}$, the superposition defect) gives $4.39$; the eight-unit shift in departure time between refinement levels 3 and 4, divided by $\ln16$, the fourth-order truncation ratio, gives ${\sim}2.9$; and the $11$--$12$-unit delay per decade of declared kick (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}) gives ${\sim}5.0$. In proper time at the minimal surface, where the static lapse is $\alpha_{\rm th}=e^{u(r_t)}=0.575$, that is $1.7$--$2.9$, i.e.\ $T=\tau\alpha_{\rm th}/R_\star=0.43$--$0.74$. These are the \emph{driven} throats of a binary, running faster than the isolated mode of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability} ($T=0.87$) rather than bracketing it (the Ref.~\cite{gonzalez2009} comparison lives there).

The budget follows. A quasi-circular pair at separation $d$ needs $t_{\rm insp}=\tfrac{5}{256}(d/M)^4\,M/\eta$ to merge by radiation reaction ($M$ the total mass, $\eta=1/4$): $202$ units from the innermost stable circular orbit $d/M=6$ and $640$ units from $d/M=8$---$1.1$ and $2.25$ orbits, counting orbits at the initial separation ($T_{\rm orb}=2\pi\sqrt{d^3/M}$), which understates the cycles actually completed and so states the budget conservatively. The instability is thus handed $t_{\rm insp}/\tau=46$ e-folds at the ISCO and $146$ for a two-orbit inspiral, so the mouths arrive at merger unchanged only if the initial perturbation is below $e^{-46}\simeq10^{-20}$, respectively $10^{-63}$. Turned around, the lifetime is $\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)$: seeds of $10^{-6}$, $10^{-10}$, $10^{-15}$ buy $0.33$, $0.55$, $0.82$ of one orbit---not one, for any nameable seed. The asymmetry is the whole argument: the orbit's demand grows as $(d/M)^4$ while the throat's endurance grows as $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, so no finer grid, quieter data or better solver closes the gap---constraint-solved initial data, cutting the seed from the superposition defect to the truncation floor, is worth $\tau\ln(2\times10^{3})\simeq33$ units, a fifth of one orbit. A companion only sharpens the verdict: the measured tidal shortening $5.9\to4.4\to3.7$ (Secs.~\ref{sec:spiral:capture} and~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}) tightens the clock the budget is spent against by $25$--$37\%$, and the near-equality of the merging and missing arms' $\tau$ is what makes it the throat's number rather than one encounter's.

Two corrections to the budget's own machinery both point the same way, and neither is small. The inspiral time above is the vacuum, quadrupole, quasi-circular result, and this binary satisfies none of its premises. Its constituents carry opposite scalar charges, so their leading radiative moment is a \emph{dipole}, not a quadrupole, and the ratio of scalar to gravitational emission measured here is $2.3$ on the same sphere (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar})---so the dominant channel is one the formula does not contain. And that channel's energy is negative, so it pumps the orbit rather than draining it. Under the ordinary sign, a radiation reaction a factor of a few stronger than assumed only shortens $t_{\rm insp}$ and hands the instability fewer e-folds while it still needs $\ln(1/\varepsilon)\gtrsim46$: the verdict is unchanged and reached sooner. Under the sign this matter actually has, the orbit is driven outward by its own emission and a quasi-circular decay does not exist to be computed. A circularised wormhole inspiral therefore fails twice over---once on the throat's clock and once on the orbit's---and the arithmetic above, which uses the vacuum formula, is the conservative of the two arguments rather than the fragile one.

The wormhole-specific signal is therefore a short transient: within a few tens of $M$ each mouth has collapsed or inflated (Secs.~\ref{sec:single:branching} and~\ref{sec:spiral:capture}), and the plunge, burst and conversion of Secs.~\ref{sec:headon}--\ref{sec:gw} are the whole of what this source class emits \emph{as wormholes}. Three qualifications are mandatory. The statement covers the matter model the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach theorem covers, a minimally coupled ghost scalar; throats held open by rotation, additional fields, thin shells or higher-curvature terms are untouched by it. The system converts rather than falls silent, so ``no inspiral'' means no \emph{wormhole} inspiral: any long chirp belongs to the objects the throats become. And the seeds measured here are numerical---superposition defect and truncation noise---which the argument tolerates for two measured reasons, not one assumed one: its dependence on the seed is logarithmic, and the defect's excitation of the mode is measured rather than modelled. Whatever the superposed data's junk transient does nonlinearly, its net effect on the radial mode is the back-extrapolated seed of $7\times10^{-4}$ above, which sits just below the declared-kick decade $10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}$ over which the response is measured linear (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}) and two decades under the $\varepsilon=0.1$ ceiling where it is not. No physical environment supplies a perturbation below $10^{-20}$.

\subsection{Wormhole collapse as a heavy-seed channel}
\label{sec:discussion:seeds}
% Arithmetic on measured numbers only (Table II, Sec. IX B energies, fM peaks
% of Sec. X C); the abundance n(M) is stipulated, and item 10 of XI C says so.

The collapse branch acts as a seeding mechanism, and Table~\ref{tab:scales} supplies its timescales. A throat of mass $M$ converts to a black hole in $54\,\mathrm{s}$ to $5$ minutes at $10^{6}\,M_\odot$ (seconds at $10^{4}$--$10^{5}$), and the remnant retains a mass of order the constituent's own: the head-on remnant keeps $M_{\rm MS}=2.2$ of a pair born at $3.0$, the lone collapse $1.24$ of $1.90$ at horizon formation before regaining a tenth (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}), and the orbital merger---the channel's one unclosed conversion until its horizon was found---carries $M_{\rm MS}=2.41$ newly formed and $2.08$ settled (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}). The seed therefore exists at essentially full mass at birth, and it is born clean: the scalar hair leaves in a single dipole pulse on the remnant's own mass-settling clock, with e-fold time ${\sim}20\,M$ from horizon formation (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), so the remnants accrete as ordinary black holes rather than hairy ones. From $10^{4}$--$10^{5}\,M_\odot$ at $z\simeq20$, growth at the plain Eddington rate (Salpeter e-fold ${\simeq}45$ Myr) reaches $10^{9}\,M_\odot$ in $9$--$12$ e-folds, $420$--$550$ Myr, inside the ${\sim}600$ Myr available to $z\simeq7$: no super-Eddington episode is required, which is the arithmetic that defines a heavy seed~\cite{inayoshi2020} (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(a)). This reproduces the population of overmassive black holes and little red dots observed by JWST~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024} without requiring an atomic-cooling halo, a critical Lyman--Werner flux, or any baryonic environment at all.

Halo independence is itself a signature. The seeds predate and ignore baryonic structure, so $M_{\rm BH}/M_*$ starts effectively infinite and relaxes \emph{downward}---the overmassive morphology of UHZ1 is the generic expectation of this channel rather than a tuning. The abundance asked of it is small: one seed per present-day massive galaxy is $n\simeq10^{-4}$--$10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ comoving, a dark-matter fraction $f=nM/\rho_{\rm DM}\simeq10^{-10}$--$3\times10^{-7}$ at $10^{4}$--$10^{6}\,M_\odot$. And the channel evades the bound that closes the conventional route: FIRAS $\mu$-distortion excludes primordial black holes of $10^{4}$--$10^{13}\,M_\odot$ formed from Gaussian curvature perturbations at any cosmologically relevant abundance~\cite{nakama2018}, but the black hole here forms by wormhole collapse, not from an enhanced curvature perturbation, the same evasion the bubble and domain-wall channels use~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017}---and Ref.~\cite{deng2017} is this paper's birth mechanism read forward: the walls that make the wormholes make the seeds.

The channel radiates, and that makes it falsifiable (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}). Each conversion emits the burst energies of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst}, so a population contributes $\Omega_{\rm GW}\simeq n\,E_{\rm rad}/[\rho_c(1+z_e)]$ at $f_{\rm obs}=(fM)_{\rm peak}/[M(1+z_e)]$; with the measured $(fM)_{\rm peak}=0.03$--$0.06$ the band is $0.3$--$30$ mHz across $10^{4}$--$10^{6}\,M_\odot$ at $z_e\simeq20$. Taking $E_{\rm rad}/M=2.2\times10^{-2}$ (spiral) to $7.4\times10^{-2}$ (fly-by---and a foam population scatters more often than it merges) at $M=10^{5}\,M_\odot$: $n=10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ gives $\Omega_{\rm GW}\sim10^{-11}$ at ${\sim}3$ mHz, $n=10^{-4}$ gives ${\sim}10^{-13}$. LISA's stochastic reach at millihertz is $\Omega\sim10^{-13}$--$10^{-12}$~\cite{lisa2017,caprini2018}, so the prediction band straddles the instrument: if the earliest supermassive black holes are drainhole relics at the one-per-galaxy level, LISA sees or excludes the conversion background. The spectral shape is distinctive twice over---a pure burst population with no $f^{2/3}$ inspiral ramp, because Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} forbids inspirals, and a scalar counterpart invisible to the strain channel---and no direct-collapse scenario radiates anything analogous, because its seed forms by hydrodynamics. The test also carries a blind spot: conversions at $10^{7}$--$10^{8}\,M_\odot$ land at $3$--$30\,\mu$Hz, below the LISA band, so that mass window is unconstrained by this instrument.

The same table carries a corollary for the present-day sky. Nothing in the mass range survives (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime}), so every observed supermassive black hole is at most a \emph{descendant} of this channel, never the throat itself: any fit of a static Ellis--Bronnikov metric to the Sgr A$^*$ or M87$^*$ shadows is excluded in-model, the fitted object dead within a day of its birth. The claim made here is deliberately bounded: the collapse branch is a heavy-seed channel with three measured properties---prompt full-mass conversion, halo independence, a $\mu$-distortion-free abundance---and one testable background; whether it is \emph{the} channel behind the JWST population is conditional on the stipulated birth statistics, and item~10 of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open} lists everything that conditionality contains.

\subsection{What is not established}
\label{sec:discussion:open}

Collected here so that no claim above has to carry its own retraction.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The spiral's horizon rests on one instrument and seven slices.} The common MOTS of Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral} is measured at $t=55$--$59$ (birth through $0.9$ before the NaN) and $98$--$100$ (settled, existence witnesses closed), all by the flow finder---validated on three geometries, but nowhere corroborated by a second instrument, the star-shaped scan being blind here by measurement---at level-3 sampling of level-5 data; the $t=59\to98$ stretch has no surviving slices, and the birth itself is unmeasured: at $t=55.0$, the earliest slice, the horizon already exists ($\ell_{\max}=8$; $\theta_-\simeq-0.05$, newly formed), so formation lies on records the walls destroyed. Why a censored wall still defeats the $16\times$ refinement ladder is unexplained (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}), and the slicing reading of that failure is itself conditional: every arm in the matrix shares the moving-puncture gauge family, the gauge arms run since---the $1{+}\log$ coefficient halved, the harmonic-class driver, the shift damping quadrupled---move the wall by units or push the whole collapse past the record's end while removing nothing, the harmonic driver killed the head-on control early and horizonless by both instruments (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}), and no curvature invariant is recorded at the failure point; a shock-avoiding or maximal slicing at the wall is the untested experiment.
\item \textbf{No spiral ringdown is measured.} The late record is transported by a core frozen from $t=57$, so its swings are propagation, not a remnant ringing; the instantaneous frequency climbs $151\to679$ Hz without settling (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}).
\item \textbf{The converted pair's own signal is not simulated.} After the wormhole transient the constituents are black holes or inflating runaways (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}); the chirp of such a converted binary---what a modelled search would meet at late times---is outside these records, which bound only the wormhole-phase morphology (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:match}).
\item \textbf{The merging spiral's mouths are measured only to $t=50$ and only at level 3.} The instrumented re-run (Fig.~\ref{fig:mouth_growth}) gives the mouth sizes through the plunge, $+12.2\%$ by $t=28$, but its per-mouth reading ends where the two scan spheres meet, and the production record itself still carries no areal-radius stream: how far the throats had gone at the level-5 death is inferred from the clock, not read off.
\item \textbf{The low-frequency strain of three channels is an upper bound.} Head-on, spiral and fly-by reconstruct with $31$--$42\%$ of their peak strain still present at the record's ends, so their sub-corner content is integration pedestal rather than measurement (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo}); the collapsing throat and the vacuum control, at $2$--$7\%$, do not have this problem.
\item \textbf{The late constraint growth on both single-throat branches from $t\approx75$ is unexplained}, as is the grid-scale wobble that appears on the outermost head-on sphere after $t\approx80$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}).
\item \textbf{The energy balance is not closed.} Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar} establishes that the scalar channel is dipole-dominated, of order twice the gravitational energy and negative---but not that $\dot M_{\rm ADM}=-F_{\rm GW}-F_\phi$ holds, because no surface-integral ADM mass was recorded on any arm. The scalar flux itself is a coordinate flux---its multiplicative gauge factors are bounded at the percent level by the measured lapse and conformal factor on the sphere, but its advective shift term is unmeasured---quoted with a $30\%$ sphere spread, and one of the five channels---the single throat---still has no scalar measurement at all: its arms predate the stream and their plotfiles are not retained, so closing it requires a re-run, not a re-read. The head-on was closed that way (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), and only its post-horizon window is usable: before the horizon the flux at the inner sphere runs ingoing, the two open mouths' static hair rather than radiation. For a system whose novelty is negative-energy matter this is the largest single gap in the campaign; closing it needs the proper geometric flux---$\alpha$, $\beta^i$ and the induced metric on the spheres---beside a surface-integral mass, none of which the dense streams carry.
\item \textbf{Most horizon statements are still star-shaped.} A deformable finder now exists and overturned the one absence claim it was pointed at---the spiral's (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral})---which measures exactly how much weight a star-shaped absence can bear. Every other horizon statement---the head-on's formation and ``born common or not at all'' (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:contact}), the fly-by's and the single throat's absences, every quoted mass but the spiral remnant's---remains a statement about coordinate spheres; the head-on remnant is the one place both instruments have run, agreeing to $2\%$. Re-hunting the remaining records shape-free is mechanical and has not been done.
\item \textbf{The comparison with Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach is a rate comparison, not a parameter-matched one.} The map from this paper's $(a,m)$ drainhole to that work's perturbation parameters has not been verified term by term, so the agreement quoted in Sec.~\ref{sec:single:instability} is ${\sim}15\%$ against the published growth times and no finer statement is supported.
\item \textbf{The heavy-seed channel of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} is conditional on stipulated birth statistics.} The birth mass function, epoch and abundance are not derived---birth is quantum, and $n(M)$ is an input---so ``seeds the earliest supermassive black holes'' is conditional on it. The collapse \emph{fraction} of a birth population is not computed: the $\pm$ selection rules are measured (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}) but the birth distribution over them is not, and ambient compression at a radiation-dominated epoch could bias the branch---unmeasured; a throat evolved in a compressive background is the one-arm experiment that would turn the conditional channel into a predicted fraction. The ghost scalar must exist at that epoch. Seed spin is unmeasured (this campaign is non-spinning) where supermassive black hole spins are observable. And each conversion deposits $2.3$ times its gravitational energy as \emph{negative} scalar energy into the early universe (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}); the cosmological back-reaction of that deposit is open (item~7) and must be weighed wherever the seeding claim is used.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every evolution in Table~\ref{tab:matrix} is one line in a registry shipped with the results pack---its single knob against a named reference, its outcome, its caveats---and every figure in this paper is regenerated by a script in the same pack from the archived diagnostic streams. The evolutions are bit-reproducible on the production hardware: two builds differing only in a passive diagnostic reproduce $2384$ shared output rows byte for byte (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}). The code is \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}; the drainhole initial data, the seeds of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}, the orientation-corrected surface scan and the parameter file of every run accompany the registry.

\section{Computational cost}
\label{sec:cost}

Every evolution ran on one NVIDIA H100 80\,GB device of a four-H100 node with two 32-core Xeon Platinum 8462Y+ processors---up to four arms concurrently while the cards were free---with \texttt{GRTeclyn} built CUDA-native against AMReX~26.02. At $\Delta t=0.02\,\Delta x$ a production arm ($L=64$, $128^3$ base, three refinement levels) advances $12$--$18$ code units per hour, so a $t=100$ evolution costs $6$--$8$ h. Depth is the expense: the level-5 arms run at $2$--$4$ units per hour---$28.2$ h for the head-on's seamless $t=0\to100$ (the level-3$\to$5 chain it replaced cost $18.6$ h for its $t=22\to100$ leg alone), $46.7$ h for the fly-by's full $t=0\to100$ on the doubled box, which peaks at $58$ of the $80$ GB with $26$-GB checkpoints against a rolling keep-list---and the production spiral's three-stage chain (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}) costs $23.5$ h end to end. The $121$ evolutions this paper draws on total $708$ GPU-hours, recomputed from each run's own logged speed and time span by a script shipped with the results pack; the archived shakedown stage adds $7$ h more, for $716$ h of packed evolution in all. Earlier drafts quoted a higher figure, which charged the campaign twice for ten runs the prune had left a second copy of; the script now drops copies and dead launches by name rather than counting directories.
