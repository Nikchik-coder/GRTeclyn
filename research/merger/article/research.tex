\begin{abstract}
We evolve pairs of massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainholes---traversable wormholes supported by a phantom scalar field---in 3+1 numerical relativity, at equal masses and one throat compactness, without spin. An isolated throat is an unstable fixed point: the sign of its perturbation selects collapse or inflation, with an e-fold time within $\clmGgsAgreementFour\%$, at our finest resolution, of the parameter-matched linear rate of Gonz\'alez, Guzm\'an and Sarbach. Like-signed scalar charges repel, so only opposite-signed pairs merge. The head-on merger forms a common horizon around both still-open throats---never one per throat---whose areal radius then shrinks by \clmHeadonShrink{} as it swallows the phantom field; the remnant rings down without a chirp. The spiral merger ends in a remnant of the same size: a shape-free finder recovers its common apparent horizon, $\clmSpiralFlowDeformation\%$ deformed, \clmSpiralFlowLeadWord{} time units before a curvature wall that survives a $\clmLadderFactor\times$ refinement ladder and every gauge tried. For throats held open by a minimally coupled ghost scalar without spin, as here, no wormhole inspiral is possible: outliving even the vacuum inspiral time from the innermost stable circular orbit requires an initial perturbation below $\clmDetSeedIsco$, so every encounter is a plunge. The most energetic gravitational-wave source is the horizonless fly-by, which radiates $(\clmGwEnergyFlybyLoAbs$--$\clmGwEnergyFlybyHiAbs)\times10^{-2}\,M$ in the dominant multipole---$\clmGwEnergyRatioFlybyLo$--$\clmGwEnergyRatioFlybyHi$ times the vacuum merger control and $\clmGwVacPassRatioLo$--$\clmGwVacPassRatioHi$ times a vacuum pass at its own momentum. The scalar field radiates comparable energy as a negative-energy $\ell=1$ dipole, which decays once a horizon forms. Rescaled to detector masses, the bursts match binary-black-hole templates at fixed duration (fitting factors $\clmDetFFWindowLo$--$\clmDetFFWindowHi$), but their frequency falls or drifts where a Kerr remnant's would hold; a $\clmDetBankSize$-template matched-filter search of $\clmDetLivetime$ h of Advanced LIGO O3b data finds no candidate. Collapse converts a throat of any astrophysical mass into a black hole of comparable mass within days. A primordial wormhole population would therefore supply halo-independent heavy seeds for the earliest supermassive black holes, evading the $\mu$-distortion bound on supermassive primordial black holes, together with a diffuse negative-energy scalar background, which dilutes like radiation and so cannot be the dark energy, and, for conversions at $z\approx\clmDetZConv$, millihertz bursts loud enough for LISA to detect one by one---each merger channel above a signal-to-noise ratio of 8 from $\clmDetDetectMassLo$ to $\clmDetDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$---at a rate set by the birth abundance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Wheeler's spacetime foam is a sea of handles---wormholes that open and close on the Planck scale~\cite{wheeler1955,wheeler1957,misner_wheeler1957}. Several mechanisms promote them to macroscopic size: inflation stretches a Planck-scale Lorentzian wormhole with the background~\cite{roman1993}; bubble and domain-wall cosmologies produce post-inflationary regions joined through wormholes~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017}; and a gas of relic wormholes has been studied as a cosmology in its own right~\cite{kirillov2008,kirillov2011}. None of this work evolves the aftermath: what a macroscopic relic wormhole does, and what two of them do to each other. That is the subject of this paper.

Two classical facts govern the dynamics. First, the simplest evolvable traversable wormhole---the Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole held open by a ghost scalar~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973,morris_thorne1988,visser1995}---is unstable~\cite{gonzalez2009,bronnikov2011}: depending on the sign of the perturbation, the throat collapses to a black hole or inflates, on a timescale set by the throat radius~\cite{shinkai2002,gonzalez2009}, as 3+1 evolutions of the massless throat confirm nonlinearly~\cite{shirokov2026}. Second, Geroch's theorem forbids classical topology change~\cite{geroch1967,tipler1977}: two wormholes cannot fuse into one, and none can be created classically. Creation is quantum~\cite{hawking1988}, so our evolutions start at $t=0$ from the newborn state---the stipulated initial data of every numerical binary, here with a physical origin attached.

Together these facts set selection rules. No astrophysical assembly channel exists: a throat decays faster than dynamical friction or radiation reaction could pair it. A primordial pair born from foam needs no assembly---birth and collision coincide, at a separation comparable to the objects---and its evolution is a race between orbital collision and throat decay, both rates fixed by the classical solution. The scalar adds a second rule: a phantom field repels like charges and attracts opposite ones, so like-signed pairs scatter while opposite-signed pairs fall together; and the sign of a lone throat's perturbation selects collapse---a wormhole-to-primordial-black-hole channel beside the standard ones~\cite{zeldovich1967,carr_hawking1974,carr2021}---or inflation. A foam population thus splits into four fates---merger, scattering, collapse, inflation---across birth separation, perturbation and relative sign, the space mapped here. The binary evolved is the exterior approximation of a pair of handles (three asymptotic ends, the far sides passive), and what merges is not two wormholes into one, which Geroch's theorem forbids, but two wormholes into one black hole containing both throats.

The observable is not a chirp but a population of bursts from the merger epoch, redshifted into whatever band the mass scale selects~\cite{caprini2018,nanograv2023,lisa2017,lvk2021}. The epoch-independent deliverable is the energy radiated per event and its spectrum in throat-mass units, which converts any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$---including the nucleosynthesis cap that holds outside every detector band~\cite{pagano2016}---into a bound on the abundance of primordial wormhole encounters. The collapse branch also bears on JWST's overmassive black holes at $z\gtrsim4$--$6$, which favour heavy seeds of $\clmDetIntroSeedLo$--$\clmDetIntroSeedHi\,M_\odot$ from formation channels that remain contested~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024,inayoshi2020}; Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} develops it into a millihertz prediction.

Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:setup} give the model and numerics. Sections~\ref{sec:single}--\ref{sec:spiral} follow the campaign from one throat to two: the lone wormhole, two throats at rest, and the head-on and spiral mergers. Section~\ref{sec:gw} treats the radiation of every channel, and Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} confronts the rescaled waveforms with LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA data. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} draws the consequences and states the scope; Sec.~\ref{sec:repro} gives reproducibility and cost. All evolutions use \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}, the AMReX-based~\cite{amrex} GPU port of \texttt{GRChombo}~\cite{grchombo}, continuing Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026b}. We use $G=c=1$.

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Field equations}
\label{sec:model:field}

The matter is a single real, massless phantom scalar: gravity couples to \emph{minus} the canonical stress tensor,
\begin{equation}
T_{ab} = -\Bigl(\nabla_a\phi\,\nabla_b\phi - \tfrac12 g_{ab}\,\nabla_c\phi\nabla^c\phi\Bigr),
\qquad V(\phi)\equiv 0,
\label{eq:phantom}
\end{equation}
so $\rho<0$ where the gradient dominates---the exotic stress that holds a throat open. The field itself obeys the canonical wave equation $\Box\phi=0$, evolved in first-order form with $\Pi=(\partial_t\phi-\beta^i\partial_i\phi)/\alpha$.

\subsection{Drainhole initial data}
\label{sec:model:drainhole}

Each body is a massive Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973}. In isotropic radius $r$ about the throat centre, with
\begin{equation}
X = \frac{r - a^2/4r}{a},\quad
\Omega = 1 + \frac{a^2}{4r^2},\quad
u = \frac{m}{a}\Bigl(\arctan X - \frac{\pi}{2}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
the exact static solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom} is
\begin{equation}
\alpha = e^{u},\quad
\gamma_{ij} = e^{-2u}\,\Omega^2\,\delta_{ij},\quad
\phi = C\arctan X,
\label{eq:drainhole}
\end{equation}
with $4\pi a^2C^2 = a^2+m^2$, $K_{ij}=0$, $\Pi=0$ and zero shift; in the evolved conformal variables $\chi = e^{2u}\Omega^{-2}$ and $h_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$. Both constraints hold identically at $t=0$. Here $a$ is the throat scale and $m$ the ADM mass, carried by the lapse rather than the conformal factor, so $\chi$ at the throat stays in $[\clmChiThroatLo,\clmChiThroatHi]$ for $m/a\le1$ and mass costs no resolution. The minimal surface sits at $r_t=\tfrac12\bigl(m+\sqrt{m^2+a^2}\bigr)$, where $X=m/a$, so its areal radius is analytic: $R_{\min}=e^{-u(r_t)}\sqrt{a^2+m^2}=\sqrt{a^2+m^2}\,\exp\!\bigl[(m/a)\arctan(a/m)\bigr]$, which is $a$ at $m=0$. The production throat $a=\clmThroatA$, $m=\clmThroatM$ has $R_{\min}=\sqrt5\,e^{\frac12\arctan2}=\clmRstar$ at $r_t=\tfrac12(1+\sqrt5)=\clmThroatCoordRadius$. The point $r\to0$ is the far side's spatial infinity, compactified ($\chi\sim r^4$). At $m=0$ the throat is ultrastatic ($\alpha\equiv1$, $M_{\rm ADM}=0$) and two such throats do not attract.

\subsection{The binary superposition}
\label{sec:model:binary}

Two throats at separation $d$ (one-body radii $r_A$, $r_B$) are superposed in excess form,
\begin{equation}
u = u_A + u_B,\qquad
\psi = 1 + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_A}-1\bigr) + \bigl(\sqrt{\Omega_B}-1\bigr),
\end{equation}
with $\chi = e^{2u}\psi^{-4}$, $\alpha = e^{u}$ and $\phi = \phi_A + \sigma\,\phi_B$, $\sigma=\pm1$; the constant tail of the $\arctan$ profiles is subtracted so that $\phi\to0$ at the boundary. Momentum enters through one Bowen--York term~\cite{bowen_york1980} per throat,
\begin{equation}
\hat A_{ij} = \frac{3}{2r^2}\bigl[P_i n_j + P_j n_i - (\delta_{ij}-n_i n_j)\,P{\cdot}n\bigr],
\end{equation}
converted by $\tilde A_{ij} = \chi^{3/2}\hat A_{ij}$, with $K=0$. The throats sit at $x=\mp d/2$ with $P^i=(0,\pm p,0)$: momenta of magnitude $p$ in units of the one-body mass, tangential to the separation and opposite on the two throats, so the pair carries no net momentum and orbital angular momentum $J=pd$; $p=0$ is a release from rest. Since $\Pi=0$ the matter momentum density vanishes and $\hat A_{ij}$ is flat-space divergence free, so the momentum constraint holds \emph{exactly} at $t=0$; only the Hamiltonian constraint carries the superposition defect, $O(a^2/d^2)+O(m/d)$. The relative sign $\sigma$ is the pair's one discrete parameter. A phantom mediator \emph{repels} like scalar charges and \emph{attracts} opposite ones, with $Q\equiv|F_\phi/F_{\rm grav}| = (a^2+m^2)/m^2>1$ always ($Q=\clmScalarQ$ for the production throat), so like-signed pairs never merge under their own gravity and $\sigma=\clmMergerSign$ is the merger channel. Two alternatives were tested and not adopted: a windowed one-body correction of the Helfer type~\cite{helfer2022}, which lowers the $t=0$ defect but moves it into the fade zone between the throats, where it stalls the orbit instead of letting it plunge; and constraint-solved data from \texttt{GRTresna}, whose puncture decomposition cannot represent a throat. Every binary is therefore superposed, its defect declared rather than removed.

\subsection{Declared seeds}
\label{sec:model:seeds}

The instability is probed with declared perturbations of the conformal factor only: a Gaussian shell on the minimal surface,
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon\, g(r)\bigr],\qquad
g = e^{-(r-r_t)^2/w^2},\quad w = a/4,
\label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
which changes the throat's areal radius by a fraction ${\approx}\clmSeedArealFactor\varepsilon$, and a quadrupolar factor
\begin{equation}
\psi \to \psi\bigl[1 + \varepsilon_2\, g(r)\,P_2(\cos\theta)\bigr]
\end{equation}
about the $z$ axis through the throat. Neither seed is constraint-solved: $\psi$ changes while $K_{ij}$ and $\Pi$ stay untouched, so the momentum constraint still holds at $t=0$, but the Hamiltonian constraint acquires a defect in proportion to the seed amplitude (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}). The spherical seed selects the branch; the quadrupolar seed has no $\ell=0$ part and gives a collapsing throat a quadrupole to shed.

\subsection{Evolution scheme}
\label{sec:setup:scheme}

We evolve CCZ4~\cite{alic2012} with damping $(\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3)=(\clmCczKappaOne,0,\clmCczKappaThree)$, $1{+}\log$ slicing~\cite{bona1995} started from the exact static lapse of Eq.~\eqref{eq:drainhole}, a Gamma-driver shift~\cite{campanelli2006,baker2006} with $\eta=\clmShiftEta$ started from zero, fourth-order stencils, RK4 and Kreiss--Oliger dissipation $\sigma=\clmKOsigma$. The time step is $\Delta t=\clmTimeStepFactor\,\Delta x$, an order of magnitude below the usual CCZ4 Courant factor, because larger steps fail: a multiplier of $\clmControlDtMedium$ departs from the production run at $t\approx\clmControlDtMediumDeparture$, and $\clmControlDtLarge$ dies at $t=\clmControlDtLargeDeath$.

\subsection{Grid, boundaries and regularisation}
\label{sec:setup:grid}

The domain is a cube of side $L=\clmBoxL$ centred on the system, with a $\clmBaseN^3$ base grid ($\Delta x=\clmDxBase$). Nested boxes, halving per level and centred on the tracked throats (half-width $\clmFinestHalfWidth$ per throat at the production finest level), are re-drawn every $\clmRegridSteps$ steps. Production runs use \clmProdLevels{} refinement levels (finest $\Delta x=\clmFinestDx$); resolution arms go to levels $\clmResArmsLevelLo$--$\clmResArmsLevelHi$ where stated, and the production spiral, the level-5 fly-by and two lone-throat arms use $L=\clmBigBoxL$: the same cells in a box twice as wide. Outer boundaries are Sommerfeld, backed by a spherical sponge about the box centre over the outer quarter of the half-width---$r=\clmSpongeInner$--$\clmSpongeOuter$ at $L=\clmBoxL$ and $\clmSpongeInnerBig$--$\clmSpongeOuterBig$ at $L=\clmBigBoxL$---in which extra dissipation ramps to $\sigma_{\rm sp}=\clmSpongeStrength$ as the \clmSpongePower{} power of the depth; every extraction sphere lies inside it. The vacuum controls (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls:bbh}) instead run \texttt{GRTeclyn}'s binary-black-hole example with its own numerical settings and no sponge.

Each throat carries a compactified second infinity at its centre, where $\chi\sim r^4$, so the evolved variables are floored at $\chi\ge\clmChiFloor$ and $\alpha\ge\clmLapseFloor$ (a variant floors $\chi$ at $\clmChiFloorVariant$ and applies $\clmChiRhsFloor$ only where $1/\chi$ is evaluated). Two further devices act only inside regions already trapped: exponential damping of the scalar where the lapse has collapsed, and an interior freeze that multiplies every right-hand side by $1-W(r)$, with $W$ a $C^2$ quintic step between radii $r_{\rm full}$ and $r_{\rm start}$. All three are numerical devices, each measured by a twin: $\chi$-floor twins at $\clmChiFloor$ and $\clmChiFloorVariant$ re-converge to \clmControlChiDigits{} digits after touching the floor, the scalar damping moves the spiral's wall by less than a unit (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}), and the freeze leaves the exterior unchanged (Secs.~\ref{sec:headon:wall} and~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}).

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\label{sec:setup:diagnostics}

Every coarse step the code writes the volume-averaged norms $\|\mathcal H\|=(\int\mathcal H^2\,\mathrm{d}V/L^3)^{1/2}$ and $\|\mathcal M\|=(\int\mathcal M_i\mathcal M_i\,\mathrm{d}V/L^3)^{1/2}$, evaluated with fourth-order differences on the base grid over the whole domain---sponge and throat interiors included---after the finer levels have been restricted onto it; they depend on the box and are compared only within one. Both throat centres are tracked, and about each the areal radius $R(r)$ is scanned on coordinate spheres; its minimum is the throat. The spheres run out to $r=\clmFlybyScanEdge$, a fixed setting of the offline instrument: wide enough for the static throat and the collapse horizons, but an inflating throat can pass it, after which the edge reading bounds the throat's areal radius from above. On the same spheres the null expansions $\theta_\pm$ are computed with the outward direction fixed by \emph{increasing areal radius}, so that a dynamic throat is marginally \emph{anti}-trapped~\cite{hochberg_visser1998}. The orientation matters: the in-code monitor fixes the outward direction by increasing \emph{coordinate} radius, which points the wrong way beyond a throat and manufactures trapped verdicts wherever $R(r)$ falls. Every horizon statement here is therefore read offline from archived plotfiles with the areal orientation; the in-code columns serve only as a live monitor.

The star-shaped scan reports the outermost coordinate sphere that is trapped everywhere ($\max\theta_+=0$, $\theta_-<0$). A trapped sphere lies inside the apparent horizon, so this sphere---``the scan's MOTS'' below---bounds the marginally outer trapped surface from inside. We quote its areal radius $R$ and its Misner--Sharp mass~\cite{misner_sharp1964},
\begin{equation}
\frac{2M_{\rm MS}}{R} \;=\; 1 + \frac{R^{2}\,\langle\theta_+\theta_-\rangle}{4},
\label{eq:misner_sharp}
\end{equation}
with the product averaged over the sphere. On an exact MOTS $M_{\rm MS}=R/2$; on the scan's spheres $M_{\rm MS}$ exceeds $R/2$ by at most $\clmShapeSystLone\%$ about a lone collapsed throat, but about a merged remnant by up to $\clmShapeSystForming\%$ while the surface forms ($\clmShapeSystFormingLevelThree\%$ at level 3), falling to $\clmShapeSystRounded\%$ as it rounds. This is the shape systematic of star-shaped scanning, and it also affects whether a surface is found at all. Because the phantom violates the null energy condition, a trapped surface need not lie inside an event horizon; ``horizon'', ``censored'' and ``black hole'' below refer to MOTSs on the evolved slices. A deformable instrument backs the scan: a spectral flow finder expands the trial surface as $r=h(\theta,\phi)$ in real harmonics up to degree $\ell_{\max}=\clmFlowFinderLmax$ ($\clmFlowFinderLmaxAlt$ where stated) and drives its expansion to zero by a damped fast flow, with the same orientation rule. It recovers Kerr--Schild Schwarzschild ($R=2M$ to $\clmFlowFinderSchw\%$ and $M_{\rm MS}=M$ to $\clmFlowFinderSchwMass\%$ from spherical and dented seeds), correctly finds no surface on the time-symmetric Ellis throat ($\theta_-\ge0$ there), and reproduces the star-shaped scan's surface on the head-on remnant.

Radiation is read from $\Psi_4$ on spheres at $R=\clmExtractRadiusTen$, $\clmExtractRadiusFourteen$, $\clmExtractRadiusEighteen$ (and $\clmExtractRadiusTwentyTwo$ on the single-throat wave arms) on the head-on and the single throats, whose $L=\clmBigBoxL$ arms keep these spheres, and at $R=\clmExtractRadiusTwenty$, $\clmExtractRadiusTwentyEight$, $\clmExtractRadiusThirtySix$, $\clmExtractRadiusFortyFour$ on the $L=\clmBigBoxL$ spiral and fly-by, decomposed in spin-weight $-2$ harmonics. \subsection{The static throat as a fixed point}
\label{sec:single:fixedpoint}

Evolved without a declared perturbation, the exact drainhole holds $R_\star=\clmRstar$ to $\clmHoldTolLevelThree\%$ for $\clmHoldTimeLevelThree$ time units at level 3 and to $\clmHoldTolOnePct\%$ until $t\simeq\clmHoldTimeOnePct$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a)); at level 4 it is exact to \clmLevelFourDigits{} digits at $t=30$. The departure moves \emph{later} by $\clmOnsetShiftLo$--$\clmOnsetShiftHi$ units per halving of the cell---a truncation-noise seed of fixed growth rate handed a smaller amplitude. Level 2 dies of an origin failure at $t=\clmLevelTwoDeath$ with the throat still exact to $\clmLevelTwoDev\%$. A lighter throat is less stable: at $m=\clmHalfMassM$ the throat holds $\clmHalfMassHoldTol\%$ only to $t\approx\clmHalfMassHoldTime$ and dies at its origin at $t=\clmHalfMassDeath$.

\subsection{The unstable mode}
\label{sec:single:instability}

The departure is a single exponential mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(a,b)): plateau fits give $\tau=\clmTauLevelThree$ (level 3) and $\clmTauLevelFour$ (level 4), i.e.\ $T\equiv\tau\,\alpha_{\rm th}/R_\star=\clmProperEfoldThree$ and $\clmProperEfoldFour$, where $\alpha_{\rm th}=e^{u(r_t)}=\clmThroatLapse$ is the static lapse at the throat, so that $T$ is the proper-time e-fold in units of the throat's light-crossing time. Our drainhole is the $\gamma_1=m/a=\clmThroatGammaOne$ member of the static family of Gonz\'alez, Guzm\'an and Sarbach (their metric with $b=a$, $\gamma_0=-\pi\gamma_1/2$), for which their linear analysis gives $T=\clmGgsMatched$; their Table~I spans $T=\clmGgsMassless$ at the massless end to $\clmGgsMassive$~\cite{gonzalez2009lin}. Solving the linearised field equations of this solution ourselves---their regularised master equation and, independently, the reduction in the gauge $\delta\phi=0$---gives $T=\clmLinT$, i.e.\ $\tau_{\rm lin}=\clmLinTau\,M$. The measured e-fold approaches it with resolution: it is $\clmGgsAgreementFour\%$ longer at level 4 and $\clmGgsAgreement\%$ longer at level 3. The seed is truncation error, and its sign flips with resolution---level 3 collapses, level 4 inflates---confirming from exact massive data the two branches found for the massless throat~\cite{shirokov2026}.

\subsection{Branching: collapse versus inflation}
\label{sec:single:branching}

A declared kick overrides the truncation seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(c)). At $\varepsilon=\pm10^{-2}$ and $\pm10^{-3}$ the fate is always opposite to the push, each $\pm$ pair crosses at the $\varepsilon$-independent $t_\times\simeq\clmCrossingMean$, and a tenfold smaller kick arrives $\clmMatchedDelayLo$--$\clmMatchedDelayHi$ units later at matched horizon radius---linear response on the same mode. The branching survives refinement: at level 4, whose own seed inflates the unkicked throat, $+10^{-2}$ still forms a horizon at $t=\clmHorizonTimeLevelFour$ and $-10^{-2}$ still inflates, the twins crossing at $t=\clmCrossingLevelFour$ against level 3's $\clmCrossingLevelThree$. The branch point is the throat's, not the grid's or the kick's. The kick also sets the clock: horizons form at $t=\clmHorizonTimeEpsTwo$ ($\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$) and $t=\clmHorizonTimeEpsThree$ ($+10^{-3}$), against $t\le\clmHorizonTimeNoise$ for the unkicked throat (its first scanned slice after departure already holds a MOTS). At $\varepsilon=\pm0.1$ the data are no longer perturbative: both signs collapse and die ($t=\clmCeilingDeathPlus$ and $\clmCeilingDeathMinus$) from a Hamiltonian violation $\clmCeilingHamLo$--$\clmCeilingHamHi\times$ that of the $1\%$ arms (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_throat}(d,e)), so the usable range ends at $10^{-2}$.

\subsection{The collapse endpoint}
\label{sec:single:collapse}

The collapse branch ends in a black hole that consumes its supporting field. On the unkicked level-3 arm the horizon's areal radius falls $\clmUnkickedHorizonRStart\to\clmUnkickedHorizonREnd$ and $M_{\rm MS}$ $\clmUnkickedHorizonMStart\to\clmUnkickedHorizonMEnd$ over $\clmUnkickedHorizonSpan$ units, with the origin lapse at $\clmUnkickedOriginLapse$; the kicked $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ arm runs clean to $t=\clmKickedRunsClean$ behind its horizon. Unlike the massless throat of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026}, which bounced at $t\approx\clmMasslessBounce{}M$ and shed its horizon by $\clmMasslessShed{}M$, no arm here sheds a horizon. The pure-quadrupole arm ($\varepsilon_2=\clmPureQuadAmp$, no radial kick) is fully instrumented (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_collapse}): a MOTS from $t=\clmPureQuadMotsTime$ at $R=\clmPureQuadMotsRStart$, shrinking to $\clmPureQuadMotsREnd$ ($M_{\rm MS}$ $\clmPureQuadMotsMStart\to\clmPureQuadMotsMEnd$), while $|K|$ grows inward and the lapse pinches to zero at the centre.

The shrinking stops. In the three arms kicked with $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$---at level 3, and at level 4 dressed with $\varepsilon_2=\clmDressedQuadSmall$ and $\clmDressedQuadLarge$---the horizon bottoms out near $t\approx\clmRegrowthFloorTime$, long before the pure-quadrupole arm reaches its floor, and grows back (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_regrowth}): by $\clmRegrowthLevelThree\%$ ($\clmRegrowthFloorR\to\clmRegrowthEndR$), $\clmRegrowthSmallQuad\%$ and $\clmRegrowthLargeQuad\%$ in areal radius, with $M_{\rm MS}$ recovering $\clmRegrowthMassLo$--$\clmRegrowthMassHi\%$; the curves are smooth and saturating. The swallowed field's negative energy first shrinks the horizon and then, leaving, lets it regrow---the local counterpart of the negative-energy flux measured at infinity (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}). Hawking's area law assumes the null energy condition~\cite{hawking1971}, which the phantom violates; this horizon runs it backwards, then forwards, in one evolution. The episode lasts minutes even at $10^{6}\,M_\odot$ (Table~\ref{tab:scales}); where the regrowth saturates is open at $t=100$.

\subsection{The inflation branch}
\label{sec:single:fate}

The inflation branch forms no horizon. The $\varepsilon=-10^{-2}$ arm at level 4, instrumented like the collapse arms (Fig.~\ref{fig:single_inflation}), grows $\clmInflRStart\to\clmInflREnd$ in areal radius by $t=\clmInflRecordEnd$ with no trapped surface anywhere, carrying instead anti-trapped shells ($\theta_\pm>0$) on the scans over $t\approx\clmAntiTrappedFirst$--$\clmAntiTrappedLast$. It leaves its unkicked level-4 twin, which inflates on its own truncation seed, by $10\%$ at $t=\clmKickedDeparture$---when the $+10^{-2}$ twin on the same level has already formed its horizon---and inflates on the same branch. The growth is not a runaway. While the deviation is small the unkicked twin grows at the mode's $\clmInflModeRate\pm\clmInflModeRateErr$ per unit; the kicked arm shows no such plateau, its local rate falling from $\clmKickedLocalRateEarly$ at $t=\clmKickedLocalRateTimeEarly$ to $\clmKickedLocalRateLate$ at $t=\clmKickedLocalRateTimeLate$. Once the radius has doubled $\mathrm{d}\ln R/\mathrm{d}t$ peaks and decays: from $\clmUnkickedPeakRate$ near $t\approx\clmUnkickedPeakTime$ to $\clmUnkickedLateRate$ at $t=\clmUnkickedLateTime$ in the unkicked arm (doubling time $\clmUnkickedDoublingEarly\to\clmUnkickedDoublingLate$), and $\clmKickedPeakRate\to\clmKickedLateRate$ in the kicked one ($\clmKickedDoublingEarly\to\clmKickedDoublingLate$). A box-doubled ($L=\clmBoxDoubledL$) copy of the unkicked arm tracks it to $\clmBoxDoubledAgreement\%$ in $R$, so the boundary plays no role. The collapse runs away; the inflation coasts.

Its end state is open, and the throat is shedding its own support: the monopole scalar flux through every sphere grows quasi-exponentially over the quotable window (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}). Three endings remain: saturation at a larger radius, disfavoured because static drainholes are unstable fixed points; unbounded coasting, as in the spherical literature~\cite{shinkai2002}; or a turnover into collapse once too little phantom support remains, which a flux still growing when its reservoir is spent would force. A downturn of $R_{\min}(t)$ followed by a trapped surface would decide it. None occurs by $t=\clmInflRecordEnd$, although the constraint norms leave their floor from $t\approx\clmConstraintFloorBothBoxes$ ($L=128$) and $\clmConstraintFloorSmallBox$ ($L=64$).

The lifetime is a logarithm of the seed: a horizon at $\clmHorizonTimeEpsTwo\,M$ for $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ and $\clmHorizonTimeEpsThree\,M$ for $+10^{-3}$, a decade costing $\clmDecadeCost\,M\simeq\tau\ln10$; the unkicked throat's horizon by $\clmHorizonTimeNoise\,M$ places the level-3 truncation seed at $\varepsilon\gtrsim\clmNoiseSeed$. A foam-born pair must collide as it is born or not at all (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}).

\subsection{The sign rule}
\label{sec:pair:sign}

Two identical throats released from rest push apart; reversing one throat's scalar turns the push into a pull (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(a,b)). Both forces share one radial law, so their ratio $Q$ is separation-independent: no gap, mass or approach speed lets a like-signed pair fall together, and $\sigma=\clmMergerSign$ is the only gravity-driven merger channel. The flip is not a tuning: $\phi\to-\phi$ leaves $T_{ab}$, and with it the one-body geometry, unchanged. The measured pull-to-push ratio at $a=\clmThroatA$ is $\clmSignRatio\pm\clmSignRatioErr$ (scatter over $15$ slices) against the predicted $(Q+1)/(Q-1)=\clmScalarQRatio$, and the force follows the throat width---the scalar charge---not the mass, which is equal in all arms.

\subsection{The force law}
\label{sec:pair:force}

\clmCountLikeSignedPairs{} like-signed pairs, identical but for the gap, are displaced at a common $t=11.5$ by $\clmDLadderTwelve/\clmDLadderFourteen/\clmDLadderSixteen/\clmDLadderEighteen$ for $d=12/14/16/18$. Inverse-square in the coordinate separation is excluded---$F\,d^2$ rises monotonically $\clmFdSquaredLo\to\clmFdSquaredHi$ across the ladder---but $F\propto(d+\delta)^{-2}$ with $\delta\approx\clmOffsetDeltaLo$--$\clmOffsetDeltaHi$ describes it: $\delta$ fitted on $d=12$--$16$ alone predicts $\clmOffsetPrediction$ at $d=18$, where inverse-square gives $\clmInverseSquarePrediction$ and the measurement is $\clmDLadderEighteen$. Since $\delta$ is comparable to the placed pair's mouth radius ($\clmMouthRadiusTwelve$ at $d=12$; Sec.~\ref{sec:pair:placement}), the force is inverse-square in an \emph{effective} separation exceeding the coordinate one by about a throat radius. Across widths the push grows at every rung ($\clmALadderOne/\clmALadderOneHalf/\clmALadderTwo/\clmALadderThree$ for $a=1/1.5/2/3$) with an effective exponent of $\clmWidthExponentLo$--$\clmWidthExponentHi$; the point-charge $a^2$ scaling is not confirmed.

\subsection{Placement systematics}
\label{sec:pair:placement}

Freshly placed pairs read $+\clmPlacementExcessFar\%$ ($d=48$) to $+\clmPlacementExcessNear\%$ ($d=6$) wider than the isolated throat, falling as $d^{-\clmPlacementSlope}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pair}(c,d)), so every same-$d$ comparison here is made against that curve rather than against the lone throat. Read that way, the released head-on pair's mouths are genuinely squeezed, $\clmScoutSeven\%$ at $t=7$ growing to $\clmScoutSqueeze\%$ by $t=\clmScoutLastInRange$---an interaction effect, since the lone throat holds its radius to $\clmHoldTolLevelThree\%$ until $t\approx\clmHoldTimeLevelThree$.

The seed sets a lone throat's fate and the grid's lifetime, not the pair's.

\label{sec:headon:wall}

\clmHeadonWallLeadWord{} units after the horizon forms, the level-3 scout dies at $t=\clmHeadonScoutDeath$ with the classic anatomy---$\chi$ floored at the midpoint, $\max|K|$ running away to $\clmHeadonScoutMaxK$, a one-step overflow. The failure is the grid's: at the same time the level-5 arm reads a smooth midpoint (lapse $\clmHeadonMidpointLapse$, $\chi=\clmHeadonMidpointChi$, $\max|K|=\clmHeadonMidpointMaxK$) and runs to $t=\clmHeadonLevelFiveEnd$ with no restart or interior device, its Hamiltonian norm staying below its value at formation (Fig.~\ref{fig:headon_collapse}(d,e)). The remnant is a black hole with both throats inside it, behind a common neck that is itself trapped, and it loses mass at every step as it swallows the phantom field (Fig.~\ref{fig:headon_collapse}(a,b,f)): from $t=\clmHeadonFitStart$, once the surface has rounded, $M_{\rm MS}$ falls on every scan row of the level-3 down-step arm, which tracks the horizon to $t=\clmHeadonDownStepEnd$, and of the three fill arms. By $t=97$ the scan's areal radius is $\clmHeadonRemnantRadius$, \clmHeadonShrink{} below its value at formation: $\clmHeadonRemnantAreaShare\%$ of the two throats' area, $\clmHeadonRemnantThroatRatio\,R_\star$, and within $\clmHeadonRemnantOverSchw\%$ of $2M_{\rm ADM}$, the Schwarzschild radius of the pair's mass. It falls on average by $\clmHeadonRadiusSlope$ per unit, with a ${\sim}\clmHeadonRadiusWobble\%$ wobble as the scan's sphere follows a ringing surface, and no asymptote is resolved. $M_{\rm MS}$ levels off: through the down-step's rows $M_\infty+b\,e^{-(t-\clmHeadonFitStart)/\tau}$ gives $M_\infty=\clmHeadonMassAsymptote\pm\clmHeadonMassAsymptoteErr$ and $\tau=\clmHeadonMassTau\pm\clmHeadonMassTauErr$ (a factor $\clmHeadonFitRatio$ better than a line), while other fit windows and the denser fill-arm scans give $M_\infty=\clmHeadonMassAsymptoteLo$--$\clmHeadonMassAsymptoteHi$; since $2M_{\rm MS}/R$ still falls from $\clmHeadonCompactnessStart$ to $\clmHeadonCompactnessEnd$ over the window as the surface rounds, part of that levelling is the shape systematic of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}.

The horizon cannot bounce. In spherical symmetry, with the double-null metric $-2e^{-f}\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}v+R^2\mathrm{d}\Omega^2$ and $T_{uv}=0$ for a massless scalar, the Misner--Sharp mass on a marginally trapped tube obeys $\partial_v m=-4\pi R^2e^{f}\,T_{vv}\,\partial_uR$ with $\partial_uR<0$~\cite{hayward1996}: a canonical field, $T_{vv}=(\partial_v\phi)^2$, can only grow a horizon, and the phantom, $T_{vv}=-(\partial_v\phi)^2$, can only shrink it---the phantom-accretion mass loss of Ref.~\cite{babichev2004}. The head-on is not spherical, but its only positive-energy influx is gravitational radiation, and the whole event radiates $\clmDetEHeadon\,M$, about $\clmHeadonGwShare\%$ of what the horizon loses. The shrink ends when the negative energy outside is gone: the end state is the Schwarzschild hole of the pair's Bondi mass, $R=2(M_{\rm ADM}-E_{\rm GW}-E_\phi)\approx\clmHeadonEndRadius$, a little above $2M_{\rm ADM}$ because the scalar radiation carries negative energy away (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), and by $t=97$ the scan lies within a few percent of it. The late decline is not accretion: between $t\approx45$--$55$ and $t\approx90$--$99$ every proxy of the phantom flux into the horizon falls $\clmHeadonFluxDropLo$--$\clmHeadonFluxDropHi$-fold, while the scan's rates fall only $\clmHeadonRateDropLo$--$\clmHeadonRateDropHi$-fold. The spiral remnant ends at the same size ($R=\clmSpiralRemnantRadius$ at $t=\clmFlowSlicesLate$--$\clmFlowSlicesEnd$; Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}), as it should if the end state is set by the pair's mass.

This arm reproduces an earlier level-$3\to5$ restart chain---$(2,0)$ waveforms within $\clmHeadonSeamWaveMin$--$\clmHeadonSeamWaveMax\%$ of peak over the burst, radiated energies within $\clmHeadonSeamEnergyMin$--$\clmHeadonSeamEnergyMax\%$---so the mesh seam carries no result. The exterior is also grid- and device-independent within that restarted family: against the level-3 fill twin, which shares its $t=\clmHeadonFillRestartTime$ restart state, the level-3 down-step arm agrees on the horizon mass to $\clmHeadonDownStepMass\%$ and on the wave to $\clmHeadonDownStepWaveMin$--$\clmHeadonDownStepWaveMax\%$ of peak over $t=45$--$100$, and fill twins agree with no-fill arms to $\clmHeadonFillTwins\%$ of peak. After $t=70$ the family and the level-5 arm from $t=0$ part, by $\clmHeadonFromZeroLateLo$--$\clmHeadonFromZeroLateHi\%$ of peak, the most on the outermost sphere.

\subsection{The half-mass pair}
\label{sec:headon:halfmass}

Halving the throat mass moves everything earlier without avoiding the horizon. At $m=\clmHalfMassMass$, $d=\clmHalfMassSeparation$, the lapse falls from $\clmHalfMassLapseHigh$ to $\clmHalfMassLapseLow$ over $t=7.5$--$13$, the tracker merges the mouths at $t=\clmHalfMassTrackerMerge$, the offline scan finds a common MOTS at $t=\clmHalfMassMotsTime$ ($R=\clmHalfMassMotsRadius$), and the run dies at $t=\clmHalfMassPairDeath$ with the level-3 scout's anatomy (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}): $\chi$ on its floor, $\max|K|$ running away and a one-step overflow of the metric inside the common MOTS. The death belongs to the pair: the lone $m=\clmHalfMassM$ throat is exact to $\clmHalfMassLoneExact\%$ through $t=14$ and fails only at $t=\clmHalfMassDeath$, and differently---in $K$, at its compactified origin, the origin failure of Sec.~\ref{sec:single:fixedpoint}.

\section{The spiral merger}
\label{sec:spiral}

\subsection{Merger or escape}
\label{sec:spiral:capture}

Varying the tangential momentum $p$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:binary} at $d=12$ separates plunge from escape. Every pair from $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$ up to $\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ plunges to a merged core: $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFifteen$ repeats $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$'s endgame (a plateau at pit separation $\clmSpiralPlateauFifteen$ against $\clmSpiralPlateauTwelve$, then the dive), and the pits of $p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwenty$ and $\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ close to $\clmSpiralStallLow$--$\clmSpiralStallHigh$ at level 3 before its wall. At $p=\clmSpiralMomentumThirtyFive$ the pair passes at $\clmSpiralPassSeparation$ and recedes to $\clmSpiralRecedeSeparation$ by $t=\clmSpiralRecedeTime$ (level 3, stopped there); $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ scatters. So every $p\le\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ merges and every $p\ge\clmSpiralMomentumThirtyFive$ does not: the boundary between merger and escape lies between these two values, at $\clmSpiralBoundaryLow$--$\clmSpiralBoundaryHigh\%$ of the circular value under the combined pull.

The $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFlyby$ fly-by ($L=128$, level 5 to $t=\clmFlybyEnd$) is a scattering pass: closest approach $\clmFlybyClosestApproach$ at $t\approx\clmFlybyClosestTime$, back out to $\clmFlybyFinalSeparation$ by $t=\clmFlybyEnd$ and still separating. No trapped surface appears on the corrected scans at any time, and the run ends without a NaN. Instead both mouths inflate: from $t\approx\clmFlybyScanEdgeTime$ the throat lies beyond the scan window ($r\le\clmFlybyScanEdge$), whose edge reading---an upper bound on the throat's areal radius---reaches $\clmFlybyEdgeRadius$ by $t=97$, so from then on ``separation'' is that of the $\chi$ pits, which no scan ties to the throats. On the equatorial slices the pair's scalar dipole is wound into a two-armed trailing spiral, and each mouth drives a crescent of $\chi>1$ ahead of it ($\chi\sim\clmFlybyCrescentChi$ by $t=92$): the encounter ends not as two receding wormholes but as an expanding, orbit-wound cloud of the field that supported them.

\subsection{Plunge and collapse}
\label{sec:spiral:inspiral}

Every ``spiral'' here is a plunge: with $M_{\rm ADM}\simeq1$ per throat, the coordinate separation $d=12$ is $d/M=\clmSpiralSeparationOverMass$, the innermost-stable-circular-orbit (ISCO) value, and the throat tracks of the momentum scan complete only $\clmSpiralRevolutionsLow$--$\clmSpiralRevolutionsHigh$ of a revolution before closest approach ($p=\clmSpiralMomentumTwenty$--$\clmSpiralMomentumThirtyFive$). Section~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral} shows that no wider pair could inspiral instead.

The production spiral ($p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$, $L=128$) is evolved as a three-stage chain. Level 3 carries it from $t=0$ through the plunge; the $t=\clmSpiralRestartTime$ checkpoint, taken before the pits meet, is restarted at level 5, which carries the merger to the curvature wall at $t=\clmSpiralDeath$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:wall}); and from that leg's $t=\clmGwFreezeStart$ checkpoint, when the common MOTS already encloses the core, the interior freeze of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:grid} is armed at $r\le\clmGwFillSkin$ and the exterior evolves on to $t=\clmFreezeEnd$. The freeze cannot reach the burst it records: its physical influence travels at most at light speed and arrives at the innermost extraction sphere only at $t\approx\clmGwLightCone$, after the burst peak has passed there, and the gauge modes that outrun it carry at most $\clmGwFreezeVsNoFill$ of peak (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:burst}); a twin with the fill window moved changes the $(2,2)$ wave by at most $\clmGwFillTwinInner\%$ of peak (Fig.~\ref{fig:fill_insensitivity}); and over the $\clmControlOverlapLength$ units in which the frozen and unfrozen level-5 arms coexist, their $(2,0)$ and $(2,2)$ modes agree to $\clmControlMSixInner\%$ of peak at the innermost sphere, to $\clmControlMSixNext$ of peak at the next and bit for bit at the outer two.

The production spiral falls from $d=\clmSpiralStartSeparation$ until its two $\chi$ pits---each wormhole's compactified far side---lie $\clmSpiralPitSepLate$ apart, and collapses at the centre with both wormholes still open (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_collapse}). The merger's constraint transient at $t=\clmSpiralTransientTime$ is the same at both resolutions---the level-3 leg reproduces the level-5 base-grid norms to a median $\clmSpiralLevelMedianHam\%/\clmSpiralLevelMedianMom\%$---and the collapse after it is clean: the lapse falls to $\clmSpiralMinLapse$, $\chi$ reaches its floor at $t=\clmSpiralChiFloorTime$, and $\max|K|$ grows $\clmSpiralMaxKGrowth\times$ over $t=50$--$58$ ($\clmSpiralMaxKStart\to\clmSpiralMaxKEnd$ across the level-5 window) on a wall that lands on the throat; $\phi$ holds $\pm\clmSpiralScalarMax$ while $\Pi$ saturates at $\pm\clmSpiralMomentumFieldMax$.

The shape-free flow finder (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}) finds a common MOTS on every death-window slice of the two level-5 arms. On the production chain its areal radius is $R=\clmSpiralFlowRadiusBirth$ at $t=\clmSpiralFlowBirthTime$ ($\theta_-\simeq\clmSpiralFlowThetaBirth$, newly formed; converged from an outer seed at $\ell_{\max}=8$), $\clmSpiralFlowRadiusSecond$ at $\clmSpiralFlowTimeSecond$ and $\clmSpiralFlowRadiusThird$ at $\clmSpiralFlowTimeThird$, $\clmSpiralFlowLead$ units before that arm's NaN; on the arm evolved at level 5 from $t=0$ it has $R=\clmSpiralFlowRadiusLate\pm\clmSpiralFlowRadiusLateErr$ at $t=\clmSpiralFlowTimeLate$, $\clmSpiralFlowLeadLate$ before its NaN, bracketed by inner and outer seeds, $\clmSpiralFlowDeformation\%$ peak-to-peak in coordinate radius about the pit, with $\theta_-=\clmSpiralFlowThetaLate$. On an exact MOTS $M_{\rm MS}=R/2$: $\clmSpiralFlowMassBirth$ at formation, $\clmSpiralFlowMassLate$ at $t=\clmFlowSliceLateTime$. On the frozen-core arm's live exterior (fill $r\le\clmSpiralFillRadius$, inside the surface) the remnant at $t=\clmFlowSlicesLate$--$\clmFlowSlicesEnd$ has $R=\clmSpiralRemnantRadius$ ($M_{\rm MS}=\clmSpiralRemnantMass$), still draining at $\clmSpiralRemnantDrain\%$ per unit, bracketed by a pointwise-trapped and a pointwise-untrapped surface. Both wormholes lie inside it. Their pits stay distinct until $\chi$ reaches its floor at $t=\clmSpiralChiFloorTime$---at $t=\clmFlowSlicesFirst$--$\clmSpiralFlowTimeThird$ they are $\clmSpiralPitSepEarly$--$\clmSpiralPitSepLate$ apart, with $\chi$ $\clmSpiralPitChiRatioLo$--$\clmSpiralPitChiRatioHi$ times higher between them---as they must, since no classical evolution changes the number of asymptotic ends~\cite{geroch1967}. For scale, a surface with both throats' area would have $\sqrt2R_\star=\clmTwoThroatRadius$: the newborn common MOTS is $\clmSpiralMotsThroatRatio\,R_\star$, $\clmSpiralMotsAreaShare$ of the two throats' area and $\clmSpiralMotsBelowHeadon\%$ smaller than the head-on's at formation, and the remnant is $\clmSpiralRemnantThroatRatio\,R_\star$, like the head-on's. At its death the remnant is therefore a black hole containing both wormholes behind a common neck: the head-on's anatomy, reached through a plunge.

\subsection{The curvature wall}
\label{sec:spiral:wall}

No grid carries the spiral through its merger. Restarted from one $t=\clmLadderRestartTime$ checkpoint at maximum levels $\clmLadderLevelLow$--$\clmLadderLevelHigh$, every arm of the $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$ run dies on an $h_{11}$ NaN, and the gains per doubling saturate: $\clmLadderGainOne$, $\clmLadderGainTwo$, $\clmLadderGainThree$, $\clmLadderGainFour$ units (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_ladder}(a)). \clmLadderFactorWord{} times the resolution buys $\clmLadderTotalGain$ units, and halving the time step on level 5 buys $\clmLadderHalfStepGain$, most of the level $5\to6$ gain. The core matter damping moves the wall by less than a unit. Starting refined does not help either: on the doubled box the $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$ arm evolved at level 5 from $t=0$ dies at $\clmSpiralFromZeroDeath$, against $\clmSpiralDeath$ for the production chain restarted at level 5 from $t=\clmSpiralRestartTime$; a truncation seed two levels smaller buys nothing, so the grid does not seed the wall. Past the wall the frozen-core stage (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}) carries the exterior to $t=\clmFreezeEnd$.

The wall is censored. The spiral's common MOTS exists from $t=\clmSpiralFlowBirthTime$ at the latest, $\clmSpiralFlowLead$ units before the production chain's NaN; the head-on's MOTS leads its level-3 death by $\clmHeadonWallLead$ units; and lone collapses die behind their horizons at level 3 too ($\varepsilon=+10^{-3}$ at $t=\clmKickThreeDeath$, $\clmKickThreeLead$ units after its horizon forms; $\varepsilon=+10^{-2}$ dressed with $\varepsilon_2=10^{-2}$ at $t=\clmDressedDeathLevelThree$, $\clmDressedLeadLevelThree$ after), while no level-4 collapse dies. Yet refinement rescues the head-on and never the spiral, although horizon presence, lead time and permanence are matched between them. Censorship is therefore necessary for a finer grid to pass a wall but not sufficient. Nor is the spiral's wall a stronger core at higher momentum, as far as $\max|K|$ can tell: across the momentum scan the level-3 wall arrives at $t=\clmWallLevelThreeLo$--$\clmWallLevelThreeHi$ with no trend in $p$; no level-5 arm shows a runaway in $\max|K|$ before its NaN, the $p=\clmSpiralMomentumFifteen$--$\clmSpiralMomentumTwentyFive$ arms dying at $\max|K|=\clmWallKLo$--$\clmWallKHi$; and the head-on passes a peak of $\clmHeadonKPeak$ and survives. What falls with momentum is the grid's leverage: refinement from level 3 to 5 moves the wall by $\clmWallGainProd$ units at $p=\clmSpiralMomentumProd$, by under a unit at $p\ge\clmSpiralMomentumFifteen$, and removes it for the head-on.

The natural suspect is the slicing. Every arm uses $1{+}\log$ slicing with a Gamma-driver shift, whose slicings can develop coordinate shocks~\cite{alcubierre2003} that refinement resolves rather than cures; a shock at a young, strongly deformed horizon would end every grid. Four gauge choices move the clock without removing the wall (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_ladder}(b); the three non-standard arms also carry the core matter damping, which alone moves the level-3 wall by under a unit). At level 3 the spiral dies at $t=\clmGaugeHalvedDeath$ with the $1{+}\log$ coefficient halved, $\clmGaugeHarmonicDeath$ with the harmonic-class driver $-2\alpha^2K$, $\clmGaugeStandardDeath$ in the standard gauge, and $\clmGaugeEtaDeath$ with the shift damping raised $\eta=\clmShiftEta\to\clmGaugeEta$, which delays the whole sequence by ${\sim}\clmGaugeDelay$ units (the tracker merging the two centres at $t=\clmGaugeEtaCoalescence$ against $\clmGaugeStandardCoalescence$, $\chi$ floor at $\clmGaugeEtaChiFloor$ against $\clmGaugeStandardChiFloor$): the level-3 clock gains because the whole sequence is late---its wall still follows its $\chi$ floor by $\clmGaugeEtaWallLag$ units against $\clmGaugeStandardWallLag$---not because level 3 resolves more. On the head-on control the same knobs give deaths at $\clmHeadonHarmonicDeath$ (harmonic), $\clmHeadonStandardDeath$ (standard) and $\clmHeadonEtaDeath$ ($\eta=\clmGaugeEta$), and trapping precedes each: the harmonic arm's MOTS appears at $t=\clmHeadonHarmonicMotsTime$ with $R=\clmHeadonHarmonicMotsRadius$ against the standard $\clmHeadonStandardMotsRadius$---the same surface on an earlier clock, $\clmHeadonHarmonicLead$ units ahead of the failure---and the $\eta=\clmGaugeEta$ arm holds whole trapped spheres about the merged pit from $t=\clmHeadonEtaTrapped$, which bounds a MOTS from inside. The head-on's censorship is thus gauge-robust; only its clock moves. Refinement narrows the gauge spread without closing it: the $\eta=\clmGaugeEta$ arm restarted at level 5 from $t=\clmGaugeEtaRestartTime$ dies at $\clmGaugeEtaLevelFiveDeath$, $\clmGaugeEtaLevelFiveGap$ units \emph{before} its level-3 clock (a restart at $t=\clmGaugeEtaRestartSixty$ fails at once, at $\clmGaugeEtaRestartSixtyDeath$), while the standard gauge on the same box and restart dies at $\clmGaugeStandardLevelFiveDeath$---a separation of ${\sim}\clmGaugeLevelFiveSpread$ units against $\clmGaugeLevelThreeSpread$ at level 3.

The wall is therefore grid-, seed- and device-independent within the Bona--Mass\'o slicings and shift dampings tried: its clock is gauge-dependent, its existence is not.

\section{Gravitational waves}
\label{sec:gw}

Every channel radiates, and the most energetic is the fly-by, which never forms a horizon. The peak $|r\Psi_4|$ of the dominant mode at the innermost sphere is $\clmGwPeakFlyby$ for the fly-by, $\clmGwPeakSpiral$ for the spiral, $\clmGwPeakHeadon$ for the head-on, $\clmGwPeakTwin$ for the vacuum twin and $\clmGwPeakThroat$ for the collapsing throat (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}). Each wormhole record propagates outward at $c$ within its output cadence (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}, right); only the vacuum twin's waveform lag exceeds its cadence, by $\clmGwTwinLagExcess$ units across its two spheres, its inner sphere sitting in the near zone. No sphere is asymptotic: the dominant periods are comparable to the extraction radii, and the peak $r\Psi_4$ falls across the spheres by $\clmGwPeakFallHeadon\%$ on the head-on and $\clmGwPeakFallFlyby\%$ on the fly-by, whose outermost reading drops below the spiral's. We therefore do not extrapolate, and carry the spread over spheres as the near-zone error bar of every energy (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(d)).

In the dominant multipole the fly-by radiates $(\clmGwEnergyFlybyLo$--$\clmGwEnergyFlybyHi)\times10^{-2}\,M$ across its spheres and the spiral up to $\clmGwEnergySpiral\,M$, against $\clmGwEnergyTwin\,M$ for the vacuum merger control---$\clmGwEnergyRatioFlybyLo$--$\clmGwEnergyRatioFlybyHi\times$ and up to $\clmGwEnergyRatioSpiral\times$ more. That control matches the spiral; the fly-by's own control is a vacuum binary at $d=\clmGwVacPassSep$, $p=\clmGwVacPassMom$. In vacuum that momentum is unbound (circular $\clmGwVacPassCirc$, parabolic $\clmGwVacPassPar$), so the black holes start at periapsis and coast apart, radiating $\clmGwEnergyVacPass\,M$, whereas the drainhole pair falls from $12$ to $\clmFlybyClosestApproach$ under a pull six times gravity's. The fly-by thus outradiates a vacuum binary of its own parameters by $\clmGwVacPassRatioLo$--$\clmGwVacPassRatioHi\times$.

\subsection{Collapsing throat}
\label{sec:single:gw}

A lone throat radiates only if its collapse is not spherical. The spherically kicked arm emits nothing above the $\ell=2$ numerical floor, while the same kick with an $\varepsilon_2=\clmGwSeedStrong$ quadrupole produces a burst of peak $|r\Psi_4|=\clmGwPeakThroat$, $\clmGwThroatOverControl\times$ the control, carrying $\clmGwEnergyThroat\,M$. The burst arrives in radius order at $\clmGwThroatSpeedLo$--$\clmGwThroatSpeedHi\,c$, carries the same $r\Psi_4$ to four spheres within $\clmGwThroatSphereSpread\%$, and is linear in the seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:seed_linearity}): $\clmGwThroatFiveFold\times$ for a $\clmGwThroatSeedRatio\times$ kick, ratios $\clmGwHalvedRatioA/\clmGwHalvedRatioB/\clmGwHalvedRatioC$ at $R=10/14/\clmGwSeedRadiusC$ for a halved seed, linear to $\clmGwLinDevLo$--$\clmGwLinDevHi\%$ across $\varepsilon_2=\clmGwSeedWeak$--$\clmGwSeedStrong$. The decay time is not measurable on this record.

\subsection{Fly-by burst}
\label{sec:gw:flyby}

The $p=0.45$ pass emits a single $(2,2)$ arch of peak $|r\Psi_4|=\clmGwPeakFlyby$ with no ringdown---there is no remnant---propagating at $v/c=\clmGwSpeedFlybyAll$ across every sphere pair; in retarded time all four spheres peak together at $t-R\simeq\clmGwFlybyRetardedPeak$. \subsection{Vacuum twin}
\label{sec:controls:bbh}

The vacuum control---same $d$, $p$ and masses, no scalar---behaves as a binary black hole must: it merges, climbs the point-mass chirp $\clmGwTwinFreqLo\to\clmGwTwinFreqHi$ Hz and turns toward the equal-mass Kerr mode, its $\Psi_4$ peak at the textbook $fM=\clmGwTwinPeakFM$. The fly-by's peak $|r\Psi_4|$ is $\clmGwPeakRatioFlyby\times$ the twin's and the spiral's $\clmGwPeakRatioSpiral\times$. The twin also calibrates the wavefront method: its flat-topped merger envelope defeats peak timing, so every speed here is a correlated lag (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_gallery}).

\subsection{Scalar channel and its sign}
\label{sec:gw:scalar}

$\Psi_4$ is blind to a spin-$0$ wave. Two opposite scalar charges---every merging pair here (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair})---carry a dipole, where a vacuum binary's leading moment is the quadrupole. The scalar stream rides the same spheres as $\Psi_4$ on the spiral and fly-by, so the two channels compare sphere by sphere (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}). The recorded kinematic flux $F_{\rm kin}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}) approximates the scalar energy crossing $S_R$ per coordinate time. That energy is the flux, through the world tube that $S_R$ sweeps out, of the matter energy current $J^\mu\equiv-T^{(\phi)\mu}{}_{\nu}\,t^\nu$ of the time vector $t^\mu=\alpha n^\mu+\beta^\mu$, with $T^{(\phi)}_{\mu\nu}$ the canonical stress tensor (the negative of Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}). Since $\nabla_\mu J^\mu=-\tfrac12T^{(\phi)\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_t g_{\mu\nu}$, the current is conserved where $t^\mu$ is a Killing vector and nearly so in the quasi-stationary exterior that the spheres sample, and Gauss's theorem on the world tube gives its flux in 3+1 form~\cite{clough2021,gourgoulhon2012},
\begin{equation}
F^{\rm geo}_\phi=\oint_{S_R}\alpha\,s_iJ^i\sqrt{\sigma}\;\mathrm{d}^2x
=-\oint_{S_R}\partial_t\phi\,\bigl(\alpha\,s^j\partial_j\phi+\beta_js^j\,\Pi\bigr)\sqrt{\sigma}\;\mathrm{d}^2x,
\label{eq:scalarflux}
\end{equation}
positive for an outgoing canonical wave, with $s^i$ the sphere's outward unit normal, $\sqrt{\sigma}$ its area element, $J^i$ the spatial components of the four-vector (not $\gamma^{ij}J_j$) and $\partial_t\phi=\alpha\Pi+\beta^i\partial_i\phi$. On a conformally flat sphere with uniform $\alpha$ and $\chi$ and no shift, $F^{\rm geo}_\phi=\alpha^2\chi^{-1/2}F_{\rm kin}$; the shift adds an advective term ${\propto}(\beta^i\partial_i\phi)(s^j\partial_j\phi)$ and a term ${\propto}\beta_js^j\,\Pi\,\partial_t\phi$ from its normal component. In the initial data the sphere at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$ has $\alpha\approx\clmGwScalarLapseInit$ and $\chi\approx\clmGwScalarChiInit$, and the area mean of $\alpha^2\chi^{-1/2}$ is $\approx\clmGwScalarFluxFactor$, so energies built from $F_{\rm kin}$ overstate $|F^{\rm geo}_\phi|$ by about $\clmGwScalarFluxCorr\%$; the shift terms are neglected.

The channel is dipole-dominated and comparable to the gravitational one. On the fly-by at $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusOuter$, $\ell=1$ carries the scalar power $\sum_{\ell m}|\dot A_{\ell m}|^2$ to one part in $10^{\clmGwScalarDipoleShare}$, the $\ell=1$ signal travels outward at $c$ from $R=\clmGwScalarRadiusInner$, and the time-integrated flux to $t=\clmGwScalarCut$ is $|E_\phi|=\clmGwScalarEnergyFlyby\,M$---$\clmGwScalarRatioSixty$ times the gravitational energy that has crossed the sphere by then (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}(a)). The ratio depends on the cut and on how $E_{\rm GW}$ is integrated. With the running integral of Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_channel}(a) it falls to $\clmGwScalarRatioSeventy$ by $t=\clmGwScalarCutSeventy$ and $\clmGwScalarRatioEighty$ by $t=\clmGwScalarCutEighty$, but that integral keeps growing after the burst has passed, accumulating the drift of the time-integrated $\Psi_4$; the band-limited energy of Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(d) levels off instead ($\clmGwScalarBandSixty$, $\clmGwScalarBandSeventy$ and $\clmGwScalarBandEighty$ at $t=60$, $70$ and $80$). The wave-zone mode sum gives $\clmGwScalarRatioWaveZone$, and the inner sphere returns $\clmGwScalarRatioInner$ for the same window. We therefore use only the channel's multipole, direction, sign and order of magnitude downstream.

Gravity couples to minus the canonical stress tensor, Eq.~\eqref{eq:phantom}, so the physical energy flux is $-F_{\rm kin}$ and an outgoing scalar wave carries negative energy; what the data establish is that the $\ell=1$ wave is outgoing (on the fly-by, and on the head-on after its horizon forms) and of the size above. If the ADM balance holds with this term, a bound pair emitting it gains energy: the scalar channel pumps the orbit where gravitational radiation damps it, and radiation reaction is \emph{anti-damped}. The same bookkeeping appears at horizons, where swallowing the phantom shrinks the head-on remnant (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}) and the single-throat horizon regrows as the swallowed field leaves (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}).

\subsection{Horizon censorship of the scalar channel}
\label{sec:gw:censorship}

The head-on, re-run at level \clmGwCensHeadonLevel{} from $t=\clmGwCensHeadonStart$ with the scalar stream, is the one encounter that forms a horizon early, and the horizon switches the channel off (Fig.~\ref{fig:scalar_censorship}). After the common MOTS closes ($t=\clmGwCensMotsTime$ on this arm's live scan), the mouths' remaining hair leaves as a single $\ell=1$, $m=0$ pulse, cresting later at each sphere in causal order, and the envelope then decays: log-linear fits over $t=\clmGwCensFitLo$--$\clmGwCensFitHi$ give $\tau=19/\clmGwCensTauMid/\clmGwCensTauOuter$ at $R=10/14/\clmGwHeadonRadiusC$, comparable to the $\tau=\clmHeadonMassTau$ on which the horizon's $M_{\rm MS}$ levels off in the down-step fit (Sec.~\ref{sec:headon:wall}). The post-horizon energies keep the sign of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}, $E_\phi=\clmGwCensEnergyInner/\clmGwCensEnergyMid/\clmGwCensEnergyOuter\,M$. Only this window is integrated: before $t=\clmGwCensIngoing$ the flux at $R=\clmGwHeadonRadiusA$ runs canonically ingoing, the two open mouths' static hair superposing.

Without a horizon the channel does not shut. The fly-by's dipole envelope is still growing at the end of its record. The spiral's horizon, present from $t=\clmSpiralFlowBirthTime$, is only \clmSpiralFlowLeadWord{} units old at its wall, against a shedding clock of ${\sim}\clmGwShedClock$, so it is too young to show the shut-off.

The lone throat shows the same contrast. Re-run at level \clmGwKickedLevel{} with the scalar stream, the $\varepsilon=\clmGwKickedEps$ throat collapses on schedule---a permanent MOTS from $t=\clmGwKickedMotsTime$, $R$ settling $\clmGwKickedRadiusStart\to\clmGwKickedRadiusEnd$---and its scalar envelope, after the burst, falls by $\clmGwKickedDropLo$--$\clmGwKickedDropHi\times$ across the spheres by $t=\clmGwKickedDropTime$, with late e-fold times of $\clmGwKickedEfoldLo$--$\clmGwKickedEfoldHi$. The unkicked level-\clmGwSingleLevel{} throat, which inflates with no trapped surface by scan or flow finder, instead grows quasi-exponentially in the $\ell=0$ monopole (e-fold ${\approx}\clmGwSingleEfold$, shortening across $t=\clmGwSingleWindowLo$--$\clmGwSingleWindowHi$), identically in a box-doubled ($L=\clmGwSingleBox$) copy through $t\approx\clmGwSingleAgreeTime$, so the growth is the throat's and not the boundary's or the sponge's; beyond $t\approx\clmGwSingleConstraintTime$ the constraint norms grow, and only $t\lesssim\clmGwSingleCountTime$ is counted. The contrast is confounded with the fate: the horizonless arms are also those whose mouths inflate toward the spheres. What is measured is that the scalar channel shuts off behind a horizon and grows while a throat inflates.

\section{Pattern matching against detector data}
\label{sec:ligo}

In $\clmDetLivetime$ h of coincident Advanced LIGO O3b data~\cite{gwosc}, a matched-filter search~\cite{allen2012,pycbc} with $\clmDetBankSize$ templates---the waveforms of Sec.~\ref{sec:gw} rescaled as $h\propto M/D$, $f\propto1/M$---finds \emph{no candidate at zero lag}, and none was expected. No static throat of astrophysical mass survives---a stellar-mass drainhole is gone in milliseconds (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime})---so LIGO could catch only the burst of an encounter or collapse at formation, never a wormhole in residence; and a primordial population forms and converts at $z\gtrsim\clmDetZConv$, far beyond the search's reach and inside LISA's band instead (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds}). At single-detector SNR $\clmDetHorizonSnr$ and optimal orientation the median horizon over each mass ladder is $\clmDetHorizonSpiral$ Gpc (spiral), $\clmDetHorizonFlyby$ (fly-by), $\clmDetHorizonHeadon$ (head-on), $\clmDetHorizonTwin$ (vacuum twin) and $\clmDetHorizonThroat$ (collapsing throat); the first three are upper bounds, since their strain, from fixed-frequency integration~\cite{reisswig2011}, ends at $\clmDetPedestalLo$--$\clmDetPedestalHi$ of peak.

The null result is not a blind spot. Cut to the signal's duration, binary-black-hole templates (\texttt{IMRPhenomD}) match the bursts with fitting factors of $\clmDetFFWindowLo$--$\clmDetFFWindowHi$, as they match the vacuum control ($\clmDetFFTwinWindowLo$--$\clmDetFFTwinWindowHi$), so burst morphology alone cannot tell the two apart. All $\clmDetInjCount$ injections into O3b strain at optimal SNR $\clmDetInjSnr$ are recovered at $\clmDetInjEffLo$--$\clmDetInjEffHi\%$ of optimal within $\clmDetInjDt$ ms; only the lightest fly-by is demoted, by the $\chi^2$ veto.

\subsection{The frequency track}
\label{sec:ligo:ringdown}

The frequency track separates a drainhole burst from a binary black hole (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw_ligo}(c)). The vacuum twin peaks at $fM=\clmGwTwinPeakFM$ and climbs the point-mass track before turning toward the equal-mass Kerr mode at $fM=\clmDetKerrFM$: a monotone rise, then a fixed ringdown frequency. No drainhole channel does this. The head-on's frequency \emph{falls}, and mass loss cannot explain it: a black hole shrinking as its remnant does rings faster, by $\times\clmDetKerrRise$ over the same window, while the measurement is $\times\clmDetHeadonFreqRatio$. The fly-by's frequency falls as the pair separates; the spiral's late record is carried by a frozen core and climbs without settling; the lone collapse rings at $\clmDetThroatOverKerr$ of the equal-mass Kerr mode. A catalogue event~\cite{gwtc3} whose post-merger frequency holds within a few percent of a Kerr value therefore excludes the head-on channel at that mass.

\subsection{Energy per event}
\label{sec:ligo:burst}

The detector-independent deliverable is the energy per event in throat-mass units, in the dominant multipole (sphere spread in parentheses): fly-by $\clmGwEnergyFlyby\,M$ ($\clmGwSpreadFlyby\%$), spiral $\clmGwEnergySpiral\,M$ ($\clmDetESpiralSpread\%$), head-on $\clmDetEHeadon\,M$ ($\clmDetEHeadonSpread\%$), collapsing throat $\clmGwEnergyThroat\,M$ ($\clmDetEThroatSpread\%$), vacuum control $\clmGwEnergyTwin\,M$ ($\clmDetETwinSpread\%$). Since $\Omega_{\rm GW}$ scales as event density times energy per event, these convert any bound on $\Omega_{\rm GW}$~\cite{lvk2021,pagano2016} into a bound on the abundance of primordial drainhole encounters, provided the ghost scalar exists at that epoch. The scalar channel adds comparable energy of opposite sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

All four fates of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro} occur, each with a measured selection rule: the relative scalar sign separates scattering from merger (Sec.~\ref{sec:pair}), the seed's sign separates collapse from inflation (Sec.~\ref{sec:single}), and the orbital momentum separates plunge from escape at $\clmSpiralBoundaryLow$--$\clmSpiralBoundaryHigh\%$ of circular (Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral}). For detection, three results combine: no inspiral exists (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), the wormhole phase is a burst whose frequency falls or drifts where a Kerr remnant's would hold (Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:ringdown}), and the scalar counterpart decays once a horizon forms (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}). A surviving wormhole signature is a drifting-frequency burst with a scalar counterpart; a settled ringdown without one is already the descendant.

\subsection{No wormhole inspiral}
\label{sec:discussion:noinspiral}

A drainhole binary cannot radiate an inspiral, and the obstruction is a logarithm. In a binary the mouths' unstable mode runs at $\tau=\clmMouthTauFlyby$ (fly-by) and $\clmMouthTauMerger$ (merger; Sec.~\ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}), faster than the isolated $\clmTauLevelFourRounded$--$\clmTauRounded$; we use $\tau\simeq\clmDetTauAdopted$. A quasi-circular pair at separation $d$ merges by radiation reaction in $t_{\rm insp}=\tfrac{5}{256}(d/M)^4\,M/\eta$ ($M$ the total mass, $\eta=1/4$): $\clmDetInspTimeIsco$ units from the ISCO, $d/M=\clmDetIscoDOverM$, and $\clmDetInspTimeWide$ from $d/M=\clmDetWideDOverM$---$\clmDetInspOrbitsIsco$ and $\clmDetInspOrbitsWide$ orbits at the initial period $T_{\rm orb}=2\pi\sqrt{d^3/M}$, which understates the cycles completed and so is conservative. That gives the instability $t_{\rm insp}/\tau=\clmDetEfoldsIsco$ e-folds at the ISCO and $\clmDetEfoldsWide$ for a two-orbit inspiral: the mouths reach merger unchanged only if the initial perturbation is below $e^{-\clmDetEfoldsIsco}\simeq\clmDetSeedIsco$, respectively $\clmDetSeedWide$ (with the isolated clock, $\clmDetSeedIscoIsolated$ at the ISCO). Turned around, the lifetime is $\tau\ln(1/\varepsilon)$: seeds of $\clmDetSeedExampleA$, $\clmDetSeedExampleB$ and $\clmDetSeedExampleC$ buy $\clmDetLifeOrbitsA$, $\clmDetLifeOrbitsB$ and $\clmDetLifeOrbitsC$ of one orbit. The orbit's demand grows as $(d/M)^4$ and the throat's endurance as $\ln(1/\varepsilon)$, so no finer grid or quieter data closes the gap: constraint-solved initial data, lowering the seed from the superposition defect's $\clmDetEffSeed$ to the level-3 truncation floor's $\clmNoiseSeed$, are worth at most $\tau\ln\clmDetSeedRatio\simeq\clmDetSeedBudget$ units, $\clmDetSeedBudgetOrbits$ of an orbit.

The inspiral time above is the vacuum quadrupole result, and this binary violates its premises. Its leading radiative moment is a scalar dipole carrying energy comparable to the gravitational channel's (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}). Were that energy positive, a reaction two to four times stronger would shorten $t_{\rm insp}$ and relax the ISCO requirement to $\clmDetEfoldsPosLo$--$\clmDetEfoldsPosHi$ e-folds ($\varepsilon\lesssim\clmDetSeedPosLo$--$\clmDetSeedPosHi$). Its energy is negative, however, so if the ADM balance holds the orbit is pumped rather than drained and no quasi-circular decay exists to compute. A wormhole inspiral fails on the throat's clock and again on the orbit's.

The wormhole-specific signal is therefore a short transient: within a few tens of $M$ each mouth collapses or inflates, and the plunge, burst and conversion of Secs.~\ref{sec:headon}--\ref{sec:gw} are all that this source class emits \emph{as wormholes}. Three qualifications apply. The argument covers a minimally coupled ghost scalar without spin, the matter model of the Gonz\'alez--Guzm\'an--Sarbach analysis, and holds only while the throat's unstable mode exists. Rotation can remove that mode: Ellis wormholes set spinning exist up to an extremal-Kerr limit~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014}; in five dimensions, with equal angular momenta, the static throat's unstable mode disappears above a critical angular momentum~\cite{dzhunushaliev2013}; in four, radial perturbations to second order in the rotation indicate the same~\cite{azad2024plb,azad2024prd}, and the non-axisymmetric modes computed at rapid rotation show no instability~\cite{khoo2024}. Other supports are untouched by the argument and include radially stable throats---thin shells for suitable equations of state~\cite{poisson_visser1995} and dilatonic Einstein--Gauss--Bonnet wormholes, which need no exotic matter~\cite{kanti2011}---so a spinning or differently supported pair that outlives an inspiral is not excluded here. ``No inspiral'' means no \emph{wormhole} inspiral: any long chirp belongs to the objects the throats become. And the seeds here are numerical, which the argument tolerates because its seed dependence is logarithmic and because the superposition defect's excitation of the mode is measured: back-extrapolating the fly-by's mouth growth gives an effective seed of $\clmDetEffSeed$, just below the declared-kick decade over which the response is measured linear (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:branching}).

\subsection{Wormhole collapse as a heavy-seed channel}
\label{sec:discussion:seeds}

The collapse branch is a seeding mechanism. A throat converts to a black hole in seconds at $10^{4}$--$10^{5}\,M_\odot$ and in minutes at $10^{6}\,M_\odot$ (Table~\ref{tab:scales}), and the remnant keeps a mass of order its progenitors': the head-on and spiral remnants both end at $R\simeq\clmRemnantRadiusMean$ (irreducible mass $\clmSpiralRemnantMass$) from pairs of ADM mass $\clmHeadonADMMass$, and the lone collapse at $R\approx\clmLoneRemnantLo$--$\clmLoneRemnantHi$ from a throat of mass $\clmThroatM$ (Secs.~\ref{sec:single:collapse}, \ref{sec:headon:wall}, \ref{sec:spiral:inspiral}). The scalar hair leaves on a ${\sim}\clmGwShedClock\,M$ clock after horizon formation (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:censorship}), so the remnants then accrete as ordinary black holes. From $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassMid\,M_\odot$ at $z\simeq\clmDetZConv$, Eddington-limited growth (Salpeter e-fold ${\simeq}\clmDetSalpeter$ Myr) reaches $\clmDetGrowthTarget\,M_\odot$ in $\clmDetGrowthEfoldsLo$--$\clmDetGrowthEfoldsHi$ e-folds, $\clmDetGrowthTimeLo$--$\clmDetGrowthTimeHi$ Myr, inside the ${\sim}\clmDetGrowthWindow$ Myr available by $z\simeq\clmDetGrowthZ$: no super-Eddington phase is needed, which is what defines a heavy seed~\cite{inayoshi2020} (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(a)). The channel could thus seed JWST's overmassive black holes and little red dots~\cite{bogdan2024,natarajan2024,greene2024,matthee2024} without an atomic-cooling halo, a critical Lyman--Werner flux, or any baryonic environment.

Halo independence is itself a signature: the seeds predate baryonic structure, so $M_{\rm BH}/M_*$ starts effectively infinite and relaxes downward, making UHZ1's overmassive morphology the generic expectation rather than a tuning. The abundance required is small: one seed per present-day massive galaxy is $n\simeq\clmDetNMin$--$\clmDetNMax\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ comoving, a dark-matter fraction $f=nM/\rho_{\rm DM}\simeq\clmDetDMFracLo$--$\clmDetDMFracHi$ at $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$. And the channel evades the bound that closes the conventional route: FIRAS $\mu$-distortions exclude primordial black holes of $\clmDetFirasLo$--$\clmDetFirasHi\,M_\odot$ formed from Gaussian curvature perturbations at any cosmologically relevant abundance~\cite{nakama2018}, but these black holes form by wormhole collapse, not from enhanced curvature perturbations---the evasion the bubble and domain-wall channels also use~\cite{garriga2016,deng2017}.

The channel radiates, which makes it falsifiable (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(b)). Each conversion emits the energies of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo:burst} at $f_{\rm obs}=(fM)_{\rm peak}/[M(1+z_e)]$; with the measured $(fM)_{\rm peak}=\clmDetFMPeakLo$--$\clmDetFMPeakHi$ the band is $\clmDetBandLo$--$\clmDetBandHi$ mHz across $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$ at $z_e\simeq\clmDetZConv$: LISA's band, not LIGO's. One at a time the bursts are loud. Rescaled with the strain of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} to $z_e=\clmDetZConv$ in a Planck cosmology~\cite{planck2018} and filtered against the analytic LISA noise~\cite{robson2019}, the merger channels reach optimal signal-to-noise ratios of $\clmDetBurstSnrLo$--$\clmDetBurstSnrHi$ at $\clmDetSeedMassMid$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$, and all three stay above $8$ from $\clmDetDetectMassLo$ to $\clmDetDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$ with the inclination averaged, the galactic confusion noise added and only each record's resolved band counted; the strain pedestal of Sec.~\ref{sec:ligo} matters only at the light end. A lone collapse, radiating only $\clmGwEnergyThroatRound\,M$ (a background of $\clmDetCollapseOmegaLo$--$\clmDetCollapseOmegaHi$), stays below detection, at a signal-to-noise ratio of at most ${\approx}\clmDetCollapseSnr$. What limits detection is occurrence: every seed converts once, so LISA records $4\pi D_c^2(z_e)\,c\,T\,n\simeq\clmDetBurstsPerN\,(n/\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3})$ bursts in $T=4$ yr, ${\sim}\clmDetRateLo$--$\clmDetRateHi$ per year for $n=\clmDetNMin$--$\clmDetNMax\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, each ${\sim}\clmDetBurstDuration$ s long. Averaged over time the same population gives $\Omega_{\rm GW}\simeq n\,E_{\rm rad}/[\rho_c(1+z_e)]$: with $E_{\rm rad}/M=\clmGwEnergySpiral$ (spiral) to $\clmGwEnergyFlyby$ (fly-by) at $M=\clmDetSeedMassMid\,M_\odot$, $n=\clmDetNMax\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ gives $\Omega_{\rm GW}\sim\clmDetOmegaHi$ at ${\sim}\clmDetOmegaFreq$ mHz and $n=\clmDetNMin$ gives ${\sim}\clmDetOmegaLo$, above LISA's four-year power-law-integrated sensitivity~\cite{thrane2013} ($\Omega\sim\clmDetLisaLo$--$\clmDetLisaHi$ near millihertz~\cite{lisa2017,caprini2018}) for $n\gtrsim(\clmDetPlsNLo$--$\clmDetPlsNHi)\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ at $\clmDetSeedMassMid$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$; at these rates, though, the signal is resolved bursts, never a stochastic background. The channel is therefore detectable by LISA for merger conversions of $\clmDetDetectMassLo$--$\clmDetDetectMassHi\,M_\odot$ with $n\gtrsim\clmDetOneBurstN\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$, where at least one burst falls in four years, and not for lone collapses, for $\clmDetSeedMassLo\,M_\odot$ seeds or for $n\lesssim\clmDetNMin\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$. The level and band scale as $1/(1+z_e)$, and $z_e\simeq\clmDetZConv$ is about the latest conversion epoch the seed arithmetic allows; a population converting at horizon re-entry would lie far below any band. Its spectrum is a pure burst population with no $f^{2/3}$ inspiral ramp (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:noinspiral}), unlike any direct-collapse scenario, whose seeds form hydrodynamically. Conversions at $\clmDetConvMassLo$--$\clmDetConvMassHi\,M_\odot$ land at $\clmDetLowBandLo$--$\clmDetLowBandHi\,\mu$Hz, below the LISA band.

The same events deposit negative energy. The encounters radiate scalar energy comparable to their gravitational energy, with negative sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:gw:scalar}), and an inflating throat sheds its support as a slower monopole of the same sign (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:fate}), so a primordial population fills the early universe with a diffuse negative-energy scalar background. It cannot be the dark energy, for three independent reasons (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(c)). Its equation of state is wrong: massless waves have $w=1/3$ and dilute as $a^{-4}$, losing a factor $(1+z_e)^4\simeq\clmDetDilution$ against $\Lambda$ by today, and with $V\equiv0$ no configuration does better, since the averaged $w=(\tfrac12\dot\phi^2-\tfrac16|\nabla\phi|^2)/(\tfrac12\dot\phi^2+\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2)$ of a massless field of either sign lies between $-1/3$ and $+1$---so the inflating branch's monopole cannot supply $w\approx-1$ either. Its sign is wrong: $\rho_\phi<0$ lowers $H^2$ where a positive $\Lambda$ raises it. And its size is wrong: $|\Omega_{\phi,0}|=n|E_\phi|/[\rho_c(1+z_e)]$ reaches $\Omega_\Lambda\simeq\clmOmegaLambda$~\cite{planck2018} only at $\Omega_{\rm WH}\equiv nM/\rho_c\simeq\clmDetOmegaWHLo$--$\clmDetOmegaWHHi$ for $z_e=\clmDetZConv$, $\clmDetOmegaWHDMLo$--$\clmDetOmegaWHDMHi$ times the dark matter, where the deposit would exceed the matter density $\clmDetDepositOverMatter$-fold at $z_e$---far past the cap $|\Omega_{\phi,0}|<\Omega_m/(1+z_e)\simeq\clmDetHubbleCap$ that $H^2>0$ imposes. Within the dark-matter budget the deposit reaches at most $\clmDetDepositCeiling$; for one seed per massive galaxy it is of the order of $\Omega_{\rm GW}$, and at $z_e$ a fraction $|\rho_\phi|/\rho_m=n|E_\phi|/\rho_{m,0}\simeq\clmDetBackreactLo$--$\clmDetBackreactHi$ of the matter density, so it does not back-react on the expansion.

Nothing in the astrophysical mass range survives (Sec.~\ref{sec:single:lifetime}), so every observed supermassive black hole is at most a descendant of this channel, and fits of a static Ellis--Bronnikov metric to the Sgr~A$^*$ or M87$^*$ shadows are excluded within this model. The claim is bounded: the collapse branch is a heavy-seed channel with three measured properties---prompt conversion at comparable mass, halo independence, no $\mu$-distortion---and one testable signature, its bursts. Whether it is \emph{the} channel behind the JWST population is a question of abundance, and the abundance is cheap: one conversion per little red dot, $n\simeq(\clmDetLrdNLo$--$\clmDetLrdNHi)\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ (the number densities of Refs.~\cite{greene2024,matthee2024}, summed over their bins), is a mass density $nM\simeq\clmDetLrdRhoLo$--$\clmDetLrdRhoHi\,M_\odot\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-3}$ at $\clmDetSeedMassLo$--$\clmDetSeedMassHi\,M_\odot$, a fraction $\clmDetLrdFracLo$--$\clmDetLrdFracHi$ of the dark matter (Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(c)). LISA would then record $\clmDetLrdBurstsLo$--$\clmDetLrdBurstsHi$ bursts in four years, so it tests the channel only if conversions outnumber the little red dots at least tenfold. The evolutions map a birth abundance $n(M)$ onto these observables; they do not fix it (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:open}).

\subsection{Scope}
\label{sec:discussion:open}

The results hold for one matter model and one family of data: a single minimally coupled ghost scalar, equal masses, one throat compactness ($m/a=1/2$), no spin, and superposed initial data whose defect is declared rather than removed (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:binary}). Horizons here are MOTSs on the evolved slices, which for matter violating the null energy condition need not lie inside event horizons (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:diagnostics}). The cosmology of Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion:seeds} takes the birth abundance $n(M)$ and the conversion epoch $z_e$ as inputs---Fig.~\ref{fig:heavy_seeds}(c) maps any choice onto its signals---and requires the ghost scalar to exist at that epoch.

\section{Reproducibility and cost}
\label{sec:repro}
\label{sec:cost}

Every evolution in Table~\ref{tab:matrix} is one line in a registry shipped with the results pack---its single knob against a named reference, its outcome, its caveats---and every figure is regenerated by a script in the pack from the archived diagnostic streams. The drainhole initial data, the seeds of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:seeds}, the orientation-corrected surface scan and the run parameter files accompany the registry; the code is \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn}. The evolutions are bit-reproducible on the production hardware: two builds differing only in a passive diagnostic give byte-identical physics streams over $\clmControlByteRows$ output rows.

Every evolution ran on one NVIDIA H100 \clmDetMemDevice\,GB device, with \texttt{GRTeclyn} built CUDA-native against AMReX~\clmDetAmrexVersion. At $\Delta t=\clmTimeStepFactor\,\Delta x$ a production arm ($L=\clmBoxL$, $\clmBaseN^3$ base, \clmProdLevels{} refinement levels) advances $\clmDetSpeedProdLo$--$\clmDetSpeedProdHi$ code units per hour, and a $t=100$ single-throat or head-on evolution takes $\clmDetCostTHundredLo$--$\clmDetCostTHundredHi$ h. Depth is the expense: level-5 arms run at $\clmDetSpeedLevelFiveLo$--$\clmDetSpeedLevelFiveHi$ units per hour---$\clmDetCostHeadonLevelFive$ h for the head-on's $t=0\to100$ and $\clmDetCostFlybyLevelFive$ h for the fly-by on the doubled box, which peaks at $\clmDetMemFlybyPeak$ of the $\clmDetMemDevice$ GB---and the production spiral's three-stage chain costs $\clmDetCostSpiralChain$ h. The $\clmDetRunsPhysics$ evolutions total $\clmDetGpuHours$ GPU-hours, recomputed from each run's logged speed and time span, a restarted leg from its checkpoint; the shakedown adds $\clmDetGpuHoursShakedown$ h.
