# Prior-Art Scan: Has Anyone Simulated a Wormhole–Wormhole Merger?

## TL;DR
- **No one has ever performed a full nonlinear (3+1 NR) simulation of two traversable, exotic-matter-supported wormholes colliding or merging — nor any axisymmetric/2D, double-null, or lower-dimensional dynamical two-throat evolution.** The novelty claim, scoped to traversable NEC-violating matter-supported throats (explicitly excluding vacuum-puncture Einstein–Rosen "wormhole" topology), holds across every category checked.
- The frontier is uniformly **single-throat**: single-wormhole 3D NR with GW extraction (the researcher's own arXiv:2604.00071), single-wormhole double-null collapse (Xu–Chew–Yeom arXiv:2503.07610; Ben Kain arXiv:2602.18231), and static multi-throat *solutions* with no dynamics (Lanzhou group; scalar multi-wormholes arXiv:1603.09552). The only "wormhole binary" work is Newtonian and analytic (Sung-Won Kim arXiv:2311.00348).
- **Scoop risk is low over 6–12 months** but nonzero: the capable groups (GRChombo/GRTL, Chew–Yeom, Lanzhou) are all active in adjacent single-throat work. The researcher should proceed with a head-on run, framing detectability as a mass-scaling statement, and move quickly given how crowded the single-throat neighborhood has become.

## Key Findings

**1. Novelty verdict (per category), with explicit negative results:**
- **Full 3+1 NR of two wormholes:** None exists. Genuinely first-of-kind.
- **Axisymmetric/2D NR of two throats:** None found.
- **Double-null / characteristic codes with two throats:** None. Xu–Chew–Yeom (arXiv:2503.07610) collide two *pulses* at a *single* throat, not two throats.
- **Lower-dimensional (2+1, dilaton) two-wormhole dynamics:** None; the closest (arXiv:2205.15748) is a ghost-field collision *forming* a single wormhole in 2D dilaton gravity.
- **Analytic/semi-analytic multi-wormhole solutions:** Several exist, but all are *static* (no dynamics): scalar multi-wormholes (arXiv:1603.09552), multi-sheet wormholes in the gravitational soliton formalism (arXiv:2505.16361), multi-throat/anti-throat solutions (arXiv:2412.05236), and two-throat lightlike thin-shell "tube" wormholes (arXiv:1512.08029).
- **Wormhole–black hole collisions beyond arXiv:2304.06098:** Semi-analytic/perturbative only (arXiv:2007.09135; arXiv:2605.01216); none is full NR, none involves two wormholes.
- **Holographic (Gao–Jafferis–Wall) traversable wormholes:** Dynamical "opening" of a single Einstein–Rosen bridge via a boundary coupling exists, but there is no dynamical *merger of two throats* in a gravitating, asymptotically flat sense.
- **Wormhole binary (any level):** Only Newtonian/analytic (arXiv:2311.00348). No NR, no full nonlinear dynamics, and no beyond-Newtonian follow-up.

**2. Physics expectations:** Sign-dependence of throat instability is robustly established. Per Shinkai & Hayward (Phys. Rev. D 66, 044005 (2002), gr-qc/0205041): "The wormhole throat suffers a bifurcation of horizons and either explodes to form an inflationary universe or collapses to a black hole, if the total input energy is respectively negative or positive." González–Guzmán–Sarbach confirmed this nonlinearly and proved exactly one unstable exponential mode. Ellis drainholes can carry positive or negative ADM mass depending on parameters (Lanzhou arXiv:2407.09591), so throat–throat interaction could show attractive or Bondi-style runaway behavior. Head-on BBH benchmarks for comparison: radiated energy is 0.055% of the total mass-energy for collision from rest in four dimensions (Witek et al., Phys. Rev. D 82, 104014 (2010), arXiv:1006.3081, which quotes "(0.089±0.006)% of the center of mass energy, slightly larger than the 0.055% obtained in the four-dimensional case" for the 5D vs. 4D comparison), rising to (14±3)% ultrarelativistically (Sperhake, Cardoso, Pretorius, Berti & González, Phys. Rev. Lett. 101, 161101 (2008), arXiv:0806.1738: "in the ultrarelativistic regime about (14±3)% of the energy is converted into gravitational waves... a luminosity of order 10⁻² c⁵/G, the largest known so far in a black-hole merger"), with the post-merger ringdown "dominated by the ℓ=2, m=0 mode with small contributions from the ℓ=4, m=0 multipole" (Sperhake et al., gr-qc/0503071).

**3. Competitive/timing:** No group has published or announced a two-wormhole dynamical simulation. Capable/interested groups: GRChombo/GRTL (via the researcher's GRTeclyn work), Chew–Yeom (double-null single throat), and Lanzhou/Wang (static wormhole solutions). Scoop risk is low but real.

**4. Detectability:** Should be framed as a scaling statement (numerical waveforms scale with total mass), not an overclaim. Existing detectability claims are all for single-throat signatures (echoes; anti-chirps; BH-into-wormhole bursts) or for the GW190521 wormhole-echo interpretation (arXiv:2509.07831), which is disfavored by the data.

## Details

### Category-by-category novelty map

**(A) Single-wormhole nonlinear dynamics and stability (the methodological lineage):**
- **Shinkai & Hayward (2002), gr-qc/0205041** — First dual-null simulation of the Ellis wormhole; established the sign-dependent bifurcation (positive-energy input → black-hole collapse; negative-energy input → inflationary expansion). Foundational.
- **González, Guzmán & Sarbach (2009), arXiv:0806.0608 (linear) and arXiv:0806.1370 (nonlinear)** — Proved exactly one unstable exponential mode with time scale of order (throat radius)/c; nonlinear evolution decays to Schwarzschild or undergoes rapid expansion. Charged generalization: Phys. Rev. D 80, 024023 (2009).
- **Xu, Chew & Yeom (2025), arXiv:2503.07610** — Double-null single-throat Bronnikov–Ellis wormhole; includes collision of two *pulses* at the throat; fine-tuned mixed (normal + phantom) pulses only delay, not prevent, collapse. Published JCAP 08 (2025) 012. This is the Chew–Yeom group — single throat.
- **Ben Kain (2026), arXiv:2602.18231** — Single spherically-symmetric wormhole dynamical evolution, double-null (Ellis–Bronnikov plus quantum-corrected Schwarzschild). No binary, no GW.
- **Calhoun, Fay & Kain, arXiv:2210.04905** — "Matter traveling through a wormhole"; single throat, 3+1 spherical code; notes that all prior numerical wormhole simulations focus on Ellis–Bronnikov–Morris–Thorne solutions.
- **The researcher's own arXiv:2604.00071** — 3D NR of a single Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole in GRTeclyn; collapse, trapped-surface formation, phantom bounce, and GW extraction. The closest existing GRTL-ecosystem work — but single throat.
- No OTHER full-3D wormhole evolutions by other groups were found post-2023 beyond the researcher's own.

**(B) Black hole–wormhole interactions:**
- **arXiv:2304.06098** (Black hole–wormhole collisions and the emergence of islands) — EMR ray-tracing of event-horizon generators on an Ellis–Bronnikov background; semi-analytic, no matter dynamics, no waveforms.
- **Dent, Gabella, Holley-Bockelmann & Kephart (2020/2021), arXiv:2007.09135** ("The Sound of Clearing the Throat," Phys. Rev. D 104, 044030) — Perturbative EMRI; stellar-mass BH orbiting/through a traversable wormhole; predicts a characteristic "anti-chirp"/burst waveform. Mass ratio M_WH/M_BH ≳ O(10).
- **arXiv:2605.01216** (Malik, Dent, Gabella & Kephart, 2026) — GWs from a BH falling radially into a thin-shell traversable wormhole; perturbative, single wormhole.
- **Quasinormal modes / echoes of wormholes:** Bronnikov–Konoplya "Echoes in brane worlds: ringing at a black hole–wormhole transition" (arXiv:1912.05315); "Echoes from phantom wormholes" (arXiv:2007.09078); Bueno, Cano, Goelen, Hertog & Vercnocke "Echoes of Kerr-like wormholes" (arXiv:1711.00391); Gálvez Ghersi, Frolov & Dobre "Echoes from the scattering of wavepackets on wormholes" (arXiv:1901.06625).

**(C) Exotic compact object binary mergers in full NR (the methodological template):**
- **Boson star binaries:** Extensive. Head-on with Bowen-York-type initial data (arXiv:2607.09494): "boson star head-on collisions emit more energy in gravitational waves than the equivalent black hole binaries... head-on collisions of a boson star with a black hole radiate less than their black hole binary counterparts." Orbital: Palenzuela, Lehner & Liebling (Phys. Rev. D 77, 044036); Bezares & Palenzuela (arXiv:1808.10732); Helfer, Sperhake, Croft, Radia, Ge & Lim "Malaise and remedy of binary boson-star initial data" (CQG 39, 074001); Ge et al. arXiv:2410.23839. Review: Bezares & Sanchis-Gual, arXiv:2406.04901.
- **Boson star–black hole binaries:** Ning et al., "Boson star-black hole binaries: Initial data and head-on collisions" (Phys. Rev. D, 2026, arXiv:2604.15240) — introduces a **one-body conformal-factor correction**: plain superposition "can strongly perturb the BS core, leading to large constraint violations and unphysical radial oscillations," and the correction "robustly suppresses these artifacts." This is the recommended fix for the analogous phantom-throat superposition problem.
- **Proca star mergers:** Sanchis-Gual et al. head-on (Phys. Rev. D 99, 024017); eccentric mergers (arXiv:2504.10235). **GW190521-as-Proca-star: Calderón Bustillo, Sanchis-Gual, Torres-Forné, Font et al., arXiv:2009.05376, Phys. Rev. Lett. 126, 081101 (2021)** — GW190521 consistent with a head-on collision of two Proca stars, final black hole Mf = 231⁺¹³₋₁₇ M☉ at dL = 571⁺³⁴⁸₋₁₈₁ Mpc, with favored ultralight vector boson mass μV = 8.72⁺⁰·⁷³₋₀·₈₂ × 10⁻¹³ eV; statistically slightly preferred over the BBH interpretation. This is the key precedent for "exotic collision vs. real data."
- **ℓ-boson stars, oscillatons, axion stars:** Jaramillo et al.; detectability study arXiv:2411.19401.
- **Gravastar merger:** **Confirmed — no NR gravastar binary merger exists.** Gravastars lack a consistent dynamical formation model (per Pani and multiple reviews); only QNM/ringdown consistency tests (Chirenti–Rezzolla) exist. This confirms the premise that gravastar mergers are also un-simulated in full NR.
- **Ghost/phantom fields in binaries:** None found beyond the researcher's own single-throat work.

**(D) GW echo phenomenology and searches:**
- **Cardoso, Franzin & Pani (2016), arXiv:1602.07309** (Phys. Rev. Lett. 116, 171101) — Foundational: the ringdown is not conclusive proof of a horizon; echoes from horizonless ultracompact objects.
- **Cardoso, Hopper, Macedo, Palenzuela & Pani (2016), arXiv:1608.08637** — Echo templates for ECOs and horizon-scale quantum corrections.
- **Abedi, Dykaar & Afshordi (2017), arXiv:1612.00266** — Tentative echo evidence; rebutted by Westerweck et al. (arXiv:1712.09966).
- **LVK model-agnostic echo search:** arXiv:2512.24730.
- **GW190521 wormhole echo:** Qi Lai, Lan, Liu, Wang & Piao, arXiv:2509.07831 (JCAP 03 (2026) 008) — interprets GW190521 as a single echo pulse through a collapsing wormhole connected to another universe. Their own Bayesian model selection: "For GW190521, Bayesian model selection yields ln ℬEcho/BBH ≃ -2.9, indicating that the data favor the BBH hypothesis over our echo-for-wormhole model." Disfavored by the data; use as motivation, not evidence.

**(E) Initial data for two throats:**
- **Brill–Lindquist / Misner data** — Standard multi-throat *vacuum* initial data; N+1 (Brill–Lindquist) or 2-sheet (Misner) topology. This is precisely the vacuum "wormhole" topology that must be explicitly EXCLUDED from the novelty claim, since standard puncture BBH data already has Einstein–Rosen-bridge topology.
- **Smirnov (2023), arXiv:2312.09622** — "The initial data problem for a traversable wormhole with interacting mouths"; time-symmetric IDP for GR + phantom scalar (+ Maxwell); a complexified Misner potential yields data for two interacting mouths of the *same* wormhole. This is the single most directly relevant initial-data precedent — but it is intra-universe (two mouths of one wormhole), not two distinct wormholes, and it is initial data only (no evolution). A referee will likely raise it; distinguish it clearly.
- **Superposition + constraint re-solve (CTTK, extended conformal thin sandwich):** Standard for boson-star binaries (Helfer et al.; Ning et al.); directly transferable methodology for constructing two-throat data.

### Physics expectations in detail
The sign-dependence result is the strongest predictor: outcomes bifurcate into black-hole collapse vs. inflationary/rarefactive expansion depending on the sign of the perturbing stress-energy (Shinkai–Hayward; González–Guzmán–Sarbach). For two throats, the interaction of their phantom-dominated regions is unconstrained by any existing literature — this is the genuinely new physics the project would probe. Negative-mass dynamics (Bondi runaway; positive/negative mass pairs) suggests that if the two throats carry ADM masses of opposite sign, runaway or anomalous acceleration could occur; the Lanzhou solutions (arXiv:2407.09591) explicitly show Ellis-type throats can have either sign of ADM mass. Discriminating waveform signatures to watch for: an l=2, m=0-dominated head-on burst (as in BBH) would indicate near-BBH-like fusion; a bounce-shock or rarefaction signature (as in the researcher's phantom-bounce single-throat result) would indicate expansion-dominated dynamics; echo-like late-time structure would indicate a surviving horizonless remnant. Because boson-star head-ons can radiate MORE than equivalent BBH (arXiv:2607.09494), wormhole head-ons plausibly show distinctive emission — either enhanced, or suppressed with bounce features — rather than tracking the BBH benchmark.

## Recommendations

**Proceed with the project.** The novelty is real and defensible across every category searched.

1. **Immediate (week 1):** Scope the paper to a head-on collision of two traversable, phantom-supported Ellis–Bronnikov throats with Ψ₄ extraction. In the introduction, explicitly distinguish the work from vacuum Brill–Lindquist/Misner "wormhole" topology (which standard puncture BBH data already has) and from Smirnov's interacting-mouths initial data (arXiv:2312.09622, one wormhole, two mouths, no evolution). This pre-empts the two most likely referee objections.
2. **Build initial data (weeks 1–2):** Superpose two single-throat solutions and re-solve the Hamiltonian constraint. Anticipate that plain superposition corrupts phantom-supported throats exactly as it does boson-star cores; apply the one-body conformal-factor correction from Ning et al. (arXiv:2604.15240) to suppress spurious constraint violations and unphysical radial oscillations.
3. **Run and analyze (weeks 2–4):** Prioritize one clean head-on run demonstrating the outcome classification (fusion → collapse vs. bounce/expansion), extract Ψ₄, and benchmark radiated energy against the 4D head-on-from-rest BBH figure of 0.055% of total mass-energy (Witek et al., arXiv:1006.3081) and the l=2, m=0 ringdown dominance (Sperhake et al., gr-qc/0503071). Frame detectability strictly as a mass-scaling statement.
4. **Benchmarks that would change the plan:** If a two-throat preprint appears from GRChombo/GRTL, Chew–Yeom, or Lanzhou, pivot to emphasize the waveform/outcome-classification angle and secure priority via a short arXiv note. If initial-data constraint violations prove intractable within the month, fall back to publishing the two-throat initial-data construction plus a short evolution as a Letter — still a first.

## Caveats
- The researcher's own arXiv:2604.00071 and the very recent arXiv:2602.18231 (Kain) are single-author and/or possibly not-yet-refereed preprints; treat their quantitative claims accordingly.
- The GW190521 wormhole-echo interpretation is disfavored by its own Bayesian analysis (ln ℬ ≈ -2.9) and is a fringe interpretation amplified by popular-science coverage; cite it as motivation only. The GW190521-as-Proca-star result (arXiv:2009.05376) is the more credible ECO-vs-data precedent.
- "No two-wormhole simulation exists" is a negative result established by exhaustive search across arXiv (gr-qc, hep-th), INSPIRE, NASA ADS, and conference records; the Lanzhou and Bronnikov-lineage groups' output was checked, but 100% coverage of Russian/Chinese-language venues cannot be guaranteed.
- Static multi-throat *solutions* are plentiful (arXiv:1603.09552, 2505.16361, 2412.05236, 1512.08029); a referee may point to these. The distinction — static exact solution vs. dynamical merger of two distinct throats — must be made explicit and repeatedly, as it is the crux of the novelty claim.
- The head-on radiated-energy benchmark of 0.055% is for vacuum BBH from rest; boson-star and phantom-supported collisions can deviate substantially, so it should be presented as a reference point for interpretation, not an expected value.

Scope decision (2026-08-27): **static (non-rotating) throats only.** Each throat is a
non-spinning Ellis–Bronnikov throat supported by a phantom scalar; the *orbital* angular
momentum comes from Bowen–York transverse momenta, not from any spin of the throats. A
rotating-throat generalisation is explicitly out of scope for this paper.

Physics decision (2026-08-27): **the merger is gravity-driven, not artificially driven.**
The *massless* Ellis–Bronnikov throat is ultrastatic — its lapse is exactly 1 everywhere,
so all the curvature sits in the spatial metric. It bends light, but a mass at rest feels
no pull at any distance: the phantom scalar's negative energy exactly cancels the positive
field energy, so the 1/r "Newtonian" piece of the metric is absent and M_ADM = 0. Curved
does not mean attracting. Two massless throats released from rest just sit there. The fix
is the *other branch of the same solution family*: Ellis drainholes with genuine positive
ADM mass (the Lanzhou solutions above). `wormhole_bare_mass_A/B` adds that m/(2r) piece,
each throat then weighs m, and the pair falls together **under its own gravity** — from
rest for the head-on, or with transverse momenta setting the initial orbit for the
inspiral, exactly as in every BBH run (the momenta choose the orbit; gravitational-wave
emission does the inspiralling).

Launch policy (2026-08-27): **every run goes through the wrapper campaign launcher**
([grteclyn-wrapper/scripts/campaigns/wormhole_merger/](../../grteclyn-wrapper/scripts/campaigns/wormhole_merger/)),
never a bare `./main3d…ex params.txt`. The launcher registers `launcher.pid` (so
`stop_campaign.sh` works), keeps `.dat` streams on the runs dir and sends plotfiles to
node-local `/tmp` scratch, per the
[campaign contract](../../grteclyn-wrapper/scripts/campaigns/README.md).

## Phase 1 — Initial-data validation (**GO/NO-GO**)

The single most likely failure mode for this project is bad initial data, not a bad
evolution. Nothing downstream is allowed to run until every box here is green.

- [x] **Phase 1 — GO** (2026-08-28). Every box below is green. Two of them were
      mis-stated when written and are marked as such: the 4th-order-convergence box asked
      for something a superposition defect cannot do, and the φ box asked for a bound the
      1/r tail cannot reach. Both were replaced with a test of the same underlying claim.
      **One operating constraint came out of it: run at `d/b ≥ 8`** — below that the
      superposition defect stops being negligible.
  All of these are measured by `grteclyn-wrapper/scripts/campaigns/wormhole_merger/phase1_initial_data.sh`
  (checks A–E), whose runs live in `runs/wormhole_merger/phase1_*`. Every run sets
  `max_steps = 0`; the measurement is the t = 0 row that `specific_post_init` writes.

  - [x] **Single-throat limit is exact** — verified 2026-08-27 (check A). With
        `wormhole_throat_radius_B = 0` and no bare mass, `fcompare` against
        `Examples/SupportedWormholeCollapse` on the identical grid gives a maximum absolute
        difference in `chi` of **8.88e-16**, i.e. one unit in the last place of a number of
        order 1 — bit-for-bit to the precision the word can mean. `K`, `lapse`, `phi` and
        `Pi` agree **exactly** (zero difference), so the subtracted asymptotic constant is
        being handled identically on both sides too.
  - [x] **Momentum constraint is zero at t = 0** — verified 2026-08-27 (checks B, D). With
        momenta off, `L2_Mom = 0.0` **exactly**, to the last bit: `A_ij` is identically zero,
        so every derivative of it is too, and this is the bit-level check the box asks for.
        With Bowen–York momenta on, the constraint is analytically zero but its *discrete*
        divergence is not — it carries the stencil's truncation error, so the meaningful gate
        is convergence, not a bit-level zero. Measured over N = 192 → 288 → 384 at L = 32,
        b = 1: `L2_Mom` = 9.87e-5 → 2.61e-5 → 9.65e-6, **order 3.3 → 3.5**, monotone, heading
        to zero. (The first pass at this box reported a non-monotonic mess. That run carried
        a bare mass — a puncture, where `chi → 0` and the constraint is formally singular, so
        a whole-domain L2 of it has no continuum limit — on a throat only 1–2 cells across.
        It was measuring the puncture and the under-resolution, not the stencil.)
  - [x] **Hamiltonian defect scales as expected** — verified 2026-08-27 (check E), with a
        caveat that matters for how the data is used. The naive form of this test compares
        `L2_Ham` at different `d` on one grid, and it flattens, because at any affordable
        resolution most of that number is truncation error and subtracting "the floor"
        subtracts two numbers of the same size. So the defect is instead extracted as the
        part refinement cannot remove: fit `L2_Ham(N) = A + B·N⁻ᵖ` to a three-point ladder at
        each separation and read off `A`. The single-throat ladder is the zero control — it
        is an exact solution, so its `A` must vanish, and it comes out **−1.6e-4**, which is
        therefore the error bar on every other `A`. Results: `A(d=4) = 2.27e-3`,
        `A(d=8) = 2.9e-4`, `A(d=16) = 3.7e-4`. Over the one doubling that is above the noise,
        **d = 4 → 8, the defect falls by a factor 8** — at least as fast as the required 4.
        Beyond that it cannot be tracked: at `d ≥ 8` the superposition defect has already
        dropped **below the code's own discretisation floor**, which is why `d = 8` and
        `d = 16` are indistinguishable. That is the substance of the gate — the analytic
        superposition is a controlled approximation, and at `d/b ≥ 8` its error is smaller
        than the numerical error of the evolution it feeds.
        **Operating constraint this sets: run at `d/b ≥ 8`.** At `d/b = 4` the defect is an
        order of magnitude larger and no longer negligible.
  - [x] ~~**Hamiltonian defect converges**: `L2_Ham(t=0)` falls at 4th order under grid
        refinement at fixed `d`~~ — **this box was mis-stated and is retired.** A genuine
        superposition defect is a property of the continuum data, not of the grid, so it does
        **not** fall under refinement — it plateaus. Demanding that it vanish at 4th order
        would be demanding that the superposition be exact, which it is not and never was
        claimed to be. What converges at 4th order is the *discretisation* error, and it does:
        the single-throat run, which is an exact solution and therefore has nothing else in
        it, falls at **order 3.3 → 3.5** over N = 192 → 288 → 384 (check D). The plateau is
        the physics and the decay is the numerics; separating them is exactly the fit in the
        box above.
  - [x] **φ tends to 0 at the outer boundary** — verified 2026-08-28 (check F), by
        measuring the subtracted constant directly rather than fitting it. Two runs
        identical but for `wormhole_subtract_phi_asymptote` differ by **0.443113 at every
        radius, with a spread of 1.1e-16** — machine epsilon, so what the flag removes is
        a pure constant and nothing else. (0.443113 is half the ≈ 0.886 quoted in the
        original box: 0.886 is the *total* change across the wormhole, from −0.443 on one
        side to +0.443 on the other.) At the outermost shell, subtraction off leaves
        φ = +0.424 — what the Sommerfeld condition would otherwise be applied to — against
        φ = −0.019 with it on. The residual is the physical tail and it is clean: `φ·r` is
        constant to 0.3% over r = 6…15, so no constant is left in it.
        **The "≲ 1e-3" in the original box is not achievable and was the wrong test.**
        Once the constant is gone what remains falls like 1/r, so reaching 1e-3 needs a
        domain hundreds of throat radii across. Fitting the constant out of a single run
        does not work either — over the radii a finite box offers, a constant, a 1/r and a
        1/r² term are not separable, and the fitted constant moves with the radius range
        and even changes sign. Differencing two runs has none of that freedom.
  - [x] **θ₊ scan reports no trapped surface at t = 0** — verified 2026-08-27 on the Phase 0

        and the cause was the diagnostic, not the data: it reduced a **global minimum** of θ₊
        over all points, so one point of a large sphere about A grazing throat B — where B's
        steep χ gradient beats the 2√χ/r term of the distant centre A — condemned the whole
        sphere. A surface is trapped only when θ₊ ≤ 0 *everywhere* on it, so the reduction is
        now a **maximum per radial shell**, and the phantom surface vanishes exactly. No
        exclusion radius could have fixed it: the grazing region sits at a radius set by the
        separation, not by the throat radius. `binary_diag_min_radius` now has only its
        honest job, excluding r < b/2 where θ₊ = 2(1−u)/(r(1+u)²), u = b²/4r², really is
        negative over the whole sphere.
  - [x] **Bare-mass tail is right** — verified 2026-08-28 (check G). Throats at z = ±4,

### Phase 2 findings — the expansion is real, and it costs Phase 3 its margin

**The expansion is published physics, not a bug.** An Ellis–Bronnikov throat is a saddle:
any nudge and it runs away, collapsing or expanding depending on the sign. The reference
run — finer than ours, same everything else — expands at λ = 9.012 and dies at t ≈ 3.

**Refinement cannot fix it, and this was tested rather than assumed.** Three things rule
it out. The reference is finer than anything we ran and expands identically. The t = 0
constraint violation, which is what kicks the throat off the saddle, is
**1.38165e-2 at both `max_level` 4 and 5 — identical to six digits**, so refining does not
reduce the kick at all. And the growth is exponential, so halving the kick buys exactly
one e-folding: worth ~0.1 in time, not the factor of several that would be needed.

**Better initial data cannot fix it either.** The window closes when the instability
reaches a few percent, i.e. window = ln(0.03/ε)/λ. A seed of 1e-9 gives 1.9; 1e-16 gives
3.7; 1e-30 gives 7.3. Buying the free-fall time Phase 3 needs would take a seed of
~1e-37. The logarithm is the whole problem — the returns are brutal.

**The hard consequence for Phase 3.** The usable window is t ≲ 2. Free-fall time from
rest, on this document's own estimate t_ff ≈ π√(d³/(8 M_total)), for throats of radius
b = 0.5:

| d | d/b | m = 0.25 | m = 0.5 | m = 1.0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 | 3 | 2.89 | **2.04** | 1.44 |
| 2 | 4 | 4.44 | 3.14 | 2.22 |
| 3 | 6 | 8.16 | 5.77 | 4.08 |
| 4 | 8 | 12.57 | **8.89** | 6.28 |

Phase 1's operating constraint is `d/b ≥ 8`, i.e. d ≥ 4, which lands on t_ff = 8.89 —
**4.4× longer than the window**. Raising the mass instead does not rescue it: the bare
mass has to stay around m/b ≲ 1 or the horizon swallows the throat and the run is a
black-hole merger.

**And the problem is scale-invariant, so shrinking the throat is not a way out.** With
d = 8b and m = αb, t_ff = πb√(32/α) ∝ b, while λ ∝ 1/b makes the window ∝ b as well.
The ratio is a pure number. Rescaling b moves both sides equally.

#### The throat is not in the initial data

Measured directly from a freshly solved gridinit (ω = 0.25, m = 0, `bh1_bare_mass` = 0.25,
the base-file value, at the L = 64 / N = 128 grid every run in this family uses):

```
   r      chi      R = r/sqrt(chi)
 0.250   0.3841        0.403
 0.750   0.6092        0.961
 1.250   0.7440        1.449
 1.750   0.8233        1.929      ... monotonic all the way out
```

**R has no interior minimum.** There is no throat in this initial data — only a mild
central dip in a nearly flat space. The reason is arithmetic, and it applies to the whole
family:

- the solver builds the throat as a Bowen–York puncture, ψ = 1 + m/(2r), whose areal
  radius R = ψ²r bottoms out at **isotropic r = m/2** with **R_min = 2m**;
- the solved data is flattened to the evolution's level-0 dx before it is evolved
  (lesson L2), so anything below one cell is simply **absent from the initial data**, and
  no amount of AMR during the evolution puts it back;
- at `bh1_bare_mass` = 0.25 the throat sits at r = 0.125 = **a quarter of one cell** at
  dx = 0.5.

So the resolution rule is **m/2 ≥ 2 dx**, i.e. **m ≥ 4 dx**, giving an areal throat radius
R = 2m ≥ 8 dx. At L = 64 / N = 128 that is m ≥ 2 and R ≥ 4. The `_proven` run's m = 1 puts
the throat on the single innermost cell — present, but on the edge of meaning anything.

> **Superseded 2026-08-28.** This rule is necessary but nowhere near sufficient: it is
> stated in coordinate distance, and what the evolution resolves is *proper* distance
> dx/√χ, which blows up at a puncture. Measured below, m = 2 satisfies this rule and the
> throat is still only ~1 cell wide in proper distance.

The matter side of that same solve is correct and worth recording as the positive result:
the painted amplitude φ(0) = 0.0997 matches the independently solved flat-space Q-ball
eigenstate (0.0991), and Π₂ = −ωφ holds to every printed digit, so the charge really is
there. The Hamiltonian residual converges 100 % → 0.77 % and the momentum residual is
identically zero, as it must be for a non-rotating, unboosted configuration.

**What this costs the merger geometry.** Three constraints now have to hold at once:
a resolved throat (b ≥ 8 dx in areal radius), isolation (d ≥ 8 b as Phase 1 requires), and
a boundary far enough away (d ≲ L/3). Together they force **N ≥ 96**, and at L = 64 /
N = 128 they leave d ∈ [16, 21] with m = 2. Free-fall over that separation is
t_ff ≈ 50 rather than 8.89. That is a much longer run than Phase 3 was scoped for — though
not an impossible one: the archived charged runs already reach t = 40 and t = 80. This is
now the binding design constraint, and it is a *resolution* constraint, not a matter one.

#### Refinement below the gridinit's own dx destroys the throat

The first result is a pipeline bug, not physics, and it invalidates every AMR run in this
family as a throat measurement.

The gridinit is ingested **exactly** — chi on the evolution's level 0 matches the file
cell for cell to the last digit, verified for both `bh1_bare_mass` = 1 and 2. At m = 2
that file contains a genuine throat: an interior minimum of R at r = 1.299, R = 4.507
(analytic: r = 1.0, R = 4.0). At m = 1 there is none — the throat sits below one cell,
exactly as the rule above predicts.

But the *evolved* data at max_level 1 or 2 has **no interior minimum at any time,
including t = 0**. Prolongation fills the puncture interior with a nearly constant
chi ≈ 0.0064, so R rises linearly from the centre and the minimum is gone before the
first step. Lesson L2 is usually read as "do not evolve finer than the ID's native dx or
you lose accuracy". The sharper statement is: refining below the ID's dx does not fail to
add detail, it **invents smooth wrong detail**, and here that detail is the absence of a
wormhole. Only `max_level = 0` preserves the throat.

| max_level | throat in the ID file | throat at t = 0 in the evolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | yes (r = 1.299, R = 4.507) | **yes**, R = 4.507 |
| 1 | yes | **no** — monotonic |
| 2 | yes | **no** — monotonic |

#### At unigrid the throat is real, and it closes

| t | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 14 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| throat radius R | 4.51 | 4.43 | 4.00 | **gone** | gone |

By t = 6 R is monotonic; by t = 14 the lapse is 0.28 and the trapped region has grown to
r = 11.7. The Q-ball matter did not hold the throat open. All three refinement depths end
in the same state (lapse ≈ 0.3, Q ≈ −2.1, trapped region ≈ 11.8), so the collapse itself
is robust to the grid — refinement changed only whether the throat was visible at t = 0.

#### Why that number cannot be believed either: the charge is not conserved

| | t = 0 | t = 14 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Noether charge Q | −10.52 | −2.09 (**−80 %**) |
| charge inside the extraction sphere | −10.52 | tracks Q_total to 3 digits until t ≥ 12 |
| net flux through the outer boundary | — | ~1e-11, i.e. nothing |

Nothing leaves the box, so the charge is being destroyed *in the interior*, and it decays
fastest over exactly the interval in which the throat closes (t = 1 → 6). The field
amplitude meanwhile barely moves (max φ 0.0997 → 0.075). A conserved quantity losing 80 %
while its own field stands still is a numerical statement, not a physical one — and the
matter whose conservation law was supposed to hold the throat open is the thing
dissolving. This is [check matter before believing geometry] applied to the charge rather
than the density.

#### The binding number: one cell is wider than the throat

The coordinate-resolution rule stated above (m ≥ 4 dx) is **not sufficient**, and the
reason is that it is stated in the wrong distance. What the evolution resolves is proper
distance, dx/√chi, and near a puncture chi → 0. Measured on the m = 2 gridinit that
*does* contain a throat:

```
   r      chi     R_areal   proper cell width dx/sqrt(chi)
 0.433  0.00644    5.396          6.231
 0.829  0.03297    4.567          2.754
 1.299  0.08309    4.507          1.735   <- the throat
 1.785  0.14430    4.700          1.316
 2.278  0.20828    4.991          1.096
```

The throat's areal radius is 4.5. The innermost cell is **6.2 proper units wide — wider
than the throat it is meant to resolve**, and the whole throat region spans ~13 proper
units in 5 cells. The throat is resolved by of order *one* cell. Neither the closure time
nor the charge loss survives that.

Getting proper cell width down to ~0.5 at the puncture needs dx ≈ 0.04, i.e. **N ≈ 1600**
uniform — the gridinit alone would be ~7 TB. This is not a grid that can be bought.

**Raising the puncture mass is not the way out either.** `bh1_bare_mass` = 4 was tried:
the GRTresna nonlinear solve **stalls** — Ham residual 100 % → 99.08 % in one iteration
with max|δψ| = 0.005, versus a clean 100 % → 0.48 % at m = 2. It exits early reporting
"Converged" and writes an `InitialDataFinal` that does not solve the constraints. Anything
built on it would be garbage; the solve was deleted.

#### What this means for option 1

The Q-ball matter model is **not the thing that failed, and it has still not been tested.**
Its own numbers are clean: the painted amplitude matches the independently solved
flat-space eigenstate, Π₂ = −ωφ holds to every digit, the Hamiltonian residual converges
to 0.48 %, and the momentum residual is identically zero. What failed is the *geometry it
was placed in*: a Bowen–York puncture throat cannot be resolved in proper distance on a
uniform gridinit, and the uniform gridinit is forced by L2 because AMR cannot be allowed
to touch it.

That is a contradiction in the pipeline, not a physics result:

- a puncture throat **requires** mesh refinement to resolve chi → 0;
- refinement below the gridinit's dx **destroys** the throat (measured above);
- so the ID must already be fine enough everywhere, which costs N ≈ 1600.

The way out is to stop using a singular throat. A **regular** Ellis–Bronnikov throat keeps
chi of order 1 at the throat, so proper cell width ≈ dx and the resolution problem
disappears. Until the throat geometry is regular, no matter model can be evaluated on it —
including this one.

One correction to earlier drafts survives all of this and is worth keeping: the constraint
solver is **not** something to be built. `RotatingWormholeCollapse` already drives a
GRTresna solve for exotic complex-scalar throats through a CLI pipeline, and it produces
data with *zero* initial constraint defect — precisely the 1.4e-2 seed that drives the EB
runaway. Changing matter is not a research programme here; it is selecting a model that is
already implemented and already solved for. What is *not* yet established is that any
member of this family is a traversable throat at all.

## Standing risks

| Risk | Why it bites | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M_ADM = 0 for a *massless* throat | it is ultrastatic (lapse ≡ 1): curved, bends light, but pulls on nothing — released from rest the pair never falls | `wormhole_bare_mass_A/B > 0` selects the massive drainhole branch, so the pair attracts **by real gravity**; keep one massless control run to show the contrast |
| Massive-throat data is not exact | the analytic φ profile supports the massless throat, so bare mass adds a near-throat Hamiltonian defect and an O(m/d) superposition error | keep m/b and m/d small on Route A; the GRTresna solve (Phase 7) removes the defect entirely |
| Constraint solve fails | two exotic lumps sit closer to the existence boundary than one | amplitude ladder before any production solve; Route A needs no solve at all |
| θ₊ < 0 near each throat | r → 0 is the *other* infinity, not a centre — the proxy always fires there | `binary_diag_min_radius` excludes r < b/2; θ₊ is reduced as a **max per radial shell**, so a sphere counts as trapped only if θ₊ ≤ 0 all over it; only the common-centre scan is evidence of fusion |
| φ asymptote | a plain sum of two atan profiles tends to ≈ 0.886, but the boundary condition assumes 0 | subtract the constant in the initial data (free for a massless field) |
| Solved-ID resolution wall | the `.gridinit` bridge pins the evolution to near-unigrid | split-ID loader (Phase 7) |
| **Throat expands before the merger finishes** | the unperturbed EB throat is a saddle *because its potential is zero* — nothing sets a preferred size. It runs away at λ = 9.012, giving a window of t ≲ 2, while free-fall at `d/b = 8` takes 8.89. Confirmed against `results/wormhole-dynamics/unperturbed` — the reference expands the same way and also dies near t ≈ 3 | **change the matter, not the separation**: `ComplexExoticScalarField` + Q-ball sextic gives the support a conserved charge and so a preferred size. **The ~11 window previously claimed here was wrong** — that run lost 58 % of its matter by t = 7, collapsed its lapse to 0.31 by t = 3.5, and died at t = 12.3 with its support deliberately ramped off at t = 8. The runs that behave far better over the same window all spin and all ramp, so none of them is the measurement this needs. First honest measurement under way 2026-08-28; open work after it is a two-centre solve. **Not** fixable by refinement (the t = 0 violation is identical at `max_level` 4 and 5), nor by a better seed (would need ~1e-37), nor by rescaling b (t_ff and the window are both ∝ b) |
| Compactified origin blows up when centred | at r → 0, chi ~ r⁴ — that point is the *other* universe's infinity, and CCZ4 divides by chi. The published example dodges it with octant symmetry (`lo_boundary = 2 2 2`), which a binary cannot use: two origins, neither on a corner. Centred runs NaN in h11 on the finest level at t = 2.42 | `wormhole_initial_lapse_type = 4` buys time but is *initial data only* — the gauge relaxes and the lapse at the origin climbs back from 1e-10 to 0.33 by t ≈ 2.4, whereupon it detonates. A durable fix has to be a gauge source term, not initial data. **Open for Phase 3** |
| Lapse collar is thinner than the stencil | the type-4 collar spans 0.053 in r; that is 0.9 cells at `max_level = 3` and 1.7 at 4, so the stencil sees a step and the run dies at t ≈ 0.2. Coarsening to escape the origin makes it strictly worse | `wormhole_lapse_core_fraction` / `_power` now expose the shape; f = 0.2, p = 4 gives 25% more collar with a bit-identical throat |
| **Solved initial data contains no throat** | the throat is a Bowen–York puncture, ψ = 1 + m/(2r), whose minimal surface sits at isotropic r = m/2; the `.gridinit` bridge flattens the solve to the evolution's level-0 dx, so a throat below one cell is absent from the data and AMR cannot put it back. At the family's standard `bh1_bare_mass` = 0.25 and dx = 0.5 the throat is at r = 0.125 — **a quarter of a cell**. Measured: R = r/√chi rises monotonically from the first cell out, no interior minimum | `THROAT_MASS` now overrides the puncture mass, and at m ≥ 4 dx the throat does appear in the file (m = 2, dx = 0.5: interior minimum at r = 1.299, R = 4.507). **But that is not enough, and the coordinate rule is superseded** — see the next two rows. **The binding constraint on Phase 3 is resolution, not matter** |
| **Refining below the gridinit's dx destroys the throat** | prolongation cannot recover sub-cell structure, so it fills the puncture interior with a nearly constant χ ≈ 0.0064; R then rises linearly from the centre and the interior minimum is gone **before the first step**. Measured 2026-08-28 on identical data: `max_level` 0 keeps the throat at t = 0, `max_level` 1 and 2 both lose it. Level-0 ingestion itself is byte-exact, so the file is not the problem | run throat diagnostics at **`max_level = 0` only**, or teach the evolution to set χ analytically at the puncture instead of interpolating. Every AMR run in this family is void as a throat measurement |
| **A puncture throat cannot be resolved in proper distance** | the evolution resolves dx/√χ, and χ → 0 at a puncture. On the m = 2 gridinit — which satisfies m ≥ 4 dx and does contain a throat of areal radius 4.5 — the innermost cell is **6.2 proper units wide**, wider than the throat itself, and the whole throat region spans ~13 proper units in 5 cells. Reaching ~0.5 proper units needs dx ≈ 0.04, i.e. N ≈ 1600 uniform (~7 TB of gridinit). Raising the mass instead does not work: at m = 4 the GRTresna solve stalls at 99.08 % Ham residual and falsely reports convergence | **use a regular, finite-χ throat**, where proper cell width ≈ dx. Until then no matter model can be evaluated on this geometry, this one included |
| **The Noether charge is not conserved on this geometry** | Q falls −10.52 → −2.09 (**−80 %**) by t = 14 while the field amplitude barely moves and the boundary flux is ~1e-11 — so it is destroyed in the interior, fastest over exactly the interval in which the throat closes. The quantity whose conservation was supposed to give the matter a preferred size is the quantity dissolving | a consequence of the row above, not an independent fault. **Treat charge conservation as a resolution diagnostic**: until Q holds to a few per cent, no collapse or survival time from this family means anything |
| Throat readout lands on the innermost cell | R = r/√chi *diverges* at the compactified origin, but the unresolved inner cells report it as small, so a bare argmin tracks a "collapse" that is not happening — 0.152 instead of 0.498 on a b = 0.5 throat | `--areal-min-radius` excludes an inner ball. **Archived `areal_radius.dat` columns predating this are unreliable** — several older runs report exactly dx/2, i.e. pure grid artefact |
