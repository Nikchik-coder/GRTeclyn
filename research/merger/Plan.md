# Prior-Art Scan: Has Anyone Simulated a Wormhole–Wormhole Merger?

## TL;DR
- **No one has ever performed a full nonlinear (3+1 NR) simulation of two traversable, exotic-matter-supported wormholes colliding or merging — nor any axisymmetric/2D, double-null, or lower-dimensional dynamical two-throat evolution.** The novelty claim, scoped to traversable NEC-violating matter-supported throats (explicitly excluding vacuum-puncture Einstein–Rosen "wormhole" topology), holds across every category checked.
- The frontier is uniformly **single-throat**: single-wormhole 3D NR with GW extraction (the researcher's own arXiv:2604.00071), single-wormhole double-null collapse (Xu–Chew–Yeom arXiv:2503.07610; Ben Kain arXiv:2602.18231), and static multi-throat *solutions* with no dynamics (Lanzhou group; scalar multi-wormholes arXiv:1603.09552). The only "wormhole binary" work is Newtonian and analytic (Sung-Won Kim arXiv:2311.00348).
- **Scoop risk is low over 6–12 months** but nonzero: the capable groups (GRChombo/GRTL, Chew–Yeom, Lanzhou) are all active in adjacent single-throat work. The researcher should proceed with a head-on run, framing detectability as a mass-scaling statement, and move quickly given how crowded the single-throat neighborhood has become.

## Key Findings

**1. Novelty verdict (per category), with explicit negative results:**
- **Full 3+1 NR of two wormholes:** None exists. Genuinely first-of-kind.
- **Axisymmetric/2D NR of two throats:** None found.
- **Double-null / characteristic codes with two throats:** None. Xu–Chew–Yeom (arXiv:2503.07610) collide two *pulses* at a *single* throat, not two throats.
- **Lower-dimensional (2+1, dilaton) two-wormhole dynamics:** None; the closest (arXiv:2205.15748) is a ghost-field collision *forming* a single wormhole in 2D dilaton gravity.
- **Analytic/semi-analytic multi-wormhole solutions:** Several exist, but all are *static* (no dynamics): scalar multi-wormholes (arXiv:1603.09552), multi-sheet wormholes in the gravitational soliton formalism (arXiv:2505.16361), multi-throat/anti-throat solutions (arXiv:2412.05236), and two-throat lightlike thin-shell "tube" wormholes (arXiv:1512.08029).
- **Wormhole–black hole collisions beyond arXiv:2304.06098:** Semi-analytic/perturbative only (arXiv:2007.09135; arXiv:2605.01216); none is full NR, none involves two wormholes.
- **Holographic (Gao–Jafferis–Wall) traversable wormholes:** Dynamical "opening" of a single Einstein–Rosen bridge via a boundary coupling exists, but there is no dynamical *merger of two throats* in a gravitating, asymptotically flat sense.
- **Wormhole binary (any level):** Only Newtonian/analytic (arXiv:2311.00348). No NR, no full nonlinear dynamics, and no beyond-Newtonian follow-up.

**2. Physics expectations:** Sign-dependence of throat instability is robustly established. Per Shinkai & Hayward (Phys. Rev. D 66, 044005 (2002), gr-qc/0205041): "The wormhole throat suffers a bifurcation of horizons and either explodes to form an inflationary universe or collapses to a black hole, if the total input energy is respectively negative or positive." González–Guzmán–Sarbach confirmed this nonlinearly and proved exactly one unstable exponential mode. Ellis drainholes can carry positive or negative ADM mass depending on parameters (Lanzhou arXiv:2407.09591), so throat–throat interaction could show attractive or Bondi-style runaway behavior. Head-on BBH benchmarks for comparison: radiated energy is 0.055% of the total mass-energy for collision from rest in four dimensions (Witek et al., Phys. Rev. D 82, 104014 (2010), arXiv:1006.3081, which quotes "(0.089±0.006)% of the center of mass energy, slightly larger than the 0.055% obtained in the four-dimensional case" for the 5D vs. 4D comparison), rising to (14±3)% ultrarelativistically (Sperhake, Cardoso, Pretorius, Berti & González, Phys. Rev. Lett. 101, 161101 (2008), arXiv:0806.1738: "in the ultrarelativistic regime about (14±3)% of the energy is converted into gravitational waves... a luminosity of order 10⁻² c⁵/G, the largest known so far in a black-hole merger"), with the post-merger ringdown "dominated by the ℓ=2, m=0 mode with small contributions from the ℓ=4, m=0 multipole" (Sperhake et al., gr-qc/0503071).

**3. Competitive/timing:** No group has published or announced a two-wormhole dynamical simulation. Capable/interested groups: GRChombo/GRTL (via the researcher's GRTeclyn work), Chew–Yeom (double-null single throat), and Lanzhou/Wang (static wormhole solutions). Scoop risk is low but real.

**4. Detectability:** Should be framed as a scaling statement (numerical waveforms scale with total mass), not an overclaim. Existing detectability claims are all for single-throat signatures (echoes; anti-chirps; BH-into-wormhole bursts) or for the GW190521 wormhole-echo interpretation (arXiv:2509.07831), which is disfavored by the data.

## Details

### Category-by-category novelty map

**(A) Single-wormhole nonlinear dynamics and stability (the methodological lineage):**
- **Shinkai & Hayward (2002), gr-qc/0205041** — First dual-null simulation of the Ellis wormhole; established the sign-dependent bifurcation (positive-energy input → black-hole collapse; negative-energy input → inflationary expansion). Foundational.
- **González, Guzmán & Sarbach (2009), arXiv:0806.0608 (linear) and arXiv:0806.1370 (nonlinear)** — Proved exactly one unstable exponential mode with time scale of order (throat radius)/c; nonlinear evolution decays to Schwarzschild or undergoes rapid expansion. Charged generalization: Phys. Rev. D 80, 024023 (2009).
- **Xu, Chew & Yeom (2025), arXiv:2503.07610** — Double-null single-throat Bronnikov–Ellis wormhole; includes collision of two *pulses* at the throat; fine-tuned mixed (normal + phantom) pulses only delay, not prevent, collapse. Published JCAP 08 (2025) 012. This is the Chew–Yeom group — single throat.
- **Ben Kain (2026), arXiv:2602.18231** — Single spherically-symmetric wormhole dynamical evolution, double-null (Ellis–Bronnikov plus quantum-corrected Schwarzschild). No binary, no GW.
- **Calhoun, Fay & Kain, arXiv:2210.04905** — "Matter traveling through a wormhole"; single throat, 3+1 spherical code; notes that all prior numerical wormhole simulations focus on Ellis–Bronnikov–Morris–Thorne solutions.
- **The researcher's own arXiv:2604.00071** — 3D NR of a single Ellis–Bronnikov wormhole in GRTeclyn; collapse, trapped-surface formation, phantom bounce, and GW extraction. The closest existing GRTL-ecosystem work — but single throat.
- No OTHER full-3D wormhole evolutions by other groups were found post-2023 beyond the researcher's own.

**(B) Black hole–wormhole interactions:**
- **arXiv:2304.06098** (Black hole–wormhole collisions and the emergence of islands) — EMR ray-tracing of event-horizon generators on an Ellis–Bronnikov background; semi-analytic, no matter dynamics, no waveforms.
- **Dent, Gabella, Holley-Bockelmann & Kephart (2020/2021), arXiv:2007.09135** ("The Sound of Clearing the Throat," Phys. Rev. D 104, 044030) — Perturbative EMRI; stellar-mass BH orbiting/through a traversable wormhole; predicts a characteristic "anti-chirp"/burst waveform. Mass ratio M_WH/M_BH ≳ O(10).
- **arXiv:2605.01216** (Malik, Dent, Gabella & Kephart, 2026) — GWs from a BH falling radially into a thin-shell traversable wormhole; perturbative, single wormhole.
- **Quasinormal modes / echoes of wormholes:** Bronnikov–Konoplya "Echoes in brane worlds: ringing at a black hole–wormhole transition" (arXiv:1912.05315); "Echoes from phantom wormholes" (arXiv:2007.09078); Bueno, Cano, Goelen, Hertog & Vercnocke "Echoes of Kerr-like wormholes" (arXiv:1711.00391); Gálvez Ghersi, Frolov & Dobre "Echoes from the scattering of wavepackets on wormholes" (arXiv:1901.06625).

**(C) Exotic compact object binary mergers in full NR (the methodological template):**
- **Boson star binaries:** Extensive. Head-on with Bowen-York-type initial data (arXiv:2607.09494): "boson star head-on collisions emit more energy in gravitational waves than the equivalent black hole binaries... head-on collisions of a boson star with a black hole radiate less than their black hole binary counterparts." Orbital: Palenzuela, Lehner & Liebling (Phys. Rev. D 77, 044036); Bezares & Palenzuela (arXiv:1808.10732); Helfer, Sperhake, Croft, Radia, Ge & Lim "Malaise and remedy of binary boson-star initial data" (CQG 39, 074001); Ge et al. arXiv:2410.23839. Review: Bezares & Sanchis-Gual, arXiv:2406.04901.
- **Boson star–black hole binaries:** Ning et al., "Boson star-black hole binaries: Initial data and head-on collisions" (Phys. Rev. D, 2026, arXiv:2604.15240) — introduces a **one-body conformal-factor correction**: plain superposition "can strongly perturb the BS core, leading to large constraint violations and unphysical radial oscillations," and the correction "robustly suppresses these artifacts." This is the recommended fix for the analogous phantom-throat superposition problem.
- **Proca star mergers:** Sanchis-Gual et al. head-on (Phys. Rev. D 99, 024017); eccentric mergers (arXiv:2504.10235). **GW190521-as-Proca-star: Calderón Bustillo, Sanchis-Gual, Torres-Forné, Font et al., arXiv:2009.05376, Phys. Rev. Lett. 126, 081101 (2021)** — GW190521 consistent with a head-on collision of two Proca stars, final black hole Mf = 231⁺¹³₋₁₇ M☉ at dL = 571⁺³⁴⁸₋₁₈₁ Mpc, with favored ultralight vector boson mass μV = 8.72⁺⁰·⁷³₋₀·₈₂ × 10⁻¹³ eV; statistically slightly preferred over the BBH interpretation. This is the key precedent for "exotic collision vs. real data."
- **ℓ-boson stars, oscillatons, axion stars:** Jaramillo et al.; detectability study arXiv:2411.19401.
- **Gravastar merger:** **Confirmed — no NR gravastar binary merger exists.** Gravastars lack a consistent dynamical formation model (per Pani and multiple reviews); only QNM/ringdown consistency tests (Chirenti–Rezzolla) exist. This confirms the premise that gravastar mergers are also un-simulated in full NR.
- **Ghost/phantom fields in binaries:** None found beyond the researcher's own single-throat work.

**(D) GW echo phenomenology and searches:**
- **Cardoso, Franzin & Pani (2016), arXiv:1602.07309** (Phys. Rev. Lett. 116, 171101) — Foundational: the ringdown is not conclusive proof of a horizon; echoes from horizonless ultracompact objects.
- **Cardoso, Hopper, Macedo, Palenzuela & Pani (2016), arXiv:1608.08637** — Echo templates for ECOs and horizon-scale quantum corrections.
- **Abedi, Dykaar & Afshordi (2017), arXiv:1612.00266** — Tentative echo evidence; rebutted by Westerweck et al. (arXiv:1712.09966).
- **LVK model-agnostic echo search:** arXiv:2512.24730.
- **GW190521 wormhole echo:** Qi Lai, Lan, Liu, Wang & Piao, arXiv:2509.07831 (JCAP 03 (2026) 008) — interprets GW190521 as a single echo pulse through a collapsing wormhole connected to another universe. Their own Bayesian model selection: "For GW190521, Bayesian model selection yields ln ℬEcho/BBH ≃ -2.9, indicating that the data favor the BBH hypothesis over our echo-for-wormhole model." Disfavored by the data; use as motivation, not evidence.

**(E) Initial data for two throats:**
- **Brill–Lindquist / Misner data** — Standard multi-throat *vacuum* initial data; N+1 (Brill–Lindquist) or 2-sheet (Misner) topology. This is precisely the vacuum "wormhole" topology that must be explicitly EXCLUDED from the novelty claim, since standard puncture BBH data already has Einstein–Rosen-bridge topology.
- **Smirnov (2023), arXiv:2312.09622** — "The initial data problem for a traversable wormhole with interacting mouths"; time-symmetric IDP for GR + phantom scalar (+ Maxwell); a complexified Misner potential yields data for two interacting mouths of the *same* wormhole. This is the single most directly relevant initial-data precedent — but it is intra-universe (two mouths of one wormhole), not two distinct wormholes, and it is initial data only (no evolution). A referee will likely raise it; distinguish it clearly.
- **Superposition + constraint re-solve (CTTK, extended conformal thin sandwich):** Standard for boson-star binaries (Helfer et al.; Ning et al.); directly transferable methodology for constructing two-throat data.

### Physics expectations in detail
The sign-dependence result is the strongest predictor: outcomes bifurcate into black-hole collapse vs. inflationary/rarefactive expansion depending on the sign of the perturbing stress-energy (Shinkai–Hayward; González–Guzmán–Sarbach). For two throats, the interaction of their phantom-dominated regions is unconstrained by any existing literature — this is the genuinely new physics the project would probe. Negative-mass dynamics (Bondi runaway; positive/negative mass pairs) suggests that if the two throats carry ADM masses of opposite sign, runaway or anomalous acceleration could occur; the Lanzhou solutions (arXiv:2407.09591) explicitly show Ellis-type throats can have either sign of ADM mass. Discriminating waveform signatures to watch for: an l=2, m=0-dominated head-on burst (as in BBH) would indicate near-BBH-like fusion; a bounce-shock or rarefaction signature (as in the researcher's phantom-bounce single-throat result) would indicate expansion-dominated dynamics; echo-like late-time structure would indicate a surviving horizonless remnant. Because boson-star head-ons can radiate MORE than equivalent BBH (arXiv:2607.09494), wormhole head-ons plausibly show distinctive emission — either enhanced, or suppressed with bounce features — rather than tracking the BBH benchmark.

## Recommendations

**Proceed with the project.** The novelty is real and defensible across every category searched.

1. **Immediate (week 1):** Scope the paper to a head-on collision of two traversable, phantom-supported Ellis–Bronnikov throats with Ψ₄ extraction. In the introduction, explicitly distinguish the work from vacuum Brill–Lindquist/Misner "wormhole" topology (which standard puncture BBH data already has) and from Smirnov's interacting-mouths initial data (arXiv:2312.09622, one wormhole, two mouths, no evolution). This pre-empts the two most likely referee objections.
2. **Build initial data (weeks 1–2):** Superpose two single-throat solutions and re-solve the Hamiltonian constraint. Anticipate that plain superposition corrupts phantom-supported throats exactly as it does boson-star cores; apply the one-body conformal-factor correction from Ning et al. (arXiv:2604.15240) to suppress spurious constraint violations and unphysical radial oscillations.
3. **Run and analyze (weeks 2–4):** Prioritize one clean head-on run demonstrating the outcome classification (fusion → collapse vs. bounce/expansion), extract Ψ₄, and benchmark radiated energy against the 4D head-on-from-rest BBH figure of 0.055% of total mass-energy (Witek et al., arXiv:1006.3081) and the l=2, m=0 ringdown dominance (Sperhake et al., gr-qc/0503071). Frame detectability strictly as a mass-scaling statement.
4. **Benchmarks that would change the plan:** If a two-throat preprint appears from GRChombo/GRTL, Chew–Yeom, or Lanzhou, pivot to emphasize the waveform/outcome-classification angle and secure priority via a short arXiv note. If initial-data constraint violations prove intractable within the month, fall back to publishing the two-throat initial-data construction plus a short evolution as a Letter — still a first.

## Caveats
- The researcher's own arXiv:2604.00071 and the very recent arXiv:2602.18231 (Kain) are single-author and/or possibly not-yet-refereed preprints; treat their quantitative claims accordingly.
- The GW190521 wormhole-echo interpretation is disfavored by its own Bayesian analysis (ln ℬ ≈ -2.9) and is a fringe interpretation amplified by popular-science coverage; cite it as motivation only. The GW190521-as-Proca-star result (arXiv:2009.05376) is the more credible ECO-vs-data precedent.
- "No two-wormhole simulation exists" is a negative result established by exhaustive search across arXiv (gr-qc, hep-th), INSPIRE, NASA ADS, and conference records; the Lanzhou and Bronnikov-lineage groups' output was checked, but 100% coverage of Russian/Chinese-language venues cannot be guaranteed.
- Static multi-throat *solutions* are plentiful (arXiv:1603.09552, 2505.16361, 2412.05236, 1512.08029); a referee may point to these. The distinction — static exact solution vs. dynamical merger of two distinct throats — must be made explicit and repeatedly, as it is the crux of the novelty claim.
- The head-on radiated-energy benchmark of 0.055% is for vacuum BBH from rest; boson-star and phantom-supported collisions can deviate substantially, so it should be presented as a reference point for interpretation, not an expected value.

        subtracts two numbers of the same size. So the defect is instead extracted as the

        superposition is a controlled approximation, and at `d/b ≥ 8` its error is smaller
        than the numerical error of the evolution it feeds.

### Phase 2 findings — the expansion is real, and it costs Phase 3 its margin

**The expansion is published physics, not a bug.** An Ellis–Bronnikov throat is a saddle:
any nudge and it runs away, collapsing or expanding depending on the sign. The reference
run — finer than ours, same everything else — expands at λ = 9.012 and dies at t ≈ 3.

**Refinement cannot fix it, and this was tested rather than assumed.** Three things rule
it out. The reference is finer than anything we ran and expands identically. The t = 0
constraint violation, which is what kicks the throat off the saddle, is
**1.38165e-2 at both `max_level` 4 and 5 — identical to six digits**, so refining does not
reduce the kick at all. And the growth is exponential, so halving the kick buys exactly
one e-folding: worth ~0.1 in time, not the factor of several that would be needed.

**Better initial data cannot fix it either.** The window closes when the instability
reaches a few percent, i.e. window = ln(0.03/ε)/λ. A seed of 1e-9 gives 1.9; 1e-16 gives
3.7; 1e-30 gives 7.3. Buying the free-fall time Phase 3 needs would take a seed of
~1e-37. The logarithm is the whole problem — the returns are brutal.

**The hard consequence for Phase 3.** The usable window is t ≲ 2. Free-fall time from
rest, on this document's own estimate t_ff ≈ π√(d³/(8 M_total)), for throats of radius
b = 0.5:

| d | d/b | m = 0.25 | m = 0.5 | m = 1.0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 | 3 | 2.89 | **2.04** | 1.44 |
| 2 | 4 | 4.44 | 3.14 | 2.22 |
| 3 | 6 | 8.16 | 5.77 | 4.08 |
| 4 | 8 | 12.57 | **8.89** | 6.28 |

Phase 1's operating constraint is `d/b ≥ 8`, i.e. d ≥ 4, which lands on t_ff = 8.89 —

mass has to stay around m/b ≲ 1 or the horizon swallows the throat and the run is a
black-hole merger.

**And the problem is scale-invariant, so shrinking the throat is not a way out.** With
d = 8b and m = αb, t_ff = πb√(32/α) ∝ b, while λ ∝ 1/b makes the window ∝ b as well.
The ratio is a pure number. Rescaling b moves both sides equally.

### Why bare mass is required — the arithmetic behind the physics decision

The scope decision above states that the merger must be gravity-driven. This is why it
cannot be otherwise. For the massless throat the conformal factor is

```
psi = sqrt(1 + b²/4r²) = 1 + b²/(8r²) + O(r⁻⁴)
```

with **no 1/r term**, hence M_ADM = 0. Newtonian attraction *is* the 1/r piece of the
time-time metric, and this solution has neither, so "curved" and "attracts" come apart:
two massless throats released from rest sit there indefinitely, and there is no bound
orbit at any separation. Residual tidal-type interactions fall off much faster than 1/d²
and cannot bind anything.

`wormhole_bare_mass_A/B` adds the puncture-like m/(2r) piece of the massive Ellis
drainhole branch. That sets the two run modes and their control:

- **Head-on (Phase 3):** released from rest, zero momenta. Newtonian free-fall time
  t_ff ≈ π√(d³/(8 M_total)).
- **Spiral (Phase 4):** transverse Bowen–York momenta set the initial quasi-circular
  orbit, `P_t ≈ m√(M_total/d)/2` for the equal-mass pair. The momenta only *choose the
  orbit*; gravitational-wave emission drives the inspiral.
- **Massless control:** one cheap m = 0 run demonstrating the pair does *not* fall.

Near a throat the two 1/r behaviours combine, psi → (b + m)/(2r), so the coordinate
inversion — and the wormhole topology — survives the added mass.

**Cost of the mass term.** The analytic φ profile supports the *massless* throat, so bare
mass introduces (i) a BBH-like O(m/d) superposition error between the bodies and (ii) a
near-throat Hamiltonian defect scaling with m/b. Both are Route-A-only defects; the
GRTresna solve (Route B / Phase 7) removes them.

### Initial data (Route A — analytic superposition)

`BinaryWormholeInitialData.hpp`, following the `BinaryBHInitialData` conventions:

```
psi   = 1 + [sqrt(1 + b_A²/4r_A²) − 1] + [sqrt(1 + b_B²/4r_B²) − 1]
          + m_A/(2 r_A) + m_B/(2 r_B)
chi   = psi⁻⁴,   h_ij = δ_ij,   K = 0,   Theta = 0
phi   = (1/√4π) Σ_X atan[(r_X − b_X²/4r_X)/b_X]  −  n_throats·(1/√4π)(π/2)
Pi    = 0
Â_ij  = Σ_X (3/2r_X²)[P_i n_j + P_j n_i − (δ_ij − n_i n_j) P·n]
A_ij  = chi^{3/2} Â_ij            (CCZ4 conversion, = psi⁻⁶ Â_ij)
```

Facts the validation phase (Phase 1 above) leans on:

- **Momentum constraint exact at t = 0.** K = 0 and Pi = 0 make the matter momentum
  density vanish, and superposed Bowen–York Â_ij is flat-space divergence-free. Any
  nonzero momentum-constraint output is a bug, not superposition error.
- **Hamiltonian defect is the entire error budget**: O(b²/d²) throat–throat (better
  than BBH puncture superposition, which is O(m/d), because a pure throat has no 1/r
  tail) + O(m/d) once bare masses are on + the near-throat massless-profile defect.
- **φ asymptote**: each atan → π/2, so a plain N-throat sum tends to N·(1/√4π)(π/2)
  ≈ 0.886 for N = 2, while `StateVariables` declares φ's asymptotic value as 0 and the
  Sommerfeld boundary uses that. `wormhole_subtract_phi_asymptote = 1` (default)
  removes the constant — exactly free for a massless field; `SimulationParameters`
  warns if it is combined with `phantom_mass ≠ 0`.
- **Single-throat regression mode**: `wormhole_throat_radius_B = 0` (with
  `bare_mass_B = 0`) removes object B entirely — the geometry, scalar and asymptote
  count all skip B — reproducing `SupportedWormholeCollapse` (Phases 1–2). A
  bare-mass-only puncture (b = 0, m > 0) is a plain Brill–Lindquist term, giving a
  cross-check against `BinaryBH`.

### Diagnostics

`collapse_diagnostics.dat` keeps the **unchanged 13-column single-throat contract**
(existing analysis scripts keep working). The binary information lives in its own
module/file, default off (SOLID rule):

`binary_throat_diagnostics.dat`, 17 columns — separation; barycentre + χ/lapse minima
per half-space (split by `binary_diag_axis`/`split_coord`); θ₊ and the outermost trapped
radius about throat A, throat B and their midpoint.

**How θ₊ is reduced, and why it matters.** A closed surface is trapped only when θ₊ ≤ 0
*everywhere on it*, so the scan bins points into radial shells about each centre and takes
the **maximum** of θ₊ per shell; the reported horizon radius is the outermost shell whose
maximum is ≤ 0. The first implementation reduced a global *minimum* over all points and
reported a huge phantom trapped surface at t = 0 (θ₊ = −1.26 at r = 5.02 for a pair at
d = 3.75): a single point of a large sphere about throat A grazing throat B sits in B's
steep χ gradient, which overwhelms the 2√χ/r term belonging to the distant centre A. That
artefact lives at a radius set by the *separation*, so no exclusion radius suppresses it —
the shell maximum removes it exactly, because the rest of that same sphere is far from
both throats and expanding. Shells the finest AMR level does not cover carry **no**
verdict (an empty shell must read "unknown", never "trapped"); if none is covered the
column holds the sentinel 1e30.

**θ₊ caveat that remains**: for an EB throat θ₊ = 2(1−u)/(r(1+u)²) with u = b²/4r², so
θ₊ < 0 over the *whole* sphere for r < b/2 — the coordinate inversion (r → 0 is the other
asymptotic end), not a horizon. `binary_diag_min_radius` (default max(b_A, b_B), i.e.
twice what is needed) excludes it. The common-centre scan raises its own cut to
sep/2 + min_radius automatically, so a sphere only counts as "common" once it encloses
both throats; only that scan going trapped is evidence of fusion.

Ψ₄: in-code `WeylExtraction` on the `BHAMR<2>` interpolator, same block as the
rotating example — modes (2,0), (2,1), (2,2), (4,0) at ≥ 2 radii.

### Parameter sanity net (`SimulationParameters::check_params`)

- error: b_A ≤ 0, b_B < 0, negative bare masses, coincident centres;
- warn: `subtract_phi_asymptote` with `phantom_mass ≠ 0`; separation ≤ 4·b_max
  (O(b²/d²) no longer small); m_total ≥ 0.2·d (O(m/d) no longer small);
- warn: **all of** m = 0, P = 0, support = 1 — "massless throats do not attract; the
  pair will not fall together" (the exact trap this design exists to avoid);
- warn: momentum on an absent object B (junk Bowen–York term);
- error: any of `wormhole_phi_{monopole_amplitude,perturbation_amplitude,perturbation_width}`
  still present — see below.

**No seeded scalar perturbation.** The single-throat example carries a Gaussian
`φ → φ + (A₀ + A_φ Y₂₀)exp(−r²/w²)` because picking the Shinkai–Hayward compressive or
rarefactive branch by hand *is* that experiment. A merger must not do this: the other
throat is the perturbation, and pre-destabilising the throats contaminates the very thing
the run measures — whether gravity alone brings them together, and what happens when they
meet. Each throat therefore starts in its own exact equilibrium. The three keys were
removed rather than defaulted to zero, and a params file that still sets one is rejected
with a message, so a stale file cannot silently mean something different from what it says.

## Standing risks

| Risk | Why it bites | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M_ADM = 0 for a *massless* throat | it is ultrastatic (lapse ≡ 1): curved, bends light, but pulls on nothing — released from rest the pair never falls | `wormhole_bare_mass_A/B > 0` selects the massive drainhole branch, so the pair attracts **by real gravity**; keep one massless control run to show the contrast |
| Massive-throat data is not exact | the analytic φ profile supports the massless throat, so bare mass adds a near-throat Hamiltonian defect and an O(m/d) superposition error | keep m/b and m/d small on Route A; the GRTresna solve (Phase 7) removes the defect entirely |
| Constraint solve fails | two exotic lumps sit closer to the existence boundary than one | amplitude ladder before any production solve; Route A needs no solve at all |
| θ₊ < 0 near each throat | r → 0 is the *other* infinity, not a centre — the proxy always fires there | `binary_diag_min_radius` excludes r < b/2; θ₊ is reduced as a **max per radial shell**, so a sphere counts as trapped only if θ₊ ≤ 0 all over it; only the common-centre scan is evidence of fusion |
| φ asymptote | a plain sum of two atan profiles tends to ≈ 0.886, but the boundary condition assumes 0 | subtract the constant in the initial data (free for a massless field) |
| Solved-ID resolution wall | the `.gridinit` bridge pins the evolution to near-unigrid | split-ID loader (Phase 7) |
| **Throat expands before the merger finishes** | the unperturbed EB throat is a saddle *because its potential is zero* — nothing sets a preferred size. Confirmed against `results/wormhole-dynamics/unperturbed` — the reference expands the same way and also dies near t ≈ 3 | **change the matter, not the separation**: `ComplexExoticScalarField` + Q-ball sextic gives the support a conserved charge and so a preferred size. **Tested 2026-08-28 and still unresolved** — the matter is clean but the puncture throat it sits on cannot be resolved, so the model returned no verdict; see [MatterDebug.md](MatterDebug.md). **The ~11 window previously claimed here was wrong** — that run lost 58 % of its matter by t = 7, collapsed its lapse to 0.31 by t = 3.5, and died at t = 12.3 with its support deliberately ramped off at t = 8.
