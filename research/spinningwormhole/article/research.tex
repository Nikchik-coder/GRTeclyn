\begin{abstract}
We investigate the stability of spinning Ellis--Bronnikov wormholes using fully nonlinear 3+1 numerical relativity simulations of the drainhole family, whose static member we evolved in our previous work. We construct the stationary metrics with a spectral solver that is exact to ${\sim}10^{-10}$ at slow spin and ${\sim}10^{-7}$ near the extremal limit, and validate it against the published symmetric family of Kleihaus and Kunz. In doing so we correct an inconsistency in a published field equation and map the drainhole family, for which no fast-spin solutions were known, up to $v_e=0.99$; the 3D initial data converge at fourth order. We find that rotation slows the radial instability and then changes its fate. Along the ladder of evolved spins the collapse $e$-fold time grows from 4.4 (static) to 8.0 at $24\%$ of the extremal angular momentum; at $29\%$ and $33\%$ the unperturbed throat turns and inflates instead of collapsing---the instability's other branch, with the seed's sign selecting the fate---and at $81\%$ the throat is stable to $t=150$ at three resolutions. The measured rate tracks the second-order prediction of Azad \emph{et al.} where both exist and extrapolates to zero at ${\approx}42\%$ of the extremal angular momentum. The unperturbed rotating collapse radiates an $\ell=2$, $m=0$ burst whose energy grows with spin, while the static collapse radiates nothing; second-order perturbative initial data instead seed the instability at the level of their own truncation error.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Ellis--Bronnikov wormhole, a throat held open by a phantom scalar field~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973,morris_thorne1988,visser1995}, is unstable: depending on the sign of the perturbation it collapses to a black hole or inflates, on a timescale set by the throat radius~\cite{gonzalez2009lin,gonzalez2009,shinkai2002,bronnikov2011,cremona2019}. Nonlinear 3+1 evolutions confirm both branches for the lone throat~\cite{shirokov2026} and carry them into binaries, whose every channel ends in collapse, inflation or a merged horizon within a few dynamical times~\cite{shirokov2026c}. If traversable wormholes are to persist anywhere, something must shut this instability off.

Rotation is the oldest candidate~\cite{matos2006}. Spinning Ellis--Bronnikov solutions are known perturbatively~\cite{kashargin2008} and, beyond perturbation theory, from the elliptic solver of Kleihaus and Kunz~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016}: a family that starts at the static throat and ends, at equatorial rotation velocity $v_e=1$, on an extremal Kerr black hole, the phantom field vanishing in the limit; no closed form exists~\cite{volkov2021}. On the stability side, Azad, Bl\'azquez-Salcedo, Khoo and Kunz~\cite{azad2023,azad2024} carried the radial mode to second order in spin: the static unstable mode weakens, with a positive $O(J^2)$ correction for every asymmetry, while for nearly symmetric wormholes a second unstable mode grows out of a static zero mode; the two cross near $30\%$ of the limiting angular momentum, where the quadratic approximation gives out, and the authors conjecture the modes merge and the instability disappears, as purely imaginary modes do in five dimensions~\cite{dzhunushaliev2013}. The charged analogue warns against optimism: there the merged modes continue as a complex pair, growth that oscillates, and the growth rate reaches zero only in the extremal limit~\cite{blazquez2025}. Which continuation rotation takes in four dimensions is exactly the question a perturbative method cannot answer, and no rotating wormhole has ever been evolved nonlinearly.

This paper builds the tools for that evolution and takes its first step. We construct two families of backgrounds as exact stationary solutions. The symmetric family ($\gamma=0$), massless when static, is the one with published data---the curves of Ref.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014} and the scalar charges of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}---and we use it to validate the solver and the 3D initial data across the spin range. The drainhole family ($\gamma=-\pi$), whose static member is the mass-1 drainhole evolved in Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}, has no published fast-spin data at all; it is the family we evolve. The solver is gated on identities it never imposes, a discipline that pays immediately: one of the published field equations is inconsistent (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12}), and second-order perturbative initial data turn out to seed the instability at their own error level (Sec.~\ref{sec:slow}). We then put the backgrounds on the AMR grid, verify them at fourth order, and evolve the drainhole along a ladder of nine spins (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). The result: rotation slows the collapse, as the perturbative sign predicts, and near $30\%$ of the extremal angular momentum the same mode changes fate---the unperturbed throat turns and inflates---while at $81\%$ the throat is stable over the full run.

Section~\ref{sec:model} gives the family and the corrected field equations; Sec.~\ref{sec:solver} the solver, the validation against the published data, and the map of the parameter space (Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds}); Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} the numerical setup and the runs (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}); Sec.~\ref{sec:slow} the evolutions; Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusions} the conclusions. Appendix~\ref{app:solver} holds solver details; Appendix~\ref{app:conv} the convergence of the evolutions at the two ends of the ladder. We use $G=c=1$; the evolutions use the code units of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}, $\eta_0=2$, in which the static drainhole has mass $M=1$.

\section{The spinning wormhole family}
\label{sec:model}

The matter model is that of our previous work~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}: Einstein gravity coupled to a phantom scalar field $\psi$,
\begin{equation}
S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(R+2\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\psi\,\partial_\nu\psi\right),
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
whose kinetic term has the reversed sign that makes $\psi$ a ghost. Varying the action with respect to the metric and to $\psi$ gives the field equations of the model [Eqs.~(2)--(3) of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}],
\begin{equation}
R_{\mu\nu}=-2\,\partial_\mu\psi\,\partial_\nu\psi,\qquad
\partial_\mu\!\left(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\psi\right)=0:
\label{eq:fieldeqs}
\end{equation}
Einstein's equations with the phantom's stress tensor as source, and the wave equation for the phantom on the curved background. Those papers evolved its best-known static wormhole, the Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole, and measured the instability that this paper asks rotation to cure. Spinning that wormhole up requires the stationary rotating solution of the same theory, which has to be constructed numerically, inside an \emph{ansatz}---an assumed form of the metric with stationarity and axisymmetry built in and a few unknown functions left for the field equations to determine. We take the ansatz of Refs.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016}, who first constructed these solutions,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
ds^2&=-e^{f}dt^2+p^2e^{-f}\Big[e^{\nu}\left(d\eta^2+h\,d\theta^2\right)\\
&\quad+h\sin^2\!\theta\left(d\varphi-\omega\,dt\right)^2\Big],
\end{split}
\label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
with $\eta\in(-\infty,\infty)$ and four unknown functions $f$, $p$, $\nu$, $\omega$ of $\eta$ and $\theta$ [Eq.~(4) of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}]. The field equation for $p$ decouples from the others, and its solution with asymptotically flat boundary conditions is $p=1$ [Eqs.~(5)--(6) of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}]; we set $p=1$ from here on. In the trivial limit $f=\nu=\omega=0$ the spatial metric is $d\eta^2+h\left(d\theta^2+\sin^2\!\theta\,d\varphi^2\right)$ with the fixed $h=\eta^2+\eta_0^2$, so $\sqrt{h}$ is the radius of the sphere at coordinate $\eta$---and by construction it never vanishes: it falls from $\eta^2$ at large $|\eta|$, bottoms out at $\eta_0$ at $\eta=0$, and rises again. The spacetime therefore has no center; a minimal sphere of radius set by $\eta_0$, the throat, joins the two asymptotically flat ends $\eta\to\pm\infty$. Choosing $h$ hard-wires this two-ended, horizonless topology into Eq.~\eqref{eq:metric}, while $f$, $\nu$, $\omega$ deform the geometry on top of it ($h$ also recurs as the weight in every equation below); conversely, $\eta_0\to0$ closes the throat and lets the same ansatz hold a black hole, which is how extremal Kerr enters in Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12}. Our previous wormhole sits inside this form: with $\omega=\nu=0$ and the closed-form $\arctan$ profile for $f$~\cite{chew2016}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:metric} is the static drainhole of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}, with $\eta_0=a$ its throat parameter and $\eta$ the isotropic radius used there, unfolded through the throat (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:id}); $f=0$ gives the massless Ellis wormhole. Substituting the ansatz~\eqref{eq:metric} into the field equations~\eqref{eq:fieldeqs} reduces them in three steps. We follow the reduction of Kleihaus and Kunz~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014}, given in full in Sec.~II of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}; equation numbers in brackets below refer to Ref.~\cite{chew2016}. First, the phantom field equation---the wave equation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fieldeqs}, which stationarity and axisymmetry reduce to a function of $\eta$ alone---integrates in closed form to $\psi=(D/\eta_0)\left[\arctan(\eta/\eta_0)-\pi/2\right]$ [Eqs.~(7)--(9)]: the scalar enters the rest of the problem only through its constant of integration $D$, the scalar charge. Second, because $\partial_\mu\psi$ points along $\eta$, the source term $-2\,\partial_\mu\psi\,\partial_\nu\psi$ survives only in the $\eta\eta$ Einstein equation; each other nontrivial component ($tt$, $t\varphi$, $\theta\theta$, $\varphi\varphi$, $\eta\theta$) is a statement about the metric functions alone, and these organize by order and role. Two are second-order elliptic equations,
\begin{align}
0={}&\partial_\eta\!\left(h s\,\partial_\eta f\right)+\partial_\theta\!\left(s\,\partial_\theta f\right)\nonumber\\
&-h s^3 e^{-2f}\!\left[h(\partial_\eta\omega)^2+(\partial_\theta\omega)^2\right],
\label{eq:feq}\\
0={}&\partial_\eta\!\left(h^2 s^3 e^{-2f}\partial_\eta\omega\right)\nonumber\\
&+\partial_\theta\!\left(h s^3 e^{-2f}\partial_\theta\omega\right),
\label{eq:omeq}
\end{align}
with $s\equiv\sin\theta$ [Eqs.~(10)--(11)]: Eq.~\eqref{eq:feq} is a Laplace-type equation for the redshift $f$, sourced by the squared gradients of the frame dragging---rotation gravitates---and Eq.~\eqref{eq:omeq} is a total divergence, the local form of angular-momentum conservation, which integrated over the domain forces the same $J$ through every $\eta=\mathrm{const}$ surface. The single off-diagonal component is only first order in $\nu$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
0&=-h s\,\partial_\eta f\,\partial_\theta f+h\cos\theta\,\partial_\eta\nu\\
&\quad+\eta s\,\partial_\theta\nu+h^2 s^3 e^{-2f}\partial_\eta\omega\,\partial_\theta\omega,
\end{split}
\label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}
so $\nu$ follows by integration once $f$ and $\omega$ are known [Eq.~(13)]. (The remaining diagonal components give a second-order equation for $\nu$, Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12}.) Third, the one sourced component, $R_{\eta\eta}=-2D^2/h^2$ [Eq.~(18)], rearranges into an algebraic identity for the charge [Eq.~(19)]: the metric functions fix $D^2$ at every point, so its constancy over the domain is a solution test that comes for free. Three unknown functions thus face more equations than they need; the Bianchi identities make the overdetermined set consistent on a true solution, and a numerical scheme must choose which subset to impose---a choice Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12} shows matters. The boundary conditions select the family: Minkowski at $\eta\to+\infty$, and $f\to\gamma$, $\omega\to\omega_\infty$ at $\eta\to-\infty$, so the constant $\gamma$ measures how unequal the two ends are and $\omega_\infty$ sets the spin.

A solution of this system is three functions and one number, $(f,\nu,\omega;D)$. Every physical quantity we quote---the columns of Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds}---is read off them directly~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016}:
\begin{itemize}
\item far from the throat the metric settles to the field of a point mass, $f\to-2\mu_+/\eta$ as $\eta\to+\infty$ and $f\to\gamma+2\mu_-/\eta$ as $\eta\to-\infty$, so the end masses $\mu_\pm$ are the $1/\eta$ coefficients of $f$;
\item likewise $\omega\to2J/\eta^3$, and the charges obey the Smarr-type relation $\mu_++\mu_-=2\,\omega_\infty J$~\cite{chew2016};
\item the throat is the sphere of minimal area, at $\eta_t=0$ when the two ends are equal ($\gamma=0$) and displaced toward the positive-mass end otherwise ($\eta_t=\eta_0/2$ for the static drainhole): its equatorial circumference gives the radius $R_e=e^{-f/2}\sqrt{h}\,\big|_{\eta_t,\,\theta=\pi/2}$, its meridians the polar radius $R_p$, its area the areal radius $R_A$---three numbers that coincide while the throat is round and split as rotation flattens it;
\item the throat is dragged at the angular velocity $\Omega=\omega\,\big|_{\eta_t,\,\theta=\pi/2}$, and $v_e=R_e\,\Omega$ is its equatorial linear velocity in units of $c$: the dimensionless spin label used throughout, 0 when static and 1 at the extremal Kerr endpoint.
\end{itemize}

We construct two one-parameter families, labelled by $\omega_\infty$ (equivalently $J$ or $v_e$):
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] \emph{the drainhole family}, $\eta_0=2$, $\gamma=-\pi$: its static member, with end masses $\mu_\pm=\pm1$, is the wormhole of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c} (the asymmetry $C=1$ of Refs.~\cite{azad2023,azad2024}).
\item[(b)] \emph{the symmetric family}, $\gamma=0$ ($C=0$), that of Refs.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016}: equal masses on both ends, the massless Ellis wormhole when static; for the 3D checks it is rescaled $\eta_0\,{=}\,1\to2$, so its throat sits on family (a)'s grid.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{An inconsistency in the published field equations}
\label{sec:model:eq12}

Refs.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016} close the system with a second-order equation for $\nu$, Eq.~(12) of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}. That equation is inconsistent with the rest. The scalar-free component $R_{\theta\theta}=0$, with the second derivatives of $f$ and $\omega$ eliminated by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:feq}--\eqref{eq:omeq}, gives instead
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
0&=\partial_\eta\!\left(h s\,\partial_\eta\nu\right)+s\,\partial_\theta^2\nu-\cos\theta\,\partial_\theta\nu+s\,(\partial_\theta f)^2\\
&\quad-h s^3 e^{-2f}\left[h(\partial_\eta\omega)^2+2(\partial_\theta\omega)^2\right],
\end{split}
\label{eq:nueq}
\end{equation}
where the printed form has $-2(\partial_\theta\omega)^2$ and no $s\,(\partial_\theta f)^2$ term. Extremal Kerr fits the ansatz~\eqref{eq:metric} in closed form (with $\eta_0=0$), and substituted symbolically, at 160-digit arithmetic, it satisfies Eqs.~\eqref{eq:feq}--\eqref{eq:constraint}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:nueq} and the $D^2$ identity exactly---while leaving a residue of up to $0.35$ (at $M=1$) in the printed $\nu$ equation alone. The error also shows dynamically: solutions of the printed elliptic system converge at every resolution, yet carry a violation of the unimposed Eq.~\eqref{eq:constraint} and $D^2$ identity that scales as $J^4$ and does not shrink with the grid---the fingerprint of a wrong continuous equation, not of numerics (first seen as a spread of $5.5\times10^{-7}$ in $D^2$ at $J=0.25$). Our solver imposes neither $\nu$ equation (Sec.~\ref{sec:solver}), and Eq.~\eqref{eq:nueq} holds on all 200 saved solutions of both families to $\le7\times10^{-8}$ of its largest term within 20 throat radii, where the printed form misses by up to $53\%$. Every published conclusion of Refs.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016} that we can test survives the correction (Sec.~\ref{sec:solver}, Appendix~\ref{app:solver}).

The solver turns the system of Sec.~\ref{sec:model} into numbers in five steps (Fig.~\ref{fig:solver}); Appendix~\ref{app:solver} gives the details. First, the infinite domain is made finite: the coordinate $x=(2/\pi)\arctan(\eta/\eta_0)$ maps both asymptotic ends to $x=\pm1$, and the unknowns $f$ and $\omega$ are expanded in Chebyshev polynomials of $x$ and even cosine modes of $\theta$. The expansion is spectral: for smooth solutions the error falls exponentially with the number of modes, so modest grids reach near machine precision. Second, the two elliptic equations~\eqref{eq:feq}--\eqref{eq:omeq} are solved for the expansion coefficients by Newton iteration---guess, linearize, correct---until their residual sits at round-off. Third, $\nu$ and the scalar charge are recovered without a second-order $\nu$ equation: the first-order constraint~\eqref{eq:constraint} and the $D^2$ identity together overdetermine $\nu$ and the single number $D^2$, so both are extracted as one linear least-squares problem---and the redundancy is a feature, since the leftover misfit measures the error. Fourth, each family is walked in spin: every converged solution seeds the Newton iteration at a slightly higher spin, with $J$ as the continuation parameter ($\omega_\infty$ is not monotone along a family; Appendix~\ref{app:solver}), and the grid refines automatically, from $48\times12$ up to $240\times60$, whenever the acceptance gates fail. Fifth---the point of the whole design---a solution is accepted only on identities the solver never imposes: the spread of $D^2$ over the domain ($\le10^{-11}$), the Smarr relation ($\le10^{-10}$), equality of the angular-momentum flux through the two ends ($\le1.3\times10^{-9}$), the asymptotic tails ($\le5\times10^{-12}$), and an independent, symbolically generated residual of the full Einstein equations, evaluated out to 20 throat radii: $1.0\times10^{-10}$ at $J=0.25$ and $4.2\times10^{-10}$ at $J=1$ against the static member's $2\times10^{-11}$ round-off floor---where the $O(J^2)$ perturbative data give $3\times10^{-5}$ and $1\times10^{-2}$ on the same meshes---staying near $10^{-9}$ up to $v_e\approx0.85$ and reaching ${\sim}10^{-7}$ at $v_e=0.91$--$0.99$. The high-spin $10^{-7}$ is round-off, not solution error: it does not shrink on finer grids, while the mass and scalar charge come out identical to 12 digits across them; and the test does catch genuine failures, scoring a deliberately under-resolved solution at $10^{-4}$, which the resolution check rejects. Beyond 20 throat radii the residual's normalization inflates round-off and the test is switched off; that outer region is covered by the resolution study, by the end charges, and by the 3D constraint checks of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}. The products: global charges stable to 10 digits across three grids and, for evolution, each background exported as a $1025\times65$ table whose bilinear interpolation error is $\le10^{-7}$ up to $v_e=0.9$.

Figure~\ref{fig:trends} and Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds} are the map of the parameter space the foundation papers left open, including the massive drainhole family, from which binaries can be built~\cite{shirokov2026c}. Two of its features matter later. First, both families violate the Kerr bound (Fig.~\ref{fig:trends}b), the symmetric family with $J/\mu_+^2\approx85$ already at $v_e=0.24$, falling monotonically to 1 at extremality since its static member is massless. A collapsing overspun throat cannot settle to a Kerr black hole of the same mass and spin, which makes these backgrounds sharp cosmic-censorship initial data. Second, the drainhole's negative far-end mass $\mu_-$ rises through zero at $v_e\approx0.7$ and turns positive: fast rotation un-does the static drainhole's negative-mass end.

\section{Numerical setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{3D initial data}
\label{sec:setup:id}

The stationary metric~\eqref{eq:metric} goes onto the Cartesian AMR grid through the isotropic radius $r$, $\eta=r-\eta_0^2/4r$: the far end $\eta\to-\infty$ compactifies to the puncture $r=0$, as for the static drainhole~\cite{shirokov2026}. The induced metric and the fields are read from the tables; the lapse and shift start from the stationary data, $\alpha=e^{f/2}$, $\beta^\varphi=-\omega$, so the extrinsic curvature $K_{ij}=(D_i\beta_j+D_j\beta_i)/2\alpha$ has only $r\varphi$ and $\theta\varphi$ components and the scalar momentum vanishes; at the puncture the frame rotates at $\omega_\infty$.

Three checks validate the pipeline. (i) At $t=0$ the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints converge at fourth order inside the throat region and are limited outside only by the tables' interpolation error (growing as $1/\Delta x^2$ and $1/\Delta x$ respectively), and the throat's ray radii converge to the solver's. We check this on the drainhole at $v_e=0.51$ on three grids (ray radii within $5\times10^{-5}$ at $N=192$, $L=64$) and, across the spin range, on the symmetric family at seven spins $v_e=0.24$--$0.99$ with three resolutions each (within $7\times10^{-5}$ at $v_e=0.24$ to $9\times10^{-4}$ at $0.99$, $N=256$). (ii) On the same symmetric set, the 3D ADM mass integral evaluated on the grid equals the same integral on the exact table metric to $\le10^{-12}$, and its Richardson extrapolation meets the solver's mass to $2\times10^{-5}$--$7\times10^{-4}$ (at worst $0.7\%$ relative, at $v_e=0.99$); the ADM angular momentum matches to between $2\times10^{-3}$ ($v_e=0.24$) and $2\times10^{-5}$ ($0.99$). (iii) Dynamically, the static drainhole run through this pipeline is a numerical twin of the production hold of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026c} over the full record: $t=0$ bit-identical, Hamiltonian norm within $5\times10^{-7}$ for all $t\le100$, and collapse milestones within one time unit.

\subsection{Evolution scheme}
\label{sec:setup:scheme}

Evolution uses \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn,amrex,grchombo} with the setup of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}: CCZ4~\cite{alic2012}, $1{+}\log$ slicing~\cite{bona1995}, Gamma-driver shift~\cite{alcubierre2003gauge}, fourth-order stencils, RK4, Kreiss--Oliger dissipation, and a floored conformal factor at the puncture. The timestep is $\Delta t=0.02\,\Delta x$ in every run. That value was adopted empirically in Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}; we traced what set it to a grid-scale mode pinned to the fixed coarse--fine interfaces of the nested refinement boxes, dissipation-starved at $\sigma=0.1$, whose growth rate rises with the timestep. Constraint norms evaluated per refinement level, with cells covered by finer levels masked, expose it by $t\approx10$; the domain-wide norm is blind to it until breakout. Raising the dissipation where the mode lives removes it---at $\sigma=0.4$ the interface-level norms sit $10^{2}$--$10^{4}$ below the $\sigma=0.1$ reference by $t=100$---but the larger timesteps this would permit do not transfer to the spinning backgrounds, which lose the far side by $t\approx22$--48 at $\Delta t=0.035$--$0.05\,\Delta x$. The production runs therefore take no speed-up, and the only scheme parameter that varies along the ladder is the dissipation (recipes S and F of Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). Its one measured side effect, a constant ${\sim}4$ time-unit advance of the collapse clock from the stronger initial smoothing, keeps rate comparisons at matched $\sigma$.

\subsection{Long evolutions: the outer boundary}
\label{sec:setup:boundary}

Evolutions that must outlive the outer-boundary crossing time $t\approx L$ need the boundary made harmless. Kreiss--Oliger dissipation cannot do it---its strength falls as the sixth power of wavelength, so the sponge shell of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c} is transparent to the long outgoing gauge pulse---and a flat-space Sommerfeld condition assumes asymptotics the wormhole does not have (mass 1--12 sits inside the box). Two changes in the same outer shell fix it. The Sommerfeld condition is imposed on the deviation of the evolved fields from the stationary background rather than on the fields themselves; and a damping layer can in addition drive every evolved field back to that background at rate $A$, $\partial_t u \to \partial_t u - A\,f(r)\,(u-u_{\rm bg})$, with $f(r)$ the sponge's quartic ramp and $u_{\rm bg}$ the run's own initial data, rebuilt exactly from the background tables; $A=0$ recovers the base scheme identically. The layer is validated on the static hold at $A=1$ against the reference evolution: $|K|$ falls two orders of magnitude across the shell, while everything inside it---the throat radii to four digits, the constraint norms, the near-zone ring---is unchanged, so it absorbs at all wavelengths without touching the interior. On the $L=64$ box, whose shell ($r=24$--32) contains the outermost extraction sphere, a side-by-side test showed the background-relative boundary alone keeps the outer levels cleaner, so those runs take the layer off; the $L=128$ boxes ($r=48$--64, outside every sphere) run with it on (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). With the boundary treated this way the $L=64$ runs, previously trusted only to the crossing time $t\approx64$, run to their stop times $t=100$--$150$, with trust windows set by the constraints instead. The same comparison shows the late-time growth previously attributed to boundary reflection is sourced by the collapse region itself, and is removed by the raised dissipation of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup:scheme}.

\subsection{The runs}
\label{sec:setup:runs}

Stability claims follow the triplet protocol of Ref.~\cite{evstafyeva2025}: a quoted growth must have an $e$-fold time that converges to a nonzero value across resolutions, constraints that converge at the code's order while it grows, and a gauge-invariant measure---the throat's areal radius, or the marginally outer-trapped surface (MOTS) found spectrally on every output---growing with it. Every run carries three nested refinement levels on the throat, and the box is picked by the rung's mass: $M_{\rm ADM}$ triples along the family (1.11 at $v_e=0.21$, 3.31 at $0.70$), so the face of the $L=64$ box recedes from $25\,M_{\rm ADM}$ of clearance at slow spin to under $10\,M_{\rm ADM}$ at $v_e=0.70$. Rungs up to $v_e=0.51$ therefore run on the $L=64$, $N=128$ grid of the static hold of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026c}, and rungs from $v_e=0.59$ on $L=128$, $N=256$ at the same finest $\Delta x$. $\Psi_4$ is extracted on spheres at $R=10$--30 ($L=64$) and $R=14$--45 ($L=128$); the signal is the $(2,0)$ mode. Resolution triplets anchor the two ends of the ladder (Appendix~\ref{app:conv}). Table~\ref{tab:ladder} lists every run, from the static reference through the $O(J^2)$ regime~\cite{azad2023,azad2024} to far beyond any perturbative result.

\emph{Perturbative initial data fail first.} Our first spinning holds used the $O(J^2)$ backgrounds of Ref.~\cite{azad2024} at $J=0.25$ and $J=1$ on the drainhole family. Both throats inflated where the static throat collapses---the same unstable mode with the opposite sign ($\tau=5.8$ and $5.6$). But expressed as the equivalent radial kick of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026c}, the push was $\varepsilon\approx-3\times10^{-5}$ at $J=0.25$ and $-7\times10^{-3}$ at $J=1$: growth by a factor ${\sim}230$ for a factor 4 in $J$, i.e.\ ${\sim}J^4$---the size of the $O(J^2)$ data's own truncation error, which the independent Einstein residual puts at $10^{-2}$ at $J=1$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:solver}). Second-order data seed the very instability under study at their own error level; nothing at slow spin can be concluded from them.

\emph{The collapse branch.} On exact data (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}) the verdict reverses, and the unperturbed throat collapses on the static mode, later the faster it spins: the areal deficit passes $1\%$ at $t=43.7$, 46.9, 51.4 and 60.2 along the rungs $v_e=0$--0.40, with $e$-fold times $\tau=4.4$, 5.0, 5.9, 8.0 (Fig.~\ref{fig:curve}). Each collapse ends in a black hole: the 0.2c horizon settles at $R=2.524$ ($M_{\rm MS}=1.26$), the 0.30c at $R=2.567$ by $t=130$; the 0.40c's MOTS leaves the throat at $t=92$ and reads $R=3.22$, still shrinking, at $t=100$. In each case the hold is seeded only by discretization error, so the ordering of rates is the ordering of $|\omega_I|$.

\emph{The inflating branch.} At $v_e=0.456$ ($28.8\%$ of extremal) the fate changes: the areal radius dips to $-1.12\%$ at $t=83$, turns, crosses zero at $t=101$ and grows exponentially, $\tau=12.1$. The 0.5c behaves the same way ($-0.30\%$ at $t=65$--68, then $+6.9\%$ by $t=100$, $\tau=8.8$), and the MOTS is lost once a throat expands---no trapped surface forms. These are the two fates of one instability, and the seed's sign selects between them, exactly as declared kicks show at 0.2c (pushed in, the throat inflates; pushed out, it collapses; both pass $1\%$ at $t\approx25$ at the same rate): the 0.5c run whose absorbing shell gives the opposite effective seed shrinks instead ($-1\%$ at $t=99$). At the fast end the 0.9c throat is simply stable, its areal radius flat to $\le7\times10^{-5}$ over the full $t=150$ window at all three resolutions (Appendix~\ref{app:conv}).

\emph{Against the perturbative prediction.} Figure~\ref{fig:curve} puts both branches on one axis. The measured rate matches the second-order $C=1$ rates of Refs.~\cite{azad2023,azad2024} within $3\%$ at $v_e=0.30$ and 0.40 (5.9 vs 6.1, 8.0 vs 8.0). Where they conjecture the instability ends, we find instead that the mode survives the crossing with its magnitude intact and only its fate changed: the $28.8\%$ rung inflates with $\tau=12.1$ against their 13.2 ($9\%$), and the $33\%$ rung is still unstable where their second-order rate has already died. A quadratic fit through both branches reaches zero at $42\%$. The comparison is close but not like for like: their $\tau$ is a linear mode in Killing time, ours a nonlinear areal-radius fit.

\emph{Gravitational waves.} The rotating collapse radiates with no nonspherical perturbation put in (Fig.~\ref{fig:wave}): one $(2,0)$ burst at light speed, whose energy grows with spin as the amplitude squared, while the spherical static collapse radiates nothing. Rotation thus imprints itself on the burst a collapsing throat emits, before any horizon forms.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Three results stand. First, the spinning Ellis--Bronnikov parameter space is now mapped as data, not curves: both families from slow rotation to $v_e\approx0.99$, including the drainhole family for which nothing was published, with the published $\nu$ equation, Eq.~(12) of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}, corrected to Eq.~\eqref{eq:nueq}. The practical lesson generalizes: a solver gated only on the equations it imposes would have passed every test while solving the wrong system; the unimposed identities caught it. Second, the pipeline from these solutions to 3+1 evolution is validated at fourth order, and $O(J^2)$ initial data are shown to be unusable for stability work: their truncation error seeds the instability itself. Third, the first nonlinear evolutions of rotating wormholes show one rate with two fates: the radial collapse slows with spin ($\tau=4.4\to8.0$ from the static to $24\%$ of the extremal angular momentum), the sign predicted at $O(J^2)$~\cite{azad2023,azad2024}; near $30\%$, where the perturbative mode crossing was conjectured to end the instability, the unperturbed throat inflates instead, with the mode's magnitude intact; the measured rate extrapolates to zero at ${\approx}42\%$, and at $81\%$ the throat is stable to $t=150$ across three resolutions---answering, for this family, the four-dimensional stability question left open in Ref.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014}. The rotating collapse also radiates a $(2,0)$ burst from unperturbed, axisymmetric data, its energy growing with spin as the amplitude squared, while the static collapse radiates nothing. What remains on the ladder is in flight: the $v_e=0.51$--0.70 rungs on the large box, which fix where between $33\%$ and $81\%$ the instability actually dies---and whether it dies monotonically or as the oscillating complex pair of the charged analogue~\cite{blazquez2025}---and the end states of the inflating branch.

\section{Solver details}
\label{app:solver}

Derivatives are spectral, and each Newton step is a direct linear solve followed by the least-squares recovery of $\nu$ and $D^2$. With $J$ held fixed in each solve, $\omega_\infty$ joins the unknowns and one extra row imposes the target $J$ (Table~\ref{tab:anchors}). Steps are taken in $\log J$, predicted by the secant through the last two solutions, growing after fast Newton convergence and halving after slow convergence or a failure; the drainhole family needs a $162\times42$ grid by $v_e=0.95$. On the symmetric family the mirror identities are satisfied at the $10^{-14}$ level, including $\omega(0)=\omega_\infty/2$, and the equatorial ergosurface law of Ref.~\cite{chew2016} holds to $10^{-12}$ wherever the ergosurface sits at $\eta>0$ ($v_e\ge0.77$). The extremal-Kerr substitution of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12} is kept in the pipeline as a symbolic regression test.
