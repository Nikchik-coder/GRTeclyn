\begin{abstract}
We investigate the stability of spinning Ellis--Bronnikov wormholes using fully nonlinear 3+1 numerical relativity simulations, for two families of solutions: the symmetric family of Kleihaus and Kunz, and the massive drainhole family whose static member we evolved in our previous work. We reconstruct the stationary metrics with a spectral solver that is exact to ${\sim}10^{-10}$ at slow spin and ${\sim}10^{-7}$ near the extremal limit, correcting an inconsistency in a published field equation and mapping the previously unpublished parameter space of the massive family, and we verify fourth-order convergence of the 3D initial data. We find that rotation slows the radial instability: on exact backgrounds the slowly spinning drainhole collapses with an $e$-fold time of 6.8 against the static 5.8, and at rotation velocity $v_e=0.51$ the throat loses only $0.6\%$ of its radius over the simulation, where the static throat has long since formed a horizon. Second-order perturbative initial data instead seed the instability at the level of their own truncation error.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Ellis--Bronnikov wormhole, a throat held open by a phantom scalar field~\cite{ellis1973,bronnikov1973,morris_thorne1988,visser1995}, is unstable: depending on the sign of the perturbation it collapses to a black hole or inflates, on a timescale set by the throat radius~\cite{gonzalez2009lin,gonzalez2009,shinkai2002,bronnikov2011,cremona2019}. Nonlinear 3+1 evolutions confirm both branches for the lone throat~\cite{shirokov2026} and carry them into binaries, whose every channel ends in collapse, inflation or a merged horizon within a few dynamical times~\cite{shirokov2026c}. If traversable wormholes are to persist anywhere, something must shut this instability off.

Rotation is the oldest candidate~\cite{matos2006}. Spinning Ellis--Bronnikov solutions are known perturbatively~\cite{kashargin2008} and, beyond perturbation theory, from the elliptic solver of Kleihaus and Kunz~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016}: a family that starts at the static throat and ends, at equatorial rotation velocity $v_e=1$, on an extremal Kerr black hole, the phantom field vanishing in the limit; no closed form exists~\cite{volkov2021}. On the stability side, Azad, Bl\'azquez-Salcedo, Khoo and Kunz~\cite{azad2023,azad2024} carried the radial mode to second order in spin: the static unstable mode weakens, with a positive $O(J^2)$ correction for every asymmetry, while a second unstable mode grows out of a static zero mode; the two cross near $30\%$ of the limiting angular momentum, where the quadratic approximation gives out, and the authors conjecture the modes merge and the instability disappears, as purely imaginary modes do in five dimensions~\cite{dzhunushaliev2013}. The charged analogue warns against optimism: there the merged modes continue as a complex pair, growth that oscillates, and the growth rate reaches zero only in the extremal limit~\cite{blazquez2025}. Which continuation rotation takes in four dimensions is exactly the question a perturbative method cannot answer, and no rotating wormhole has ever been evolved nonlinearly.

This paper builds the tools for that evolution and takes its first step. We construct both families of backgrounds as exact stationary solutions: the symmetric family ($\gamma=0$), massless when static, which carries every published number---the curves of Ref.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014}, the scalar charges of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}, the mode structure of Refs.~\cite{azad2023,azad2024}---and so validates the pipeline; and the drainhole family ($\gamma=-\pi$), whose static member is the mass-1 drainhole evolved in Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}, for which the literature has no fast-spin data at all. The solver is gated on identities it never imposes, a discipline that pays immediately: one of the published field equations is inconsistent (Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12}), and second-order perturbative initial data turn out to seed the instability at their own error level (Sec.~\ref{sec:slow}). We then put the backgrounds on the AMR grid, verify them at fourth order, and evolve the drainhole's slow-spin end. The result so far is one-sided but clean: rotation slows the collapse, as the perturbative sign predicts.

Section~\ref{sec:model} gives the family and the corrected field equations; Sec.~\ref{sec:solver} the solver, the validation against the published data, and the map of the parameter space (Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds}); Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} the 3D initial data and the evolution protocol; Sec.~\ref{sec:slow} the slow-spin evolutions; Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusions} the conclusions. Appendix~\ref{app:solver} holds solver details and the comparison with the anchor solutions of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}. We use $G=c=1$; lengths are quoted in units of the throat parameter $\eta_0$.

\section{The spinning wormhole family}
\label{sec:model}

The matter model is that of our previous work~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}: Einstein gravity coupled to a phantom (ghost) scalar field $\psi$,
\begin{equation}
S=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(R+2\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\psi\,\partial_\nu\psi\right),
\label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
gives $R_{\mu\nu}=-2\,\partial_\mu\psi\,\partial_\nu\psi$. Those papers evolved its best-known static wormhole---the Ellis--Bronnikov drainhole with throat parameter $a=2$ and end masses $\pm1$---and measured the instability that this paper asks rotation to cure. Spinning that wormhole up requires the stationary rotating solution of the same theory, and none is known in closed form~\cite{volkov2021}: it has to be constructed numerically, inside an \emph{ansatz}---an assumed form of the metric with stationarity and axisymmetry built in and a few unknown functions left for the field equations to determine. We take the ansatz of Refs.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016}, who first constructed these solutions,
\begin{equation}
ds^2=-e^{f}dt^2+e^{-f}\!\left[e^{\nu}\!\left(d\eta^2+h\,d\theta^2\right)+h\sin^2\!\theta\left(d\varphi-\omega\,dt\right)^2\right],
\label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
with $\eta\in(-\infty,\infty)$. The fixed function $h=\eta^2+\eta_0^2$ is what makes this a wormhole ansatz. In the trivial limit $f=\nu=\omega=0$ the spatial metric is $d\eta^2+h\,d\Omega^2$, so $\sqrt{h}$ is the radius of the sphere at coordinate $\eta$---and by construction it never vanishes: it falls from $\eta^2$ at large $|\eta|$, bottoms out at $\eta_0$ at $\eta=0$, and rises again. The spacetime therefore has no center; a minimal sphere of radius set by $\eta_0$, the throat, joins the two asymptotically flat ends $\eta\to\pm\infty$. Choosing $h$ hard-wires this two-ended, horizonless topology into Eq.~\eqref{eq:metric}, while $f$, $\nu$, $\omega$ deform the geometry on top of it ($h$ also recurs as the weight in every equation below); conversely, $\eta_0\to0$ closes the throat and lets the same ansatz hold a black hole, which is how extremal Kerr enters in Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12}. Our previous wormhole sits inside this form: with $\omega=\nu=0$ and the closed-form $\arctan$ profile for $f$~\cite{chew2016}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:metric} is the static drainhole of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}---$\eta$ is the isotropic radius $r$ used there, unfolded through the throat by $\eta=r-\eta_0^2/4r$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}), and $\eta_0=a$ its throat parameter---while $f=0$ gives the massless Ellis wormhole. Everything the ansatz~\eqref{eq:metric} leaves open---the metric functions $f(\eta,\theta)$ (the redshift of the two ends), $\omega(\eta,\theta)$ (the frame dragging) and $\nu(\eta,\theta)$, together with the phantom field---must come from the field equations: determining them at each spin is what Sec.~\ref{sec:solver} is for. Substituting the ansatz~\eqref{eq:metric} into the field equations reduces them in four steps. The reduction is not ours: it is the recipe of Kleihaus and Kunz~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014}, derived in full in Sec.~II of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}, whose equation numbers we quote; we retrace it here in words only. First, the equation for a fourth metric function $p$, usually carried alongside $f$, $\nu$, $\omega$, decouples from the rest and is solved by $p=1$ under flat boundary conditions, eliminating it from the start [Eqs.~(5)--(6) there]. Second, with $p=1$ the phantom field equation---the curved-space wave equation, which stationarity and axisymmetry reduce to a function of $\eta$ alone---integrates in closed form to $\psi=D\left[\arctan(\eta/\eta_0)-\pi/2\right]$ [Eqs.~(7)--(9) there]: the scalar enters the rest of the problem only through its constant of integration $D$, the scalar charge. Third, because $\partial_\mu\psi$ points along $\eta$, the source term $-2\,\partial_\mu\psi\,\partial_\nu\psi$ survives only in the $\eta\eta$ Einstein equation; each other nontrivial component ($tt$, $t\varphi$, $\theta\theta$, $\varphi\varphi$, $\eta\theta$) is a statement about the metric functions alone, and these organize by order and role. Two are second-order elliptic equations,
\begin{align}
0&=\partial_\eta\!\left(h s\,\partial_\eta f\right)+\partial_\theta\!\left(s\,\partial_\theta f\right)
-h s^3 e^{-2f}\!\left[h(\partial_\eta\omega)^2+(\partial_\theta\omega)^2\right],
\label{eq:feq}\\
0&=\partial_\eta\!\left(h^2 s^3 e^{-2f}\partial_\eta\omega\right)+\partial_\theta\!\left(h s^3 e^{-2f}\partial_\theta\omega\right),
\label{eq:omeq}
\end{align}
with $s\equiv\sin\theta$ [Eqs.~(10)--(11) of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}]: Eq.~\eqref{eq:feq} is a Laplace-type equation for the redshift $f$, sourced by the squared gradients of the frame dragging---rotation gravitates---and Eq.~\eqref{eq:omeq} is a total divergence, the local form of angular-momentum conservation, which integrated over the domain forces the same $J$ through every $\eta=\mathrm{const}$ surface. The single off-diagonal component is only first order in $\nu$,
\begin{equation}
0=-h s\,\partial_\eta f\,\partial_\theta f+h\cos\theta\,\partial_\eta\nu+\eta s\,\partial_\theta\nu
+h^2 s^3 e^{-2f}\partial_\eta\omega\,\partial_\theta\omega,
\label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}
so $\nu$ follows by integration once $f$ and $\omega$ are known [Eq.~(13) there]. (The remaining diagonal components give a second-order equation for $\nu$ as well---the published Eq.~(12), shown misprinted in Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12}; we never use it.) Fourth, the one sourced component, $R_{\eta\eta}=-2D^2/h^2$ [Eq.~(18) there], rearranges into an algebraic identity for the charge [Eq.~(19) there]: the metric functions fix $D^2$ at every point, so its constancy over the domain is a solution test that comes for free. Three unknown functions thus face more equations than they need; the Bianchi identities make the overdetermined set consistent on a true solution, and a numerical scheme must choose which subset to impose---a choice Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12} shows matters. The boundary conditions select the family: Minkowski at $\eta\to+\infty$, and $f\to\gamma$, $\omega\to\omega_\infty$ at $\eta\to-\infty$, so the constant $\gamma$ measures how unequal the two ends are and $\omega_\infty$ sets the spin.

A solution of this system is three functions and one number, $(f,\nu,\omega;D)$. Every physical quantity we quote---the columns of Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds}---is read off them directly~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016}:
\begin{itemize}
\item far from the throat the metric settles to the field of a point mass, $f\to-2\mu_+/\eta$ as $\eta\to+\infty$ and $f\to\gamma+2\mu_-/\eta$ as $\eta\to-\infty$, so the end masses $\mu_\pm$ are the $1/\eta$ coefficients of $f$;
\item likewise $\omega\to2J/\eta^3$, and the conservation law~\eqref{eq:omeq} forces the same $J$ on both ends; the charges obey the Smarr-type relation $\mu_++\mu_-=2\,\omega_\infty J$~\cite{chew2016}, which the solver never imposes and uses as an acceptance gate;
\item the throat is the minimal sphere at $\eta=0$: its equatorial circumference gives the radius $R_e=e^{-f/2}\sqrt{h}\,\big|_{\eta=0,\,\theta=\pi/2}$, its meridians the polar radius $R_p$, its area the areal radius $R_A$---three numbers that coincide while the throat is round and split as rotation flattens it;
\item the throat is dragged at the angular velocity $\Omega=\omega(\eta{=}0)$, and $v_e=R_e\,\Omega$ is its equatorial linear velocity in units of $c$: the dimensionless spin label used throughout, 0 when static and 1 at the extremal Kerr endpoint.
\end{itemize}
The same read-offs, applied to the 3D grid data instead of the solver's functions, are how Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} verifies the initial data against Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds}.

Two one-parameter families, labelled by $\omega_\infty$ (equivalently $J$ or $v_e$), are evolved in this work:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] \emph{the drainhole family}, $\eta_0=2$, $\gamma=-\pi$: its static member is the Ellis drainhole with throat parameter $a=2$ and unequal end masses $\mu_+=1$, $\mu_-=-1$, the wormhole of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c} (the asymmetry $C=1$ of Refs.~\cite{azad2023,azad2024});
\item[(b)] \emph{the symmetric family}, $\gamma=0$ ($C=0$): equal masses on both ends, massless and identical to the Ellis wormhole when static; the family of Refs.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016} and the only one for which the second mode and the mode crossing of Ref.~\cite{azad2023} are published. For evolution it is rescaled $\eta_0\,{=}\,1\to2$, so its throat sits on family (a)'s grid.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{An inconsistency in the published field equations}
\label{sec:model:eq12}

Refs.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016} close the system with a second-order equation for $\nu$, Eq.~(12) of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}. That equation is inconsistent with the rest. Extremal Kerr fits the ansatz~\eqref{eq:metric} in closed form (with $\eta_0=0$), and substituted symbolically, at 160-digit arithmetic, it satisfies Eqs.~\eqref{eq:feq}--\eqref{eq:constraint} and the $D^2$ identity exactly---while leaving an $O(10^{-2})$ residue in the printed $\nu$ equation alone. The error also shows dynamically: solutions of the printed elliptic system converge at every resolution, yet carry a violation of the unimposed Eq.~\eqref{eq:constraint} and $D^2$ identity that scales as $J^4$ and does not shrink with the grid---the fingerprint of a wrong continuous equation, not of numerics (first seen as a spread of $5.5\times10^{-7}$ in $D^2$ at $J=0.25$). We therefore never solve the printed $\nu$ equation: $\nu$ and $D^2$ are obtained jointly from Eq.~\eqref{eq:constraint} and the $D^2$ identity, and the printed equation is kept only as a reported diagnostic. Every published conclusion of Refs.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014,chew2016} that we can test survives the correction (Sec.~\ref{sec:solver}, Appendix~\ref{app:solver}); the published positive-asymmetry scalar charges do not (Appendix~\ref{app:solver}).

The solver turns the system of Sec.~\ref{sec:model} into numbers in five steps; Appendix~\ref{app:solver} gives the details. First, the infinite domain is made finite: the coordinate $x=(2/\pi)\arctan(\eta/\eta_0)$ maps both asymptotic ends to $x=\pm1$, and the unknowns $f$ and $\omega$ are expanded in Chebyshev polynomials of $x$ and even cosine modes of $\theta$. The expansion is spectral: for smooth solutions the error falls exponentially with the number of modes, so modest grids reach near machine precision. Second, the two elliptic equations~\eqref{eq:feq}--\eqref{eq:omeq} are solved for the expansion coefficients by Newton iteration---guess, linearize, correct---until their residual sits at round-off. Third, $\nu$ and the scalar charge are recovered without ever touching the misprinted second-order $\nu$ equation: the first-order constraint~\eqref{eq:constraint} and the $D^2$ identity together overdetermine $\nu$ and the single number $D^2$, so both are extracted as one linear least-squares problem---and the redundancy is a feature, since the leftover misfit measures the error. Fourth, each family is walked in spin: every converged solution seeds the Newton iteration at a slightly higher spin, with $J$ as the continuation parameter ($\omega_\infty$ is not monotone along a family; Appendix~\ref{app:solver}), and the grid refines automatically, from $48\times12$ up to $240\times60$, whenever the acceptance gates fail. Fifth---the point of the whole design---a solution is accepted only on identities the solver never imposes: the spread of $D^2$ over the domain ($\le10^{-11}$), the Smarr relation ($\le10^{-10}$), equality of the angular-momentum flux through the two ends ($\le1.3\times10^{-9}$), the asymptotic tails ($\le5\times10^{-12}$), and an independent, symbolically generated residual of the full Einstein equations, evaluated out to 20 throat radii: $1.0\times10^{-10}$ at $J=0.25$ and $4.2\times10^{-10}$ at $J=1$ against the static member's $2\times10^{-11}$ round-off floor---where the $O(J^2)$ perturbative data give $3\times10^{-5}$ and $1\times10^{-2}$ on the same meshes---staying near $10^{-9}$ up to $v_e\approx0.85$ and reaching ${\sim}10^{-7}$ at $v_e=0.91$--$0.99$. The high-spin $10^{-7}$ is round-off, not solution error: it does not shrink on finer grids, while the mass and scalar charge come out identical to 12 digits across them; and the test does catch genuine failures, scoring a deliberately under-resolved solution at $10^{-4}$, which the resolution check rejects. Beyond 20 throat radii the residual's normalization inflates round-off and the test is switched off; that outer region is covered by the resolution study, by the end charges, and by the 3D constraint checks of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}. The products: 462 converged solutions over 20 continuation legs, global charges stable to 10 digits across three grids, and, for evolution, each background exported as a $1025\times65$ table whose bilinear interpolation error is $\le10^{-7}$ up to $v_e=0.9$.

Figure~\ref{fig:kk2014} is the validation: in the normalization of Ref.~\cite{kleihaus_kunz2014}, our symmetric family lands on the digitized curves of their Fig.~1 to the digitization's own reading error. Their four anchor solutions at $\omega_\infty=0.5$~\cite{chew2016} split: the $\gamma=0$ and $\gamma=-1$ scalar charges agree to all printed digits, while $\gamma=1$ and $\gamma=2$ differ by $2\times10^{-4}$ and $6.6\times10^{-3}$ from our grid-converged values---their finite-difference error, growing with $\gamma$ (Appendix~\ref{app:solver}).

Figure~\ref{fig:trends} and Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds} are the map of the parameter space the foundation papers left open. Published data exist only for the symmetric family and only as figures; the massive drainhole family---the one whose static member has been evolved, and the one from which binaries can be built~\cite{shirokov2026c}---is mapped here from slow spin to $v_e=0.99$. Two of its features matter later. First, both families violate the Kerr bound: the symmetric family everywhere ($J/\mu_+^2\approx85$ already at $v_e=0.24$, falling monotonically to 1 at extremality, since its static member is massless), the drainhole family by up to $\sim20\%$ at intermediate spin. A collapsing overspun throat cannot settle to a Kerr black hole of the same mass and spin, which makes these backgrounds sharp cosmic-censorship initial data. Second, the drainhole's negative far-end mass $\mu_-$ rises through zero at $v_e\approx0.7$ and turns positive: fast rotation un-does the static drainhole's negative-mass end.

\section{3D initial data and evolution setup}
\label{sec:setup}

The stationary metric~\eqref{eq:metric} goes onto the Cartesian AMR grid through the isotropic radius $r$, $\eta=r-\eta_0^2/4r$: the far end $\eta\to-\infty$ compactifies to the puncture $r=0$, as for the static drainhole~\cite{shirokov2026}. The induced metric and the fields are read from the tables; the lapse and shift start from the stationary data, $\alpha=e^{f/2}$, $\beta^\varphi=-\omega$, so the extrinsic curvature $K_{ij}=(D_i\beta_j+D_j\beta_i)/2\alpha$ has only $r\varphi$ and $\theta\varphi$ components and the scalar momentum vanishes; at the puncture the frame rotates at $\omega_\infty$. Evolution uses \texttt{GRTeclyn}~\cite{grteclyn,amrex,grchombo} with the setup of Refs.~\cite{shirokov2026,shirokov2026c}: CCZ4~\cite{alic2012}, $1{+}\log$ slicing~\cite{bona1995}, Gamma-driver shift~\cite{alcubierre2003gauge}, fourth-order stencils, RK4, Kreiss--Oliger dissipation, $\Delta t=0.02\,\Delta x$, and a floored conformal factor at the puncture.

Stability claims follow the triplet protocol of Ref.~\cite{evstafyeva2025}: every quoted growth must have an $e$-fold time that converges to a nonzero value across three resolutions, constraints that converge at the code's order while it grows, and a gauge-invariant measure---the throat's areal radii, or the marginally outer-trapped surface (MOTS) found spectrally on every output---growing with it. The production grid is a box of half-size $L=128$ with three nested refinements on the throat, run at $N=192/256/384$ base points (finest $\Delta x=0.083/0.0625/0.042$); the evolutions reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:slow} ran on the $L=64$, $N=128$ grid of the static hold of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026c} (same finest $\Delta x=0.0625$), whose outer-boundary return sets their trust window at $t\approx64$.

Three checks validate the pipeline. (i) At $t=0$, on the symmetric family at seven spins $v_e=0.24$--$0.99$ and three resolutions each, the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints converge at fourth order inside the throat region and are limited outside only by the tables' interpolation error (growing as $1/\Delta x^2$ and $1/\Delta x$ respectively); the throat's ray radii converge to the solver's, within $7\times10^{-5}$ ($v_e=0.24$) to $9\times10^{-4}$ ($0.99$) at $N=256$. (ii) The 3D ADM mass integral evaluated on the grid equals the same integral on the exact table metric to $\le10^{-12}$, and its Richardson extrapolation meets the solver's mass to $2\times10^{-5}$--$7\times10^{-4}$ (at worst $0.7\%$ relative, at $v_e=0.99$); the ADM angular momentum matches to between $2\times10^{-3}$ ($v_e=0.24$) and $2\times10^{-5}$ ($0.99$). (iii) Dynamically, the static drainhole run through this pipeline is a numerical twin of the production hold of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026c} over the full record: $t=0$ bit-identical, Hamiltonian norm within $5\times10^{-7}$ for all $t\le100$, collapse milestones within one time unit, and a fitted $e$-fold time $\tau(\delta R)=5.27$ against 5.12 there.

\section{Slow spin: rotation slows the collapse}
\label{sec:slow}

\emph{Perturbative initial data fail first.} Our first spinning holds used the $O(J^2)$ backgrounds of Ref.~\cite{azad2024} at $J=0.25$ and $J=1$ on the drainhole family. Both throats inflated where the static throat collapses---the same unstable mode with the opposite sign ($\tau=5.8$ and $5.6$). But expressed as the equivalent radial kick of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026c}, the push was $\varepsilon\approx-3\times10^{-5}$ at $J=0.25$ and $-7\times10^{-3}$ at $J=1$: growth by a factor ${\sim}230$ for a factor 4 in $J$, i.e.\ ${\sim}J^4$---the size of the $O(J^2)$ data's own truncation error, which the independent Einstein residual puts at $10^{-2}$ at $J=1$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:solver}). Second-order data seed the very instability under study at their own error level; nothing at slow spin can be concluded from them.

\emph{On exact data the throat collapses, more slowly the faster it spins.} On the solver tables the verdict reverses. The $J=1$ drainhole ($v_e=0.213$) collapses on the static mode: the equatorial radius falls $1\%$ by $t=46.9$ and $10\%$ by $t=61.8$, against the static hold's $t=44.1$ and $57.0$---an $e$-fold time of 6.8 against 5.8 by the same measure. At $v_e=0.513$ the collapse slows much further: the MOTS radius is down only $0.61\%$ at $t=64$, the end of the trust window, with no horizon formed---where the static throat had shrunk $2.5\%$ by $t=49$ and detached a horizon at $t\approx61$. Constraints hold at the static run's level throughout, and in each case the unperturbed hold is seeded only by discretization error, so the ordering of rates is the ordering of $|\omega_I|$.

\emph{Against the perturbative prediction.} The static rate itself checks the linear theory: $\tau(\delta R)=5.27$ against $\tau\approx5.1$ from the $C=1$ static mode of Ref.~\cite{azad2023} at $\eta_0=2$. For the spin trend, Ref.~\cite{azad2023} predicts $\Delta\omega_I^{(2)}>0$ for every asymmetry---the main unstable mode weakens with spin. The measured sequence $\tau=5.8\;(J=0)\to6.8\;(v_e=0.21)\to{>}\,$several tens $(v_e=0.51)$ is that prediction's sign, now established nonlinearly and, at $v_e=0.51$, far beyond the formal reach of the $O(J^2)$ expansion.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Three results stand. First, the spinning Ellis--Bronnikov parameter space is now mapped as data, not curves: both families, from slow rotation to $v_e\approx0.99$, as solutions exact to the $10^{-10}$ level at slow spin and the $10^{-7}$ level near extremality (Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds}), including the massive drainhole family for which nothing was published---and including the correction of a field equation, Eq.~(12) of Ref.~\cite{chew2016}, that closed-form extremal Kerr convicts directly. The practical lesson generalizes: a solver gated only on the equations it imposes would have passed every test while solving the wrong system; the unimposed identities caught it. Second, the pipeline from these solutions to 3+1 evolution is validated at fourth order, with the static limit a numerical twin of the production run of Ref.~\cite{shirokov2026c}, and $O(J^2)$ initial data are shown to be unusable for stability work: their truncation error seeds the instability itself. Third, the first nonlinear evolutions of rotating wormholes show the radial collapse surviving but slowing with spin---$\tau=5.8\to6.8$ from $J=0$ to $v_e=0.21$, and an order-of-magnitude slower decay at $v_e=0.51$---the sign predicted at $O(J^2)$~\cite{azad2023,azad2024}, now beyond perturbation theory. Whether the two predicted modes merge and the instability disappears near a third of the limiting spin, or continues as an oscillating complex pair as in the charged analogue~\cite{blazquez2025}, is now a question the validated pipeline can put to the backgrounds of Table~\ref{tab:backgrounds} directly.

The unknowns $f,\omega$ are expanded in Chebyshev polynomials of $x=(2/\pi)\arctan(\eta/\eta_0)\in[-1,1]$, which compactifies both ends, and in even cosine modes of $\theta$; derivatives are spectral, and the nonlinear system \eqref{eq:feq}--\eqref{eq:omeq} is solved by Newton iteration with a direct linear solve. At each Newton step, $\nu$ and the constant $D^2$ are recovered together from the overdetermined pair---the first-order constraint~\eqref{eq:constraint} and the $D^2$ identity---as one linear least-squares problem, which is what makes the acceptance gates of Sec.~\ref{sec:solver} meaningful: the reported $D^2$ spread, Smarr defect, flux equality and full-Einstein residual are all computed from equations that never entered the solve. Spin is walked by pseudo-arclength continuation in $(\text{state},J)$, since $\omega_\infty$ is not monotone along a family (Table~\ref{tab:anchors}); grids refine automatically from $48\times12$ when the acceptance gates demand it, to $162\times42$ at $v_e=0.95$ on the drainhole family. On the symmetric family the mirror identities are satisfied at the $10^{-14}$ level, including $\omega(0)=\omega_\infty/2$, and the equatorial ergosurface law of Ref.~\cite{chew2016} holds to $10^{-12}$ wherever the ergosurface sits at $\eta>0$ ($v_e\ge0.77$). The extremal-Kerr substitution of Sec.~\ref{sec:model:eq12} is kept in the pipeline as a symbolic regression test.
