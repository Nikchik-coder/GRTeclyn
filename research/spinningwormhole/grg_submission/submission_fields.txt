Rotation slows the instability of Ellis-Bronnikov wormholes

ABSTRACT
Rotation weakens the radial instability of Ellis-Bronnikov wormholes in slow-rotation perturbation theory. We investigate its nonlinear effect by evolving stationary rotating drainholes supported by a phantom scalar field. We construct the backgrounds with an independent spectral solver, validate them against published symmetric solutions, and identify a misprint in a published field equation that did not affect the original authors' numerical results. Evolutions perturbed only by discretization error show that rotation slows the collapse. At intermediate spins some throats expand, with the direction of departure depending on the effective perturbation. At the fastest evolved spin, the throat's areal radius shows no growing departure through evolution time t = 150 in code units, about 28 e-folding times of the static instability, at three resolutions. Two other rapidly rotating models also show no growth over their reliable evolution intervals. Collapsing rotating throats emit an axisymmetric quadrupolar gravitational-wave burst whose energy increases between the two spins compared. Second-order initial data excite the instability through their background truncation error. The results establish slower collapse and prolonged absence of detected radial growth for the sampled asymmetric backgrounds, over finite times from initially axisymmetric data.
